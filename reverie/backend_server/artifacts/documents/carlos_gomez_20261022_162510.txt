Creative Writing Workshop — Peer Feedback  
Participant Poem: “The River Remembers”  
Reviewer: Carlos Gomez  
February 13

Hey,

I read your poem twice, which is more attention than most people deserve, so consider yourself lucky. The central image—the river carrying memory—is obvious, maybe painfully obvious, but you clearly want to say something about time, loss, and the way the past refuses to stay buried. That’s decent. Not revolutionary, but decent. I’ll say that much.

The biggest problem is your language. You keep reaching for fancy words when plain ones would hit harder. “The river remembers” is a strong opening, but then you pile on phrases like “ancient wounds whispered through silver veins.” Come on. That sounds like every moody poem written under a gray sky. Be specific. Tell me what the river remembers: a flooded basement, a red shoe, the smell of rain on hot pavement. Sharp details make abstract grief feel real.
