The Trees Do Not Ask Permission

Monday, February 13

The river barges through granite  
with the arrogant confidence  
of a man who owns the moon.  
I lean over the bank and listen:  
water grinding its teeth,  
wind combing branches,  
every leaf insisting  
that it matters.

I came here to steal a few bright words,  
but the moss has no vocabulary,  
and still it covers everything  
like a green fire  
too patient for flame.

By evening, my hunger  
had curled itself into hunger—  
not for food,  
for something sharper,  
something with an edge  
that can cut through a room  
when my voice gets too loud  
and my words slam the walls.

I wrote this under a sycamore,  
its bark peeling in dead languages.  
I pressed my palm against the trunk  
and felt the pulse beneath it:  
slow, brown, and certain.

Let the poem be clear.  
Let every line sing.  
Let it hit the chest  
hard enough to leave a bruise.

I signed my name  
in the wet earth,  
then watched the river  
take it without...
