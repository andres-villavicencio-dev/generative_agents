Workshop Draft — Nature Collection

The forest wakes in rust and rain,  
its green mouth swallowing the pale sun.  
Every branch argues with the wind;  
every root grips the dark earth  
like a clenched fist beneath the soil.  

I wrote this version because the first one was weak—plain, embarrassingly plain, like calling a storm “a little rain.” Stronger images, I was told. More force. Fine. I can do force. The pines thrust their needles upward, sharp as accusations. Moss crawls over fallen logs in slow, emerald fire. Somewhere hidden in the branches, a bird cuts the morning open with one bright, impossible note.

Beneath the trees, the creek mutters over stones,  
chasing their silver bodies downhill.  
It sounds impatient, almost rude,  
like someone refusing to wait  
for the water to catch up.  

I like that image. It gives the creek a temper. The whole poem needs teeth—not teeth like a predator, but teeth like roots, teeth like branches, things that dig in and hold on.
