THE DESK BLEEDS INK

I cleared the desk today—  
threw the old envelopes into the dark,  
the dead stubs of cigarettes,  
the crumbs that had forgotten  
where they came from.

What a beautiful mess!  
The room stinks of stale coffee  
and the ash of yesterday’s anger,  
but the window insists on spring.  
Light crawls across the floorboards  
like a yellow dog  
begging for attention.

Outside, the trees are rehearsing  
their green, their ancient rustling script.  
No one asks them to be quiet.  
No one tells them their roots  
are twisted and inconvenient.

I want to write a poem  
with no wasted motion—  
a poem that smells of rain  
on hot pavement,  
of pollen clinging to the tongue,  
of birds arguing  
in a language brighter than mine.

But first, I must tame this machine.  
Its keys are small white teeth,  
ready to bite.  
I press them down  
and the paper begins to tremble.

Let the whole world hear it:  
I am still angry,  
still loud, still difficult.  
Still here.
