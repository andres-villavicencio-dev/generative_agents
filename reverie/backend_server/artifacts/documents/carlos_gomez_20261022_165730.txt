The Earth Keeps No Receipts

Monday morning, and the sun  
cracks over the hills  
like a landlord knocking.  
I feed the paper, tighten the ribbon,  
and tell the world to wait  
while I make something honest  
from all its borrowed shine.

The trees stand there, green and smug,  
drinking rain like whiskey,  
spelling “survival”  
with every crooked branch.  
A bird throws one bright syllable  
into the wires  
and ruins the silence  
for the sake of showing off.

I want to call this beautiful.  
I want to kneel beneath the weather,  
press my hands into the mud,  
and thank the dirt  
for carrying my weight.  
But tenderness, once misplaced,  
looks like poison on a spoon,  
so I write with my teeth:  
The forest grows, and so do wounds.  
The river bends because it cannot forgive.

By noon, the page will smell  
of oil, dust, and oak.  
By evening, the garden will flare  
red and impossible,  
a fire behaving  
like the heart I refuse to name.
