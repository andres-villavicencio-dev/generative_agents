THE WORLD SPITS GREEN

The world spits green  
from between the teeth of concrete,  
a stubborn rumor of roots  
in the dark.

I walk through the park  
at six in the morning,  
slamming my shoes  
against the sidewalk like punctuation.  
The trees stand there,  
all green elbows and stolen sunlight,  
looking smug,  
looking alive,  
as if chlorophyll  
were not a little act of magic.

Give me one blackbird  
and I’ll forgive the whole morning.  
Give me one feather  
falling without permission,  
and I’ll call this city beautiful,  
though my voice wakes the pigeons  
and makes the river nervous.

I love the wild world  
in the way I love a fight:  
loud, bruised,  
beautiful in the middle of being too much.  
I love how moss drinks  
from the north side of a stone,  
how nightshade dresses  
in secrets,  
how the desert keeps  
one gold cup hidden  
behind every dry throat.

The oak holds its rusty fists  
and never explains.  
The moon leans down  
with its pale, careless face. ...
