FIRST STANZA — “THE WORLD WON’T QUIET”

The sun cracks open like a jealous fist,  
spilling gold across the waking hills;  
the river argues with the stones,  
then smooths its anger into silver threads.  
Birds stitch their bright voices through the branches—  
sharp, triumphant, impossible to ignore.  

I stand beneath the trees with ink-black hands,  
smelling rain in the loosened soil,  
while clouds drag their heavy skirts  
across the bruised and burning sky.  
Every leaf demands attention.  
Every wind rehearses a rumor.  
Even the old road, cracked and stubborn,  
refuses to say where it has been.  

Nature does not whisper, no.  
It bellows through the grass,  
kicks at the windows,  
shakes the orchard loose of its rotten fruit.  
I love it for that honesty—  
for every thorn, every thunderclap,  
every fist of flower opening late.  

So I will type until the page trembles,  
until the rhythm breaks like a stone  
beneath the heel of a careless season.
