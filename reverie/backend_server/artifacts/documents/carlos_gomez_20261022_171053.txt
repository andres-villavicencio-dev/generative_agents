The World Will Not Look Twice

I have watched the river lose its name  
beneath a skin of silver rain,  
and I have heard the pines complain  
of axes dressed in human shame.  

The mountains keep their ancient scars,  
the valleys breathe beneath the stars,  
while every restless, bleeding sun  
burns through the day and calls it done.  

I do not ask the trees for mercy.  
I do not beg the wind to hold.  
The earth has no command from me—  
but I can steal a fragment’s gold.  

I write because the silence bites,  
because my thoughts arrive like storms,  
because the night unfolds its dark  
and asks me how I change its forms.  

Let the gulls cry over salted glass,  
let winter sharpen every stream:  
I will not kneel to make it last.  
The world has flashes, not a dream.  

And when the final page lies still,  
when all my noisy words are gone,  
the forest will remain,  
and I will count its light  
until the ink has carried on.
