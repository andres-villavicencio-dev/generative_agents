THE WORLD DOESN’T WAIT FOR QUIET

The city kicked me awake at seven,  
traffic grinding teeth beneath the windows,  
red lights ugly as old bruises.  
I shoved my poem into the typewriter—  
ten clean fingers, no friends,  
a machine that coughs black ink  
when the whole world gets too loud.

I wrote: The street is crowded,
then crossed it out.  
Crowded means soft.  
Crowded means hiding.  
The street was hunting.

By five, the sky had bruised purple,  
and hunger made a fist inside my ribs.  
I swallowed the day in bitter little pieces,  
then took my notebook to the river,  
where water moved without apology,  
cold and honest,  
cutting through the reflected towers  
like a blade through cheap stage curtains.

On the other bank, a heron stood  
perfectly still,  
a white feathered fist  
against the green.

I wrote again: The river does not hurry.  
Clang. Clang. Clang.  
The words sounded like wings.
