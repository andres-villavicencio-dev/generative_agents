The Tree Remembers Rain

The typewriter rattles like a loose-toothed dog,
each key strike biting through the quiet.
Outside, the February branches
scratch their black nails across the sky.

I smell wet bark, crushed leaves,
the cold metallic breath of soil.
A robin coughs in the hedge,
red and furious, demanding silence.

The oak remembers every rain—
the silver hammer of April,
the slow green flood of June,
winter’s cracked and brittle hands.

I wish I were that careless,
letting roots drink darkness,
letting wind pull loose
whatever has grown sharp inside me.

At 10, the machine will sleep.
The night will fold its heavy coat
around the garden, around the stars,
around the small fierce pulse
of worms beneath the frozen earth.

By morning, light will pale the windows.
I will touch the keys again,
angry as a crow, as tender as rain,
pressing my voice into the page—
a root seeking soil,
a branch refusing to forget the sky.
