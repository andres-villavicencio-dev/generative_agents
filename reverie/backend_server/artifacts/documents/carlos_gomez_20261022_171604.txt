The Lake at Dawn

The lake wakes before the sun,
stretching silver beneath the pines;
mist curls around its quiet lungs,
and swallows stitch the waiting sky.
I came here to escape the noise—
the traffic in my skull, the sharp words
I toss like stones into dark water.
Still, the lake refuses to hide.
It holds every loose reflection:
my anger, pale and trembling;
my grief, green beneath the surface;
the hard, bright shame I cannot name.

Now I lean against the cold rail
while morning spills its gold across the hills.
The trees do not forgive my curses.
The wind moves through the branches,
steady, rude with truth:
That I am small, and tired, and worth the water.
That every wound is sheltered here.
I watch the rough bark loosen
one green thread at a time,
and type until the birds begin again.
