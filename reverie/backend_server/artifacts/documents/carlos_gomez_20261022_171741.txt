THE WORLD WON’T SIT STILL

Monday morning rattles through the window,  
a tin can full of crooked light.  
I drink the bitter dark,  
let coffee burn my tongue,  
and blame the sun for being too bright.

I want a poem that moves  
like water down a stone,  
but the river keeps its secrets,  
and so do I.  
The trees stand there, green and smug,  
their leaves applauding my bad mood.

I tell the flowers to stop showing off.  
I tell the rain to hurry up  
and get this miserable weather  
off my shoulders.  
Still, the birds keep singing—  
bright, reckless little brats—  
as if joy were something simple.

By five, the sky turns copper.  
I type until the words go soft,  
until the letters lose their edges  
and the page begins to breathe.  
Perhaps that is what the earth does too:  
opens itself again and again,  
even to the loud, the rude,  
the ones who never learned  
how to say a thing gently.

Outside, the wind rehearses a new poem.  
I steal its first line.
