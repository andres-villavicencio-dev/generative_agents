Problem Statement: Imagine Our Neighborhood as a Living Creative Community

At Oak Hill College, physics has taught me how systems move, how forces shape motion, and how energy can be redirected. I’ve also learned how a Twitch stream can become a way for strangers to connect and discover each other’s interests. This experience is making me question what makes a community feel alive: shared space, creative expression, and the willingness to help others see possibilities they cannot see alone.

In our neighborhood, we have enough to work toward. We have sidewalks, parks, community centers, and local businesses. But we also have too many barriers to creativity: limited space, conflicting uses of public areas, and a tendency to take the neighborhood for granted. My community creativity project asks, “What if every resident contributed to making our neighborhood more imaginative?”
