Ana Abad designed The Rose and Crown Café, a contemporary Dominican-inspired café that combines warm local aesthetics with modern hospitality. The building’s main facade features exposed brick and terracotta accents, with a prominent entrance framed by a curved wooden arch. A large central window displays an inviting counter and seating area, while the entrance includes a welcoming sign, a glass door, and a small porch with a bench and lantern.

The café’s entrance system is organized for efficient customer flow: a wide opening accommodates carts and groups, with separate access for guests, service staff, and deliveries. A visible entrance path leads to the counter, while side doors provide access to service areas and storage. The space uses durable materials such as concrete, wood, and metal, with warm lighting and artwork displaying local dishes and community stories.

The core innovation is the café’s menu and service workflow, centered around authentic Dominican recipes, including a new plantain dish and a plantain-based coffee. Ana and her team tested the recipes, adjusting flavors and cooking times while using readily available ingredients. The menu balances familiar favorites with inventive creations, encouraging repeat visits and community interaction.

The café was created because Ana wanted to turn her café experience into a community hub. Its simple, welcoming design and strong local food identity make it feel approachable and memorable. By combining familiar traditions with practical hospitality and careful budgeting, the café is not only a place to eat but also a model for inclusive, neighborhood-based dining.
