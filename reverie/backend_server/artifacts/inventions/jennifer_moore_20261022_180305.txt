To prepare for the exhibition, I built a simple cataloging system for my finished watercolor paintings. It works like a large, carefully organized reference book: each painting receives a number, and I record its title, dimensions, medium, and completion date. I also note the series or theme, the materials used, and where each work is stored. Short notes describe the inspiration, while photographs or digital images show the composition and help me compare works.

The catalog is made with acid-free archival paper, sturdy card-stock dividers, a printed grid, protective sleeves, and color-coded labels. A digital spreadsheet backs up the information, and a small file box holds prints and sketches. I created it because, after more than fifty years of painting, my body of work had become too large and varied to remember accurately by sight alone. The system makes selecting, transporting, and installing the exhibition easier while preserving the history of each painting. It also allows me to share clear information with younger artists whom I mentor, showing them that thoughtful documentation is an important part of preserving creative work.
