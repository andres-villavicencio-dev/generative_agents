Maria built a Park Trail Pathway Planner, a small digital tool designed to help hikers organize their route, manage time, and make informed choices along the park’s main trails. The app uses a simple three-step process: it asks the user to enter the starting point and finish destination, then suggests nearby trail options based on distance, elevation, difficulty, and nearby facilities. For each option, it calculates total travel time, estimated walking distance, and a suggested pace. Maria chose to make the planner accessible to people who may be unfamiliar with the park’s layout, so she included clear color-coded maps, estimated wait times, and links to nearby cafés, restrooms, and parking areas.

She created it using paper trail maps, a tablet, and her organized notebook, combining her journalism and fundraising background with her creative craft. She made the tool more useful for newcomers by adding a “newcomer mode” that highlights unfamiliar paths and lists helpful local resources. Maria designed it because she had noticed how confusing park trails can make walking feel exhausting, and she wanted to help people enjoy the outdoors without getting lost or spending too much time searching. The planner also became a personal tool for planning her own walks and crafting, reflecting her disciplined, organized style.
