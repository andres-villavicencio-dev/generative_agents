Rajiv Patel built a simple, portable landscape study sketchbook with a waterproof cover, a small brush, and a grid of lightly ruled paper. The device is designed to help artists record natural forms—trees, hills, water, and sky—before they blur or change. It works by allowing the painter to draw directly onto paper while viewing the scene through a clear window. A simple pen connects the sketchbook to a nearby notebook, keeping the artist focused on the subject while still being able to jot down brief observations.

Rajiv chose this approach because he wants to paint quietly and explore everyday landscapes without losing his creative momentum. The waterproof cover protects his work from rain, and the grid helps him maintain a consistent perspective. The small brush and ruled pages make the process accessible for both experienced painters and beginners. He intends to use it for his weekend community connections, recording the people and places he meets while continuing his daily practice.
