Sharon Antle developed a line of sustainably packaged pastries with Isabella Rodriguez, centered around a slow-fermentation croissant recipe. The croissant begins with a refined dough: butter and flour are combined, then the dough is folded, kneaded, and allowed to rest. The signature twist is a one-hour cold fermentation, during which the dough absorbs the butter and develops its distinctive crumb structure.

The pastries are shaped by hand and baked in a small, rustic oven. Sharon uses a clean kitchen towel to cover each pastry, preventing the skin from drying before it’s fully formed. The packaging is made from upcycled paper and compostable bags, with the croissant’s outer layer folded over and sealed before being placed in a reusable carrier.

Sharon made the line because classic New England recipes were becoming outdated: the ingredients were becoming scarce, and the packaging was losing its freshness. Rodriguez’s input ensured that the recipes remained authentic, while the slow-fermentation process added a subtle, artisanal texture. The result is a simple, recognizable product that can be packaged responsibly and still feel like homemade art.
