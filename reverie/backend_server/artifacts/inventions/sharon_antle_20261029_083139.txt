Sharon Antle created Hobbs Cafe’s Midnight Croissant, a signature pastry inspired by her midnight raids and sustainable sourcing. It is a golden-brown, flaky croissant with a crisp, buttery exterior and a soft, airy interior, flavored with cinnamon, cardamom, and a touch of vanilla.

To make it, Sharon uses all-purpose flour, unsalted butter, water, salt, and a pinch of baking powder. She kneads the dough at room temperature, shapes it into a tight log, and lets it rest before rolling it into a thin, even circle. The pastry is brushed generously with butter, baked at 425°F (220°C) for 12–14 minutes, and then folded and brushed again with melted butter before being sliced or eaten whole.

She made the croissant because classic New England recipes can feel rigid. By adding her own secret spices and letting the dough rest, she created an indulgent yet fresh experience that balances the cafe’s refined mission with personal creativity. The simple, recognizable format also makes the pastry accessible, while the subtle seasonal notes allow it to feel uniquely inviting. Sharon developed it as part of a broader collaboration with Isabella Rodriguez, blending familiar flavors with spontaneous experimentation while keeping the process simple enough to replicate and share.
