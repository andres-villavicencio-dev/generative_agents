Sharon Antle invented Midnight Croissant, a sustainably packaged pastry that combines classic New England buttery layers with her personal artistic experimentation. The croissant is baked twice: first in a traditional Dutch oven, then in a commercial oven, with the second bake finished in the oven’s warming drawer. Sharon uses a large, well-seasoned Dutch oven, a commercial oven, parchment paper, and her own artistic recipe notes, which she shares with Maria Lopez during taste-testing sessions.

The package is a 100% compostable, recyclable, and reusable paper bag, with a resealable inner pouch. The bag is water-resistant but not waterproof, so it can be reused if the pouch is damaged. The pouch is designed to be torn open and the croissant to be eaten directly from the paper bag.

Sharon made the croissant for three reasons. First, the double-bake method ensures a more even rise and a flaky texture without sacrificing the classic New England shape. Second, her artistic experimentation allows the croissant to incorporate unexpected ingredients—such as citrus zest, sea salt, or fruit compote—which add visual and flavor interest. Third, the compostable, reusable packaging gives the café a smaller environmental footprint while keeping the croissant fresh and convenient.
