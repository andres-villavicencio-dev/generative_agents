Sharon Antle recently developed a line of sustainably packaged, zero-waste pastries for Hobbs Cafe and for sale at Harvey Oak Supply Store. Each batch is made from whole wheat flour, olive oil, and locally sourced seasonal ingredients, then wrapped in recyclable paper and lined with biodegradable wax paper. The packaging is designed to be sturdy enough to keep the croissants fresh while reducing food waste, with the edible wax protecting the pastry’s delicate crust.

The pastries are baked before dawn, then kept warm in a low-maintenance setting so they remain soft until the afternoon. The line includes classic New England recipes like cinnamon bun and apple pie, as well as spontaneous experiments with sourdough, lavender-glazed muffins, and seasonal fruit tarts. Sharon and Maria Lopez’s daily tastings help shape the recipes, while Isabella Rodriguez’s input ensures the designs fit Hobbs Cafe’s aesthetic.

The project was made to honor sustainable kitchen practices and turn what would normally be discarded scraps into creative food. By combining reliable baking with thoughtful packaging, Sharon has created a repeatable, artistic product that supports local farms, minimizes waste, and keeps the cafe’s signature style alive.
