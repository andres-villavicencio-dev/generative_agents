Tamara Taylor invented The Pocket Story Kit, a simple, portable craft that lets young readers turn everyday items into their own small stories. The kit is a clear plastic pouch with three labeled sections: My Character, My Setting, and My Story.

Tamara uses inexpensive household items—paper towels or socks for fabric, a small wooden toy, a marker, and colorful yarn. She folds the fabric into a gentle character shape, attaches the toy’s face, and writes a few sentences in the designated story section. A tiny string or ribbon can be added so the story can move or change. The pouch has a convenient zipper closure, making it easy to carry anywhere.

Tamara designed it to encourage creativity while saving money. The project is affordable, requires only common materials, and is easy to make with a little patience. It can be completed in ten minutes or more, depending on the reader’s age. The kit works well for beginners because it combines storytelling, creative assembly, and play, helping young readers develop confidence and imagination without spending a large amount of money.
