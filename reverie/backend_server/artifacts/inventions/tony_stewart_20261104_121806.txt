Tony Stewart invented the Willow Clinic Emergency Visual Reminder, a portable, neon-lit mural board designed to help staff quickly identify emergency procedures, evacuation routes, and patient triage needs. The device uses a flexible, waterproof backing that holds preprinted motifs—such as heart symbols, crosswalks, and clinic symbols—and is illuminated with a bright, safe LED strip. It can be mounted on walls, doors, or emergency stations, with the design repeated and expanded to create a cohesive visual guide.

Tony built it using recycled neon tubing, adhesive-backed vinyl sheets, LED strips, and sturdy mounting hardware. A small battery pack allows the mural to remain visible in dim emergency areas while keeping the power cord accessible. He added small decorative elements—like traffic lights, arrows, and patient icons—to make the design more memorable and easier to recognize in a stressful environment.

The device was created to reduce confusion during emergencies. A calm, repeating visual cue can guide people toward the correct route and help staff quickly reference the appropriate procedure. It also serves as a creative outlet for Tony’s love of emergency medicine and street art, helping him connect the calm, creative glow of art with the urgency of real-world situations.
