Tony Stewart did not build an original device or invention; rather, he created a community emergency-preparedness system called the Willow Clinic Rapid Response Kit. It is a portable, collapsible triage unit that quickly assesses a patient’s vital signs, identifies life-threatening conditions, and directs them to the appropriate care area. The kit uses a color-coded triage chart, a rapid blood-pressure screen, a portable oxygen analyzer, and a simplified emergency checklist.

The system works by allowing paramedics to evaluate a patient’s breathing, circulation, consciousness, and blood pressure in minutes while the clinic provides oxygen and connects residents with telehealth. The color-coded triage chart helps determine urgency, and the blood-pressure screen enables immediate treatment of hypertensive emergencies. The kit was designed to connect the clinic’s emergency-preparedness staff, patients, and residents, while also supporting Dr. Chad Kibler’s certification preparation.

The materials include durable, non-slip surfaces, lightweight medical-grade packaging, reusable oxygen canisters, and printed patient flow cards. Tony chose the color scheme and lighting to match the neon mural behind the clinic, making the functional kit visually engaging and helping the clinic feel like an active part of the community. The system was developed to support both immediate care and long-term resilience, especially during the clinic’s telehealth pilot and park-upkeep projects.
