Victor Rosales designed a modular, load-bearing trellis for the garden, combining structural engineering principles with decorative Mexican artistry. The trellis features a repeating lattice of rectangular panels and curved trellis elements, with vertical posts reinforced by hidden steel supports and horizontal cross-bracing to distribute weight evenly. Each panel is made of reclaimed cedar and locally sourced stone, secured with copper nails and epoxy resin for durability. The curved trellis pieces are carved from cedar, while the stone supports provide a sturdy, weather-resistant base.

The design was inspired by traditional Mexican garden structures, with the lattice pattern echoing the intricate murals of Día de los Muertos. It allows climbing plants to grow naturally while offering a structured backdrop for the family gathering. The combination of natural materials and precise woodworking reflects Victor’s engineering background and his desire to create functional, culturally meaningful objects. The project also serves as a teaching example of how traditional design can combine aesthetics with practical engineering.
