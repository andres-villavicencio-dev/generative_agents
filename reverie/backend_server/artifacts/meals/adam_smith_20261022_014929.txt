Adam prepared a slow-simmered mushroom and barley stew, a warmly satisfying nod to his Scottish roots. The key ingredients were pearl barley, cremini mushrooms, onion, carrots, garlic, vegetable stock, rosemary, thyme, and a final splash of cream. He sautéed the mushrooms and aromatics in olive oil until deeply golden, toasted the barley, and then combined everything in a heavy pot. The stew simmered for forty minutes, until the barley was tender and the broth had turned rich and glossy.

The kitchen filled with the earthy aroma of browned mushrooms, rosemary, garlic, and warm grain. Adam ladled the stew into a shallow cream-colored bowl, garnishing it with a rosemary sprig and serving it alongside rustic brown bread and a bright salad of watercress, lemon, and olive oil. It was a quiet, nourishing dinner for a reflective Valentine’s Day evening—a restorative pause between completing the day’s notes and returning to his ideas.
