Adam Smith prepared a simple “Philosopher’s Breakfast Porridge”, made after waking at 4 a.m. while beginning notes for his book on creativity. He carefully measured rolled oats, milk, water, a pinch of salt, brown sugar, and one sliced banana onto the kitchen counter, reflecting on the importance of precision and balance.

He brought the oats, milk, and water to a gentle simmer, stirring steadily as the grains softened and the mixture thickened. Once the porridge was creamy, he removed it from the heat and folded in the banana, letting its sweetness and warm aroma mingle with the toasted grains. The finished dish smelled mildly sweet, warm, and comforting, with a subtle note of cinnamon-like spice.

He served it in a plain white bowl, placing the sliced banana in a neat fan along the surface and dusting the top lightly with cinnamon. The presentation was modest but carefully ordered—an appropriate meal for a reflective morning of writing. It was an ordinary pre-dawn breakfast, yet also a quiet occasion for Adam to explore a new idea: how creativity, like carefully measured ingredients, can transform a simple routine into something nourishing and meaningful.
