Adam prepared a honey-maple croissant, the centerpiece of his Wednesday breakfast. He used two thin, layered doughs, brushing the pastry with a little butter before folding it. The filling was a warm mixture of softened butter, honey, maple syrup, and a pinch of cinnamon, with a few sliced apples and a small piece of candied ginger added for texture.

He brushed the tops with a little honey, then sprinkled the honey-maple glaze over the golden, flaky crusts. The croissants were arranged on a white ceramic plate, with a glass of cold milk beside them. Warmth filled the room as the cinnamon and maple aroma rose from the pastry.

The occasion was a quiet Wednesday morning at Adam’s café, a time to start the day with something simple yet thoughtfully prepared. The meal reflected his intellectual curiosity—combining familiar flavors with the unexpected—but it was also a deliberate gesture of care, balancing the richness of the honey with the brightness of maple syrup and the gentle warmth of cinnamon.
