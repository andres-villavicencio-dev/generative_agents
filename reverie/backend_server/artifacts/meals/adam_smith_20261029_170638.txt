Adam Smith prepared a simple, affordable lunch of pan-seared chicken with roasted vegetables and a lemon-herb sauce. He used a whole chicken, bone-in thighs, boneless breasts, potatoes, carrots, and onions. He washed and trimmed the chicken, marinated it in olive oil, lemon juice, dried thyme, garlic, and salt, then seared it until the skin turned golden and the meat was crisp. He roasted the vegetables in a small oven-safe dish until they were tender, then sliced the chicken and plated it with the vegetables, drizzling the sauce over the top.

The meal was prepared on a quiet Friday morning, likely for himself or a neighbor, and served as a simple, nourishing lunch. Its aroma carried the scent of roasted potatoes, garlic, thyme, and lemon. The presentation was modest but inviting—the golden chicken, colorful vegetables, and creamy sauce created a warm contrast with the white ceramic plate. It was neither a grand feast nor a hurried meal, but an intentional, purposeful choice that reflected his thoughtful, practical approach to everyday life.
