On Tuesday, February 14, Amber prepared Grandmother’s Garden Soup, a warm, rustic lunch that reflected her lifelong love of cooking and storytelling. The soup was made with chicken stock, carrots, onions, celery, potatoes, pearl barley, and fresh herbs, seasoned with garlic, thyme, and a little lemon juice.

She simmered the vegetables and broth together until the potatoes were tender and the barley soft, then added the chopped chicken and served it over a bed of spinach. The meal was served in a simple white bowl, garnished with a sprinkle of parsley and a thin slice of lemon. A small side of crusty bread and a glass of cold water completed the meal.

As Amber ate, the kitchen filled with the comforting aroma of roasted chicken, carrots, and earthy herbs. The presentation was modest but inviting, with the golden potatoes and bright greens creating a cheerful contrast. It was a quiet, purposeful lunch—not rushed or overly elaborate—meant to nourish her body and give her the calm attention needed before a Saturday story hour at the Oak Hill College library.
