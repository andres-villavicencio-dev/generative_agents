On a bright Monday morning, Amber Williamson prepared Hobbs Cafe’s Signature Breakfast Pot Pies. Her recipe used a flaky pastry shell filled with creamy scrambled eggs, diced smoked chicken, fresh herbs, and a generous portion of golden honeyed maple syrup. The filling was gently folded by hand, while the edges were sealed with a little hot water and a pinch of salt.

She arranged the golden, warm pies in a wooden serving tray, placing a small glass of cold honey beside each one. Each pot pie looked like a radiant golden flower—creamy, savory, and inviting, with the syrup glistening in the sunlight.

The aroma filling the kitchen was unmistakably comforting: the rich scent of eggs, herbs, and caramelized honey, accompanied by the subtle warmth of smoked chicken. The occasion was a Saturday story hour at the Oak Hill College library, where Amber, Andrea Lovetro, and Jennifer Moore planned to share tales with neighbors. The pot pies were prepared as a cheerful, neighborhood-style treat—warm, generous, and fit for storytelling.
