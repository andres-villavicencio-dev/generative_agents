On Tuesday, February 14, Amilcar Rosales prepared Cocido Mexicano—a hearty, slow-simmered stew of pork, black beans, potatoes, and chorizo—served warm with cornbread and a fresh tomato salad.

The dish was cooked in a heavy cast-iron pot over low heat for several hours, allowing the meat to become tender and the flavors to meld. Amilcar added chopped onions, garlic, tomatoes, and a generous handful of black beans, then covered the pot and let it simmer until the potatoes softened and the broth thickened. He added the chorizo near the end to preserve its smoky aroma without overpowering the stew’s savory depth.

The aroma filled the kitchen with the rich, earthy scent of chorizo, caramelized onion, and roasted potatoes, mingling with the subtle sweetness of the beans and the bright tang of the tomatoes.

For presentation, Amilcar arranged the warm stew in a deep serving bowl, placing a large tortilla and sliced avocado on top. A crisp tomato salad was served on the side, while a handful of cornbread and a glass of cold white wine completed the table. The meal was prepared for a quiet Tuesday dinner—simple, family-style, and the kind of comforting meal Amilcar would prepare before turning his first field into certified organic production and installing solar panels on his barn.
