Ayesha prepared a simple yet nourishing breakfast of sourdough toast with roasted tomatoes and chives, served alongside a cup of warm herbal tea. She toasted two slices of whole-grain sourdough, then sliced them into thin pieces and spread them with olive oil, salt, and freshly chopped chives. The tomatoes were roasted until their skins softened and their flesh turned bright red, adding a gentle sweetness and savory depth. The chives provided a fragrant, peppery finish.

The kitchen smelled of toasted bread, savory herbs, and caramelized tomatoes, creating a warm, inviting aroma that made the quiet morning feel cozy. Ayesha arranged the toast on a white plate, adding a small bowl of sliced tomatoes and a sprig of fresh chives for visual contrast.

This was her Wednesday breakfast before diving into her senior thesis research on language and literature. The meal felt both practical and reflective—a moment of calm before she returned to the Johnson Park meeting, the old oak and hawthorn canopy, and the more demanding work of connecting her Shakespeare research to gentrification and housing.
