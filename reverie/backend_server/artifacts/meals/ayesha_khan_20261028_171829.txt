Ayesha Khan prepared a simple fruit-and-toast breakfast for her Wednesday routine. The meal was a toasted fig and lemon crisp, served with a small side of warm oatmeal. She toasted slices of whole-grain toast with the golden crust and a little olive oil, then added fresh figs, lemon juice, and a pinch of cinnamon. For the oatmeal, she used rolled oats, water, a small amount of honey, and a few dried apricots.

The kitchen filled with a warm aroma of toasted bread, cinnamon, citrus, and dried fruit. Ayesha arranged the crisp on a white plate, placing the oatmeal beside it and garnishing the oatmeal with a few apricot halves. She presented the breakfast in a calm, balanced way, letting the contrasting textures of the crisp, soft toast, and creamy oatmeal create visual harmony.

The meal was part of her structured Wednesday, balancing classes, her senior thesis on Shakespearean language, and growing connections with gentrification and housing. It was neither hurried nor formal—a quiet, nourishing pause before her late-afternoon research work.
