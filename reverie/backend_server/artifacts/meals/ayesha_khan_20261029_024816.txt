Ayesha prepared a Chicken Tikka Masala with Basmati Rice, a comforting meal that reflected her busy day as a college student balancing literature, cooking, and civic research.

The chicken was marinated overnight with yogurt, garlic, ginger, turmeric, cumin, paprika, and fresh herbs, then cooked in a pan with oil until golden and tender. It was then layered with sautéed onions, tomatoes, and spices, simmered briefly, and finished in the oven until the gravy thickened. A generous portion of long-grain basmati rice was added alongside the dish, and a lemon wedge was sliced for the table.

The aroma of warm, spiced chicken and toasted vegetables filled the kitchen, while the rich orange-red gravy and fragrant rice created a welcoming atmosphere. Ayesha presented the meal in a shallow white bowl, using a spoon to arrange the rice around the chicken and garnish it with crushed coriander and a squeeze of lemon.

She cooked it for dinner on February 14 to honor her birthday, but the meal was more reflective than celebratory. She had prepared it in her apartment kitchen, using simple ingredients that she wanted to explore through a familiar lens: how food, conversation, and inclusion could nourish her thesis and everyday life.
