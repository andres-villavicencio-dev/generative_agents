Ayesha Khan prepared a comforting Shakshouka—a Middle Eastern breakfast dish of eggs, tomatoes, bell peppers, onions, and warm spices, traditionally served with crusty bread. She began by sautéing garlic and onions in olive oil, adding crushed tomatoes, and stirring in lamb or chicken until the flavors melded. She cracked eggs into the pan, covering the dish so the whites set while the yolks remained runny, then topped it with fresh parsley and served with warm pita.

The kitchen filled with the rich aroma of cumin, paprika, garlic, and toasted nuts. Ayesha arranged the golden eggs, vibrant tomato base, and fragrant bread on a small white plate, presenting the meal with a simple, inviting aesthetic. The occasion was her Thursday lunch break while researching Shakespeare, where she reflected on how the cold, impersonal tone of Macbeth could be mirrored in the repetitive, transactional language of menus—both creating a sense of ownership while excluding original residents.
