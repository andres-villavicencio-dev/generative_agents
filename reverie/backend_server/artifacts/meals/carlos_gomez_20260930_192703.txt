Carlos Gómez prepared a quiet, nourishing bowl of miso-glazed salmon at his desk-located dining table. The dish features flaky wild-caught salmon glazed with homemade miso sauce, served atop warm rice and pickled daikon radish. Prepared the night before, the salmon gently poached in its glaze to preserve texture. The aroma is earthy—miso’s umami mingles with a hint of grilled garlic and fresh ginger, rising like a soft incense as he lifts the bowl. Plated simply: white ceramic dish, steam curling upward, a single sprig of shiso for color. It was made on his Tuesday routine, a deliberate act between morning focus and afternoon stillness—a small tribute to balance.
