Carlos Gomez prepared Breakfast of the Old Oak, a hearty panini-style breakfast that honored the café’s display of seasonal ingredients and the oak tree nearby. The dish was filled with toasted sourdough, creamy ricotta, crisp cherry tomatoes, sun-dried tomatoes, roasted garlic, chives, and a drizzle of olive oil and balsamic glaze. He added a small side of warm cornbread and a cup of herbal tea.

The kitchen smelled warm and aromatic—richly toasted bread, caramelized cheese, the sharp scent of garlic, and the subtle earthiness of the dried tomatoes. He toasted the sourdough until it was crisp, layered the fillings carefully, and pressed the panini under the broiler until the cheese melted and the bread turned golden.

Presented on a white ceramic plate with a sprig of chives and a small lemon wedge, the dish looked inviting and balanced. The occasion was an unplugged Wednesday breakfast: a quiet moment after a busy creative day. Carlos ate it while reviewing poems from the Natural-World Collection, reflecting on his growing body of work and how his poetic identity could still contribute meaningfully to the community.
