Cledith prepared a simple rosemary lemon-chicken with crisp green beans for his breakfast. He chose a breast of chicken thighs, gently searing them in a skillet until the skin turned golden. He sliced the chicken into portions, added a few lemon wedges, and poured a small amount of chicken broth over the pan. The chicken was cooked until the juices were clear and the breast was fully cooked, while the green beans were blanched briefly and then tossed in with the pan juices for a minute to retain their brightness.

The kitchen filled with the warm aroma of roasted poultry, fresh lemon, rosemary, and the soft scent of butter. Cledith arranged the chicken and beans on a clean white plate, garnishing it with a few leaves of fresh rosemary and a bright slice of lemon. He served it with a plain, warm crusty bread, making the meal appear wholesome and inviting.

The meal was prepared on a Tuesday morning in late February, during the week when Cledith was drafting a National Endowment for the Humanities grant proposal and mentoring students. The quiet, nourishing breakfast gave him the focus he needed to shape the project’s narrative while reflecting his scholarly, nurturing side.
