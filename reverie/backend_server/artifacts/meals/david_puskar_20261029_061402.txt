On a quiet Wednesday morning, David Puskar prepared Rose & Crown’s “Herb-Crusted Rack with Root & Roasted Tomato” for the pub’s evening. His meticulous approach began with precise measurements: 2.5 pounds of pork rack, 1 lb of baby potatoes, a pound of carrots, and ½ lb of firm root vegetables, all washed and chopped into uniform cubes. He sautéed the vegetables in olive oil until golden, then added garlic, rosemary, thyme, and a generous splash of lemon juice. The pork was seared until crisp, seared once, and finished under the broiler until the skin caramelized and the meat was tender.

The kitchen was filled with the earthy aroma of roasted root vegetables, rosemary, garlic, and the subtle warmth of browned fat. David plated the dish on a slate board, arranging the golden vegetables and crispy pork in alternating rows with a small mound of roasted tomatoes and a drizzle of balsamic glaze. The presentation felt both rustic and refined, evoking the refined yet hearty Midwestern comfort that he turns into edible art.

He prepared the meal during the pub’s late-night shift, serving a few guests who arrived for dinner around 4:00 PM. The occasion was a quiet family-style dinner, but the effort transformed the communal meal into an intimate experience of craftsmanship and shared warmth.
