The first fusion dish was Polish Borscht with German Croutons, a rustic soup that honored the hearty, comforting traditions of both cultures while creating a bold, earthy palate experience.

Key ingredients included beetroot, carrots, celery, onion, canned tomatoes, beef broth, pickled turnips, horseradish, and a creamy sour cream finish. The German twist came from coarse whole-grain and seeded croutons seasoned with paprika, garlic, and a pinch of smoked paprika.

David prepared the soup by blanching the root vegetables, cooking them with the tomatoes and broth until tender, then simmering them with pickled turnips and a splash of crème fraîche. He finished the dish with fresh horseradish and served the golden broth alongside the crunchy, smoky croutons. The aroma was warm, savory, and rich—herbs and root vegetables mingling with the tangy acidity of the sour cream.

The presentation featured a deep crimson soup in a matte ceramic bowl, surrounded by golden, herb-crusted bread and a small sprig of parsley. It was designed as part of the tasting menu for a collaborative dinner at Arthur Burton’s Rose and Crown Pub, intended to showcase David’s ability to merge Midwestern comfort with German-Polish flavors.
