Ethel Langley prepared Morning-Heirloom Breakfast Casserole, a warm, comforting meal she made after waking at 5:00 a.m. for her campaign breakfast at the cafe. She combined golden old-field eggs, sautéed mushrooms, caramelized onions, crushed tomatoes, and fresh chives in a large skillet, then transferred the mixture to a greased baking dish. The eggs were folded in gently, and the casserole was baked until the edges turned crisp and the interior was set.

Its warm aroma drifted from the kitchen: savory egg, toasted bread, herbs, and a hint of caramelized onion. She served the casserole against a plate of crusty bread with a cup of coffee, arranging the golden egg mixture in the center and scattering a few crispy bits around the edges. The occasion was a quiet campaign breakfast, but the meal felt practical and nourishing—fitting for a retired electrician who had spent decades wiring homes and small businesses.
