On a Tuesday morning, Ethel prepared Chicken and Vegetable Casserole for a simple, practical lunch. She used bone-in chicken thighs, diced carrots, onions, bell peppers, canned tomatoes, canned diced tomatoes, and a pinch of dried basil. The chicken was browned in a skillet, then transferred to a baking dish with the vegetables, broth, and seasonings, and baked until tender and golden.

The kitchen filled with the savory aroma of roasted chicken and caramelized carrots. She served the casserole with crusty bread and a side of green salad, arranging the vegetables and chicken in neat rows. It was a comforting, nourishing meal, and Ethel took her time to serve it carefully.

The occasion was a quiet Tuesday lunch after a long workday; she had also prepared opening remarks for the school’s bulletin and finished a final presentation to Abigail. The casual, homey presentation of the food reflected Ethel’s practical, compassionate nature—simple, familiar, and meant to bring warmth to her visitors.
