Ethel prepared a simple Breakfast of the Day for Abigail Chen’s café—a warm, comforting meal she would have prepared for herself.

The dish consisted of eggs, bacon, and toast, seasoned with salt, pepper, and a little lemon. Ethel cracked the eggs into a bowl, whisked them with a splash of milk, and fried the bacon in a pan until crisp. She sliced the toast, buttered it, and arranged it on a plate beside the eggs.

Aromatic, the kitchen smelled of toasted bread, savory bacon, and the sharp tang of lemon. The presentation was modest but inviting: the golden toast, bright green eggs, and plump strips of bacon arranged neatly on a white plate.

On Tuesday, February 14, the meal was part of a quiet morning before Ethel’s busy day. She wanted something familiar and nourishing to lift her spirits, and she hoped Abigail would enjoy the simple, warm breakfast.
