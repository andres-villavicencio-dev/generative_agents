Ethel prepared Smothered Roast Chicken with Root Vegetables and Lemon-Caper Sauce, a comforting dinner she made for herself before heading to town hall on Monday, February 13.

The main component was a whole chicken roasted with rosemary, garlic, lemon, and a splash of white wine. Root vegetables—carrots, potatoes, and onions—were caramelized in the oven until tender. The sauce was reduced until glossy, with a touch of butter, salt, and capers.

As she worked, the kitchen filled with the warm scent of roasted herbs, citrus, and savory meat. After removing the chicken, she tossed the vegetables in the sauce, arranging them neatly on a large platter. The golden chicken and bright orange vegetables were presented alongside a steaming bowl of rice and a glass of water, creating a rustic, inviting dinner.

She ate in silence, letting the meal settle while she drafted her campaign. The occasion was quiet and personal: a final home-cooked meal before an evening mentoring session, before her town hall filing, and before an early night.
