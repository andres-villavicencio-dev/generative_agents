On Monday, February 13, Giorgio Rossi prepared a Mediterranean Vegetable Tart with Fennel, Feta, and Thyme. The tart was baked in a crisp, golden crust and filled with finely chopped fennel, feta cheese, sun-dried tomatoes, and a generous pinch of dried thyme. Giorgio layered the vegetables with a lightly beaten egg and olive oil, then baked them until the edges turned crisp and the filling bubbled gently.

The aroma was warm, earthy, and subtly aromatic: fennel and thyme mingled with the savory richness of the feta and the bright citrus note of the tomatoes. Giorgio presented the tart on a slate platter, arranging the golden crusts and colorful vegetables in a loose, asymmetrical pattern and serving it with a crisp salad and a small bowl of plain yogurt.

The occasion was a quiet Thursday dinner at home, the result of Giorgio’s evening research. While reviewing patterns in natural systems, he was also considering who might run the local mayor’s election next month, so his meal felt both nourishing and thoughtfully prepared—a small act of curiosity turned into a shared evening.
