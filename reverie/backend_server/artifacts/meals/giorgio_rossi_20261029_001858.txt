For his Tuesday lunch, Giorgio prepared a Heirloom Tomato and Basil Caprese Wrap, a simple yet elegant side dish. He used ripe heirloom tomatoes, fresh basil, a little extra-virgin olive oil, a pinch of salt, and a small knob of butter. He thinly sliced the tomatoes and basil, then wrapped them in a warm tortilla, drizzling the dressing and butter over the top.

The preparation was quick and methodical: Giorgio carefully folded the wrap so the tomato and basil were visible, then toasted the tortilla until it was crisp. As he ate, the aroma of basil, tomato, and olive oil filled the kitchen. The wrap was presented on a slate platter with a sprig of fresh basil, giving it a rustic, mathematical symmetry.

It was a quiet, contemplative lunch after a full day of research and civic discussions. Giorgio had chosen it deliberately—simple enough to eat while thinking about mathematical patterns, and just flavorful enough to satisfy his palate. The meal reflected his eccentric curiosity: practical, thoughtful, and entirely his own.
