For his Tuesday lunch, Giorgio prepared Calabrian Tomato & Olive Stew with Grilled Pork. He began by gently sautéing diced pork with garlic, chili flakes, and a splash of olive oil until the fat rendered and the meat became lightly browned. He added canned tomatoes, sliced onions, carrots, celery, and a handful of olives, then simmered everything for twenty minutes until the vegetables softened and the sauce thickened.

The aroma was unmistakably Mediterranean—savory pork, roasted garlic, caramelized tomatoes, and the crisp zest of fresh herbs wafting from the kitchen. Giorgio plated the stew in a shallow, ceramic dish, arranging the pork in the center and scattering the colorful vegetables around it. Fresh parsley, crumbled feta, and a few whole roasted peppers completed the presentation.

He served it with crusty bread and a side of lemon-turkey salad, intending to eat it while reviewing his election research. The meal was simple, purposeful, and carefully arranged, reflecting Giorgio’s analytical approach to everyday cooking: each ingredient had a clear role, the preparation followed a logical sequence, and the final presentation balanced visual appeal with practical sustenance.
