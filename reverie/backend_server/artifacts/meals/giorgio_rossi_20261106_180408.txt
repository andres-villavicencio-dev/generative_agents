Giorgio Rossi prepared a Savory Sage and Herb Roasted Root Vegetable with Crispy Prosciutto, a modest side dish for his lunch. He used a small Dutch oven to roast red potatoes, carrots, and parsnips with garlic, thyme, rosemary, and a splash of lemon juice, then finished them with a thin layer of crisp Prosciutto. The oven-roasted vegetables retained a bright, earthy sweetness, while the crisp bacon provided a salty, savory contrast.

As he worked, Giorgio stirred the vegetables with care, paying close attention to their color, texture, and even the small irregularities in their shapes. The aroma rose from the pan: warm carrots mingling with garlic, thyme, rosemary, and the faint, rich scent of cured pork. He plated the dish in a shallow ceramic bowl, arranging the golden vegetables around a central mound of crisp Prosciutto, leaving generous gaps for breathing room.

The meal was a quiet, intellectual pause—not a celebration, but a thoughtful break after a demanding day. It reflected Giorgio’s analytical nature: simple ingredients combined in a deliberate, balanced design, each element contributing to harmony. It was also a modest nod to his broader interests, including the mayoral election, where he discussed the candidates with others.
