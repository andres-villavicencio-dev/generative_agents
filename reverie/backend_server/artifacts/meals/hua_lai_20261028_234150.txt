Dinner: Fig Croissant with Sage Butter

Hua Lai prepared his Tuesday dinner around the small kitchen in the Smallville police station, starting with a few cups of water and a handful of figs. He sliced them into thin rounds and placed them on a sheet pan, then added a few sprigs of fresh sage and a drizzle of olive oil. The kitchen filled with the warm, earthy scent of figs and sage, followed by the buttery aroma of the pastry.

He preheated the oven to 375°F and baked the figs until the edges were crisp and golden. While that happened, he opened the refrigerator and chose the organic eggs and Fig Croissant he had picked up from Eugene. The croissant was buttery, slightly crisp on the outside, and soft on the inside, with the figs providing a subtle sweetness and the sage adding a fresh herbal depth.

When finished, he plated the croissant with the warm, golden figs, sprinkling the sage over it. The bright yellow of the figs contrasted beautifully with the golden-brown pastry and pale egg yolks. Hua Lai served a simple side of cold butter and toasted bread. It was a modest Tuesday dinner, neither celebratory nor rushed, but satisfying enough to give him a small sense of accomplishment before heading home at 6:00 p.m.
