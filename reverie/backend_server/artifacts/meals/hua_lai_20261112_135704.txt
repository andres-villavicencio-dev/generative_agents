Hua Lai prepared Chili-Roasted Pork with Kimchi Greens and Roasted Root Vegetables, a hearty, neighborhood-style meal he cooked in the small apartment kitchen. For the pork, he thinly sliced shoulder meat, marinated it with soy sauce, garlic, ginger, and a splash of sesame oil, then roasted it on a small pan until the skin turned crisp and the meat was tender. Meanwhile, he stir-fried fresh kimchi greens and a mix of carrots and potatoes with oyster sauce, scallions, and a little chili oil, seasoning them with coarse salt and black pepper.

The kitchen filled with the warm aroma of roasted meat, fermented kimchi, and roasted vegetables—rich, savory, slightly spicy, and fragrant. The meal was served in a simple white ceramic bowl, with the pork arranged on the left, the colorful kimchi greens in the center, and the roasted vegetables nestled beneath. A small plate of sliced radish, a handful of pickled mustard greens, and a glass of chilled ginger tea accompanied the dinner.

It was a quiet Tuesday evening meal: Hua had finished his associate-degree coursework and patrol assignments, then returned to the pub with Arthur. The prepared dinner offered him a chance to pause, share a meal with a familiar friend, and take a short walk toward the park.
