The meal was Roasted Tomato Basil Soup with Roasted Root Vegetables, prepared for Ingrid’s quiet Friday night. She washed a basket of carrots, potatoes, and onions, then chopped them into small, uniform pieces. After sautéing the vegetables in olive oil until lightly caramelized, she added tomato paste, red wine, and a generous handful of fresh basil. The warm pan dripped with oil and rose with a rich, earthy aroma.

She poured the vegetables into a pot, added a can of crushed tomatoes, water, and a pinch of salt, then covered it and let it simmer gently over low heat. As the soup thickened, she ladled it into a large bowl, garnishing it with toasted bread and fresh basil leaves. The dish was presented in a simple white ceramic bowl, with the golden vegetables glowing beneath the steam.

It was an easy, comforting dinner for Ingrid and Ethel, prepared without much fanfare, but with the patient, warm attention that made every meal feel meaningful.
