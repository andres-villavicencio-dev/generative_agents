Ingrid Larsen prepared Grandmother’s Classic Breakfast with Scrambled Eggs and Toast, a simple yet comforting meal she often makes for herself after a busy weekend.

She cracked six large eggs into a bowl, seasoned them with salt and pepper, and heated butter in a non-stick pan until the edges began to turn golden. She gently stirred the eggs over medium heat, then added two slices of sourdough toast, a handful of fresh spinach, a drizzle of honey, and a few slices of blue cheese. She finished the pan with a knob of butter and a generous sprinkle of cracked black pepper.

The aroma filled the small kitchen with the rich, savory scent of baking eggs, toasted bread, and herbs. She arranged the eggs in a neat circle around the toast, garnishing them with a sprig of parsley. Ingrid sat down, taking a sip of coffee and eating slowly, her expression one of quiet satisfaction. This was her usual Tuesday breakfast—a peaceful way to start the day before she dealt with the tutoring and teaching opportunities at Johnson Park.
