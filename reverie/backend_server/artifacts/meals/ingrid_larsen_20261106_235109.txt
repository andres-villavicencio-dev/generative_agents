Ingrid Larsen prepared a lunch of lentil and vegetable stew with crusty bread, a simple, nourishing meal that reflected her patient and grounded approach to cooking.

The stew contained lean ground beef, chickpeas, carrots, celery, onion, garlic, tomato, and a splash of yogurt. Her lentils were simmered alongside the beef until the meat was tender and the vegetables had softened. A little lemon juice and fresh herbs brought the colors to life. She added a generous slice of whole-grain toast at the end, allowing the bread to absorb some of the stew’s rich broth.

The kitchen filled with the earthy, savory aroma of roasted vegetables, lentils, and warm herbs, with a hint of nuttiness from the chickpeas. She presented the meal in a shallow bowl, placing the bread beside it and arranging the stew in a neat, inviting mound.

This was a quiet, solitary lunch—no one was around to judge her cooking. It was a moment of comfort and patience, celebrating the satisfaction of feeding herself and preparing a simple meal without the pressure of making others feel special.
