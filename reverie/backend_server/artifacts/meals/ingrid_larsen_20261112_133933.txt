Ingrid Larsen prepared a hearty chicken-and-vegetable pan-roast for dinner. She sliced a whole chicken with the skin on, seasoned it with salt, pepper, garlic powder, and dried herbs, then placed it in a large skillet with onions, carrots, and potatoes. She browned the chicken first, then added the vegetables and covered the pan to steam them gently. The aroma of roasted garlic, savory chicken, and caramelized carrots filled the kitchen.

She turned off the heat and let the food rest briefly before plating it. The chicken was golden and tender, the carrots and potatoes were bright orange and soft, and the vegetables retained a pleasant moisture. She served it with a simple green salad and a small portion of rye bread.

The occasion was a quiet Tuesday evening meal. After a long, purposeful Monday, Ingrid had just finished checking the Hobbs Café shift and practicing with Ryan Park’s math problems. The meal reflected her calm, patient nature and her growing interest in mentoring young people—she had chosen this familiar dish to celebrate her accomplishment before settling into the evening.
