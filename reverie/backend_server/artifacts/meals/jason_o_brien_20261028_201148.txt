For his lunch on Monday, February 13, Jason prepared Sunset Garden Soup, a warm, comforting soup inspired by the seasons and the contemplative rhythm of his life as a chaplain and artist.

The soup was made by simmering beef broth, carrots, celery, potatoes, onions, and thyme until the vegetables softened and the broth had rich, savory depth. A generous handful of leeks, chopped parsley, and crumbled feta were added at the end, giving the dish a bright, herbaceous finish. A spoonful of red apple was stirred in for a subtle tang, while a few sliced shallots provided a gentle crunch.

As the soup simmered, the kitchen filled with the warm aroma of herbs, caramelized onions, and beef, reminiscent of fields at dusk. It was presented in a simple white bowl with the feta and parsley garnished on top, the apple slices arranged neatly beside it.

The meal reflected Jason’s interest in blending theology, art, and storytelling. The comforting soup was meant to be a place for reflection—just as he often found solace in music, painting, and conversation while preparing his lunch.
