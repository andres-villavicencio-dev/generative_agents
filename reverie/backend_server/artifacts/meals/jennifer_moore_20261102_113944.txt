Jennifer prepared Golden Egg Drop Soup for a quiet Saturday morning. The dish consisted of gently simmered chicken broth, chopped carrots and celery, a handful of baby spinach, and several small eggs that had been cracked into the pot. She stirred the mixture carefully, checking that the eggs coagulated and the vegetables softened without becoming mushy. When the broth thickened slightly, she added a pinch of salt and a splash of cream, then gave the soup a final gentle stir.

The kitchen filled with the warm aroma of roasted chicken, carrots, and softened greens. She served the soup in a small, clear ceramic bowl, garnishing it with a few fresh herbs and a thin slice of lemon. The presentation was simple and dignified, with the creamy broth and golden egg droplets catching the light in the open kitchen.

It was Jennifer’s first proper breakfast before her Saturday errands, and the slow preparation made her feel calm and grounded. Though she had a full day ahead—watercolor painting, gallery visits, conversations with family and friends, and support for younger artists—this simple meal gave her a sense of purpose and comfort.
