Jennifer prepared a comforting winter soup called Warm Garden Roots. She sautéed onions, carrots, celery, and garlic in a little vegetable broth, then added carrots, parsnips, turnips, leeks, and fresh herbs. A few minutes later, she poured in the warm broth and stirred in handfuls of chopped kale and radishes until the vegetables softened. For the final touch, she added a small handful of fresh thyme and a splash of lemon juice.

The aroma of the kitchen filled the room: earthy onion, crisp greens, warm broth, and the faint scent of dried herbs. The soup was served in a clear glass bowl, with the colorful vegetables arranged neatly around the center, their bright orange, green, and purple hues contrasting against the pale broth. It was a simple, nourishing meal prepared for her Tuesday, February 14, with a quiet purpose behind it.

As she stood over the finished dish, Jennifer reflected on the neighborhood she was exploring, imagining how the rain and her watercolors would shape the exhibition she was developing. The meal was more than nourishment—it was a pause in the rhythm of her busy days, reminding her to slow down and notice the small, vivid details around her.
