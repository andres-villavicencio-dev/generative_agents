On Saturday, February 18, Job prepared a simple Roast Chicken with Roasted Vegetables, a warm lunch meant to match the day’s steady rhythm of work and home life. He seasoned the chicken with salt, black pepper, garlic powder, dried thyme, and a splash of lemon juice, then arranged the chicken with carrots, potatoes, onions, and Brussels sprouts on a large baking sheet.

Job tossed the vegetables in olive oil, sprinkled them with salt and pepper, and brushed the chicken with oil before roasting them in a preheated oven at 400°F (200°C) for about 30 minutes. As the oven roared, Job added the chicken to the pan and covered it with foil so the skin would brown gently without drying out. He basted the chicken with pan juices every few minutes, letting the golden, aromatic skin crisp around the edges.

The finished meal was presented on a wooden cutting board in a rustic ceramic bowl. Golden-brown chicken pieces, caramelized vegetables, and a bright lemon wedge were arranged alongside freshly cracked black pepper, giving the dish a colorful, inviting appearance. It smelled warm and savory—an unmistakable aroma of roasted herbs, garlic, olive oil, and citrus.

Job ate it at his desk while overseeing the updated delivery route-checklist and other municipal tasks, choosing the meal because its simplicity made it the perfect companion to the day’s practical work. It was more than a lunch; it was a moment of care, reflection, and satisfaction after a long day.
