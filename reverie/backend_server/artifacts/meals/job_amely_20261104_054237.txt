The meal was a Sunday-Style Egg and Tomato Breakfast, prepared on a small wooden kitchen table with the morning sun streaming through the window. Job cooked six eggs in buttered cast-iron skillets, seasoning them with salt, pepper, and a pinch of garlic before serving them alongside sliced tomatoes, fresh chives, and a simple cornbread loaf.

The aroma filled the small kitchen as the eggs crackled, golden around the edges, and began to steam. His warm, patient voice and the sound of the pan gave the meal a gentle domestic rhythm. He arranged the eggs in a neat circle on a white plate, garnishing them with bright green chives and adding a small mound of toast for dipping.

This was a quiet, nourishing breakfast—not a formal dinner, but a moment of comfort and connection. Job had spent the week repairing pipes and roofs, but this meal reminded him that craftsmanship should also serve people. He ate slowly, savoring the warm eggs and taking a few minutes to remember how the work had felt.
