John prepared Chicken with Ginger Garlic Rice for his family’s dinner on Monday, February 13.

He sliced chicken breast into thin strips, marinated them with ginger, garlic, soy sauce, and a splash of sesame oil, then pan-seared them until golden and juicy. He cooked the rice in a little oil, adding ginger and garlic while stirring in a handful of frozen peas and carrots. For the sauce, he simmered soy sauce, sugar, and a little water until it thickened.

The aroma filled the kitchen with warm spices, roasted poultry, and the savory scent of garlic. When he plated the dish, the chicken rested on the rice, garnished with sprouts and a thin slice of lime. He arranged the bowl neatly for Eddy, who was eager to try the first taste of the meal.

The occasion was a simple family dinner—no special event, just a quiet moment of homecoming and shared meals. John’s patient, organized approach made the preparation feel calm and rewarding, while the familiar smell of ginger and garlic reminded him that cooking was more than a chore—it was a way to connect with those he loved.
