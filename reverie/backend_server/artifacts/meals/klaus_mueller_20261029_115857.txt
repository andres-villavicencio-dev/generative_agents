On Thursday, February 16, Klaus Mueller prepared Korean-Style Beef and Kimchi Stir-Fry. The dish featured tender beef strips marinated in soy sauce, garlic, ginger, sesame oil, and a touch of rice vinegar, along with vibrant red, green, and yellow kimchi and chopped scallions.

Klaus preheated the oven, checked the refrigerator and pantry, and organized his groceries, then cooked the stir-fry in a large skillet. He sliced the beef thinly, fried it until it became slightly crisp, and added the kimchi and vegetables to a lightly oiled pan. The ingredients were stirred for five minutes until the beef was tender and the vegetables were bright and slightly caramelized, with the sauce thickening to coat everything.

Aromatic, savory, and spicy, the meal filled the kitchen with the warm scent of ginger, garlic, soy, and fermented vegetables. He plated the stir-fry in a shallow dish, arranging the beef and colorful vegetables in neat rows before garnishing it with sliced scallions and a few fresh cilantro leaves. It was a simple, home-cooked dinner prepared before his Wednesday research revision, reflecting his thoughtful approach to everyday life.
