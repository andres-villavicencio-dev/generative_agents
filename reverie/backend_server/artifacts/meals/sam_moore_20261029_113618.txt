On a quiet Thursday afternoon, Sam made a simple chicken and vegetable stir-fry, a meal that echoed the military-style efficiency he loved and his ready-to-share humor. He chose a breast of chicken that had been marinated with soy sauce, ginger, garlic, and a little sugar, along with bell peppers, broccoli, snap peas, and carrots.

The chicken was quickly seared until the skin was golden, then tossed in a pan with the vegetables. He added a splash of soy sauce, a pinch of oil, and a generous spoonful of ginger-garlic paste, cooking everything over medium heat until the chicken became tender and the vegetables softened but still crisp.

The kitchen filled with the sharp aroma of ginger, garlic, caramelized peppers, and the savory, earthy scent of cooked chicken. Sam presented the dish in a shallow white bowl, arranging the colorful vegetables around the pale chicken and sprinkling sesame seeds on top. It was a warm, colorful plate that looked inviting enough for an early dinner, but also practical enough to eat quickly.

He prepared it for himself after helping Jennifer with her exhibition, but the finished meal felt purposeful: nourishing enough for the family, interesting enough to share with his neighbors, and a small reminder of the disciplined, resourceful way he used to lead.
