Sharon Antle prepared Morning Croissant with Fig Compote, a simple yet refined breakfast for Isabella Rodriguez’s Hobbs Cafe. She rolled the dough herself, using a classic New England pastry base with subtle hints of warm cinnamon and ginger. The dough was brushed lightly with softened butter, then divided into round, slotted-cup shapes before being baked until golden and crisp.

After baking, she arranged the warm croissants on a wooden board alongside a fragrant fig compote, a bright orange lemon curd, and a dollop of crème fraîche. She paired them with a warm slice of toasted sourdough and a small bowl of fresh berries. The scent of butter, dried fig, citrus, and cinnamon filled the kitchen, inviting a quiet morning.

The occasion was a weekend baking workshop at Hobbs Cafe, where she was sharing her evolving croissant recipes and encouraging guests to experiment with seasonal ingredients. The meal felt both nostalgic and creative, blending familiar comfort with the spontaneous artistic flair that had made her baking a cherished tradition.
