Sharon Antle prepared Dawn Croissant, a rustic yet refined breakfast croissant made with butter, flour, salt, cold water, and a generous amount of egg. She layered the dough, folded it carefully, and baked it until the golden-brown crust became crisp and the filling melted into a rich, savory custard.

She used butter from the store’s bakery, flour from the pantry section, and eggs from the refrigerator. The kitchen was filled with the comforting aroma of baking butter, warm flour, and savory egg—like the scent of a freshly opened bakery. She arranged the croissant on a white ceramic plate, garnished it with a few fresh thyme leaves, and placed it beside a glass of iced tea.

The meal was a quiet Wednesday morning breakfast for Isabella Rodriguez’s Hobbs Cafe, an early gathering for the weekend baking workshops. Sharon’s meticulous, artistic approach turned the simple croissant into a centerpiece of hospitality, while her thoughtful presentation reflected her passion for fresh, community-centered baking.
