Sharon Antle prepared a Molten-Butter Croissant for a quiet Wednesday lunch at Hobbs Cafe. She used unsalted butter, flour, sugar, cinnamon, vanilla extract, and a generous amount of warm egg wash, then folded in a little softened cream cheese for a subtle tang. After chilling the dough overnight, she rolled it into a thin crescent, brushed it with egg, and baked it until the outer edges were crisp and the interior melted into a gooey, golden-brown center.

The kitchen filled with the earthy scent of butter, vanilla, and warm cinnamon, giving the room a cozy, inviting aroma. She sliced the croissant open and served it with a small amount of cream cheese, a drizzle of honey, and a dusting of sea-salt flakes. The presentation was simple and elegant: the croissant rested on a white ceramic plate, surrounded by neatly arranged fresh eggs, baking ingredients, and a small glass of water.

She prepared the meal while checking the store for supplies, balancing the quiet rhythm of her baking routine with her purposeful work at Hobbs Cafe. It was a peaceful, nourishing lunch—the kind she needed to sustain her creative experiments and prepare for the uncertain opening of her pastries.
