Sharon Antle prepared a Noir-Crème Croissant, a refined pastry featuring a buttery, golden-crumbed croissant layered with a rich, dark cream infused with toasted hazelnuts and a hint of vanilla.

The croissant was rolled from chilled butter using a sharp pastry wheel, folded into a rectangle, and brushed with a little pastry butter before baking until it turned golden brown and became crisp on the edges. She topped it with a thick layer of cream, adding crushed toasted hazelnuts and a dusting of cinnamon. A small spoon of warm hazelnut syrup was served alongside the croissant.

The aroma was unmistakable: butter, toasted nuts, warm cream, and cinnamon. The pastry’s pale, buttery exterior contrasted with the rich, dark interior, while the warm hazelnut syrup added a glossy sheen.

The meal was a special morning treat for Isabella Rodriguez’s Hobbs Cafe, where Sharon was preparing for a weekend baking workshop. She had brought it to taste-test the new sustainable-packaging line Maria Lopez had developed, and the café’s customers had praised its elegant presentation and comforting flavors.
