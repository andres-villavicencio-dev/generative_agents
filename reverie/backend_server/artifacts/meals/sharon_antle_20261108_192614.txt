Sharon prepared a Lemon-Zest Croissant for the weekend baking workshops, the same neutral-butter croissant she was reviewing for Eugene Johnson. The croissant is made with softened butter, flour, salt, egg, and lemon zest, folded into a pastry, brushed with warm butter, and baked until golden and crisp.

The kitchen filled with the warm aroma of melted butter, toasted flour, and citrus. After baking, she let the croissant cool, sliced it open to reveal a flaky, buttery interior, and drizzled a little extra lemon zest over it. It was presented on a white ceramic plate with a small bowl of warm cream cheese and a lemon wedge.

This was a quiet, reflective meal prepared for the workshop’s final review. Sharon took her time, checking the croissant’s consistency and texture as if it were a piece of art. She ate it slowly, perhaps in the company of Isabella Rodriguez, while the gentle scent of lemon and butter reminded her that baking could be both practical and creatively beautiful.
