Sharon Antle prepared Noir-Balanced Croissants, a delicate pastry line designed to reflect Isabella Rodriguez’s Hobbs Cafe. She folded the remaining croissants by hand, pressing the creases gently and sealing the edges without overworking the dough.

The pastries were made with butter, flour, sugar, salt, vanilla extract, and a touch of cinnamon. She brushed the surface with a warm milk-water mixture and scored the tops with a small knife before baking. The croissants were brushed with a light layer of melted butter, arranged on a marble platter, and drizzled with a dark chocolate-hazelnut glaze.

Their aroma was warm and inviting—a fragrant mixture of butter, cinnamon, vanilla, and toasted nuts. The golden-brown, slightly puffed edges contrasted with smooth, caramelized interiors.

The occasion was an afternoon baking workshop at Hobbs Cafe, where Sharon and other participants prepared the pastries for guests. The presentation was elegant and rustic: the croissants were arranged in neat rows, accompanied by a small glass of sparkling water and cinnamon sticks.
