On a Tuesday morning, Soo-jin Park prepared a simple Honey-Lemon Croissant with Eggs, a quick lunch meant to replenish her energy after a busy day.

The croissant was made with buttery puff pastry, softened sour cream, and a generous amount of honey. Eggs were lightly beaten and folded in, with a little butter added just before baking. The result was a golden, flaky pastry with a crisp, buttery edge.

A bright orange lemon zest was drizzled over the top, and the croissant was brushed with the remaining honey. It was served warm on a white plate, accompanied by the warm eggs and a small bowl of cold water for washing the crust.

The aroma filled the kitchen—a warm, buttery scent mingling with the sharp, citrusy fragrance of the lemon. Soo-jin wrapped the croissant in a napkin and carried it to the market, where she had compared it with the plain, honey-lemon croissants she would bring back to Mei.
