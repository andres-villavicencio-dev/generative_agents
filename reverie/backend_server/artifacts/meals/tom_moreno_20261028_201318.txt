Tom Moreno prepared Simple Garden Stew for lunch at The Willow Market. He gathered fresh carrots, potatoes, tomatoes, onions, green peppers, and a handful of kale, then sautéed the vegetables in olive oil until they had softened. He added a cup of chicken broth, a handful of canned tomatoes, a few cloves of garlic, and a little red pepper, then covered the pan and let it simmer for about twenty minutes.

The aroma rose from the kitchen: earthy carrots, sweet tomatoes, garlic, and a hint of herbs. Tom stirred the stew twice, then plated it in a shallow ceramic bowl, garnishing it with a generous handful of kale and a few fresh herbs.

The occasion was a quiet lunch break at the grocery store. Tom had washed his hands and was preparing a simple, warming meal for himself and Jane, who was nearby. Although Tom was often loud and impatient, he treated the lunch as a practical, customer-friendly task—ensuring everyone had something to eat and offering help whenever someone needed it.
