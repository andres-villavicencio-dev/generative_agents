Victor prepared Mole Negro with Corn Tortillas and Roasted Vegetables, a warm, rich Mexican side dish for his neighbor Amilcar Rosales’s garden.

The dish combines Mexican mole negro with saffron, smoked paprika, chocolate, chili, garlic, onion, and cumin, along with corn tortillas and roasted peppers, onions, and tomatoes. The tortillas are lightly charred and stacked on a wooden board, while the vegetables are lightly roasted until crisp and golden.

The preparation was meticulous: Victor sautéed the aromatics, simmered the mole in a clay pot, then finished it with a small amount of butter and smoked paprika. He arranged the tortillas neatly beside the vegetables, garnishing them with fresh cilantro and a generous dusting of chili.

The aroma was unmistakably Mexican—smoky, earthy, warm, and fragrant with hints of chocolate and toasted cumin. The presentation was simple yet elegant, with the colorful vegetables and golden tortillas creating an inviting contrast.

The meal was prepared for Día de los Muertos, a family gathering honoring the departed. Victor cooked it with devotion, balancing the communal nature of the holiday with the precise, disciplined care he practiced as a retired engineer.
