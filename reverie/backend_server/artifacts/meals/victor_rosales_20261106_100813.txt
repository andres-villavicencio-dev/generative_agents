On Tuesday, February 14, Victor prepared Mexican-style oatmeal with cinnamon and toasted pecans for his family breakfast. The key ingredients were rolled oats, milk or water, cinnamon, a pinch of salt, and chopped pecans, with a few slices of orange for a bright finish.

Victor first measured the oats and milk carefully, then stirred them gently in the kettle until they softened. He prepared the pecans by toasting them in a dry skillet until they turned golden, then cracked them into a small bowl. He added the cinnamon and orange zest, drizzled the toasted pecans over the oatmeal, and heated everything until the mixture was warm and fragrant.

He served the oatmeal in a simple white bowl, arranging the golden nuts and orange shards around it, with a spoon resting beside it. The aroma filled the kitchen with warm milk, toasted nuts, cinnamon, and a hint of citrus. For his family’s Día de los Muertos gathering, Victor had prepared the meal with the same care and devotion he brings to his work: the meticulous preparation, balanced flavors, and thoughtful presentation reflected his disciplined nature, his roots in Mexican cooking, and his desire to nourish and celebrate his loved ones.
