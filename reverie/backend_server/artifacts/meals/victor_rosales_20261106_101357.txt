On Tuesday, February 14, Victor prepared a simple yet warming breakfast for his family: Oatmeal with Eggs and Fresh Fruit.

He used rolled oats, water, cinnamon, and a pinch of salt, then cooked them gently in a small pan until the oats became creamy and slightly chewy. He cracked two eggs into a separate pan, seasoned them lightly with salt and pepper, and cooked them until the whites were firm and the yolks remained soft. The fruit—apple slices, banana, and a small orange—was arranged around the oatmeal in the serving dish.

The kitchen filled with the aroma of toasted oats, caramelized eggs, cinnamon, and warm fruit. Victor presented the meal on a white ceramic plate, the eggs arranged in neat rows beside the oatmeal, and the fruit distributed among the oatmeal pieces. As he served a bowl of warm milk with sugar and a spoonful of honey, the family gathered around the table.

The occasion was a quiet family breakfast, an early preparation for the upcoming Día de los Muertos gathering. Victor’s meticulous, disciplined approach reflected his engineering background—he carefully measured each ingredient and arranged the food with deliberate care—while his devotion to family traditions made the meal feel both comforting and meaningful.
