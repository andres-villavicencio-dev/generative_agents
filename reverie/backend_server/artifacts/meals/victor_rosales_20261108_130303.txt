Victor prepared Carnitas al Ajillo with corn tortillas and refried beans for breakfast, while he rehearsed traditional songs for his family’s Día de los Muertos gathering.

The dish consists of pork shoulder marinated in garlic, onion, cumin, oregano, and a little lime juice, then slow-cooked until the meat is tender and the skin is crisp. It is served with a side of fluffy corn tortillas and warm refried beans topped with diced tomatoes and cilantro.

Victor cooked the meat on a large cast-iron skillet over medium heat, turning it frequently until it developed a golden-brown crust and a soft interior. The kitchen filled with the warm aroma of roasted pork, garlic, and toasted cumin.

He arranged the golden pork, crisp tortillas, and colorful beans on a white platter, garnishing the table with the red and orange petals of a marigold. The meal was part of his quiet morning ritual, balancing the discipline of his woodworking with the comforting traditions of his Mexican heritage.
