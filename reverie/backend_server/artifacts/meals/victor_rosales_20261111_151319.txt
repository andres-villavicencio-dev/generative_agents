Victor prepared Carnitas con Arroz de la Abuela, a comforting family feast for Día de los Muertos. The main dish consists of slow-cooked pork shoulder, bell peppers, onions, garlic, and tomatoes, seasoned with cumin, coriander, paprika, and a touch of lime. It is slow-roasted until the meat is tender, then shredded and served over a large pot of rice seasoned with vegetable broth, chili powder, and fresh cilantro. A side of queso fresco with jalapeños adds a bright, crunchy contrast.

The kitchen fills with the savory scent of caramelized pork and warm spices. Victor carefully arranges the shredded meat and rice into the serving dishes, placing small huatl—traditional sugar-dusted flower-shaped offerings—near the plate. He adds a small bowl of coco crema, sliced fresh fruit, and a few dried flowers. The meal is presented with a festive, handcrafted Mexican tablecloth and a simple candlelit centerpiece, echoing the solemn yet joyful spirit of the family gathering.

At the small college café, Victor meets Sam Moore and prepares to discuss one-on-one mentoring with at-risk youth, while the trellis he is carving adds a quiet, creative rhythm to his afternoon.
