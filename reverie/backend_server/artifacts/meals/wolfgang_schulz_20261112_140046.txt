Wolfgang prepared Pan-Seared Chicken with Roasted Vegetables and Lemon-Herb Dressing for lunch. He used chicken breast, red bell pepper, red onion, cherry tomatoes, garlic, olive oil, lemon juice, fresh parsley, and a pinch of salt and pepper.

He sliced the chicken, seared it in a hot pan until golden and cooked through, then added the vegetables. He tossed them in lemon juice, olive oil, and parsley, serving everything in a shallow white bowl. The dish was arranged in colorful rows of bright red peppers, green onions, and golden chicken, garnished with a few parsley leaves.

The aroma was warm and herbaceous, with the distinct scent of garlic, roasted onion, and lemon balancing the savory richness of the chicken. Wolfgang ate it in the classroom, pausing occasionally to review his chemistry notes for the first organic substitution reaction—specifically identifying how electron-donating and electron-withdrawing groups affect reactivity. He enjoyed the simple, nourishing meal alongside his dedication to improving his organic chemistry skills.
