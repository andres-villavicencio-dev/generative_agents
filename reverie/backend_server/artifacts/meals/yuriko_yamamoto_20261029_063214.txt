Yuriko prepared Omakase Sashimi with Tamago and Scallion Pancakes, a refined Japanese breakfast or light dinner. She marinated small tuna and salmon, then seared them thinly over a hot pan with a dash of soy sauce, mirin, and a pinch of sugar. After briefly blanching the fish, she sliced it against the grain and served it alongside soft, golden tamago and crisp scallion pancakes.

The preparation method was gentle and precise: the pan was heated until the oil shimmered, the fish was seared until just cooked through, and the remaining sauce was finished off with a spoon. Yuriko arranged the fish in a neat, parallel row, topped the tamago with a tiny dollop, and placed the pancakes beside it. She added a few green onions for color and a sprinkle of sesame and chili flakes for aroma.

The kitchen filled with the sharp aroma of smoked fish and toasted soy, the sweet scent of the simmered tamago, and the crisp fragrance of the scallion pancake. The meal was served at Yuriko’s local office on Wednesday, February 15, a quiet, purposeful time to finish her tax compliance project and prepare for the upcoming local mayor election.
