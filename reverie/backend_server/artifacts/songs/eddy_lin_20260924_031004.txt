I wake at seven, still soft as light,  
Brush teeth, make the bed, then face the night.  
Breakfast slow with coffee's quiet hum,  
Phone buzzes—just a thread, a moment's numb.  
Music theory whispers through my page,  
A rhythm born from thought and age.  

At nine thirty, doors swing open wide,  
Class begins where sound and time collide.  
Lunch with friends at Hobbs Cafe’s low,  
Ideas like sparks we let go slow.  

The library holds my focus, deep and vast,  
Lines drawn in margins on a student cast.  
Until five—my feet take flight, I roam,  
Campus paths echo in a soft refrain.  

Dinner's waiting, calm and full of grace,  
Piano after, filling space with space.  
I sleep at eleven, the day’s end done,  
But dreams are where new songs become one.
