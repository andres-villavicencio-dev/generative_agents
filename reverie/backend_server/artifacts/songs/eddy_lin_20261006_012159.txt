The morning light hits the dorm room floor,
I crawl out of bed, wanting something more.
Coffee in hand, I walk to the hall,
Waiting for the bell to ring out and call.
Classes start at ten, the schedule is tight,
But I don't mind the pressure, I don't mind the fight.
I grab my notebook, I grab my pen,
Trying to find a place where the music begins.

At Hobbs Cafe, the steam rises high,
I watch the people walk by.
A latte sweet, a pastry warm,
I’m trying to find a different form.
The ambient noise, a gentle hum,
Helps the ideas come, helps the rhythm come.
I sketch out a progression, minor to major,
Hoping that the notes won't caper.

But when the evening comes and the sun goes down,
I sit at the piano, looking around.
The strings are waiting, the wood is cold,
A story waiting to be told.
I play a chord, then I play it again,
Trying to find where the melody’s at.
It’s not about being perfect or fast,
It’s about the feelings that are built to last.

So I write the verses, I write the refrain,
Putting the emotions back into the brain.
A curious mind, an analytical ear,
Making the music disappear... then reappear.
This is the life of Eddy Lin,
Finding the rhythm, finding where to begin.
