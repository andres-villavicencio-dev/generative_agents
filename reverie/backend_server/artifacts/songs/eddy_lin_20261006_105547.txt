The train rattles through the city outskirts,
A metal snake shedding concrete skin.
I watch the shadows lengthen on the walls,
Stretching toward a place where the noise begins.

The clock above the lecture hall reads four,
But my mind is still back in the theory room.
We talked of resolution and dominant sevenths,
But the silence in the practice booth is a tomb.

I grab my coat and I walk to Hobbs Café,
The espresso machine hisses like a snake.
The barista nods, a rhythm in her step,
As I slide into a booth, awake.

The steam rises, curling above the table,
A ghost of warmth in the afternoon air.
I pull out my notebook, staff lines waiting,
For the melody that no one else can hear.

It starts with a single note, suspended,
Floating above the bustle of the street.
A minor third, then a gentle rise,
Chasing the weight of the day down the concrete.

The chorus swells, a wall of sound,
Not of anger, but of wanting to be found.
I sing of the days that blur into nights,
Of the quiet fights and the fading lights.
Of the student who’s learning how to breathe,
Through the music, and the things that he believes.

The final chord resolves into silence,
The cafe clock ticks hands that never cease.
I close the book, the ink still wet,
With the song complete, and the world asleep.
