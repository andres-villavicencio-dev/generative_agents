The morning light cuts through the blinds at seven,
A quiet shift before the campus wakes.
I brew a pot of black, let the steam rise,
While the city hums its distant wake.

Ten o'clock, the lecture hall fills with sound,
Professors speak of counterpoint and key.
I sit with notebook open, pencil tapping,
Mapping out the geometry of a melody.

But the classroom isn't where the magic lives,
It waits for the hours that follow.
Three p.m., I slip my bag onto my shoulder,
Walk the five blocks to Hobbs Cafe.

The bell above the door chimes a welcome song,
The scent of espresso and fresh bake.
A corner table, window looking out,
I pull out my sketchbook, pen in hand.

The barista asks if I want something sweet,
I nod, a croissant, butter and flaky.
But my mind is elsewhere, drifting on a chord,
A minor seventh, resolving to the G.

I write of rain on a tin roof,
Of trains that never quite arrive on time.
Of a love that was a harmonic progression,
Building tension, waiting for the rhyme.

An hour passes, then two, the cafe fills,
Students typing, conversations blending.
But I am in my own world, constructing,
Building bridges between the notes I’ve learned.

Five o'clock, the sun begins to dip,
I close the notebook, satisfied.
The work is done, the ideas on paper,
Waiting for the night to give them life.

I walk home as the streetlights flicker on,
The day's lessons settled in my mind.
Another chord drafted, another phrase refined,
Ready for the quiet that I’ll find.
