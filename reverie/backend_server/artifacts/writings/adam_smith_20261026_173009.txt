Wednesday, February 15.

I began the day with a quiet purpose: to compare the three candidates’ proposals, including Francisco’s, with the mural and path options I have been developing. The morning routine, including the walk and the lunch, has kept my thoughts clear. Between creative writing, book planning, research, and the detailed work of comparing budgets and funding sources, I have remained focused and productive.

Francisco’s proposal has several strengths. Its clear structure, practical budget details, and emphasis on the mural and path make it easy to evaluate. However, its funding sources appear inconsistent, and the path options lack sufficient comparison. These weaknesses are not fatal, but they do make the proposal less compelling.

My own plan is more flexible than his. The mural should reflect a unified visual theme, while the path should offer a clear route, accessible crossings, and sufficient public space. I need more concrete funding sources and a more thorough comparison of the three proposals, including their costs, timelines, and sustainability.

I also need to consider the work of the book. Its strongest supporting evidence comes from Smith’s own observations of human nature, particularly how people seek clarity when confused and use shared experiences to make decisions. The weakest passages, by contrast, are speculative and occasionally repetitive. They need to be revised so the argument remains grounded in evidence rather than impression.

I feel connected, supported, and confident as I work through these comparisons. I hope to produce a proposal that is well-reasoned, well-funding, and well-designed.
