This Wednesday, February 15, has felt full and purposeful. My corrected proposal, the mural and path options for comparison with Francisco’s proposal, and the budget and funding sources for the three candidates remain at the center of my day. I have spent the morning reviewing the plans, comparing their practicalities, and identifying the points of divergence. By lunch, I had not yet lost the confidence that Francisco’s vision, though imaginative, still needs a clearer foundation.

Afternoon work has been quieter, though not empty. I reviewed the books I have been reading, not merely to note plot points, but to find recurring themes and metaphors that illuminate the ideas in my own research. One passage reminded me of the way ideas can travel through a work, taking the original form and carrying something essential into a new context. Another image about light and shadow suggested that clarity often requires a little sacrifice of immediate comfort. These reflections may appear abstract, but they also help me ask questions: What does each proposal preserve? What does it introduce? Where does it become merely decorative?

By evening, I took a quiet moment to distinguish between the three candidates. I did not dismiss Francisco’s creativity, and I did not assume his plans were perfect. I also did not treat the budget and funding sources as simple numbers. Each offers a different balance of ambition, feasibility, and continuity. The comparison requires patience, but the process has made me feel supported, connected, and increasingly confident in my own choices.

I will carry these distinctions into the follow-up on Tuesday, February 14. I may not be able to make an immediate decision, but I can carry forward the questions I have asked and the evidence I have gathered. The routine supports me: the morning routine, the writing and research, the lunch, the afternoon work, and the evening reflection. They form a reliable path, and they also make the uncertainty feel manageable.

I will continue to write. Writing does not always produce answers, but it keeps them alive.
