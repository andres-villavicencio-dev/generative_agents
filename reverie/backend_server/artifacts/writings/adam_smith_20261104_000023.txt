I have read a book on creativity, and I feel that it deserves more than a superficial endorsement. Creativity is not merely the production of a new idea; it is the willingness to question the assumptions that shape our lives, to imagine what might be, and to pursue it even when the path appears uncertain. The world we inhabit is not fixed by convention. Local governance is no exception.

In my view, the mayor should not be the sole architect of the community. A good leader is less a commander and more a servant of the people’s judgment. The most important civic participation is not the formal voting booth, but the willingness to engage with the administration, to understand its plans, and to offer constructive alternatives. This may seem simple, but it is rarely practiced. Many citizens believe they have no real influence, while others remain content with merely symbolic approval.

Creativity begins with inquiry. The local government should encourage citizens to ask difficult questions: What are our actual needs? What are the costs of our current arrangements? How can we solve them without imposing our own preferences? When the mayor listens to concerns that have been ignored, when policies are revised after evidence rather than after partisan advantage, and when local institutions are given the space to innovate, the community itself begins to become a creator.

The election next month is an opportunity for this process to be tested. The candidate who proposes policies that require active participation and measurable improvement may appear less certain, yet that uncertainty can be valuable. It reminds voters that leadership is not merely the execution of a plan, but the establishment of conditions in which thoughtful citizens can shape the future.

Finally, I wish that creativity would not be treated as a rare talent reserved for the brilliant few. It is a habit of mind: a steady curiosity, the courage to revise one’s own conclusions, and the confidence that a better way may still exist. The same attitude should guide our discussion of the next mayor.
