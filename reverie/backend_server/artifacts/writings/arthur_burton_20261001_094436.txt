The morning light slips through the window like a soft secret, warm but gentle—just enough to wake me without startling me awake. I stretch slowly, stretching my arms as if pulling up threads of quiet joy from last night’s silence. A glass of water, cool and clear, sits beside me while I sip, letting the world blur into stillness.

By ten-thirty, Jane is curled beside me with The Secret Garden, its pages turning like petals in spring. We read without hurry, our voices barely above a whisper when we speak. Lunch finds us at The Rose & Crown Pub, where sandwiches are piled high and stories linger as long as the afternoon sun does on the garden wall.

At one, I return to my armchair by the window, pen in hand, journal open. No rush, just thoughts: the way laughter rings through this town, how kindness is a habit we carry like a stone in our pocket. Today’s seed envelope rests in my palm—succulents, wildflowers. I’ll plant them when the earth remembers warmth.

TV flickers on at seven, Jane reviewing notes like a quiet song. We watch until eight, tea steaming between us. Dinner comes home at four, a meal shared over chipped plates and shared silence. And then—candlelight. The flame catches the paper, the words begin to flow: I am here. I am gentle. I plant tomorrow.
