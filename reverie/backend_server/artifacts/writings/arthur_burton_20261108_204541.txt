Tuesday, February 14, The Rose and Crown

I’ve been thinking a lot about cocktails lately. I like the idea of something with a little personality—something that makes people smile and remember the night. I’m not trying to make every drink perfect, but I want them to feel special and leave with a good story.

Tonight’s theme: The Midnight Rose. It’s a dark, rich old-fashioned with a splash of blood orange, a dash of grenadine, and a touch of orange liqueur. The glass is dark amber, the garnish is a thin rose petal, and the name says everything I want to say.

Another idea: The Velvet Hourglass. A gin-and-tonic, but with a little elderflower syrup and an edible gold leaf over the glass. The gold makes it look like it’s been carefully poured, and the elderflower gives it a quiet, floral finish.

I’m also considering The Golden Hour, a lemon-lime cocktail with a twist. Lemon-lime is already a crowd favorite, but I’d like to add a splash of cucumber and a hint of cucumber liqueur. The result should be refreshing, clean, and slightly unexpected.

I’m also thinking about The Rose and Crown, a signature drink named after the pub. It could be a gin-based cocktail with a splash of champagne, a little rosemary, and a twist of lemon. The name should sound warm and inviting, not pretentious.

For now, I’m gathering ingredients, testing the ratios, and making sure each drink feels like a celebration rather than a chore. I want people to sit down, smile, and say, “This is something I’ll remember.”

Tonight, I’m going to make the Midnight Rose, then take a moment to notice the people around me. I want every guest to feel like they’ve arrived at a place where they belong.
