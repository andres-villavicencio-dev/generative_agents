One weakness I noticed was that I had described guilt and moral responsibility in my analysis, but I had not always explained how Shakespeare develops them through conflict rather than through direct statement. Strengthening that distinction should make my writing more precise.

My thesis examines how guilt, responsibility, and moral uncertainty shape the characters in the plays I am studying. The argument cannot rest on the claim that the characters experience conflicting emotions, because that observation alone is too general. A stronger analysis would explain the circumstances that produce those emotions and show how the characters’ choices expose the limits of their judgment. Guilt, in particular, should not be treated simply as the result of a wrongful action. It is also shaped by social expectation, personal values, and the inability to undo what has been done.

I also want to vary my prose. Some of my sentences begin with the same structural patterns, and several rely on vague phrases such as “very important” or “has a great impact.” Replacing those expressions with specific claims will make the analysis sound more confident and academic. I should also distinguish between textual evidence and my own interpretation rather than presenting interpretation as if it were directly stated by Shakespeare.
