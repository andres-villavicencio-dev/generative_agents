Monday, February 13

Today felt productive, though I know that saying can be dangerous. I spent the morning in my classes at Oak Hill College, and the afternoon at the library, working steadily on my senior thesis. My argument centers on the use of language in Shakespeare’s plays, particularly how dialogue reveals character while also shaping the audience’s understanding of the dramatic world.

I revised the section on Hamlet for a long time. At first, I thought the key was simply Hamlet’s famous speeches, but the closer I read, the more I realized that his language changes depending on whom he is addressing. With Polonius, he plays the fool and hides behind jokes. With Claudius, his words become sharp and accusatory, even when he appears to accept the situation. With Ophelia, his tenderness and uncertainty are painfully clear. I added a paragraph about how Shakespeare makes us complicit in Hamlet’s thinking, almost inviting us to interpret the same uncertainty differently.

After dinner, I returned to the library and read several analyses of the play. I do not always agree with them, but that is useful. Their interpretations make me question my own claims instead of accepting them too quickly. I wrote down three questions I still need to answer, including whether I am focusing too heavily on spoken language and neglecting the physical actions that give the speeches meaning.

I also worked on a writing exercise for class. It was harder than I expected because I kept trying to sound overly academic. My professor once told us that clear thinking produces clear writing, so I tried to simplify a few sentences and cut unnecessary words.

It is now 10:00 p.m. I feel tired, but satisfied that I made real progress. Tomorrow I will organize my notes, strengthen the argument about Hamlet, and continue looking closely at the language Shakespeare gives his characters. I am learning that good literary analysis is not about finding one perfect answer. It is about asking precise questions and following the evidence wherever it leads.
