Tuesday, February 14

My thesis feels more like a conversation than a finished argument. I’ve been working through Shakespeare’s use of language, but I’ve been noticing something that often goes unexamined: the gestures that accompany what people say. A gesture is not merely decoration. It shapes how a word lands, how a conflict is understood, and how the audience joins the play.

Jane Moreno’s photography offers me a useful model. In her images, subjects often move together—a hand resting on a shoulder, a raised hand in agreement, an arm reaching toward another person. These shared gestures suggest that language does not exist in isolation. Words carry meaning, but they are also performed through the body. When dialogue moves from a quiet exchange to a deliberate gesture, the conversation becomes more urgent, more socially connected, or more emotionally honest.

I am considering how Shakespeare’s characters might respond to these gestures rather than treating them as interruptions. Does a raised hand in All’s Well That Ends in Good signal trust or resistance? Does a repeated gesture in The Tempest reveal whether a character is trying to communicate or to conceal? I want to trace how gesture and dialogue interact to create meaning.

For now, I have no fixed schedule or location for Tuesday. I am still developing my argument, and I want to balance my classes with careful reading and writing. I do not need every idea to be published immediately, but I need to make sure I understand the work before I say what I think.

I am curious, I am determined, and I am willing to spend time on something that matters. I will read, write, and revise. I will also keep looking at gestures, because they may hold the key to what I am studying.
