Tuesday, February 14, was not a quiet Tuesday. It was full. I woke at 6:00, brushed my teeth, and carried the routine like a promise I had made to myself. By 10:00, I’d finished my classes, the senior thesis, and a few pages of creative writing. Dinner was five, and then I had time to reflect, connect with friends, and let the house settle around me.

I sat at the table with my notebook, the silver spoon resting against the bowl. Outside, the streetlights were beginning to glow. One of them was brighter than the other.

I had been thinking about language lately. Shakespeare, gentrification, housing, displacement—the words were tangled together in ways I had not anticipated. I wanted to understand them, not just admire them. I wanted to know how a single word could change a person’s world, how a line could make someone feel safer or more alone. My research had been precise, but the answers were still arriving slowly.

Tonight, I wrote down what I’d learned: language is not merely decoration. It carries power. It can soften anger or sharpen it. It can make a stranger feel welcome or make them feel unwanted. I had not always been so observant. I had been too busy chasing the next sentence, the next discovery, the next story.

As I looked out the window, the light from the window had shifted toward the other side of the street. The brighter light had changed color. The warmth was returning.

I closed my notebook and sat back down. The words were still there, waiting. I did not need to finish everything today. I only needed to keep noticing.

Gentrification had not always come with a bright sign. Sometimes it came quietly, beneath the surface of a street, a small shop, a quiet room. Sometimes it came with noise, with demands, with a promise that people would stay. I had spent much of my life wondering whether I could build something new without displacing the old.

Tonight, I would listen. I would listen to the street, to the voices, to the way the light moved across the sidewalk.

And I would write again. Not because I had to, but because I wanted to know what I could find.
