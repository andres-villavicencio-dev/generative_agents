Monday, February 13, 10:45 AM

I arrived at Oak Hill College with the same quiet energy I carry everywhere I go. The library smelled of old paper, coffee, and the faint scent of ink. I wanted the afternoon to feel productive, but instead, it kept drifting toward something more useful.

I spent the first hour reading the plays. Shakespeare’s language is dense and layered, and I wanted to understand it properly. By the second act of Hamlet, I noticed how the repetition in “to be or not to be” changes the weight of the opening scene. I marked the moment the soliloquy becomes a question rather than a statement.

At the same time, I was reviewing the thesis outline. My introduction was too broad. I had focused on language, but the real question was how it shapes each character’s choices, which, I was beginning to realize, is not just about syntax. I needed to connect the words to the larger human questions the plays raise.

Around 12:30 PM, I organized my sources in a spreadsheet. I kept the plays in chronological order, grouped them by theme, and added a column for each play’s strongest linguistic example. I also marked which passages needed deeper reading.

By 2:15 PM, the last class ended, and I walked back toward the dorm. I needed to eat something solid before heading home. I ate dinner at 5:00 PM, and by 10:00 PM I was asleep.

I still have three pages to revise, and I’m not sure whether I will finish the thesis tonight. Sometimes the work I value most is the part I haven’t finished. But I’m determined to finish it with some kind of clarity.
