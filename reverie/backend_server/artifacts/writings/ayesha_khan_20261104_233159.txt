I wanted to apologize, but the truth was harder to admit. The day had been busy and purposeful, yet also overwhelmed.

Today, I’m continuing my senior thesis on language in Shakespeare, and I’ve found myself asking a question that kept returning: what is language doing in the plays? Ayesha Khan, who is also taking a writing class and learning to identify parallels between texts, seems to be noticing the same patterns. Perhaps that is why I feel a little uncertain about how much I am supposed to understand and how much I am supposed to say.

At the writing class, the instructor asked us to compare how each author creates tension between order and chaos. Ayesha Khan pointed out parallel structures and repeated motifs, noting that the same rhetorical device can make a character feel both controlled and trapped. Her reading of the play helped me see that language is not just decoration; it is a tool, a way of revealing what we cannot easily put into words.

I’m grateful for her attention. I’m also grateful for the class. It is difficult, sometimes exhausting, to keep searching without losing myself. Ayesha Khan made me think that curiosity is not just about knowing everything—it is about paying attention, noticing connections, and asking difficult questions.

I still have groceries waiting, Wolfgang still waiting at the store, and a lot of unfinished thoughts. But I also have a thesis, a class, and a day to finish. I will carry the feeling that this day was busy and purposeful, but also exhausted. I will carry it with me. I will also carry the feeling that sometimes I am not enough for everything I am asking. And perhaps I do enough for now.
