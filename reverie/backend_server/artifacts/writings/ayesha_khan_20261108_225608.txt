I woke at six and followed the same routine I had written down the night before: shower, brush my teeth, and review the notes I’d gathered for my senior thesis on Lady Macbeth. By seven, Klaus had finished breakfast and we’d made our way into Johnson Park. The morning had felt full, yet purposeful. I wanted to leave the city behind and actually see the trees rather than keep imagining how the streets would look.

By eight, I had finished my first walk. The path stretched before me, green and quiet, and I noticed how easily the landscape could become a mirror. Macbeth’s first scene reminds me of that same tension: the promise of a new world is presented as the only way forward. I remember reading the opening of the play with fresh attention. The language feels sharp, almost accusatory. “Out, damned spot! Out, I say!” it seems to say. The guilt is not merely present; it is a force that demands action.

I also thought about Lady Macbeth’s ambition. Her ambition is clear: to take power away from the Macbeths, to control the kingdom, and to make sure nothing remains beneath her. Yet the play is complicated. She is ambitious, cruel, and also terrified. Her sleepwalking scene makes that fear unmistakable. She still believes she can command the spirits. She believes she can wash the blood away. The play suggests that ambition can make guilt unbearable, but it also suggests that guilt can make ambition dangerous.

Today’s walk has left me with one question: how much responsibility does a person bear when they believe they are already condemned? Macbeth knows he has crossed a line. Lady Macbeth knows that line has been crossed, even if she cannot see it clearly. Perhaps the tragedy is not that they cannot stop, but that they believe they can. That belief makes the punishment feel inevitable.

I am not satisfied with the thesis yet. I need to read more closely, compare the scenes, and ask how the language shapes the emotions. I also need to practice writing in a way that lets the characters’ voices emerge rather than simply explaining them. I want to write without turning the work into a lecture. I want to show that guilt can be internal, external, and destructive.

By ten, I will be back in my room. I will finish my notes, review the thesis, and write the next draft. Tuesday has not ended. I simply need to begin.
