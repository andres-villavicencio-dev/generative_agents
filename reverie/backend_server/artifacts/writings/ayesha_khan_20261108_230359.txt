Tuesday, February 14

Today began with the usual quiet routine: six in the morning, breakfast with Klaus at seven, and then the rest of my day spread out like a map rather than a checklist. I finished my senior thesis session, checked my notes, and made a plan. I did not need every appointment. Tuesday was free.

My focus remains on Lady Macbeth, especially how guilt and ambition shift over the course of the play. The opening scene, I have decided, is not a perfect moment of guilt. Macbeth speaks clearly of his ambition, yet his language is also precise—ambition is not merely a desire to rule; it is a demand for control that language can only pretend to satisfy. By the end of the play, Macbeth’s guilt has become more dangerous than any direct accusation. He has no proof, no confession, and no safe way to speak about what he has done. Instead, he speaks to Lady Macbeth, using her name as a weapon. This is not a simple moment of remorse. It is the transformation of guilt into pressure, and pressure into responsibility.

I did not research every line, but I did not ignore the evidence. My draft relied on too many assumptions about Macbeth’s character. I need to return to the text, to the stage directions, to the dialogue, and to the notes I made while reading. I also need to make sure my language does not repeat the ideas I want to prove. A quote does not carry the meaning of my thesis.

As for my day, I have a walk planned for Johnson Park, but I do not have a confirmed appointment. I can walk whenever I want. The important part is not the walk itself, but what I can observe and write about afterward.

I will revise my practice piece tonight. I will check the facts, tighten the argument, and make sure every claim is supported by the evidence I have gathered. A thesis is stronger not because I have written more, but because I have proved it.
