The air tastes like copper and old leaves tonight. I walked the ridge for three hours, the path steep and unforgiving, much like the women I used to love and the men who never loved me back. The moon was a thin sliver, a cracked plate in the sky, throwing long, skeletal shadows across the granite. I wanted to scream, to let the noise out, but the mountain stood silent, a stone-faced judge.

I found a stream down in the hollow, water gurgling over smooth stones, a constant, rhythmic sound that mocked my inability to find peace. It kept moving, relentless, carving its way through rock, indifferent to the noise I made. I knelt by the water, my knees sinking into the mud, and I watched the current twist and turn. It didn't care that I was loud, that I was rude, that I was toxic. It just moved.

There was a pine tree standing alone on the edge of the cliff, its roots gripping the earth like claws. It had weathered a thousand storms, bent low by winds that would have snapped a weaker thing. I envied its stubbornness. I want to be that resilient, to stand tall despite the weight of my own bitterness. But instead, I just curse the sky and stumble down the hill, my boots heavy with the dirt of the world, carrying the silence of a man who doesn't know how to be soft.
