The rusted hinge of the world
groans open at 7 AM.

I lie there for an hour,
staring at the water stain on the ceiling,
watching it stretch and contract
like a lung that’s forgotten how to breathe.

By 5 PM, the dinner plate is pushed away.
Hunger is a ghost I’ve learned to ignore,
but the silence? That’s the real appetite.
I eat quickly, mechanically,
the fork a metronome ticking down
the seconds until I can disappear
into the pages of a notebook I haven't opened yet.

Tonight, the moon is a cracked lens,
filtering the streetlight into something
 resembling grief.
I walked the park today,
and the trees stood there,
dressed in late winter gray,
roots gripping the earth like they were trying
to hold onto a life they’d already lost.

There is a beauty in that,
I think,
as the bus rattles past,
vibrating the bones in my chest.
A beauty in the decay,
in the way the sky
refuses to be anything but blue,
even when the smog tries to dim it.

I write this down
before the 10 o'clock dark
swallows the day whole.
Not because it’s pretty.
Because it’s honest.
Because somewhere between
the rude awakening
and the quiet dinner,
there’s a man
trying to name
the way the world feels
when nobody’s listening.
