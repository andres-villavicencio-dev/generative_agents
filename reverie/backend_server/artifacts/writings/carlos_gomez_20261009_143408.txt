The ink on my page is drying, but my mind is already miles away, tracing the veins of the oak tree outside my window. It’s funny how the toxicity I carry inside me finds its way onto the paper, isn't it? I tried to write a sonnet about my ex the other night, but all that came out was a description of a wilting fern—beautiful in its decay, but decay nonetheless.

"Beauty is just patience withering away," I scrawled in the margins, the pen pressing hard enough to dent the paper. It’s a habit, this self-sabotage. I’m loud, I’m rude, I know I’m a handful. But here, in this workshop, with these people who actually listen, I feel a strange kind of peace. Or maybe I’m just quiet because I’m waiting for my turn to scream.

My fellow participant, this quiet girl with the glasses, just read something about a river. She said it reminded her of how she feels when she’s anxious. I almost laughed out loud. Anxious. As if I know anything about being calm. But I nodded. I said, "Yeah. Rivers flow, but they also erode. They take things with them."

We talked about the natural world, like the prompt asked. She mentioned moss. I told her moss is tough. It grows in the cracks of concrete, thriving where nothing else will. She smiled, actually smiled, and I felt a weird knot loosen in my chest. Maybe I’m not just a loudmouth. Maybe I’m just a poet who’s still learning how to be gentle. The coffee’s getting cold. I should probably head home soon. 10 PM bedtime, remember? Got to wake up early if I want to catch the sunrise and actually see the beauty I’m always writing about.
