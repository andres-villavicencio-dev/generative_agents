It’s Monday, February 13, and I woke up at seven feeling like the sun had personally insulted me. I need coffee, quiet, and about three hours in which nobody asks me to be “vulnerable.” Damn birds chirping like the world didn’t wake up angry. My neck is stiff, my head is foggy, and my dreams were useless. I was trying to escape, but even in my sleep, I kept forgetting the route. How completely stupid.

I’m still building the collection about the natural world, and I hate how sentimental everyone gets when they say “nature.” Trees are not gentle friends. They stand there, green and silent, watching us rot. Rivers don’t care if we drink their water. Mountains don’t owe us peace. Still, when the wind moves through the pines at dusk, something in me unclenches. Annoying. I want to tear that softness out and turn it into something honest.

The workshop teacher said my images were “vivid and emotionally charged,” which is workshop language for “you make everything feel like a threat.” I said, “Good. Nature is threatening. It has teeth, weather, disease, wolves, and bugs.” Half the room laughed. The other half probably saw a poem in it. Whatever. I’m not here to make them comfortable. I’m here because I want to write lines that hit like stones in a boot.

The pines refuse the morning,
green shadows standing shoulder to shoulder.
The river keeps its secrets,
cuts through the valley
like it knows every scar I buried.

I hear wind combing through the branches—
a cold hand, rough with dirt,
testing the locks of my ribs.
Below, the river flashes its teeth
and carries my name toward the sea.

I wanted the woods to teach me peace.
Instead, they taught me hunger:
to want, to hunger, to be wanted back.
Even the stones look tired.
Even the roots pull underground.
And I stand here, loud and thirsty,
letting the leaves fall
like sentences I cannot say.

I’m taking this to workshop tonight. I’ll read it without apologizing. If they call it “raw,” I’ll call them cowards. If they call it beautiful, I’ll tell them beauty is just pain that learned to sit still.
