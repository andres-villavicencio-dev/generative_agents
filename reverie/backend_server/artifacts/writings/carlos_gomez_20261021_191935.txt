Again, the sun comes back, and I’m supposed to feel grateful. Fine. The sky is doing its pale blue imitation of hope, while the February branches rake their bare fingers across the wind. I stood outside long enough for the cold to bite through my gloves. Good. I wanted something honest.

I’ve been thinking about the workshop, about how everyone there talks about “finding beauty in ordinary things” as if beauty doesn’t have teeth. A robin landed on the fence this morning, plump and furious, stealing crumbs from absolutely nothing. I admired the robbery. At least it had nerve.

My head is loud today. Every thought knocks over the next one. Write about trees, it says. Write about rain. Make it graceful. Make it sound like your heart isn’t a clenched fist. I wrote: “The oak stands with the patience of a grudge.” The instructor called it vivid. I called it accurate.

Here’s the poem:

The river keeps moving
because stopping would mean remembering
where the water came from.

Snow loosens its white fists
one shadow at a time.
The creek carries every fallen branch
like bad news it cannot refuse.

I do not ask the sun
to teach me brightness.
I ask what survives
the long night
without pretending
it was never afraid.

By five, dinner will be waiting. I’ll eat, wash the plate, and sit here until ten, turning sentences over like stones with suspicious shapes hidden underneath. Maybe tonight the trees will say something worth stealing. Maybe I’ll listen long enough to notice that the wind is not empty, only crowded with places I haven’t learned to name.
