Monday, February 13.

I’m awake at seven, which means the world can deal with me for at least an hour. Coffee first. Black. If I sip it like some delicate little robin, the morning will think I’m tame. I’m not. I’m here to tear the page open and leave tooth marks.

Intention: write until the thoughts stop looking polished. No polite nonsense. No pretending every dead leaf is a profound symbol. Sometimes a dead leaf is just a dead leaf, brown and curled up like an old man who lost an argument.

I want to write about the trees today, but I don’t want to sound like every soft-footed poet in the workshop sniffing a lavender bush and claiming it “opened their heart.” The trees don’t whisper. They hammer. They drink in the dark and stand like ugly accusations against the sky. An oak is not gentle just because it has survived. Sometimes survival is just rage with better posture.

The river kept moving after the rain.
It did not ask the stones
whether they had a past
or if their roughness
was a wound it understood.
It just moved,
and the stones
got quieter.

Fuck elegance, anyway. The real river smells like mud and fish and crushed weeds. That’s what gives the poem its spine. The workshop instructor wants us to “notice beauty.” Fine. The dead branch against a blue sky is beautiful because it refuses to hide. The crow on the fence is beautiful because it looks like a black numeral in a book written by a bastard editor. Beauty should have teeth.

I wish the pines would stop pretending they’re monks. They’re loud, green towers, chopping sunlight into pieces. Even their shadows look like they’ve got somewhere to be. Roots underground, making silent arguments with the soil. I like that. No speeches. No group hugs. Just pressure, hunger, and growth so stubborn it hurts.

By eight, my coffee is cold and the notebook smells faintly of rain. Good. I’m awake enough to bully a stanza into existence.
