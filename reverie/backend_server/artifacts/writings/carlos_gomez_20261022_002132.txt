The workshop room smelled like wet paper and coffee that had given up. John Lin sat near the window, scrolling through his phone like he was waiting for the whole damn thing to end. I almost said something mean. Then I remembered the line I’d written yesterday: “The trees kept their secrets until the wind demanded them.” I wrote it down again, underlining it twice.

Nobody asks what a tree knows. Nobody asks what I know. I have spent years making sure my silence looks like ignorance, but silence is just anger wearing a respectable shirt. At home, my morning pages were full of weather, memory, the same old hunger to turn everything into a poem because ordinary truth feels too small, too stupid, too human.

I crossed out: “I miss the version of me who believed renewal was possible.” It was beautiful. It was also bullshit. People do not change because they wandered beneath a row of oak trees and felt inspired. Still, I copied it into my notebook before the workshop could talk me out of it.

John looked up. “You still doing those nature walks?”

“Every chance I get. The birds don’t lie to me.”

“ Birds don’t talk at all.”

“That’s what makes them trustworthy.”

He laughed. Good. I had missed him, though I would rather chew glass than say so. We compared lines after lunch. I read the oak-tree line first. John said it sounded like a greeting card, which was rude, so I told him his latest poem sounded like a grocery receipt written by a depressed robot. We laughed. The room softened.

On the walk, the afternoon went gold and ugly all at once. I wrote: “I came outside angry at the world, and the world answered with moss.” I wrote: “Memory is a door painted shut.” I wrote: “Renewal is not a dawn. It is the knife-edge of the blade.”

Then I underlined the strongest ones and crossed out the rest, even the ones I loved. Revision is supposed to be murder, somebody said once. Fine. I can kill a sentence. I can even kill the man who wrote it.

By the time I got home, my hands were cold, my head was loud, and I had three lines worth keeping. That was enough. That was more than enough.
