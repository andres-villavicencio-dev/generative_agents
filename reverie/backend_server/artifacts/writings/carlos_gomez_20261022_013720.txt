I woke at seven with daylight pressing its cold fingers against the blinds. I brushed my teeth, splashed water on my face, and opened the notebook before the world could start demanding things from me. Morning pages are ugly at first. They spit out the sediment: old arguments, the heat in my chest, the names I carry like stones in my shoes. I let them fall. Gratitude can wait. First, the truth.

I wrote about the trees along the river, how their roots hold on underground even when storms make them look thin and defeated. I wrote about memory, that jealous house where the past keeps every window lit. I wrote about longing until the word wore smooth. Then I wrote about anger—sometimes a locked door, sometimes a match, sometimes a dog snapping at its own reflection. I wrote about identity, which is less a statue than a weather system, changing in the mouth of every person who speaks my name.

At the workshop, John Lin read his lines and smiled. We talked about metaphor, risk, and the danger of making poetry so safe it becomes furniture. I laughed loudly enough to make one of the younger poets glance up. Good. Let the room hear me. I have spent too many years lowering my voice to keep other people comfortable.

Later I walked through the park. The air smelled of wet bark and winter. Every branch pointed somewhere. Even the dead leaves seemed to be practicing a return.

Tonight I will close the notebook, but I will not close the door. Something in me has cracked open—not broken, not fixed, just opened. I am still angry. I am still tender. I am still becoming. And for once, those facts do not argue with each other.
