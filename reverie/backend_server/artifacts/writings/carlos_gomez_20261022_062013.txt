The forest before sunrise was wet with rain, the stones giving off a dull shine beneath my boots. I stood there breathing in the smell of pine and turned earth, listening to the river argue with the current. Somewhere past the trees, a storm kept folding itself together, and I thought about all the things I had carried without naming. I had spent so long mistaking volume for strength, and rage for honesty, that I was surprised when the anger in me revealed what it had been protecting. Sometimes I missed the person I was before I learned to bite back. Sometimes I missed the version of myself that could be soft without being weak.

All of that is still here, but I do not have to let it drive.

I found a bench near the water and opened my notebook. The pages were crooked, the ink smudged, and the first line sounded like something anyone might write. I crossed it out. Then I wrote about my mother’s hands, how they rested on my shoulders whenever I came home angry. I wrote about the town I left, the streetlights that made the rain look golden, and the last thing my father said before the door closed. The memories arrived without permission, each one carrying its own weather.

By the time the sun broke through, I had drafted three lines. They were not perfect, but they moved. I know how to make plans that survive contact with the world. I can wake, write, walk, return to the page. I can build something steady without pretending the ground beneath me has never shifted.

The river kept going. So did I. I tore out the bad line, folded the page, and started again. Not because I had become gentle, but because I had learned that even a loud man can turn his noise into a song.
