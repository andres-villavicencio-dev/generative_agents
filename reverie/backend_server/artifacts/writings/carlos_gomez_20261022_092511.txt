Thursday. February 16.

I woke up at seven with the same headache sitting behind my eyes like a creditor who knows I’ve been avoiding him. No appointment. No clock telling me where to be except the one inside my skull, always running, always louder than it has any right to be. I brushed my teeth, drank water, stood in front of the mirror, and told myself I looked tired. That was generous. I looked like a man who had argued with a river and lost.

Morning pages from 7:30 to 9:00. The pen is already warm in my hand. I’m going to write until the thoughts stop pretending they’re orderly.

I’ve been thinking about memory again. How it keeps the ugliest things in glass and places the beautiful ones in a drawer without labels. I remember the sound of my mother calling my name from another room. I remember the cold metal of a railing pressing into my chest. I remember being young enough to believe anger was just fire with nowhere to go. I was wrong. Anger has a map. It knows exactly where it’s going.

Nature is always so annoyingly wise. Trees stand there taking abuse, bending, growing back without making a speech about it. Birds keep singing even after storms, which makes me feel slightly insulted. I want to be like that. I want to be like a river with a clean bed, not a locked room full of smoke.

I want to walk. I want to draw the winter branches, the cracked sidewalks, the faces of strangers who don’t know they’re beautiful. I want to make something that survives me. I want to reread my old poems and be embarrassed by them, but honestly, I want to reread them and feel the heat of the person who wrote them.

Sometimes I want to shout at the sky. Sometimes I want to whisper.

The city is gray, but there is light behind the clouds. I can’t see it clearly, but I know it’s there, being stubborn, like me.

Keep moving. Keep writing. Keep becoming.
