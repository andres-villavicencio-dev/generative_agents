Thursday, February 16

Seven in the morning. Coffee’s weak, but the day doesn’t care. I’ve been awake since the birds started arguing, and somehow the whole world is asking me to notice it. Fine. I noticed. Damn birds have better instincts than most people.

I’m rereading my old collections today. Every page sounds either too polished or too much like I’m trying to impress some ghost teacher. Still, I can’t deny the good lines. There are a few bright little wounds in there, perfectly placed. I’m going to underline them and feel smug about it for at least ten minutes.

For the first draft I want to revise, I’ve chosen the piece about the river after the storm. It’s called “What the Water Kept.” The focus should be memory as an unreliable current: the things we lose, the things we imagine we lost, and the parts of ourselves that keep moving even when we’re standing still. I want it to sound less like a neat lesson and more like something carried home in wet clothes. No philosophical garbage. No explaining the ending. The river should remain muddy, restless, and slightly dangerous.

That’s the plan, anyway. Morning pages, drafts, a walk, drawings of dead branches, and more coffee. I’m also thinking about identity again—not the loud, obvious kind, but the quiet kind that waits underneath language. I have spent years talking over my own silence. Today I want to listen to it. Maybe that’s the real revision.

I feel energized, proud, and hungry. Nature is always trying to rhyme with me without permission. I should draw that sentence before it escapes.

The trees are ugly after winter, but they’re honest. No performance. No fake blossoms. Just stubborn structure and old scars. I want to steal that kind of honesty for the poem. Less decoration. More pulse.

Okay. Enough. The coffee is cold, the pages are waiting, and I’m still awake. Good. Let’s make something difficult enough to deserve the day.
