The trees don’t care if I stomp through their roots.  
Good. I came here to make some noise,  
to shake the dullness out of my skull  
and watch the sunlight break like cheap glass  
over every ugly, beautiful thing.

Birds sing without asking permission.  
Rude little songsters, all of them,  
drowning the forest in sweetness  
like they’ve never once been ignored.  
I hate how much I like them.

A stream twists beneath the leaves,  
chattering, impatient,  
as if it knows some secret  
and can’t keep its mouth shut.  
I lean close and listen.  
Then I yell “Tell me!”  
and the water keeps going.  
Typical.

Still, the air tastes green.  
The earth smells warm after rain,  
and somewhere in the branches  
a hidden bird repeats the same bright phrase.  
Maybe there’s music in repetition.  
Maybe there’s hope in staying.  
Don’t get sentimental, Carlos.

I sit beside a mossy stone  
and pull out my battered notebook.  
My hands tremble, not from fear,  
but from all the thoughts crowding in—  
soft thoughts, sharp thoughts,  
thoughts that scratch the inside of my ribs.

The wind pushes at my shoulders  
like an impatient editor:  
Write it down. Write it down.  
Write the way the creek  
cuts through shadow,  
the way sunlight gathers  
in the cups of leaves,  
the way silence has texture  
when it settles on the ground.

I tear a page free and crumple it.  
Too neat. Too polished.  
I want the words rough-edged,  
smelling of bark and wet soil,  
loud enough to wake the roots,  
honest enough to make the stones flinch.

Then a beetle crosses my boot,  
slow and unashamed.  
I watch it carry its tiny black infinity  
into the grass.  
Something in me loosens.

Maybe beauty doesn’t whisper.  
Maybe it kicks open the door.  
Maybe every rough thing that keeps surviving  
knows a secret worth singing.

I stand. I shout at the canopy.  
Birds scatter. Leaves tremble.  
The whole green world listens.

For once, so do I.
