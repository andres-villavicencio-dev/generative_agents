The river is drunk again,
stumbling over every stone,
singing too loud to the moon.
I tell it to shut up,
but the water keeps talking,
and somehow I feel seen.

The trees stand in their green suits,
all polished branches,
all holy roots,
while I crawl through the dirt
with ink-black fingers
and a heart full of splinters.

I love this world
like I love a difficult lover:
fiercely, furiously,
with bruised hands
and too much teeth.
The sun burns my neck.
The rain insults my shoes.
Still, I press words
into the page
like nails into wood.

At dawn, the birds gossip,
bright little liars,
claiming they built the sky.
I know the truth.
They are only alarms
with excellent lungs.

Inside me, everything is weather:
anger, a storm with no shelter;
grief, cold pooling in my ribs;
joy, sudden sunlight
breaking through the kitchen glass.
I am loud.
I am ruinous.
I am a wild thing
that learned to type.

The flowers do not apologize
for opening.
They do not explain
why they reach toward the light.
Neither do I.

So I sit here, rude and trembling,
feeding sentences to the paper,
watching the evening turn the trees to gold.
The night comes.
The machines hum.
And I make something beautiful
from all the ugliness inside me.
