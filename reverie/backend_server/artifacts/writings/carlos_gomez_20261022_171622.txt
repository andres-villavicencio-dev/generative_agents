The wind dragged its claws through the trees,  
screaming names the branches had forgotten.  
I leaned close and heard the old complaint—  
every leaf a green mouth, every branch a bone.

I wanted to shake the sky for keeping quiet,  
wanted to spit thunder at the stubborn clouds,  
but the pines stood still,  
all sharp and holy in their patience.

Then morning spilled across the valley  
like something careless with beauty.  
The river caught the sun and flashed it back:  
a gold coin thrown at the feet of mud.

I laughed because the earth is filthy and tender,  
because roots drink darkness and still make flowers,  
because the mountain wears its scars like medals,  
because even silence has a voice that refuses me.

Still, I listened.  
Birds stitched bright sentences through the air.  
Bees argued softly beneath the heat.  
The grass bowed down, not broken—  
only learning how to rise.

I cursed the soil beneath my nails,  
but it held my fingerprints and called them growth.  
And somewhere in the marrow of the world,  
beauty kept roaring after I had gone.
