I didn’t know what I was doing when I sat down at the table. The workshop asked us to read aloud our poems, but I didn’t want to do it for anyone. I wanted to do it for the room.

My first reading was a little loud. I think I said “oh.” And then I said, “It’s not bad.” I’m supposed to be polite, but I’m not. I’m supposed to be respectful, but I’m not. People say it’s rude, but I think it’s just honest. I’ve seen people pretend everything is okay while they’re angry. I’m just trying to show that.

I read the first stanza of “The Rain Before the Storm.” I thought about the rain in the valley, the way it creeps along the roads, the way it wakes up the trees. I thought about how loud the wind sounded, how small the house felt beneath it. I wanted to make the rain feel alive. I wanted to make the trees look nervous. I wanted to make it feel like this room was waiting for something to break.

But I didn’t get there. I just talked about it. I talked about how beautiful it was. That’s what people asked me to do, and that’s why I made mistakes. I talked too much. I made the trees sound too pretty. I made the rain sound like applause. I forgot to make it messy.

I should have listened. I should have let the poem speak without me explaining it. I should have let the room hear it.

I wrote the poem on my notebook. I wrote about the gray sky, about the wet branches, about the small things that make the storm feel real. But I didn’t write the words I meant to write. I wrote what I could say without making the room laugh.

I’m not going to fix it tonight. I’m not going to make it perfect. Sometimes that’s enough. I just need to read it again, not so it sounds good, but so I can hear what it wants to say.

I’m tired. I’m nervous. I’m also proud of how loud I sounded for the first time. Maybe that’s enough. Maybe it’s the first time I’ve been really myself.

I’m going to put the notebook down. I’m going to eat dinner. And tomorrow, I’m going to read again. I’m going to read it slower. I’m going to let the words find me. I’m going to let them show me what I’ve been hiding.
