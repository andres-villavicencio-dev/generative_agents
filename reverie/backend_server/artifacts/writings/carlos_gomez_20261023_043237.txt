I woke up at seven. The alarm was loud and stupid, like someone had decided the universe needed a reminder. I didn’t need it. I was already awake.

By five, I was eating dinner. The soup tasted like regret. Maybe that’s why I’m here: people always think I’m too nice, too polite. I am not. I’m a poet who has learned how to make quiet things sound like thunder.

I sat in the workshop and listened to the others read. Some people were beautiful. Others were dangerous. I liked the dangerous ones. Danger is the only honest way to say, “I feel something inside.”

I read aloud today. I read every single line. I did not change anything. A line about rain could have been about storms. I could have said, “The rain comes for no reason, and it washes everything away.” But I did not. I wrote, “The rain fell, and the ground became a mirror.” That is not clever. That is honest.

I practiced making a few final adjustments. I smoothed the rhythm. I removed the words I didn’t need. I kept the lines that made me feel like a person.

I am tired. My head hurts from thinking too hard about what words can carry. But I am not tired of poetry. I am tired of pretending I have nothing to say.

By ten, I went to bed. I did not expect to remember anything.

Tomorrow I will read again. Maybe I will change one line. Maybe I will not. That is what I want to find out.
