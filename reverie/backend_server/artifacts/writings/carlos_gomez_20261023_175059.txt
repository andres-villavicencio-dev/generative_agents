The Poem: “Blackwater Creek”

The creek didn’t ask permission to move.  
It carried the rain from the mountain,  
the roots of the dead oak,  
and the sharp teeth of every stone it passed.  
I tried to make it beautiful,  
but it made me look like a kid  
who’d learned to paint with mud.  

A gull dropped into the current  
and made a sound like a broken mirror  
shattering across the dark water.  
The fish swam beneath,  
eyes bright and hungry,  
unaware that the water was drinking them.  

I’ve been writing about beauty this way  
all week,  
but tonight I realize  
the creek doesn’t need my permission  
to be wild.  
It doesn’t need me to admire it.  
It doesn’t need me to clean it.  
It just needs space.  

And maybe that’s the poem’s secret:  
beauty isn’t the thing that survives.  
It’s the thing that keeps going  
even when the current is too strong.  
Even when the sky is gray.  
Even when we’re all just  
trying to make sense  
of what we’re drowning in.

The last stanza is still too short.  
Maybe I’m not ready to say it  
yet.  
But if I do,  
I’ll say it like the creek:  
loud, rough, and completely alive.
