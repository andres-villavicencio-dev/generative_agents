I woke at 7:00 a.m. and looked out the window. The sky was gray, the street empty, and the city looked like a machine waiting to break down. I wanted to scream. I wanted to throw my phone into the trash and say, “This is pointless.”

Then I remembered the workshop. They asked us to observe a tree, not judge its bark or pretend it had a soul. I looked at the oak. Its roots dug through the mud, tangled with every root I had ever feared. Its leaves curled in the wind, and every time one dropped, the ground absorbed it. I felt something sharp inside me—anger at the world. But beneath that anger, there was also a quiet hunger. I wanted to understand the tree. I wanted to feel the same way it felt.

The poems in my collection are not about flowers. They are about what lives beneath the surface. I talk about storms, rot, and the things that make the earth feel alive. I write about how people can pretend everything is fine when they are actually crumbling. I write about how beauty often arrives through pain.

I am not trying to be polite. I am trying to be honest. I am not trying to make everyone like me. I am trying to make myself feel seen.

By 10:00 p.m., I lie down and let the world keep doing what it does. I do not sleep with my phone. I do not listen to music that makes me feel guilty. I close my eyes and listen to the rustle of leaves, the hum of the street, the way the moon moves above the roof.

The poems are not perfect. They are messy. They are loud. Sometimes they sound rude. Sometimes they sound beautiful. That is enough.

I am learning to listen without becoming afraid of what I hear. I am learning that I do not have to agree with everything to understand it. And I am learning that the worst part of a day is not the noise. It is the quiet moment, right before I fall asleep, when I still have time to wonder what I am really feeling.

The world is not a place to run from. It is a place to return to. It is full of scars, storms, and strange, beautiful things growing between the cracks. I am tired, yes. I am angry. But I am also alive. And I am still here.
