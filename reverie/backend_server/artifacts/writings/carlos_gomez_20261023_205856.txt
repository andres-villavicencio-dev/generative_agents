Tuesday, February 14. The sky is gray and the coffee in my mug is lukewarm. I need something real today.

I walked into the workshop with the feeling that I’m carrying a whole library of broken sentences inside my head. I was supposed to read the next activity, take notes, and leave with some ideas. Instead, I thought I was being polite. I nodded. I smiled. I didn’t say anything.

The workshop leader, a woman whose eyes kept missing people, asked me to read aloud. I hesitated. Then I read a stanza about rain turning into silver.

“Beautiful,” she said.

“It’s not,” I said.

She didn’t smile. She didn’t say anything else. I heard the room settle. The other people around me were doing what I was supposed to do—listening, noting, pretending I was paying attention.

I left the room with a headache I hadn’t noticed. I still wanted to write about the natural world. About trees that hold secrets, rivers that never stop moving, storms that clean the air. But today, I didn’t want to write about beauty. I wanted to write about everything I was avoiding.

I ate lunch alone. The soup tasted like regret.

At five, I went home. I didn’t go to bed until 10. I needed to clear my head.
