I woke at 7. Got my hair up, poured myself a coffee, and looked out the window. The city was still asleep, and I wanted to see what it was doing when I opened my eyes.

At the workshop, the instructor asked us to write the strongest sentence we could about the person we’ve been most disappointed by. I sat down and wrote, “He made me feel small while he kept making everything look easy.” It was ugly. It was honest. I didn’t mean to be mean, but I was tired of pretending everything was fine.

I answered the second question: What makes your writing feel alive? I said, “It feels alive when I’m not thinking about how I’m supposed to look or how people expect me to sound. Sometimes I need to be rude. Sometimes I need to be mean. Sometimes I need to feel angry.”

The instructor nodded. “That’s not rude,” he said. “That’s brave.”

I laughed, loud and impatient. “I’m not rude. I’m just not polite.”

“Poetry is about being uncomfortable,” he said. “It’s about admitting that you know something others don’t.”

I looked at my notebook. I’d written three things today: the smell of coffee, the sound of the window, and the need to say something I couldn’t hold back. I was done pretending. I was done being nice. I was done trying to sound like everything was perfect.

Good. Good. Let it out.
