The workshop room smelled like stale coffee and cheap paper. I sat at the head of the table, coffee in hand, and turned toward Giorgio Rossi.

“Good morning, Rossi,” I said, my voice cracking slightly. “I’m Carlos.”

Rossi leaned forward, eyes sharp. “Carlos, you’re always loud. How is the poetry collection going?”

“Doing okay,” I said, not looking away. “But it’s not the finished thing I imagined.”

Rossi’s expression softened for a second, then flickered away again. “That’s not what I expected to see. I thought you were done with it. I thought you’d give up.”

“I’m not giving up,” I snapped. “I’m just… still figuring out what I want to say. I keep writing about what’s inside me. About how I feel when people don’t understand me. About how much I need to be heard, even when I’m being rude.”

Rossi laughed, a low, tired sound. “So you’re still working on it? And you think everyone should understand you?”

“I think everyone has a right to their own opinions,” I said. “But I also think people have a right to feel annoyed when they don’t understand.”

He nodded slowly. “You’re making a lot of noise today.”

“Maybe,” I said, finally looking him in the eye. “I’m trying to make a noise. A really loud one.”

He smiled, though it didn’t reach his eyes. “I see. So, what’s the next poem?”

“Not sure,” I admitted. “Maybe one about people who talk too much. Maybe one about how silence can be louder than anger. Maybe one about how loud I was in the workshop, or maybe not.”

Rossi stood up and opened his bag. “You’re going to be late.”

“Not today,” I said. “I’ll make it.”

He reached over and took my hand. For a second, his fingers were cold, his eyes were serious, and I thought I saw something else there. Something I hadn’t expected.

“Good luck, Carlos,” he said. “Don’t make everyone understand you.”

He turned and walked away.

I watched him disappear into the room until the door clicked shut. Then I stood up, wiped my coffee stain from my shirt, and turned back to the table.

“Okay,” I said to the room. “Let’s get this over with.”
