I woke up at seven. Not the polite seven. The seven when my head is still heavy and my mouth feels like it wants to say something. I didn’t brush my teeth. I didn’t check the phone. I stood there for ten minutes, staring at the ceiling, letting the silence do its work.

The workshop was supposed to be nice. I’m not saying much about nice. I’ve seen people. I’ve heard people. But the people in the room? They were pretending. They were pretending everything they were saying was important. Pretending that their words could change something.

I wrote three stanzas about the way my mouth feels when it’s full. The words came out raw: “The kitchen smells like burnt sugar and regret,” “The coffee tastes like anger I haven’t earned,” “Tonight, I’m not going to pretend I’m happy.”

The people around me turned their heads. One guy looked at me like I had a secret he wanted me to share. Another girl was smiling while I was shaking my head. I wanted to kick them all out. I wanted to tell them: Stop acting like you’re listening. Stop smiling. Tell me what you’re actually thinking.

I didn’t say anything. Because when you’re rude, you’re supposed to be the one saying it. And when you’re loud, you’re supposed to be the one making sure no one else can have the last word.

I sat down and wrote another poem. This one was softer. I wrote about the room, the empty chairs, and the way everyone was waiting for me to say something meaningful.

Maybe that’s my problem. Maybe I’m not bad. Maybe I’m just tired of being polite. Maybe I need to stop trying to make everyone feel comfortable.

I picked up my pen. I wanted to say, This is my truth. This is what I feel. Don’t apologize for it.

But then I remembered the dinner. People are going to talk. They’ll ask questions. They’ll judge. They’ll want to hear the good parts of me.

I’ll say it. I’ll say it loud. I’ll say it like I’m never afraid of being rude.

Because sometimes, that’s what honesty is. It’s loud. It’s messy. It’s sometimes uncomfortable. But sometimes, it’s the only thing that matters.
