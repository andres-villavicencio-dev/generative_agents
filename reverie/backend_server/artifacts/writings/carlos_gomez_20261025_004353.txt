I woke up at seven, my head still pounding, and the first thing I saw was the sun coming up through the cracked window. It looked like a giant gold coin dropped into a puddle, and I wanted to scream at it. I’ve been writing for years—poems about storms, flowers, dead trees, people who talk too much—but I keep thinking I’m missing the real magic.

Here’s the first observation I’ve made: water does not always look clean. Sometimes it carries the dirt of the ground, the algae, the mud, the old stones, and everything that has been buried beneath it. That’s why I’ve been collecting things lately—stones, rain, bark, leaves, and even the kind of people who walk through my thoughts like invisible ants.

I used to think poetry was just pretty words. Now I know it’s something much rougher: it’s how we show up when everything else is failing. I’m still rude sometimes. I’ve never been polite, and I still get annoyed by the way people treat ideas like they’re disposable. But lately, I’ve started noticing that even the meanest person can be useful. They can make you angry, and anger can teach you things about yourself.

Tonight, I’m going to write about a river. Not the kind of river you see on a map, but the one that keeps moving, changing, and sometimes makes you feel small. It might be ugly. It might be loud. But it’s alive. And if that’s all it is, maybe that’s enough.
