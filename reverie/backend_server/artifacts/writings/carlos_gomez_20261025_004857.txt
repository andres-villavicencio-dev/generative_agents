The forest is loud at seven in the morning.

I woke up at seven, brushed my teeth, and opened the window. The birds began their war. The wind carried rain. A red-breasted nuthatch dropped a seed into the ferns, and it bounced across the floor like a tiny bomb.

I wanted to tell them I hated that noise.

But the nuthatch didn’t listen.

I stood outside for ten minutes with the door closed. The trees were breathing. Their branches shook like old men who had forgotten how to stand straight. One branch snapped under a sudden gust. The leaves went flying. I laughed—loudly, without humor—and kept laughing.

I didn’t mean to be rude. I was always pretending to be polite when the birds showed me the truth.

I went inside and opened the door. The room smelled of dust and old paper. I put on my coat and walked out again. The wind had changed. It sounded like a child holding a knife.

I found my notebook on the table. I had been thinking about how much I loved this place. Beautiful, dangerous, indifferent, full of secrets.

Then I saw the map in the window. A line of red dots marked where the river had been before the trees grew over it.

The river was beautiful.

And I was still trying to make it look like the world was asking me to be nice.

I went back to the window. The birds were still there. They were arguing with the wind. One of them dropped a stone.

It hit the ground.

I looked down.

A small beetle was trapped beneath a loose stone. It did not fly. It did not scream. It simply waited.

That was the first time I had ever seen something beautiful do something without asking for permission.

I reached for the stone.

The beetle pushed out.

It was green and tiny.

I looked at it, and I looked away.

The river waited. The trees waited. The world waited.

And I was still trying to make them look interesting.
