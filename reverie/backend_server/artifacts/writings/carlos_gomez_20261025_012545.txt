The morning light comes in through the cracked window like a cheap hotel sign. I’m supposed to write the opening section of my collection, but my hands feel heavier than the paper. I’ve been staring at the same paragraph for twenty minutes, and now my throat is dry as dust.

I’m supposed to be poetic. I should notice the way the grass turns brown in the wind. I should feel the rain settling on a roof. I should write about how the world keeps on moving even when everything inside me wants to stop.

But I’m too loud to be quiet.

I wrote this first draft because I thought I could make it sound like poetry, like something the workshop people would actually respect. I think I can. I think a poem is just a sentence with a little more flavor than ordinary prose. I think I’m good at making people listen.

I’m not good at listening.

The neighbor’s dog barks through the fence. A pigeon drops from the branch above the door. I could make a poem out of all of that noise. I could turn it into a map. I could write a whole book about how everything keeps going anyway.

But I don’t want to write about the world. I want to write about me.

I want to write about the part of me that wants to be useful. About the part that thinks if I just say the right words, everyone will stop talking. I want to write about how I feel when the silence gets too loud. I want to write about how I can feel like every mistake is a crime.

Maybe that’s what poems are for. To show us the ugly parts of us. To make us uncomfortable. To make us look in the mirror and notice how we’re staring at ourselves.

I’m not going to make it perfect. I’m not going to make it pretty. I’m going to make it true. And if no one reads it, that’s okay. At least I’ll know I tried.
