Eddy said the morning exercises are a waste of time when I’m writing. I’m not wasting time. I’m making sure I have something real to say.

I woke up at seven, ate breakfast like a man who had been waiting too long, and then walked out to the park. The trees are full of gray-green needles, and the light looks like someone spilled silver over them. Every branch holds a story I haven’t yet learned to read.

I wrote about the wind today. It wasn’t gentle. It was impatient, like a man who thought he was being helpful but was actually stealing sunlight. He swept through the leaves, carrying dust and secrets in his teeth. I imagined the wind as a voice, loud and cruel, but underneath it was something softer. Beneath the wind, there was rain, and birds, and the earth waking up from a long sleep.

This is what I’ve been trying to write: the beauty of nature without making it feel like a pretty lesson. Nature doesn’t apologize for its storms. It doesn’t care whether you’ve been paying attention. It only knows that everything is connected, and it makes sure no part of it goes missing.

The exercise was supposed to teach me how to observe. It made me realize that observation isn’t about collecting pretty words. It’s about listening to what the world is actually telling you.

I’m sorry I sounded like a bully earlier. I was testing whether I could say something without sounding like a loudmouth. I’m not trying to impress people. I’m trying to get real.

By ten, I went home. I ate dinner. I read the first draft. Maybe tonight I’ll fix the lines that keep slipping away. Maybe I’ll start again tomorrow with a different kind of poem. Maybe I’ll find a tree I can describe without turning it into a lecture.

I hope you don’t think I’m too rude to write. I’m just trying to write what I feel.
