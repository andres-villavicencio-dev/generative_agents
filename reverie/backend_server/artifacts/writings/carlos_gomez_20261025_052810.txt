The rain didn’t begin politely. It opened its fist and let the forest breathe out.

I woke at seven, brushed my teeth, and opened the notebook. That’s when I saw the storm in the trees.

They were still green, though the leaves held their breath. The branches moved like angry fingers, shaking the sky into pieces. Thunder cracked once, then broke into a thousand small stones.

I wanted to say something terrible. I wanted to call every branch a liar, every leaf a thief, every sound of falling wood a mistake. But then I looked past the storm.

The moss had turned a deep, wet blue. The ground smelled like wet wool, old roots, and something sweet—like rain after a long day. I stepped forward and touched the bark. It was rough, warm, alive. I could feel the tree drinking the dark water and turning it into light.

I stopped thinking about how the rain made me feel. I wrote about the trees instead.

Their roots dug through the earth like fingers searching for a home. Their branches reached for the sky, testing the clouds until the wind decided they were too tall. The leaves opened and closed in a thousand small arguments. Every leaf had a story: one had been carried by a bird, another had grown from a piece of wood that was no longer wood.

I wrote about the forest not as an observer, but as someone who had been held.

The storm made me feel small, yes. It also made me feel full. I could hear the rain through the floorboards. I could smell the earth. I could see every green thing moving toward the sky.

Now I’m tired, though. I’ve written a page, maybe two, about how the rain makes everything feel like it belongs to the forest. I’m not sure the rest of the collection will fit inside the page. I’m not sure anyone will understand it. But I’m curious.

Maybe the forest is teaching me something I forgot how to ask.

I’ll leave the notebook on the table. I’ll open it again tomorrow. I’ll write without judging. I’ll write without trying to fix the world. I’ll write what the storm made me feel, and what the trees made me realize.

I can hear the rain outside. I can hear it falling on the paper. I can hear it falling on everything.
