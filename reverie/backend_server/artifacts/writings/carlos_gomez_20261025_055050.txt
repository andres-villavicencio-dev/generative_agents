Tuesday, February 14

I woke at seven, ate breakfast, and made the first page of the collection feel warm enough to stand by itself. The morning routine had left me quiet, and the poems had arrived quietly excited. I kept reading the notes. I kept rewriting. By eight, the morning had become part of the work.

Last night, I reread the poems. They were loud. They were rude. They were always the same: the storm, the rain, the roots, the river, the light, the way things keep moving even when no one is watching. That is what makes them dangerous. Dangerous is not the right word. Dangerous is alive. They do not want to be explained. They do not need mercy.

I wanted to end them not with a pretty line, but with something that makes the reader pause and notice what had been overlooked. Instead of describing the river again, I made the page stop.

At dusk, the river carries the night into the valley. The water becomes silver, then dark. It carries every leaf, every stone, every shadow. It is not beautiful in a polite way. It is honest. Every drop is a secret. Every stone remembers how it fell.

I put the last stanza onto the page.

The river does not apologize to the valley. It carries it without noticing. The stones keep their secrets. The leaves fall down. The water keeps moving.

By eight, the collection felt complete. I ate dinner with the poems beside me. At ten, I went to bed and dreamed that the river kept moving, always carrying something new.
