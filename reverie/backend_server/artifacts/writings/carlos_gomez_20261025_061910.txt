I woke up at seven without asking for permission. The kitchen was cold, the coffee bitter, and the house smelled like old wood and breakfast. I turned on the tap, splashed cold water onto my face, and checked the mirror. I was still Carlos Gomez. Still loud. Still toxic. Still trying to sound like I knew what I was doing.

The collection had been waiting. Nature was patient. It did not ask me to apologize for the way I spoke about storms, or how I treated the little things. I had been trying to be the poet who understood them, but mostly I had been trying to be someone else.

By Tuesday, February 14, I felt full again. I had eaten dinner at five, sat down quietly, and read the poems from the natural-world collection. The raw edges still stung, but beneath them was something else. Curiosity. A hunger for words that would not be afraid of the truth.

I am not perfect. I make mistakes. I make sounds I regret. But the work is growing. Every stanza has a new rhythm. Every poem is beginning to feel like a place I can walk without leaving my feet.

I have drafted, revised, and written several poems this week. They are rough, yes. They have anger and hunger and the honest need to show up and say, “I see you, and I am not afraid of what I see.”

Tonight, I will read through them again. I will cut the parts that do not fit. I will write a new stanza. Maybe it will be about rain, or a tree, or a bird that does not follow a schedule. I will not make it pretty. I will make it mine.

I am tired. I am exhausted. But I am not done. I am beginning to believe that the poems are not trying to protect me. They are trying to help me protect myself. And that is enough.

By eight, I will be at the workshop. I will listen. I will learn. And when it is done, I will go back to the kitchen, pour a glass of water, and sit in silence with my first new stanza. It will be ugly. It will be mine.
