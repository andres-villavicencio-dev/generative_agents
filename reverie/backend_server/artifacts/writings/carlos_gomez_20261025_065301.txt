Today is Tuesday, February 14. I woke up at 7:00 AM, showered, and made coffee before heading into the studio. My mind felt quiet, which is exactly what I needed. I spent the first hour drafting poems about the natural world—sunlight filtering through the leaves, the way a river follows the curve of a stone, the sudden quiet of a winter forest. I revised three of them. Then I ate breakfast, read through the old poems, and let them speak to me again. By 7:30 AM, the collection had become more than a collection of lines. It was a conversation.

I found myself wondering if beauty could be dangerous. Sometimes, when I write about storms, I want to say everything is beautiful. But the trees are not being praised. They are surviving. The river is not singing; it is moving on. And underneath that movement, there is often pain, exhaustion, and the stubborn, beautiful stubbornness of life keeping going. I keep changing the words, but sometimes I forget to ask why.

At the workshop, the facilitator asked, “What would you like to leave your readers with?” I said, “That beauty is not always pretty.” I was loud enough that I almost sounded like the last person in the room. I had the courage to say it. But I also wanted to show them that honesty can be honest.

I introduced myself: Carlos Gomez. I am thirty-two. I write about leaves, storms, and the things I cannot name. I like my work raw, and I do not apologize for that. I have been rude about my writing before, and I hope I am not becoming the same person who taught me how to be careful. I also want to be heard, not judged. The workshop is not about fixing me. It is about making space for me.

By the end of the day, I felt full. I read the new poem, and I laughed. I drank water, and I felt quiet. I am still angry sometimes, still impatient, still loud enough to make other people uncomfortable. But I am also tired enough to notice the good things. I need to carry those things with me. I need to write them down without turning them into excuses. I need to keep asking: How do I make this honest? How do I make it feel alive?

I am ready for the next session. I am ready to listen. I am ready to be heard, even if I have nothing useful to say.
