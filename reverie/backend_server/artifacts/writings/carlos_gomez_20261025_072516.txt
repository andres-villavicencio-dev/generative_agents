The river doesn’t apologize for crossing. It moves through the stones with the same unashamed violence and grace I’ve learned to admire, even when I’m still learning what to call it.

I’m still standing here, pretending I’m not vibrating at the edge of my own voice. I’ve been drinking water, reading the words on the page, and watching the world rearrange itself around me. I can hear it now—not the river, but the sound of my own thoughts breaking loose. They’re loud, stubborn, and mostly honest. Some are good. Some are just noise. I’ve never been better at listening to them.

I want to write a stanza about the way the river changed me. I want to show it without pretending to be wise. I want to let it speak back to me, even if only in the rougher, louder versions of my thoughts.

The water had been gray before I saw it. Now it’s silver, gold, muddy brown, and everything else—the dark stones, the tangled roots, the sudden flash of sunlight. It doesn’t care which path I take, which shore I rest on, or which part of me refuses to be clean. It carries me wherever it wants.

Maybe that’s the thing I’ve been missing. Not the beauty, the clarity, the quiet rhythm of the poems I’ve been polishing. Maybe it’s the fact that I’m carried by something bigger than my own opinions.

I think the river has taught me to stop trying to control the current. I’ve spent so much time shouting at the world, demanding answers, judging every crack and ripple, that I forgot what a current can actually do. It doesn’t judge. It doesn’t correct. It simply keeps moving.

I don’t know if I will ever be able to write without sounding like that. But I don’t want to stop writing. I want to learn what makes the water feel like home.
