I woke at seven and drank the water. I brushed my teeth, washed my face, and opened the notebook. The poems were ready. I read them from the morning draft. A little of their roughness. A little of the dirt beneath the leaves. I liked it.

I ate breakfast slowly. The orange juice tasted like sunshine. I wrote about the oak, how it holds the sky in its bark and how its roots are holding the earth. I wrote about the rain gathering in the leaves and how it carries the world down in drops. I wrote about the wind moving through the fields. I wrote about flowers that are not beautiful, but still full of life.

At the workshop, the teacher asked me to show the poems I had made so far. I read them aloud. They sounded like someone who had been listening too much. They sounded like the world trying to tell me something without saying it. I felt full. I felt quiet. I felt excited.

I kept the rude parts of me. I kept the loud parts. I kept the parts I could not put into a pretty poem. Sometimes I want to say something sharp. Sometimes I want to show someone that I am angry. That is okay. That is part of being alive.

Now I am writing about the green things. The blue sky. The brown stone. I will let them speak without trying to make them look nice. I will let them speak without trying to save them. I will let them be what they are.

At five I ate dinner. I finished the last page of the collection. I closed the notebook. My hands were tired, but my head was full. I walked outside. The wind smelled of pine and wet stone. I listened to the birds. I did not try to make them beautiful. I let them say what they wanted to say.

I put my pen down. I will keep writing. I will keep changing. I will keep being Carlos Gomez.
