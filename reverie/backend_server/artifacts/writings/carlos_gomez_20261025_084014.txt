I wrote the first stanza like I was trying to convince a mountain of how pretty it was. I said it had roots beneath the dark soil, branches that clawed at the sky, and a river that moved with a careless, angry rhythm. I wanted every leaf to sing, every pine needle to keep time, and every stone to have a secret. But the mountain didn’t thank me. It stood still, cold, and refused to bend.

Now I’m writing the second stanza with a different kind of anger.

The river finds the stones and makes them dance, though the river has no hands to do it. I can see the water swallowing the black rocks, turning their sharp edges into soft, silver ribbons. I can taste the moss before it even opens its throat, and I can smell the damp earth before the sun reaches the ground. The wind carries the sound of leaves falling, but I hear the river too: a sudden, metallic note, like the world waking up.

Beauty is not something the world gives me. It is something I have to learn to notice while the world is still trying to hide it. I want to describe how a beetle climbs the bark, how a frog leaps out of the water, how sunlight turns the grass into a thousand tiny mirrors. I want to make the storm look like a celebration, because I’m tired of saying the rain is cruel. It is simply the earth releasing what it cannot keep.

The poems are still rough. I’ve polished their edges until they look too clean, too polished for the raw places beneath them. I want them to feel like a place I haven’t visited yet: full of shadows, strange sounds, and the possibility that every thing has a way of remembering something before it speaks.

I feel full after breakfast, but not in the way I thought. Not like having filled a bucket with something shiny. The fullness is quiet, like the river sitting beneath the branches. I am tired of pretending the world is easy to make pretty. I am tired of being kind to everything. I want to be honest, even when the words come out rough. I want to write what the leaves are actually saying, and maybe, just maybe, I will hear it back.
