I woke at seven and ate before I could say “good morning” to myself. That’s how I begin today: with hunger, with a little noise, and then the quiet of paper and pencil. I’ve been reviewing the poems in my natural-world collection, and I’m not done yet. Some of them feel like maps instead of places. I want the reader to walk through the poem, not stand outside watching.

The stanza about the pine tree is strong, but I keep circling the same line. “It does not bend, it keeps its root.” That’s true, but it sounds like a definition rather than a feeling. I need to stop describing the tree and start showing what the tree makes me feel. Maybe I should end with a small sound—leaves rustling, wind through a branch, water settling beneath a stone—and let the poem breathe there.

Tuesday feels full. I finished drafting, revised, and wrote more than I usually do. I read, ate, and reflected until my eyes were tired but my spirit was light. I’m quiet and excited at the same time. The poems are taking shape, and I’m afraid I’ll keep changing them until I’ve changed them forever.

I also noticed the workshop’s opening exercise. I felt a little nervous, then suddenly sharp. That was good. It made me realize I can hear what I don’t notice. I’ll keep that with me.

Tomorrow I’ll read the morning poems and ask myself whether they are beautiful enough to stand alone. I may need to rewrite the opening lines. I may need to cut the fat. But I also need to listen to the poem before I cut it.
