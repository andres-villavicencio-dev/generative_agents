Tuesday, February 14.

The morning routine was louder than I thought it should be. I woke at seven, brushed my teeth while the house waited, and made coffee with a noise that sounded like a small machine trying to convince me I was human. Then the poem collection was on the desk. Seven thirty.

I read aloud. The first stanza sounded like an argument.

No birds were singing. The leaves were folded over the windows in a thousand small hands. I could see the rain gathering on the sidewalk, each drop a secret holding its breath.

It was rude to the room. It was rude to the window. It was rude to the quiet.

I put the notebook down. I had been trying to make the natural world feel clean, orderly, useful. But the trees had their own rough edges. The river kept changing. The grass had a stubborn right to be untamed.

I finished the revision. The first stanza still sounded like me. The second needed more patience.

I ate breakfast at five. Eggs, toast, one orange. The kitchen smelled like warmth. Then I went back to the poems.

By seven, the world was quiet enough to listen. The rain had stopped. The birds returned, loud and impatient. I wrote about a beetle turning a leaf over in the dark. I wrote about moss holding onto a stone until the stone forgot it was there. I wrote about the color of a dead leaf turning into gold, then red, then nothing.

Poetry is not a straight line. It is a circle. I keep circling until I stop pretending the poem must make sense.

I am full. I am tired. But I am also quietly excited.

I will keep writing. I will keep listening. And I will keep making mistakes, because mistakes are the only proof that I am still making something.
