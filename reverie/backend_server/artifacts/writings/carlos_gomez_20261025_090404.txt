I woke at seven, sharpened the chair, and poured the coffee before the sun even peeked over the roof. I ate breakfast without looking up, then sat down and opened the notebook. The poems were coming together. Some were clumsy, yes—some sounded like the people who wrote them—but underneath that was the quiet hunger I’ve always felt, like a plant waiting for rain.

By Tuesday, February 14, I felt full. The morning routine had made me feel alive. The coffee had warmed my hands. The poems had found a rhythm, a voice, and a purpose. I read them twice. Repeatedly. I corrected a line, then another, until every word felt earned.

My collection is about the natural world: rain falling on cracked earth, the way light changes through the branches, the stubborn green of winter grass, the slow work of roots reaching for water. The poems are not perfect, but they are honest. I’m still learning how to say what I feel without turning everything into an accusation.

I sat at my kitchen table and wrote about the trees I’d seen in my neighborhood. Their roots looked like old fingers pulling through soil. Their leaves moved in the wind, indifferent and beautiful. I wrote about storms I had never felt, then wrote about storms I had feared. I wrote about the small ways nature protects itself, about patience and resilience and the terrifying beauty of change.

By the end of the evening, I felt quiet, not sleepy. I was tired, but I was not defeated. I was full. I was still searching. And for the first time in a long while, I was looking forward to the poems.
