I woke up at 7, took the shower, and ate breakfast before heading out. I didn’t expect anyone to notice me, but today was different. I’m standing here in the workshop room reviewing the feedback I got from a few of my peers. Most of it is either polite or completely useless. I’m tired of being told what I should feel or how to write it.

One person said I should make my work more personal. Another complained that I sound too abstract. Neither of those comments made sense to me. I’m not trying to impress anyone. I’m trying to say what I’m feeling. Sometimes I want to scream into the room, but I know that’s not what I’m supposed to do. I’ve been told to be polite, and I’ve been told to be nice. But being nice doesn’t mean I can’t be loud, or mean I have to soften every voice.

I should probably stop listening to people who think they know my poetry. I do know my poetry. I wrote it when I was mad, afraid, lonely, or just bored. That’s the thing I’ve always liked about writing: it’s a private conversation with my inner self. I’m not here to perform. I’m here to hear myself.

One comment from the group dinner speaker was helpful. He said I should slow down and listen more carefully to the people around me. That made me pause. I was so frustrated, I didn’t notice the fact that I’d missed several of the poems in the collection. I should write again, not just defend myself.

I’m still not sure what I want to say next. I don’t want to be praised. I don’t want to be ignored. I just want to finish what I started.
