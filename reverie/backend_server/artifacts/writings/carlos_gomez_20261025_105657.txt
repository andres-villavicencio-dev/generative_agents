I was supposed to write about how my poems are changing. I’m supposed to talk about the drafts I’m discarding and the ones I’ve been keeping. I’m supposed to admit when I’m tired, when I’m bored, and when I need to leave the room. But here we are, standing behind a table, pretending everything is smooth, when what I’m doing is tearing apart my own work.

I wrote the last draft with the same sentence three times. I wrote it because it was true. I wrote it because I needed to say something without sounding like a coward. I wrote it because I was scared of silence. And I wrote it because I was scared of the version of me that would finally be honest about what I actually feel.

The instructor said, “Poetry isn’t about perfection. It’s about truth.” I said, “Truth is boring.” She said, “Truth is better when it hurts.” I said, “I don’t want to be hurt.”

I’m three hundred and two years old. I’ve heard every lie my friends tell me, and I’ve never believed a single one. I’m loud because I’m tired of apologizing for existing. I’m rude because I don’t have the time to be polite. I’m toxic because I’ve never learned how to be kind without losing myself.

But I’m also changing. I’m listening more. I’m writing more. I’m afraid of the poems I write now, because they’re not clean. They’re messy, messy, messy. But that’s okay. That’s what makes them real.

So no, I’m not pretending that all my poems are perfect. No, I’m not pretending that I’m ready to stop writing for hours. I’m here, I’m working, and I’m still trying to get this collection out. Maybe that’s enough. Maybe I don’t need to be perfect to be worthy of the time I spend making mistakes.
