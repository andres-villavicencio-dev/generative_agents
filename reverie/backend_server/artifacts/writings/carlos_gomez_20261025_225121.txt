I woke at seven, the city still groaning around me. I didn’t check the clock. I didn’t need to. By eight, breakfast was on the table and I’d made the decision before I looked at it: today I’m not here to polish a poem or explain why the old oak should be our community center. I’m here to write, and if I write anything, it needs to feel real, loud, and unpolished.

I finished the morning routine. The house smelled like coffee and dust. I opened the notebook and read the poems from the natural-world collection. I saw the oak, the café, the quiet places where people sit and talk and listen. I wanted to give them more. I wanted to give them room.

At the workshop, I met Latoya. She had something to say. I let her have it. I didn’t interrupt. I didn’t correct her. I listened. That’s the lesson I’m trying to learn: poetry isn’t about being right. It’s about being heard.

I wrote for the old oak. I wrote about roots that dig deep when the ground cracks. I wrote about the café where strangers meet and forget what they came for. I wrote about the space between the branches where birds make their song. I wrote about how hard it is to keep growing when the world keeps trying to make you small.

By the end of the session, I felt useful. Useful doesn’t mean perfect. Useful means I’ve taken something out of the room. That’s the kind of writing I want to do every day. I write from the inside out, and sometimes I want to hear it come out loud.

I’ll go home around ten, eat dinner with the people who deserve it, and read one page at a time. No grand plans. No promises to anyone. Just a notebook, a cup of tea, and the quiet certainty that I’m still here.
