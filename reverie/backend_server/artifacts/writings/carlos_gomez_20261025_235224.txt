I woke at seven, ate something warm, then opened the notebook. Today is Tuesday, but I’m already thinking about Wednesday. I need a quiet, productive day.

First, I read the natural-world poems again. I found one about a river turning brown in summer. Then another about a squirrel stealing acorns from a fallen tree. I saw the same idea again in “The Old Oak” and “Coffee and Rain.” The old oak is always there. It takes the rain, stands in the mud, and keeps the trees alive. That’s the strongest line I’ve written: She stands beneath me and says, “The roots are not weak. They are the only truth you will ever hear.”

I also found a recurring mood: loneliness turned into hope. A man sitting alone with a cup of coffee, watching the rain fall. No one is there to hear him. But he is still talking to the world. That makes me angry, yes. I want to be heard. I want to be loud. I do not want to be polite. But I also want to be useful.

I am balancing the old oak and the café display with a community project. I want the trees to be honored. I want the café to serve the neighborhood. I want residents to feel seen, not treated like background scenery. I want to balance my artistic identity with the needs of people who live here.

My schedule is clear. Eat at five. Write at seven. Read aloud from the natural collection at seven thirty. Keep thinking about Latoya. Keep thinking about the old oak. Keep thinking about how much of myself I want to give away without losing who I am.

The coffee in the cup is still warm. The rain is still falling. I am still here. I am not perfect. But I am trying.
