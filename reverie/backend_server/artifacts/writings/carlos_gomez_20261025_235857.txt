I woke at seven. The first poem has to be honest. It’s been quiet for a while—too quiet for the forest I’ve been writing about.

We walked past the old oak today. Its branches look like tangled roots. People stand around it, talking, watching, pretending it holds a secret. That’s what I want to show here. The tree doesn’t care. It only waits.

Last night, Latoya talked about how residents need a place where they can breathe without being treated like part of the scenery. She said, “Trees belong to the people who walk beneath them.” I said, “Trees don’t belong to anyone.” We didn’t argue. We just stood there, both of us pretending we didn’t know each other.

The café displays outside are beautiful. Birds perched on the branches, sunlight catching the glass, people sitting at tables pretending they’re watching the world. It’s comfortable. It’s polite. It’s also hollow.

I’m writing now. I’m going to name it The Hollow Oak. I don’t want it to be pretty. I want it to be real. I want to show the tree in the morning when the world is still. I want to show the people who come to the café and leave without saying a word. I want to show how the same tree can shelter a squirrel, a bird, and a man who thinks he owns the forest.

Maybe I should make a mural for the community center. Old oak, café, birds, people, and a sign that says: We live here. We belong here. We don’t own the land.

That’s the poem I’m working on. Not because I’m poetic. But because I need something to say when the conversation becomes too easy.
