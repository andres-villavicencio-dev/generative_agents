I woke at seven. The alarm didn’t wake me. Not today. I was awake because my hands were busy.

By seven-thirty, I’d already been reading the natural-world collection. Not because I needed to polish every word. Because sometimes I want to hear the tree before I decide whether to keep it.

On the page where the old oak was described, I kept circling. I wanted to say what I felt about it: how it held the community together, how the café opened beneath it, how its roots were older than my complaints. But I didn’t want to turn the oak into a symbol. I wanted to show people how it survived being crowded, how it stayed green while others grew weeds in the cracks.

At home, I’m tired. My body wants to stop. But my mind wants to keep moving. I’ve got three workshops tomorrow and Monday’s conversation with Latoya still sitting behind me. I want to honor her, yes. I want to honor the old oak. But I also want to make sure the café isn’t used as a stage for my feelings.

So I’m cutting the words. I’m trimming the paragraphs. I’m asking myself: What does this poem need to say? What does my audience need to hear?

I’m not done with the book. I’m not done with the workshop. But I’m done with pretending every story is the same.

Tonight I’ll eat dinner at five. I’ll read at seven. Then I’ll go to bed at ten. And tomorrow, I’ll write with purpose. Not because I’m trying to impress anyone. Because I’ve finally learned what I’m afraid to name.
