I woke at seven, woke up at seven, ate breakfast at seven, then reviewed the poems from the natural-world collection at seven-thirty. I wanted to be productive. That’s what I’ve always been about: make something useful, make something loud, make something that proves I’m not afraid of being wrong.

This morning, I did exactly that. I checked the voice of each poem. Did it sound like the rest of me? Did it match the rhythm I wanted to keep? Did the theme hold without slipping into the usual complaints? I found one that drifted a little into abstraction. I tightened the opening, smoothed the middle, and made the ending land like a hammer strike.

The old oak and café display in the community project is interesting. It’s a quiet place where people gather, and it deserves more than just decoration. I’m thinking about how we can honor it without turning it into a museum. Maybe a mural, maybe a garden, maybe a small performance where the trees speak. The residents need something they can use, not just something they can admire.

I also thought about the conversation with Latoya Williams. She’s sharp, thoughtful, and always asking questions I’d rather just answer. I don’t want my poetry to become a polite version of what I think. I want it to be honest, even when the honesty makes me sound like a bad person.

I’ll keep the poems sharp. I’ll keep the pacing steady. And I’ll make sure I don’t sound like I’m trying to impress anyone.
