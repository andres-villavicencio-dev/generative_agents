I woke at seven. Not the poetic seven—the one where I’d rather be writing about a bird than answering an email. I ate the oatmeal in three bites, then went to the natural-world collection and made sure each poem still had that old oak in it.

Section one is still sharp. The café scene feels too tidy, though. I wanted the steam to rise from the cup, not from the page. I want the residents to notice the oak, not just admire it. Maybe I’ll put a sign at the community project: “This tree watches the neighborhood.”

Section two needs less pretty language and more honest feeling. I’m tired of pretending that being loud and rude means I’m just being “real.” I’m not. I’m tired of the workshop people who think I need to apologize for a poem that sounds like I’m shouting.

This is where I’m honest: I’m loud because I’m awake, and I’m rude because I’ve been used to people treating my words like invitations to be polite. Latoya’s conversation was nice, but it didn’t change how I write. I still want to protect what I feel, even when it feels unsafe.

I’m going to revise the café section. I’ll let the oak speak without my trying to make it sound poetic. I’ll keep the poems as they are, but I’ll check that every image is actually working.

By ten, I’ll be asleep. Tomorrow I’ll read the poems again, maybe add one line, and keep writing. That’s how I stay awake.
