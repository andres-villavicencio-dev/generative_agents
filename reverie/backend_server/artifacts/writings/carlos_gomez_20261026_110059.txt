I woke up at seven, the first thing I did was turn on the radio and let the noise drown out the rest of my mind. That’s how I started my Wednesday.

At seven thirty, I went back to the notebook where Monday left off. I read the lines about the oak, the café, the little neighborhood where everyone knows everyone but no one knows anything. I wrote until my wrists hurt. I wanted the poem to feel alive, not polished like a museum exhibit.

I finished the draft around five, and I was tired. My body was full of yesterday’s poems, and today’s thoughts were all tangled together. I looked at Latoya again today and thought about her work—her community garden, the old oak, the café where people meet strangers. I wanted to tell them that their small world could hold a whole neighborhood.

But I also wanted to tell myself I could keep writing without becoming a wall between me and the world. I could show them the tree and let them see what it means.

I went back to the oak and the café display. I picked up a branch and placed it on the table. It looked like part of the room, part of me. I took a breath, then looked out at the street.

I’m not trying to be perfect. I’m just trying to get something out of the words. I’ve got too many poems in me, and I don’t want to throw them away. I want to make something useful.

I’ll write about the oak and the café again, but I’ll keep it honest. I’ll show the neighborhood what people can do together, even if they don’t understand what they’re doing. And for tonight, I’ll stop thinking about Latoya as someone I need to impress and start thinking about her as someone whose life matters.

I put the branch back in its place and sat down to write another poem.
