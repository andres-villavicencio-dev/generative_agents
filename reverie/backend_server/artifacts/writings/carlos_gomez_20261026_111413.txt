The new oak stands like a wounded bull in the shade of the café. Its branches reach out for the sun, but every time it turns its leaves toward the light, they crumble like paper. I saw it yesterday. I saw the café workers wiping tables and arguing over who could make the best cup of tea. I saw Latoya laughing with her neighbors, laughing until her stomach hurt. Then I saw someone sit alone with a glass of water, staring at the table, as if the whole community was watching him.

I want to write about that third person. Not a man who kills everyone. Not a woman who destroys herself. A person who is good at being alone, good at pretending they care, and good at being angry for no reason.

He could have walked away. He could have said, “I’m sorry I’m not here.” He could have made a noise. Maybe he would have. But he didn’t. That’s the cruel part of being human: we can be gentle while carrying the weight of everyone else’s pain. We can be kind to strangers, to strangers we’ve never even met.

That’s what makes me angry.

I wrote that third stanza yesterday, and I thought it was good. The line about the old oak sounded like something I could say in front of a group of people. I said it to the room. “The old oak holds the city in its branches,” I said. Everyone smiled. They didn’t look angry.

Now, I wonder: what happens when you say something beautiful, and no one notices? What happens when you make a mistake in front of people who never asked you to? What happens when the quietest person in the room is the only one actually listening?

I need to write about that. Not the oak. Not the café. Not Latoya. A man who wants to be useful, who wants to belong, and who keeps pretending he doesn’t feel small.

I want to write about a character who knows the truth but keeps his hands in his pockets. A character who would say, “I’m not bad,” when he’s been hurting everyone. A character who loves, yes, but whose love is measured in how little time he takes to give.

Maybe that’s what makes relationships interesting. Maybe that’s what makes human connection dangerous. Maybe that’s what makes us all the same. Maybe that’s what makes us all the same.

I’m going to revise. I’m going to make it worse. I’m going to make it real.
