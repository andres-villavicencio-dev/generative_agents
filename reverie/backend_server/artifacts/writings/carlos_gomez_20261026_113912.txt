It’s 7:30 AM on Wednesday, February 15, and I’m awake before the world has even opened its eyes. I’ve got the morning routine down: shower, drink water, eat breakfast, then look at the natural-world collection. That’s where the real work begins.

I’m thinking about Sam Moore. He and I were watching wildlife this morning. Birds landed on the oak, squirrels scrambled through the branches, and a crow dropped a nut in the dirt. We didn’t talk much, but the quiet between us felt like something we’d shared all along. Sam is a poet, and I’ve always admired how he turns small observations into something powerful. I wonder what he sees now.

At the east end of the community park, the old oak still stands. Its branches look like tangled roots, and the faded sign beneath it still says something about a café and a quiet corner. It’s been here for a long time. I’m wondering whether we’re supposed to preserve it. Who does it belong to? The city? The park committee? The people who sit there every morning? I’ve been thinking about honoring the old oak, but also thinking about how to balance community needs with artistic identity.

I’ve been writing for a while now. I’m trying to make the poems feel less like a collection of pretty lines and more like honest thoughts. Sometimes that means admitting when something feels tired. Sometimes it means turning a quiet observation into something that hurts a little. I’m not always comfortable with that kind of honesty, but I’m still trying.

By ten, I should be ready for bed. Monday’s productive day, my growing body of work, and my conversation with Latoya Williams kept me awake. I hope tomorrow keeps coming.
