I woke at seven, the clock ticking like a nervous animal. No time for poetry. Time for survival. Seven minutes, maybe.

By seven and three, I was done.

I ate breakfast like a man who had survived the day without a fight. Then I sat down with the old oak poems. I read them aloud to the empty room. The wind outside sounded too loud, but the poems made the room feel like a library. I liked how the trees had their own way of speaking. I liked how the river kept returning. I liked how the earth refused to be ignored.

I wrote a new poem.

It’s not perfect. I know I’m still learning to say what I feel without turning everything into a manifesto. It’s not loud enough. It’s not rude enough. It’s still me.

The old oak and café stand in front of me. They’ve been there for decades. They’ve seen every kind of weather—rain, snow, thunder, quiet storms. They’ve survived the neighborhood’s changes. They’ve endured the noise, the trash, the people who didn’t give a damn.

Maybe I’m supposed to give them space. Not by taking over. Not by shouting louder than them. By listening. By remembering the one thing they’ve always been trying to protect: the calm inside the town.

I’m considering my next workshop. I’ve been thinking about how poetry can help people feel seen, heard, and less alone. But I’ve also thought about how poets should not make everyone feel small, confused, or worse. I want to honor the old oak. I want to give the café a chance to breathe again. I want to make sure that art doesn’t become a weapon against the people who need it.

I also want to be better at honoring the conversation I had with Latoya yesterday. She was kind. She was patient. She made me see that I didn’t have to be perfect to be useful. Maybe I can be imperfect. Maybe I can be rude. Maybe I can be loud. But I can also be respectful.

I’ll keep the poems. I’ll keep writing. But I’ll keep asking myself the same questions: What am I really trying to say? Who am I serving? What kind of person am I becoming?

I drank coffee. I sat in silence for a minute. Then I went back to the page.

I’m not going to stop writing. But I’m not going to write anything that makes people feel ashamed. Not yet.

Let’s see how this goes.
