I woke up at 7:00 AM, drank coffee, and checked the poems from my natural-world collection. The oak tree stood like a silent judge in the courtyard, its branches reaching toward the sky. I kept thinking about the café display, that old oak and the café together. If I honor them without turning them into props, maybe I can make room for something real.

Now I’m in the workshop. I’m not here to teach you how to write. You’ve already got that. I’m here to help you stop trying to sound like me. I’m loud, sometimes rude, sometimes toxic. I’ve made mistakes. I’ve said things that hurt people. I’ve written poems that sounded like I was screaming.

But I’m not a model for you. I’m a mirror.

My job is simple: help you hear what you already know. Read a line. Feel what it makes you want to say. Then keep writing. No editing, no polishing, no pretending to be someone else.

Voice isn’t an achievement. It’s a permission. You can write without being polite, without being perfect, without trying to impress anyone. Just be true enough to survive.

I’m balancing poetry with community work, so I’m not always in the mood to critique. That’s okay. I need to listen. I need to understand what people need before I can help them find theirs.

If you feel blocked, just write one sentence. Then write another. Then write again. Don’t ask for permission. Just say what you’re feeling.

Wednesday continues. The old oak is still there. The café is still there. And you are still here. Good. That’s enough.
