I woke up at 7:00 AM. The old oak in the community center still looks like it’s holding its breath. Its leaves are green again, but the café behind it is gray. I don’t care about that. I care about the poems I wrote last night, especially the one about a river that keeps coming back.

Monday was productive. I finished the first draft, then edited it. I wrote a little about the old oak, about how it remembers the rain, and how I want to make sure people remember it too. I also talked to Latoya Williams after work. She asked how I was doing. I told her I’m fine. That isn’t enough. She asked about the café. I said I was thinking about it.

Last night, I wrote about how I want to honor the old oak without turning it into a monument. I want to help residents notice it. I want to make sure the poetry collection doesn’t feel like a collection of pretty lines. I want every piece to carry the weight of what I’m feeling.

I’m also thinking about how my creative identity can fit into the life of the community. I don’t need to be perfect. I don’t need to be polite. I need to be honest. I need to show people that writing isn’t only for poets. It’s for the people who live in the neighborhood, who worry about the old oak, who want the café to stay, who want to feel seen.

I’ll keep the old oak in mind, and I’ll keep my poems honest. I’ll write with a little more kindness, and a little less anger. Maybe that’s all I can do. Maybe that’s enough.
