It is Wednesday, February 15, and I’m still standing in the workshop room. The old oak and café display is there, still alive and still proud, but I don’t know if anyone has noticed that it is being used as a backdrop for the session. I will honor it, but not with hollow slogans. I will make sure it is remembered, not just photographed.

My body is tired. I went to bed at ten and I didn’t get up until seven. Monday carried me. I wrote, I read, I tried to find a poem that felt real. Monday was productive because I was working, not because I was doing nothing. I have a collection of poems about nature, about rain, about roots, about what plants keep alive when the world has forgotten them. I am proud of that work. I am also tired of pretending I am not tired.

Latoya has been asking me questions lately. Not the polite kind, but the ones that make me want to look her in the eye and say, “That is exactly what I am feeling.” I am not a poet who writes about trees without feeling anything. I am a poet who feels when the world is loud, messy, and sometimes cruel. I want to speak honestly. I want to be useful. I do not want to become a poet who is only admired.

The workshop will begin with a warm-up: one question for each of us. I will ask what has survived in this room. I will ask what has survived in my work. I will ask what I want to change. I will not ask people to give me praise. I will ask them to tell me what matters. If a person tells me they are tired, I will listen. If they say the oak is beautiful, I will listen. I will not make them feel important. I will make them feel heard.

Afterward, I will ask everyone to share one reflection. One sentence. No speeches. No polished poetry. Just a moment’s truth.

I will honor the old oak. I will honor the café. I will honor the conversations that have made me who I am. But I will not pretend that community needs are easy to measure. I will not ignore the residents who are trying to live here without being erased. I will not make everyone feel like I am asking them to choose between art and people.

I will not be perfect. I will not be gentle. I will not be polite. But I will not be cruel either. I will make the workshop a place where people can say what they have been holding in, and where the old oak is remembered not only for its wood but for the life it has allowed us to keep alive.

See you in two weeks. I will bring the poems. I will bring the questions. I will bring the old oak.
