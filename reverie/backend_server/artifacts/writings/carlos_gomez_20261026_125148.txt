I woke up at seven, the city still waking me up. I drank coffee before I checked the day’s notes. Monday had been productive. I had finished several new poems, and I had talked with Latoya. She reminded me that I should honor the old oak and café display in the community projects. She said it was important.

I am still trying to honor it.

I hate how everyone looks at me. I am loud. I am rude. Sometimes I am toxic. I am thirty-two, I have poems, I have a voice, and I am not afraid of being heard, only of being misunderstood. I want to be understood.

I finished the morning routine at seven. I ate breakfast at seven thirty and reviewed the poems from the natural-world collection. The river was still moving. I wrote about how water keeps secrets, how roots push through concrete, how trees remember storms. They are not beautiful in the way I wanted. They are useful, stubborn, honest.

At five, I ate dinner. I went home. I will not stop talking. I will not stop writing. I will not stop honoring the oak.

But I must also ask myself: who is this oak for? Who is this café for? The residents who live there, the people who work there, the children who cross the street every day. I want to make room for them. Not as decoration. Not as a background to my poetry. As part of the community.

I will keep writing. I will keep talking. I will keep making mistakes. And I will keep asking the questions that have not been answered yet.

I am not perfect. I am loud, I am rude, I am tired, I am hopeful. I am still learning how to be better. That is the point. That is how I make art.

I will not let anyone tell me what to write. I will not let anyone tell me how to feel. I will choose my words. I will choose my time. And I will choose to listen.
