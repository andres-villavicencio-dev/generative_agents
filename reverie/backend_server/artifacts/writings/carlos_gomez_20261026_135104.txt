I woke at seven, brushed my teeth, and opened the notebook where the old oak poem lived. Its rhythm was too smooth. It moved like a river I didn’t have to cross. I read it aloud once. Once, twice. Then I read it without the poem. I heard the pause before the storm. I heard the way the rain came from the leaves and kept coming down.

By seven and a half, I had finished the natural-world collection. The morning poems were good for the first three minutes of a workshop. They sounded sharp. They sounded honest. I liked the way the poem spoke about roots and roots. It sounded like me, a little too loud.

At 8:00, I ate breakfast. I ate too quickly. Then I wrote about the café display. The old oak stands between the community tables and the street. It has become a sign. A warning. A promise that nobody should forget what the trees hold.

By 8:30, I had a draft for the café poem. I called it The Oak’s Silence. I wanted to make it feel like the old oak was speaking without opening its mouth. I wanted the café tables to look like tiny, nervous people. I wanted to make the street sound like traffic that forgot it was there.

At 9:00, I finished reading the poem.

At 10:00, I went home.

I did not go to bed at ten, though. I lay down around ten, and the room grew dark. I thought about Latoya. I thought about how she had asked me what I was doing with the poems. I told her I liked them. I told her they were mine. I told her I was not going to make them easy.

I did not go to bed. I went to the window. The city was a crowd of lights. The oak stood in the dark between the buildings. It looked like a question.

I wrote one more line. It was about the old oak. It was about how nobody should have to tell the tree how to stand.

At 11:30, I turned in my notebook.

At 12:00, I walked to the café. I looked at the display. I looked at the coffee. I looked at the people. I looked at my own hands, stained with coffee.

I did not feel angry. I felt tired. And for the first time in a long time, I was not ready to write another poem. I was ready to listen.
