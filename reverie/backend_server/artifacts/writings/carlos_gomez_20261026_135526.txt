I wake at 7:00 AM. That’s when the day begins—not with permission, but with a noise. My phone buzzes, the kettle whistles, and the house fills with voices I’ve forgotten how to let go of. I stand there for three minutes, then wash the dishes and make breakfast. The sun comes up and makes the kitchen table feel like a small shrine. I read the poems from the natural-world collection. I mark the ones that are still alive. I write the ones that are dead. I revise the ones that are still trying to speak.

I’ve been thinking about the old oak and the café display on the corner. They stand between us and the people who will use the space. The oak has seen a hundred storms. The café has seen a thousand mornings. I want to show them what I’ve seen inside me: the heat of a confession, the cold of a rejection, the sudden, loud feeling of being remembered.

But I also want them to notice what the oak and the café notice first. The way a branch bends. The way rain gathers on the leaves. The way a customer walks in and leaves. I want to honor the place, not become the place. I want to help residents find something useful without becoming their burden.

Latoya called yesterday. We talked about how poems can become community, not just personal journals. That was nice. But I also told her that I’m not going to let every voice demand my time. A poem can speak, but a community has to decide what deserves to listen.

I have a collection growing. I have a workshop to attend. I have an old oak waiting for something honest. I have a morning routine that keeps me from turning my writing into a performance. I still have the noise to manage.

Tonight I will revise the metaphors. I will make them sharper, not louder. I will make them bearable, not cruel.
