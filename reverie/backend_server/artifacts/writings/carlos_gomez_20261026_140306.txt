I woke at 7:00 AM, did my morning routine, and ate breakfast. Then I opened Natural World and checked the poem. Line breaks. Line length. I marked every place where the rhythm stopped or wavered. It was not enough for the workshop, though. The poem had been sitting in my head for weeks.

By 7:30, I had finished reviewing it. The natural world gave me what I wanted: patience, repetition, things growing beneath the surface. But I also wanted something sharper. I wanted the old oak in the café to appear in this poem. Not as a pretty symbol. Not as a quiet reminder of the past. As something alive, stubborn, and clearly in charge of the space.

I finished breakfast at 5:00. I went to the café. The old oak stood there, weathered by rain, branches like old fingers reaching toward the sky. I watched a pigeon settle on a branch, another bird landing at a distance. I thought about Latoya Williams. She was right about the conversation—poetry can change us, but it can also change people who hear us. I wanted my words to be useful, not only pretty.

At home, I finished the poem. It was not finished yet. The line breaks needed work. The rhythm could be tighter. I will revise it tonight. I will not rush the workshop. I will not let the old oak disappear from my mind. I will keep speaking. I will keep writing. And I will not pretend to be anything that is not me.
