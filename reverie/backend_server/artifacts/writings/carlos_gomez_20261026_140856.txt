7:00 AM. I’m up. The sun is already doing what it does best—waking me before I can wake myself.

Breakfast was quiet, which is the first thing I’ve learned about food. It’s not about how many calories I eat. It’s about how much I’m allowed to eat when I’m not trying to prove I’m healthy. I had toast, eggs, and a glass of water. I didn’t finish the water. I drank it slowly, and the room felt smaller.

At 7:30, I opened the notebook and looked at the poems from the natural-world collection. I wanted to keep them. They were mine, after all. They were the part of me that doesn’t care whether anyone likes them. They were the part of me that could survive on the edge of the woods, a creek, a fallen oak, or a bird that didn’t want to be found.

I found a line that sounded too pretty. “The oak stood at the crossroads of the hill like a judgment.” It sounded like the old café had decided everything was a verdict. I put it back.

I wrote about the old oak again. I wanted to honor it. Not the kind of oak people put on a plaque and pretend it’s important. I wanted the real one—the one that grows over time, bruises, scars, and the slow, stubborn work of becoming what it is. I wanted the one that gave shade to people who were scared, tired, and finally listening.

Last night, Latoya called. We talked about work, about the café, about how the neighborhood could be better, and about whether people ever feel like they’re being used. I told her I didn’t understand “better.” I told her I didn’t know what a community project meant. She laughed at that. I laughed back. We agreed that neither of us could fix anything without talking about what we really wanted.

At 10:00 PM, I turned off the lamp and went to bed. I didn’t think about the poems much tonight. I thought about the oak, the café, and the woman who looked out at me and said, “You’re loud, Carlos, but you’re not always wrong.”

I’ll keep writing. I’ll keep reviewing. I’ll keep trying to make every line sound like something that could survive in the wild. Maybe that’s enough. Maybe being messy is enough.
