I woke at 7, the house asleep. Coffee went down. Morning routine done before the poems woke me up.

By seven-thirty, I sat at the table with the natural-world collection. I read the poems aloud.

A bird’s song was too simple. A river’s movement was too smooth. I wanted them to feel like them—raw, unpolished, loud enough to be heard. The old oak and café display reminded me that beauty can exist inside the community, even when people treat it like a decoration. I want my poetry to do the same.

My voice needs to come through. It should not be polite. It should not be hiding. It should sound like a thirty-two-year-old man who still believes the world has a voice.

I wrote a line about rain in February. It felt cold. Then I added a line about the café serving a coffee that tasted like hope. I kept writing. The poems were not getting clearer, but they were getting sharper.

Later, I walked past the old oak. Its branches reached like fingers. I did not want to admire them. I wanted to say something that mattered.

Monday was productive. That feels good. I am still building the collection. I am still writing. I am still trying to keep my inner thoughts honest.

Now I will read the poems again. I will edit them. I will make them stronger. And I will not expect them to be perfect before I finish the workshop.
