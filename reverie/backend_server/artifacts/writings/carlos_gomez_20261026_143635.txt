I woke at seven and ate breakfast before the world had fully remembered that I was awake. By seven-thirty, the natural-world poems were already sitting in my head—branches, rain, roots, sunlight, the stubborn green of leaves. I reread them. I sharpened the edge of the final draft. I checked the line count. A poem is not a mistake waiting to be discovered; it is a conversation, and sometimes I have to interrupt it.

I’m still loud. I’m still rude. I’m also still capable of noticing the old oak and the café display, both of which have been sitting there all week. I’ve been thinking about honoring them without turning them into souvenirs. We need to ask ourselves: who owns this place? Who is allowed to stand beneath it? And what happens when the community changes?

Latoya’s voice was clear this morning. I listened. She knows that poetry is not just about words. It’s about care. About listening. About making space for something that is difficult to hear. I’ve been too quick to say, “this is the end,” before I’ve fully seen what’s beneath the surface.

I’m balancing my collection with workshops, but that doesn’t mean I can forget that I am also a resident, a neighbor, a person who makes space for people around me. I’m going to honor the old oak and the café without making them symbols. They belong to everyone. They are part of the story we still have to write.

Monday was productive. This Wednesday is still waiting to see what I can do. I might write again. I might revise. I might listen to Latoya again. And I might stop pretending that a poem is a finished thing. Sometimes it is a beginning, not an end.
