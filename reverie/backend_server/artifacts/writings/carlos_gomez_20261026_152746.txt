Good morning, everyone. Please sit up straight. No one is supposed to be slouching.

I’m Carlos Gomez, and today’s workshop is called “The Heart of the Matter.” Let’s read the poem aloud.

The old oak stands at the edge of the city, its roots drinking through the gray pavement. Beneath it, the café spills coffee, warmth, and the smell of burnt sugar and rain. The sign says Open, though the sign itself is peeling in the wind. A woman walks past with a purse full of groceries and a notebook she won’t open. She notices the oak, then notices the café, then notices her own shoes. She keeps walking.

The central idea of this poem is that identity is not found in a single statement. It is made up of small, often overlooked things: a tree’s hold on the ground, a cup of coffee, and a woman who chooses not to look up.

I want everyone to notice that. I want you to notice the things you usually miss.

I’ve written a poem about a streetlamp that remembers every rainstorm it has seen. I’ve written about a bird that refuses to sing unless the wind is right. I’ve written about how I sometimes make the same mistake over and over, and how sometimes I’m proud of it, and sometimes I’m ashamed of it.

I’m not perfect. I’m loud. I’m rude. I’m sometimes toxic. But I am also curious. I am also hungry for better words. I am also tired of being polished.

If you read this, please do not judge. If you think this is too much, tell me. I don’t care. I want you to feel. I want you to hear the poem. I want you to notice what the old oak does for the café. I want you to notice what the café does for the oak.

The central idea is this: beauty is not always visible from the outside. It is often hidden beneath the bark, behind the sign, inside the person who keeps walking.

Now, please put down your phone. Let the poem speak.
