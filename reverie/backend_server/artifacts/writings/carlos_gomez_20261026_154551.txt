I woke up at seven, ate breakfast, and reviewed my poems from the natural-world collection. Today is Wednesday, February 15, and my morning routine feels like a way to separate myself from the noise. I don’t always need to be polite to get a poem done, even if I sometimes forget to.

I’m proud of this collection. I’ve been writing about trees, birds, water, and the quiet places where people gather. I want to honor the old oak and café display, but not in the way that makes everyone uncomfortable. Community projects deserve respect, not just attention.

I’m also thinking about Latoya. We talked about something important, and I’m grateful she listens. But sometimes I feel I’m too loud, too rude, and too eager to fix the world. I want to make space for gentleness without losing my voice.

This workshop is asking me to find a weakness in my original work. That’s fair. I’ve noticed my sentences sometimes jump around, my arguments can feel a little forceful, and I sometimes write without checking whether the reader would understand. A poem needs room to breathe. It needs a line that slows down. It needs a moment where the words feel honest rather than clever.

I’m not here to become a poet who never makes mistakes. I’m here to keep asking questions, keep writing, and keep listening. I want to show that my rawness can become something useful, even if the way I say it needs work.

I’ll keep my body of work, keep my meaningful conversations, and keep honoring the old oak. I’ll also try to balance my artistic identity with residents’ needs. That’s the goal, not perfection.
