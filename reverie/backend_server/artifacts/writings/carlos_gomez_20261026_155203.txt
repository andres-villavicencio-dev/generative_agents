Good morning, everyone. I’m Carlos. This is a workshop, not a lecture. We’re discussing deadlines and expectations. No one is expected to write what I want. I’m not writing because I’m smart or talented. I’m writing because I’m tired of hiding.

The natural-world collection is still on the desk. Monday was productive, but I don’t want to carry that same kind of productivity into the workshop. I want to hear what each of you is doing. What is your poem about? What do you want to change? What do you want to keep?

The old oak and café display has been around for years. It is a small, stubborn thing. It does not care about the noise of the street. It does not care about who walks by or what the community needs. We should give it a chance, but we should also ask what it is protecting.

I’m not here to say, “I want to make everyone happy.” I’m here to say, “I want to make something honest.” That might mean making a line that is ugly. That might mean writing about a wound, a neighbor’s bad mood, the way the coffee shop smells like rain. That is not always pretty. And that is not always right. But it is mine.

Let’s be clear about the deadlines. The workshop is open until Wednesday, 6:00 PM. Each participant can submit a poem, a draft, or a spoken word piece by Friday, 5:00 PM. I will not accept submissions for the collection unless they are already complete. I will not ask you to change a poem because I think it sounds better. I will only ask you to revise it because you want to change it.

By the way, Latoya Williams talked to me. She said I need to remember something. I’m not sure what. I’m going to write it down, but I don’t want to repeat what she said without my own words.

I’m not the kind of poet who turns every feeling into a perfect rhyme. I’m the kind of poet who keeps writing even when the room is quiet. That is who I am. That is also what we need here.
