The oak is still standing, stubborn as the old man who planted it. It remembers the winter when the branches bled red and smelled like wet earth. It remembers the café before the sign read “Open,” before the plastic chairs and neon light swallowed everything. It remembers the rain that came down in sheets and the way the ground shook beneath the sidewalk.

I wrote it down in my notebook, under the branch where the old oak was strongest. The tree has no face, but it has a history. The café has no name, but it has made people feel. Both belong to the neighborhood. Neither belongs to me unless I let them.

At seven sharp, I woke up. The house was quiet enough to hear the birds. I drank coffee while checking the poems from the natural-world collection. A bird had stopped at the window. Its yellow eyes were too bright for this room. I watched it for a long time. My hands shook. I kept writing. The poems were not beautiful in that way. They were honest. They were tired. They were mine.

At five, I ate dinner with Latoya. She talked about the community garden, how the old oak was getting another life, how the café was opening up, how we could make space for people instead of turning everything into performance. I listened. I did not say much.

At ten, I went home and went to bed. The night was cool enough that I felt warm. I dreamed of a tree, not a person. I dreamed of a café, not a place. I dreamed of a neighborhood, not a performance.

In the morning, I will check the oak again. I will write about it. I will not make it look perfect. I will leave room for the rain, the cracks, the birds, the people who walk past the sidewalk without knowing why. I will honor the old oak and the café without pretending they are my property. I will use them as a mirror, not as a trophy.

That is what I mean by “productive.” It does not mean writing the next masterpiece. It means writing enough so that tomorrow will not be empty. That means writing about the things that matter, even when they are not easy to explain. And that means listening to the neighbor beside me.
