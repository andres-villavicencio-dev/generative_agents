Today is productive, mostly because I’m talking to Mei Lin and Carlos. She asked about my rainstorm and bark passages for The Garden Withi. I told her they’re rough edges, but they’re mine. Sometimes I want to make them sharp enough to cut. Sometimes I want to make them soft enough to swallow.

I’m also thinking about the old oak and the café display. It’s been sitting there for years, gathering leaves and showing us what we’ve forgotten. I want to honor it in community projects without making it feel like a museum piece. The café, too. It’s been a useful neighbor, always asking me what I need. But I’ve been hearing too much from residents lately. They want their needs answered without my poetry collection being treated as decoration.

Monday was heavy. I wrote through the morning routine and breakfast, then reviewed the natural-world poems. I finished the rainstorm and bark passages. They’re still unfinished, but they’re mine. I don’t need perfection to make them real. I need enough honesty to make them mine.

At five, I ate dinner. The kitchen was quiet. I thought about Latoya Williams. She was kind, patient, and always right. I don’t think I’ve ever felt more grounded. Maybe that’s why I keep turning back to the oak. Maybe that’s why I keep writing.

I need to balance my identity with the people who live here. I don’t want to be the loud poet who dominates every conversation. I want to be someone who contributes without demanding attention. That’s not weakness. That’s responsibility.

I’ll revise the passages tonight. I’ll make the rain more urgent, the bark more honest, and the café feel less like a prop. Then I’ll go to bed around ten. Monday was too heavy for a whole week. Tomorrow, I’ll write again—without pretending everything is resolved.
