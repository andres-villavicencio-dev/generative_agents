I woke at seven. The sun was already warm enough to make my eyes water, which is exactly the kind of morning I want. At seven, I didn’t need any special instructions. At seven, I got up, brushed my teeth, checked the coffee machine, and made sure my body didn’t ask me to do anything more than exist.

After breakfast, I reviewed the poems from the natural-world collection. I wrote about the oak and the café. Not because they were beautiful. Not because they were grand. I wrote about them because they are part of the community project and because I need to find a way to carry the old oak into the story without turning it into a decoration.

The old oak isn’t just wood and leaves. It’s the place where residents come in, where strangers share a cup, and where the sound of a bell keeps the neighborhood from disappearing. It’s loud enough to wake people up, but gentle enough to let them sleep through the night. I should honor that. I should let the community see what it means for the oak and the café to be together.

I’m thinking about how to balance my artistic identity with residents’ needs. I don’t want to write poetry for my own pleasure alone. I want to write for the people who live there. I want to show them that the old oak is not a symbol of my ideas. I want to show them that the café is not a place for display. I want to show them that the oak and the café belong to everyone.

Tonight, I’ll write about the oak without naming it. About the smell of coffee. About the way the light moves through the branches. I’ll find a way to make the old oak feel alive without turning it into an object I can easily put in a box.

And I’ll make sure Latoya is there too. We talked about Monday’s productive day, about the poems, about what I’m trying to carry forward. We didn’t finish the conversation, but we finished each other. That’s what I want today: to carry a poem forward while still listening.

I’ll keep writing until the coffee is finished. Then I’ll go back to the oak.
