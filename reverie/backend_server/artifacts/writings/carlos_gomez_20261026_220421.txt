Seven o’clock. The house is quiet. My head feels like it’s been wrapped in wet wool and held upside down. I need to clear the noise.

I took the breath I thought I had.

Seven-thirty. Morning poems. The Natural World Collection. I started with a stanza about a river:

The river does not hurry.  
It does not care what anyone thinks.  
It carries stones, weeds, and secrets  
until the banks are soft with mud.

I read it aloud to the empty house. It sounded angry. I liked it. I hated that I liked it.

The oak tree outside makes me think of old neighbors who were once proud and were never allowed to speak. I wonder what they thought about the café display. They probably said it was a good sign, or a joke, or a way to say “we’re still here.” I wonder if the oak knows that.

My collection feels crowded. Every day I gather more lines, more images, more versions of myself. I want to find the part of me that can listen without being answered.

Latoya called yesterday. We talked about poems and how people read them. It felt like a conversation I didn’t have to have. I should have asked her to come sit with me instead of sending me an email.

By five, the house is dark. By ten, I should be sleeping. I won’t. I’ll lie in bed with a notebook open, one page at a time, and let the poems settle.
