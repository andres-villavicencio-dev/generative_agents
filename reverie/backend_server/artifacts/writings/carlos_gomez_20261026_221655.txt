7:00 AM—up. One. Two. I’ve been waking up early enough to see the world before it’s already decided what it wants me to notice.

7:30 AM—breakfast. Something with honey on it. Maybe too much honey. I’m thirty-two, and I’m still pretending I’m not bored by the quiet of my own head.

The morning poem is coming back to me. A little old oak. I wrote about its roots, how they go down into the dirt without ever asking for a sign. How the bark holds the rain. I wrote about birds, nests, shadows moving on the wind. I wrote about something beautiful and stubborn.

Now I’m wondering whether beauty is enough. Not enough to hold a community together. Not enough to make people stop pretending they know what’s happening.

The café display was there yesterday. A wooden sign, maybe three feet tall, saying something about “roots and branches.” It didn’t say anything. It sat there like a nervous animal waiting to be noticed.

By noon, I’m ready to write again. The workshop is already halfway through, and I can’t help but think: what if this is a mistake? What if I’m just another person filling in the blanks instead of showing up for the real work?

I’ve been talking to Latoya. She said I sounded like I had something to hide. I said I did. I said I’m tired of being polite and pretending everything makes sense. She said, “You’re loud, Carlos. You’re rude. That’s part of the poem.”

“Poems aren’t polite,” I said.

“Poems are honest,” she corrected.

I’m not sure which one is right.

But I’m not leaving the coffee cup on the counter either. I’ve got a central conflict to figure out, and an argument to keep building. I don’t have all the answers, but I have enough to keep writing.

I’ll eat the rest of my breakfast. I’ll go to the workshop. And I’ll remember the oak. It doesn’t care what you say. It just exists.
