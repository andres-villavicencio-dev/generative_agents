I woke at seven, which is when I should have been doing something. I was supposed to review the poems from the Natural-World collection. I found a line about roots tangled beneath the city’s broken pavement. I wrote it down. I kept writing. I was not supposed to be writing. I was supposed to be editing.

At first I wanted to cut the whole thing. Cut the lines that sounded like I was apologizing for having feelings. Cut the ones about rain, light, and the way a tree remembers the ground. But the strongest moments were the ones I didn’t think anyone would notice. The line about the old oak and the café display. The quiet moment when I looked at a neighbor’s dog and thought, “I’m not the only one who wonders why they keep their heads down.” Those were the poems.

I reread the revision notes. “Tone is too aggressive,” I wrote. “You sound like a man shouting into a microphone.” I laughed. It was a good laugh. I was angry about how many poems I wrote. I was angry at the world. I was angry that I could not find enough truth in the sentences.

Today I will not shout. I will not say everything I feel. I will write the poems I already have. I will edit them until they are honest, not loud. I will leave the rough edges and keep the ones that make me want to stand up and say, “Look, I’m still here.”

By ten I will be gone. I will eat my dinner, review the day’s work, and make sure the old oak and the café display still have space. I will not make them look like a museum. I will not make them look like a performance for me. I will honor them as they are.

Monday was good. I remember Latoya’s conversation. I remember the workshop and the noise and the small, stubborn things we all keep. I do not carry all that in my head. I carry the poems. I carry the anger. I carry the hope. And I carry the belief that I can still make something useful.

I can edit. I can revise. I can still write.
