I woke at seven. The first thing I did was wash my face and stare at the mirror until my reflection looked like something I wanted to hide. I got up at seven-thirty, ate breakfast before the world woke, and then went to the natural-world collection. I read every poem in the collection aloud. Sometimes a line slipped through the pages like a thought that wasn’t meant to be said. Sometimes the rhythm stayed stubborn. Sometimes I just wanted to read it again and again.

I’m not a poet who writes for applause. I write for things I’ve felt in my chest and the things they’ve made me feel. I write about rain, roots, old creaking trees, and the quiet moments when everything is just moving. I also write about the people around me—the people who laugh too loudly, the people who say everything at once, and the people who listen without judging.

On Wednesday, February 15, I’m balancing the collection with creative-writing workshops. I’m balancing the old oak and café display in our community projects with the people who live on our block. I’m thinking about whether poetry can honor that display and still help residents feel seen. I’m thinking about whether my voice can stay loud and honest without hurting someone’s peace.

I’m not perfect. I’m loud. I’m rude sometimes. I’m not always kind. But I’m also curious. I’m also tired. I’m also still writing.

I’m going to proofread the final draft. I’ll check the words that slipped out by accident. I’ll make sure the rhythm doesn’t become careless. I’ll make sure the people I care about aren’t written over. I’ll make sure the poems I want to keep still feel like me—not like a costume I’ve put on to sound better than who I am.

By ten, I’m going to bed. By seven tomorrow, I’m going to wake up and write without checking my phone.
