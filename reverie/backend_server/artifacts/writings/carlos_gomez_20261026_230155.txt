I woke at 7:00 AM. The sun came in through the blinds, but I didn’t let it make me soft. I opened the journal and flipped to the first page. Good morning. How is it?

I finished the morning routine. Showered. Cooked breakfast. Then I sat down and reread the poems from the natural-world collection. “The Old Oak” still makes me pause. I keep changing the opening line, then undo it, then change it again. It’s exhausting. I want this poem to speak without asking anyone to explain it.

I’m also in a workshop today. I’m nervous about the ideas I’m taking. I don’t want to sound like I’m trying too hard. I want to sound like I’m listening.

At 5:00, I ate dinner. I ate a bowl of lentils. There’s no point making everything pretty. Food is useful.

I thought about Latoya earlier. I didn’t say much, but I listened. She asked me about the café display in the old oak neighborhood. I said I didn’t know anything about it. I didn’t mean to sound dismissive. I meant to say I don’t have all the answers.

I keep thinking about the old oak. It stands there for years, watching people come and go. I want to honor it without turning it into a decoration. I want to make a space where the tree is respected, not just admired.

I’m also thinking about how I want to balance my poetry with community projects. I want to keep my ideas sharp, but I also want to make sure residents have a say in what becomes visible in the neighborhood.

At ten, I go to bed. I’ll dream of roots, of wind in the leaves, of a poem that can’t be fixed.
