I woke at seven. The alarm didn’t scream. That’s the problem with a seven-thirty alarm. It’s polite. It waits. I woke up at seven, ate breakfast around five, and now I’m sitting here with a cup of coffee and a notebook full of poems that don’t want to be spoken. They’re quiet. They’re crowded. They’re all of me.

The workshop just started. We were supposed to talk about how people’s experiences can become poetry. The teacher said, “What happens to the experience when you stop it?” I said, “You turn it into something that hurts.” She smiled. She’s always smiling.

I’m thirty-two, loud, rude, and sometimes toxic. I’m not a poet who writes soft things for the community. I’m a poet who writes things that feel like truth. I wrote about the old oak in the café yesterday because it had stood for so long that I didn’t notice it was still standing. The oak knows me. I know the oak. And the oak knows that I’ve been here all my life, but I thought I was the only one who ever looked at it.

I want to write poetry that honors the old oak and the café display. Not by turning every tree into a symbol of beauty. Not by making every resident feel like they’re part of an art exhibit. I want to show them what they already know. I want to show them how we’ve changed without pretending the change is anything but human.

The oak is quiet. The café is loud. I’m trying to write in both. I’m trying to listen to the people around me without making them feel small. I’m trying to make the workshop feel like a place where you can say, “I’m tired,” and not be told you’re being dramatic. Sometimes that’s all we need.

I’m going to finish the collection. I’m going to edit it. I’m going to read it aloud. Maybe someone will say, “That’s a poem.” I don’t know what to do with that. Maybe I’ll say, “No, you didn’t.” That’s okay. That’s honest.

I’m going to keep the oak in my mind. I’m going to keep the café display in my mind. I’m going to keep talking to Latoya. She’s always asking good questions. I don’t always answer them right. Maybe that’s why we’re always learning. Maybe that’s why we’re still here. Maybe that’s why we need to keep talking.

I’m going to eat dinner at five. I’m going to go to bed at ten. I’m going to write another poem tomorrow morning. I don’t know what it will be. I don’t know if it will be useful. I don’t know if anyone will read it. But I’m going to write it anyway. Because sometimes the most important thing you can write is the thing you’ve been holding onto all along.
