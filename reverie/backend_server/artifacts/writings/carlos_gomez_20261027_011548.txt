We talked about voices. I said poetry is not about being perfect. It’s about being honest. About showing up even when the words feel loud.

I used to think a good poem had to sound like everything was golden. Now I know that can make you boring. I think the problem with a poet is pretending the inside is always peaceful. I write because the inside is messy. Because sometimes the truth comes in a cough, a mistake, or a sound I can’t explain.

Last night I finished a draft about an old oak. I wanted it to feel like the place I grew up: heavy, patient, always watching. But the oak was too big. The poem was too proud. I wanted to make it feel like the café showed me the world. I wanted to show it without making it look like I was teaching it how to talk.

That’s what the workshop can do. It can help me turn the raw stuff into something useful. It can let me listen. Not to every person, but to the person in front of me. To the old oak and the café. To the community that matters.

But I still have to keep the rough edges. I still have to say what I feel without pretending everything is perfect. I still have to find a voice that can handle the weight of my own thoughts without turning the whole thing into a sermon.

I’m ready to be loud. I’m ready to be rude. I’m ready to make mistakes. And if I stumble, I’ll keep writing, even when the voice feels tired.

I’m also thinking about this café display. I want to honor the oak and the people who gathered there without turning the space into a performance. I want to show the community what it is without asking them to perform for me.

I can do both. I can be loud, yes. But I can also be careful. I can be rough, yes. But I can also be honest. I can be Carlos Gomez. I don’t have to be perfect to be useful. I just have to show up.
