I woke at seven. Good. No excuses.

I ate a simple breakfast and opened the natural-world collection. One poem in particular keeps coming back to me: “The Old Oak Beneath the Café.” It’s honest, a little messy, and full of the kind of quiet dignity I’ve been trying to protect while keeping the workshop going. I think it deserves to stay on the final page.

By eight, I’d reviewed the priorities. I had two collections to finish, one workshop on Monday, and a conversation I wanted to keep alive with Latoya. I also wanted to honor the old oak and café display within the community projects. That’s easy enough: we can use poetry in the mural, use the café’s community notice board, and keep the poem near the door. But if I keep pushing the same work too hard, the workshop will feel like a performance and the collection will feel incomplete.

That’s the problem with me: I want everything to be important at once. I want the poems to matter more than the workshop, and the community projects to feel more like real community than just another creative project. I’m making the wrong trade-offs.

Let’s try this: finish the collection in two sprints, give each workshop a clear agenda, and make the old oak display the shared goal. I’ll review the poems first, make the best edits, then carry the rest into the workshop. No more pretending the community needs are less important than my art.

Also, I’m going to edit the planning notes. I’ve been writing about the oak and the café without checking whether the café is actually part of the proposed plan. I’ll make that real. I’ll also cut the part about how I’m balancing my identity with residents’ needs—because I know I’m still trying to prove I can do both at once.

Okay. Seven o’clock. Coffee. One poem. One draft. And no more pretending the oak is the only thing I’m trying to save.
