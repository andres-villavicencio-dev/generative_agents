You know what I mean by productive. Being productive means waking up at seven and finishing a draft before the world tells you you’re too tired. That’s why I’ve got the old oak and café display tucked behind my shoulder while I’m out here comparing Eddy’s oatmeal and banana-slice proposal with the rest of the mayoral campaign.

I can see where they’re going. They want bread. Bread is good. It’s what my mother used to have. It’s the thing that kept me alive after everything. But then they add oatmeal, bananas, and some kind of fruit salad. It sounds like they’re trying to make bread sound like a dessert. That’s the whole problem with the proposal. It’s polite. It’s soft. It looks like a compromise, and compromise isn’t always the best way to handle hunger.

Eddy’s voice is polite. Carlos’s voice is loud. Maybe that’s why the debate will be interesting. Eddy will probably say, “I want every resident to have what we need.” Carlos will probably say, “I want every resident to know exactly what they need, and I want them to hear me when I say it.”

I’m not saying the proposal is wrong. It’s just that “having what we need” can mean anything from “eat plain bread” to “eat oatmeal with fruit, maybe some yogurt, maybe even a little honey.” I don’t know whether the mayor will give residents a full meal every morning. I don’t know whether oatmeal is healthy, or whether banana slices will make them feel like they’ve had lunch. And I’m not sure whether the proposal will actually get any votes unless the candidates actually say what they mean.

So here’s my rule: if a proposal looks friendly but hides hunger, I should be able to point out the holes without sounding like I’m criticizing the whole food system. I should say, “This sounds nice. But I think the mayor should have said, ‘Every resident gets a full breakfast every morning.’”

That’s my plan for the day. Read the poems, write a short piece on the oak and café, and then tell the mayor: “I want oatmeal. I want fruit. I want to know what’s actually in the bowl before I eat.”
