Eddy Lin just sent me a picture of a candidate’s oatmeal-and-banana-slice proposal. I stared at it for a long time until it looked like a suggestion from a politician who had never eaten oatmeal.

Carlos Gomez, the mayor who thinks his oatmeal is a political platform, sounds ridiculous. But I respect the fact that he’s asking the community what it wants rather than waving a spoon around and pretending everything is perfect.

I’ve been thinking about how to honor the old oak and the café display while balancing my poetry collection with creative workshops. I don’t want to make this about the candidates. I want to make it about residents.

Last week, I had Latoya Williams on the phone. She asked, “How are you doing, Carlos?” and I told her, “Good, thank you.” She didn’t say, “You’re doing great.”

I don’t believe in perfect words. I believe in honest ones. I’ve written about the natural world, and lately, I’m wondering whether poetry can be useful without turning everyone into a seminar.

The oatmeal proposal is one of those things. It’s simple, practical, and oddly hopeful. The mayor says, “We’ll give everyone oatmeal and a slice of banana.” I’d give him a banana, but the oatmeal sounds like something politicians say to make people comfortable.

I don’t want to sound like I’m judging the candidates. I’m just noticing that good ideas should come from somewhere other than the podium. They should come from a community that knows what it needs and says it clearly.

So here’s what I’m thinking: maybe we should test the oatmeal proposal. Maybe we should see what people actually want. Maybe we should ask residents what they would eat instead of listening to a speaker who can’t be bothered with the details.

Carlos Gomez, you’re a poet. You’ve written about the old oak. You’ve written about what people value. You’ve also written about how hard it is to be useful. That makes you interesting.

But I’m not here to make you useful. I’m here to make sure you’re actually listening. And that’s a much better idea.
