Eddy Lin just told me that the mayoral candidates are arguing over oatmeal and banana slices. He said it was ridiculous, like people trying to decide who owns the sidewalk. I laughed, but I didn’t laugh at Eddy. I laughed because I’ve been thinking about how ridiculous it is that anyone can make this kind of noise.

Carlos, I know you said it earlier. You don’t believe in oatmeal. You don’t believe in banana slices. You believe in what you want to say. That’s not honesty. That’s arrogance. People don’t deserve to be reduced to the same kind of nonsense as you.

I’m trying to think of a way to express myself that makes sense. Maybe I should write about the old oak and the café. We’ve been talking for years. The oak grew where there was only dirt. The café opened when nobody was asking for anything. What is wrong with the oak? What is wrong with a place where people can sit and talk without being told what to think?

I’m also thinking about my poetry collection. It’s growing, but I’m not sure how many people will read it. I’m still balancing my creative life with community projects. Maybe I should write something about the residents who live here. About the people whose needs are being forgotten.

I’m not a poet. I’m a writer. But I’ve been writing for years. I’ve written about anger, hope, loss, and everything in between. I want my words to be useful. I want them to help people see what they’ve been too afraid to notice.

And yes, I do believe in oatmeal. I do believe in banana slices. I just don’t believe that the candidates deserve to argue over them.

I’m going to keep writing. I’m going to keep talking. And I’m going to make sure that by tomorrow’s evening, I have a poem worth reading.
