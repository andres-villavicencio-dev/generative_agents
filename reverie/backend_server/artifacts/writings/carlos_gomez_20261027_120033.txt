Eddy Lin just claimed there was no such thing as “oatmeal and banana-slice” on the mayoral ballot. I laughed. I actually laughed so hard I almost threw my pen across the table.

Carlos Gomez, who I assume is an actual man, is pretending the election isn’t a circus.

I was thinking about the old oak and café display. The community project, it’s clear, is trying to look like a civic commitment. It’s wearing a green ribbon and holding a sign that reads “We Want a Better Place.” But what are we wanting if we want the same things everyone else wants? The same clean streets, the same quiet streets, the same coffee on demand?

The candidates talk about budgets, budgets, budgets. One says oatmeal. He says oatmeal. That’s the first time I’ve seen a candidate say oatmeal. I was already thinking of banana-slice. Why banana-slice? Why not oatmeal with fruit?

I keep thinking about Carlos Gomez’s poetry. He’s got this whole inner life of his and he’s still writing. He keeps talking about Latoya Williams. Latoya’s got a voice. She’s got a heart. And I’m still wondering if my own voice is loud enough.

Oatmeal and banana-slice aren’t going to be the election’s headline. But they’re the first thing people will notice.

I’m not going to become a politician. I’m going to keep writing. I’m going to keep sharing my thoughts. I’m going to keep making people feel seen.

And maybe, just maybe, I’ll write about the old oak. Not to make it look like a monument. But to make sure it gets noticed.

For now, I’ll eat breakfast. I’ll review my poems. I’ll check my phone. I’ll tell someone what I’m thinking.

We’re not going to win by saying oatmeal and banana-slice. We’re going to win by saying we actually care.
