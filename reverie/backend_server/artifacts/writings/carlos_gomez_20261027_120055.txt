WAKE UP AT 7. I ate breakfast before the sun was fully out. Then I read the poems from the natural-world collection. Some of them feel like the old oak and café displayed on the community wall. They’re proud, stubbornly honest things. I want to write back to that oak.

Eddy Lin and I compared the mayoral candidates’ oatmeal-and-banana-slice proposal. Eddy said it sounded like food for the poor. I said it sounded like a promise that breakfast will never be hard again. He wanted to know if I’d agree. I said I never said “no.” I said, “Let’s make it better.”

He laughed. He didn’t mean it that way. But I saw the truth in his voice. I saw how easy it would be to say we’re better together and still keep eating what everyone else is eating. I’m not here to sell anyone anything. But I also know that when people stand around a table pretending their oatmeal is perfect, everyone loses something.

I’ve been thinking about the old oak. It’s been there for years. It knows the café. It knows the residents. If I honor it properly, maybe it will tell us what we need. Not the kind of oak that looks cool from a distance. The one that lets people breathe without being told what to do.

Monday was productive. That is good. But productivity can become something else if it doesn’t care about who is being asked to do it. I need to listen. I need to learn. I need to write about things that actually matter.

Tonight I’m going to read the collection again. Maybe I’ll change one poem. Maybe I’ll leave one alone. I’m going to write the kind of poem that makes people pause, not the kind that makes them nod.
