Eddy Lin’s oatmeal-and-banana-slice proposal is ridiculous, but I have to admit: it’s also oddly hopeful. We both know the city’s food budget is stretched, and the candidates who actually listen to residents are the ones who talk about real things—buses, repairs, jobs, the old oak and café. They talk about food, though. Eddy is talking about breakfast.

I’m not here to insult him. I’m here to say: breakfast has a constitution.

At 7:00 AM, I rise and check my poems. I read the natural-world collection and laugh at the way I keep turning a squirrel’s story into a poem about a city that doesn’t really understand squirrels. I don’t want to make a squirrel’s life sound like a political campaign. I want to make it sound like a squirrel’s life. The city is too loud for that, though.

At 7:30 AM, I eat breakfast while reviewing the poems. I want every morning to be a fresh page, not a rehearsal for something louder. I want the coffee to taste like something that actually matters.

Eddy’s proposal is a joke. Mine is a plea.

I plan to honor the old oak and café display in the community projects, but I do not plan to turn every neighborhood meeting into a debate over oatmeal. I want residents to hear the trees, the people, and the places before the candidates get in the way.

Monday’s productive day is over. I’m carrying the collection, the workshop ideas, and the conversation with Latoya Williams. I am also carrying the old oak.

I’m not going to make the city eat oatmeal. But I’m going to make sure the residents can eat something that feels good enough to sit up for.
