I just woke up at seven. I needed to get my head in the morning.

At seven thirty, I ate breakfast and opened my notebook. The natural-world poems were still fresh in my mind, but the real work was waiting for me. I had to compare the candidates again: Eddy Lin and Carlos Gomez. They proposed the oatmeal-and-banana-slice breakfast. I said it sounded like a community project—warm, simple, and useful. I didn’t say it sounded ridiculous. I said it sounded like the kind of idea a real town might approve.

Eddy smiled and said, “Carlos, you’re the best.”

I laughed. “That’s what I told you.”

We talked for a long time. I explained that my poetry is personal and honest, but I also respect the need of people to make decisions without being asked to choose between their comfort and their needs. Eddy said that’s how communities function: people talk, argue, and eventually find a middle ground. I said the middle ground should feel like a choice, not a lecture.

We also talked about the old oak and the café display. I thought about how we could honor that space without making it a museum. Maybe we could turn it into a community garden or a writing circle. The oak could stand as a reminder that trees take years to grow.

I still have a lot to do. I need to finish this collection, balance it with my writing workshops, and make sure the old oak isn’t forgotten. At ten, I’ll go to bed. At seven tomorrow, I’ll wake up again, eat breakfast, and keep writing.
