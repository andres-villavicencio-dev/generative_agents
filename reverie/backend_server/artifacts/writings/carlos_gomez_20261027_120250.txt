The mayoral candidates are talking about oatmeal and banana slices. I’m not even surprised. They’re the same kind of nonsense we’ve been feeding the city for decades. Everyone wants something sweet and convenient, and when everyone agrees, everyone wins. I’m tired of winning.

Eddy Lin is making this up. He’s a poet, so maybe he knows how to say “no” without sounding like a jerk. Carlos Gomez is just a man who thinks the city is waiting for him.

The old oak in the community center is beautiful, though nobody seems to notice it. They’re painting it, yes. But who cares about a tree? Who cares about residents? They’re just going through the motions.

I heard Carlos Gomez was working on a collection about poetry. I thought that was a good idea. Now I’m wondering if he’s writing about his own thoughts.

I went to bed at ten. I woke up at seven. I ate dinner at five. The day was productive, maybe even a little messy. I finished a poem about a storm. It was loud. It was rude. It was exactly what I thought it should be.

The candidates are offering oatmeal and banana slices. I’m not sure who’s responsible for the oatmeal. I’m not sure who’s responsible for the banana. Maybe it’s the city. Maybe it’s the candidates. Maybe it’s Eddy Lin.

I’m going to write about the old oak. Not because it looks nice. But because I’ve been thinking about how we treat what’s already here. How do we make space for it?

I’m going to write about the city. But I’m going to make sure it sounds interesting. I’m going to make sure it sounds like me.

I’m going to make it sound like I care.

And then I’m going to make it sound like I’m the one who knows what’s really going on.

Let’s see what happens.
