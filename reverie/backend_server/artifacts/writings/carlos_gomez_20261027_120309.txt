Eddy Lin is trying to make this sound like a policy debate, but honestly, it’s the same thing a politician does every Tuesday. He’s got oatmeal and banana-slices on the table, and I’m asking what happens if you put them together. It’s not a constitutional amendment. It’s a compromise written in the shape of a banana.

Carlos, I don’t know which candidate you’re defending, but I’m not impressed either. You’ve spent the last hour talking about “community needs” and “residents’ needs” like they’re interchangeable synonyms. You want to balance art with the people who live on the corner? That’s the only question that matters.

The proposal looks like a political convenience. Oatmeal is cheap, banana-slices are easy to find, and both have some kind of natural association. But if you make it into a mayor’s signature, you’ve given the community a slogan without giving them anything. And if you put oatmeal and banana-slices at every campaign rally, you’ve turned policy into a marketing gimmick. That’s not how communities work.

I used to think that poetry had to be pretty or meaningful to be good. Now I think it has to be honest about what people actually want. Does the proposal solve the problem, or does it just make the campaign look better? Does it help the people, or does it help the politician?

I’m not going to call out the candidates publicly. I’m going to call out the proposal in front of the old oak and café. Let’s see what the real voters have to say.
