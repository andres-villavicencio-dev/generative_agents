I woke at seven. The kettle was boiling. I ate breakfast at the table with the newspaper spread out, checking in on the poems I’d been writing for a week. The natural-world collection is getting heavy. I keep coming back to the oak tree, to the café, to the quiet corners of the neighborhood. It keeps saying, you belong here. But belonging doesn’t mean you have to stay.

I’ve had the good days. I’ve had the bad days. Sometimes the writing feels like coming home. Sometimes it’s loud and angry—like I’m trying to get through the noise with something real. That’s the point. I’m not here to make everything quiet. I’m here to make it honest.

Latoya called me yesterday. She asked about the mayoral proposals. I told her what I thought about Mei Lin and Yuriko’s comparison. She said, “You’re saying the same thing but in different words.” I said, “Mei Lin wants to make the city work. Yuriko wants to make the people feel like they belong.” She didn’t laugh. She looked me right in the eye.

I wrote that down. Not because I need to defend myself or prove anything. I wrote it because I wanted to hear it.

I’m going to the old oak this morning. I’m going to sit with it. I’m going to listen. I don’t have all the answers, and I don’t have to give them all at once. I can just be here.

By the way, I’m not rude. I’m just tired of pretending I’m easy.
