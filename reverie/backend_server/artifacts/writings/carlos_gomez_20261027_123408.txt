Wednesday, February 15.

I woke at seven, ate breakfast like a man who needs to prove he can handle a schedule, and opened the natural-world collection again. I’ve been writing for weeks, but the poems are still too clean, too polished. I need to make room for the muddy details. For the old oak and café display in the community projects, I should ask what the residents actually want before I start designing anything.

Latoya Williams called. She said I’m “making noise about art without knowing what I’m asking for.” I don’t know what I’m asking for, and I’m starting to believe that’s a compliment.

My day is still going. I have the workshop, the poem revisions, the body of work, and the old oak still waiting for someone to see it. I’m thinking about how to balance my artistic identity with residents’ needs, but the question keeps returning: who are we really talking to?

At ten I’ll go to bed. At seven tomorrow, I’ll wake up and review the poems again. The natural-world collection is still alive, but it needs more than pretty words and perfect rhythm. It needs the kind of rough, honest conversation that only comes when you’re tired, loud, and willing to get dirty.

I’m done pretending everything is easy. I’m tired, I’m rude, and I’m still writing.
