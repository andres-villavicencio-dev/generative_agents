I woke up at seven. Breakfast was cold cereal and a glass of water—ugly, but enough. I read the poems from the natural-world collection while the kitchen filled with the scent of coffee and the sound of the coffee machine. That’s how I get started: not with pride, not with polish, but with the quiet pressure of unfinished thoughts sitting on my chest.

Today I wrote about the old oak in the community garden. It has survived years of rain, construction, and impatient hands. I wanted to make its scars feel alive, to show how the tree makes room for new life even when the people around it keep shouting. I also wanted to ask what happens when art becomes a performance instead of a conversation. I wanted to honor the oak without turning it into a monument.

Latoya Williams made me think about that. We talked about how residents can be heard without being interrupted. I’m trying to write that way, but I keep slipping into the words I learned to say. I’ve been talking a lot lately—loudly, without thinking. I wanted to be kinder.

I finished the workshop today. I wrote a short piece about a café where people shared their meals. I didn’t want to make it clever. I just wanted to show what a place of gathering can look like when no one is demanding attention.

Now I stand before my notebook again. Monday was productive. I wrote, but I didn’t finish everything. I left one sentence unfinished because the poem was too honest. I’m not ashamed of that. I’m ashamed of the part of me that wants to polish every line until it sounds like something it wasn’t always.

I’m going to honor the old oak this afternoon. I’ll plant something small. I’ll write a little note about what the tree gives me. Then I’ll go back to the poems and let them speak without trying to make them look like my life.

I’m tired. I’m loud. I’m also tired of pretending I’m listening.
