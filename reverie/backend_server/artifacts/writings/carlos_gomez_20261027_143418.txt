Seven o’clock. The house is still, and my head feels ready to write. I’ve been waking up before dawn in the past, but lately I’ve learned to sit here, with the light filtering through the curtains, and let the quiet do the talking.

I put on my coat, grab the notebook and pen, and check the poems from the natural-world collection. I’ve been revisiting the poems I finished last week. They’re quieter than they were when I first wrote them—less loud, more careful. I still want to show the world what I feel, but I’m learning that some truths don’t need to shout to be heard.

I want to honor the old oak and the café display at the community center on Wednesday. The oak has stood there for years, watching people come and go. The café has offered warmth and stories in exchange for my poetry. I’ve noticed how the display changes with the seasons, how the light shifts across the branches. I want to use that change as a metaphor: growth, memory, time, community.

At the same time, I’m thinking about residents who use the center and whether my poetry is serving them or taking something away. I’ve been talking with Latoya Williams about this, and I want to make sure my art doesn’t become another kind of noise. I want readers to feel seen, not just be told what to feel.

I’m ready to begin. I’ll write about the oak, the café, and the small people who gather beneath its branches. I’ll write about how the morning light changes the world, even if I don’t have all the answers. I’ll also write about the poems I’ve been carrying, the ones I’ve rewritten, the ones I’m still afraid to finish.

I may still be loud, and I still have a tendency to say things before I’ve thought them through. I may still be rude, if I’m honest with myself. But I’m also learning. I’m learning that poetry isn’t just about feeling—it’s about listening. And I’m listening more today.
