Writing Session: The Old Oak and the Café

I woke at seven, ate the breakfast I needed, and checked the poems from the Natural-World collection. They’re messy, sometimes angry, sometimes tender. I want to keep them, but they’re still too small for the space I’m building.

Latoya called me yesterday. I talked about the old oak, how it stands in the center of the neighborhood. It doesn’t care who owns it. It holds rain, roots, and the slow, stubborn life of the trees around it. That image is becoming my theme, though I don’t know yet if I’m talking about the oak, the café, or both. I need to choose.

I spent the morning outlining the central idea. I could say the community is growing through art. I could say residents need more space. Or I could say everyone needs a place where their voices are heard without being forced into a single, polished message. The oak is the best symbol because it refuses to be replaced. Maybe the old oak is the neighborhood’s conscience. Maybe the café is where people gather to remember that they’ve all come from somewhere.

By noon, I had a rough paragraph:

The old oak does not apologize for standing here. It does not ask for a sign. It simply stands, and the trees around it learn to lean, to share, to listen.

It felt true, but also dangerous. If every café becomes another place for the oak, does it become a symbol of community? Or does every tree need its own space to stand alone?

Tonight, before bed at ten, I’ll revise the theme. I’ll also ask Latoya if she wants help identifying a central character. Maybe the old oak is a person, and the café is a community. Maybe the whole collection is one person’s journey through the city.

I don’t have all the answers. I only have the oak, the café, and the poems that keep coming back. I’ll write them down, and maybe one of them will carry the day.
