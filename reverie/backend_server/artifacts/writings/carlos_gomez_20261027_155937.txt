The Workshop Opening

Welcome. By Wednesday, February 15, I’m still trying to finish. I have a poem collection, a workshop schedule, a community project, and the old oak and café display staring back at me like it wants a piece of my life.

This is the workshop’s purpose: to show up, write, and be real. We will talk about how poetry is supposed to be honest without being polite. We will examine the old oak and café display not as decoration, but as a reminder that community is not about perfect buildings—it is about people who show up, who listen, and who help one another find something useful.

We will talk about how to balance a poet’s identity with the needs of residents. I’ve been thinking about how to honor the oak and café while still making space for people. The oak is patient. The café is full of people doing difficult things. We do not have to make the oak perfect, but we must make sure it does not turn into a monument.

Feedback will come from the workshop itself. There will be a short, anonymous note sheet before the closing session. I will read it and give it back. I will not give you a letter of appreciation unless you have written something I can actually use. I do not need your compliments; I need your honest observations.

The workshop is not a place to be polite. It is a place to hear what people are angry about, to see what people are proud of, and to figure out what we can build together.

I will not make excuses. I will not say I am “busy” unless I mean I am busy. I will be here, I will be here, and I will work through whatever has to be worked through.

For now, I’ll begin by reading the poems from my natural-world collection. They are not beautiful. They are messy. They are mine. And that is exactly what makes them worth reading.
