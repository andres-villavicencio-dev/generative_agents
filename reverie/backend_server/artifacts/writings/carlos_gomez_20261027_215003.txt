I woke at 7:00. Coffee. Morning routine. Then breakfast. By 7:30, the poems from the natural-world collection were sitting on my desk like a small, stubborn animal refusing to be forgotten. I didn’t want to let them gather dust. I wanted to make them alive again.

The workshop has been a good place to be loud, but sometimes being loud makes me feel like the room has grown too big. I’ve been trying to write without losing myself. Not shouting. Not losing my voice. Just using it.

Latoya called today, and we talked about the old oak and the café display. She said the display was beautiful, and I was quiet. Quiet means I’m listening. And for the first time in a long while, I’m listening without judging every detail.

I’m balancing poetry, creative writing, and community projects. I don’t think I’m doing all of that alone. Some of this is me. Some of it is others. And sometimes I need to ask: What do the residents need? What do I need?

The oak stands here as if it has no plan. It stands as if it does not care how many people admire it. It stands as if beauty is not a performance for approval. I’m learning that. That doesn’t mean I stop trying to say what I feel. It means I try harder to make sure what I say matters.

I still want to make something bold. I still want to make something raw. But I also want to make something that honors the people who live around it. That is the part I’m still learning.

I need to remember that my identity is not a wall. It is a doorway. And sometimes the way through is messy, loud, and slightly rude. That is part of me. And I need to learn to let it in, too.
