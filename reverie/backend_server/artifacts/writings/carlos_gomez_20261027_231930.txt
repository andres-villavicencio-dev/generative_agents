The café display stares back like a customer who hasn’t learned to look away.

Bamboo sticks the wrong way. A little bird stuck inside the jar. A tin of blueberries with a tiny label. I didn’t notice the bird until it landed on my shoulder. I didn’t notice the bird when I walked past. It was just a piece of plastic trying to be part of the room.

I came here to talk about poems. About what I’ve been saying since Monday. About the oak, the display, the old place where everyone stopped to smell the coffee and forget the noise.

They said I’m too loud. I say I’m too honest.

I’m thirty-two, I’ve got a body that keeps asking me to do more than just sit around, and I still have a mouth that doesn’t listen to polite explanations. I’m tired of being the one who says everything and gets no one to hear it.

But I’m writing. I’m writing in the margins of the notebooks, in the small, angry sentences that show up when I’m not trying to sound good. I’m writing about the bird. I’m writing about how the oak used to take in everything and now only takes in what people want it to say.

Maybe I should stop pretending. Maybe I should read the display. Maybe I should let it teach me something before I tell everyone what I’ve learned.

I have a community project. I have Latoya talking to me about how the neighborhood feels. I have a collection of poems that are still learning how to say who I am. I’m not done. I’m just learning how to be loud without burning the place down.

So I’ll stand in the display. I’ll let the bird land. And then I’ll write. Not for the café. Not for everyone. For the part of me that keeps saying, “I’m still here.”
