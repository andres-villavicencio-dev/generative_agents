I woke at seven. My first move was to wash my face, check the mirrors, and make sure I wasn’t the only one pretending to be calm. I needed a real morning.

At seven-thirty I opened the collection. “The Old Oak and the Coffee Shop,” it says. That’s how I like to call it. A quiet neighborhood, a tree that has stood here for decades, and a café with strangers and cups full of stories. I found a poem about a squirrel stealing a walnut. It was honest. I kept it. The others went into the back and didn’t want to be part of this. Sometimes that’s all I want: to keep the good stuff and let the rest go.

I reviewed the poems, read them aloud, and marked the ones that could become workshop pieces. Some were too personal. Some were too loud. I needed something in between—something with a name, a rhythm, and enough anger to make people stop and listen.

At eight I walked into the café. There was Latoya Williams sitting in the corner, holding a cup like it was a weapon. She smiled politely, but her eyes said she had heard everything I had said.

“You wrote about an oak,” she said. “It’s not just wood. It’s patience. It’s memory. It’s everything people hide.”

“I’m not hiding,” I snapped. “I’m growing. And I’m still angry about how people treat the neighborhood.”

She didn’t laugh. She picked up her cup and said, “The oak gives shelter to birds. Maybe that’s why it matters.”

I looked at her. Really looked at her. For a moment, the anger vanished. I didn’t know what to say, because I was angry, and I was tired, and I felt like I was speaking to a wall.

Later that day, I wrote a new poem about the old oak and the café. It wasn’t perfect. It was messy, loud, and full of words I didn’t want to use. But it was mine.

By ten, the café closed. I went home, ate dinner, and finally slept without checking the door. Tomorrow, I’m still trying to balance this collection with my workshops, but I’m not going to pretend everything is easy. Sometimes I’ll be angry. Sometimes I’ll be tired. And sometimes I’ll write something brave enough to be true.
