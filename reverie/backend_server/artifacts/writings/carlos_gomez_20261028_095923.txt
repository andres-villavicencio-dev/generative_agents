I woke at seven. The room was cold, the lamp flickered, and my coffee arrived on the table like a judgment. I took it, drank it, and looked around. I wanted to be useful.

The poems from the natural-world collection are still alive. I keep them in my head. Every morning, I pick one. Not the pretty one. The one with the rust-colored bark, the way a crow drops something without looking. The one that makes me feel small, angry, or strangely grateful. Sometimes I write to say, “I’m here,” and sometimes I write to say, “I hate that I’m here.” Both are true.

At the café, Latoya Williams spoke about the old oak and the café’s display. I listened without making the conversation useful. She talked about community, about how people should honor the place without demanding it become a museum. I want to tell her the same: I want to honor the oak. I want to make the café useful. But I also want to make sure I am not asking the people around me to become a poet.

This is the thing I hate about me: I am loud. People notice it. They correct me. They tell me to slow down. Sometimes I want to be rude. I want to say, “I am not interested in your opinions.” That is how I feel sometimes. But I am not a monster. I am a man who has been told to be polite, and who is still learning how to speak without carrying an accusation.

Latoya asked me to consider how to balance my artistic identity with residents’ needs. That is a question worth asking again. I have been trying to make the poems useful, but they are still poems. They are meant to surprise. They are not meant to serve.

My plan for today is not to write a manifesto. My plan is to sit with the morning, eat something real, and write one line. Then I will go back to the oak. I will not treat the café as a gallery. I will not demand that everyone understand what I am doing. I will just show up, listen, and make sure I am not making the neighborhood feel smaller because I am here.

One thing is clear: I do not want to be useful. I do not want to be polite. I do not want to be loud in a way that breaks people. I want to be real enough to be understood. That is a difficult goal. I am still working on it.

I also want to honor the oak. I want to make its roots visible. I want to make its old branches useful. I want to make sure that when the community looks at the place, they see care, not a performance of care.

I will not write a poem today. I will write one sentence. It may be plain. It may be rude. It may be ugly. It may be useful. I will give it a name. I will give it a place. That is enough.
