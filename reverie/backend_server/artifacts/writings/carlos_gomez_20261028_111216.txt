I woke at 7:00 a.m., ate breakfast, and looked over the poems in the natural-world collection. I’m tired of writing the same old sentences.

“Every leaf trembles in the wind.” That’s fine, but it sounds like a nursery rhyme. “The wind trembles every leaf.” No, that’s still too literal. I need to find a way to make the leaf feel alive without making every sentence a description.

I should also cut the paragraph on community projects. It’s too long and sounds like I’m talking to a committee instead of the people who will live in this place. I’m trying to balance my poetry with helping residents, but I keep letting my ideas sound like demands.

Latoya’s conversation reminded me to listen. She said she needed space to write without being judged. I should honor that. Old oak and café are already in my thoughts, and I should use that to make my project feel like an offering, not a lecture.

On Monday, I made time for a workshop, even though I’m not sure I’ll finish it. Being productive isn’t about finishing every thing I write. It’s about showing up, even when I can’t make it perfect.

I’ll revise the main paragraphs. I’ll tighten the diction, make the evidence clearer, and let the poetry speak without explaining everything.
