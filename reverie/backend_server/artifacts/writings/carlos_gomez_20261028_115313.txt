I woke up at seven. Didn’t check the phone. That’s the first rule.

By seven thirty, I was reviewing the natural-world poems again. I’m tired. My hands shake when I sit down to write. Yesterday, I wrote about the old oak and the café display. I wanted to honor both. I wanted to honor the old oak.

But I don’t honor people with poetry. I make them notice.

The oak is quiet. The café is loud. The birds are loud. That’s why I keep coming back to them. Not to comfort the world. To prove I can make it bearable.

I’m proud of the poems I’ve written. But I’m also embarrassed by the ones I’ve been holding back. The ones where I want to be useful. The ones where I want to listen.

I’ve been meaning to talk to Latoya about this. About how I can honor the oak and the café without turning the whole community into a display. Maybe I should ask the café owners what they need. Maybe I should ask the oak what it wants.

The oak is old. It’s learned what a tree does. It doesn’t teach. It doesn’t give advice. It stands. It grows. It gives shade.

I’m going to write a poem about the old oak. Not to praise it. To say what I’ve been saying since I was a kid: I want to belong to something. I want to belong to a place where people can look and feel like they’ve been there.

I want to be useful. But usefulness without respect is just noise.

I’m going to eat dinner at five. Then I’ll sleep at ten. Monday’s productive day is finally over. I need to remember that my collection is not just my work. It’s also a conversation with the people I’ve made.

Let’s keep talking.
