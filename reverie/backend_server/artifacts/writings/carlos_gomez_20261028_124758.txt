I woke at seven, ate the stupid oatmeal for breakfast, and opened the notebook. The poems from the Natural-World collection feel like a little garden I’m trying to tidy up. Every day, I’ve been adding something new—something raw, something honest. I don’t write for applause. I write because I’m scared of silence. Because I’ve got everything inside me, and I’m not afraid of being loud when I’m alone.

At the café, I saw the old oak and its café display. The sign said, “Honor the old oak and café.” I laughed. I felt like the old oak was talking back at me. It’s been standing here for years, collecting rain, wind, and careless people who think they can change the world by leaning on its trunk. I wanted to help, but I also wanted to say something that mattered.

Latoya came over this afternoon. She said she wanted to hear my poems. I said, “I want to hear what I’ve been hiding.”

I spent the afternoon in my room, listening to a new line, rewriting the next one. I’m not going to pretend I have all the answers. I’m going to let the poems come from me, not from a notebook I’m trying to make look perfect. I’m going to write about the quiet people in the neighborhood, about the oak that watches every passing day, and about how I’m still learning to be useful.

I’ll invite Latoya, but I won’t make her the center of everything. I’ll let her see what I’ve been doing, and I’ll give her the space to hear me too. Maybe we’ll have a workshop together. Maybe we’ll argue a little, laugh at the same stupid lines, and end up with a poem that feels like something real.

The oak is waiting. The cup of coffee is waiting. I’m ready to write again.
