I woke at seven. Not like the poets I used to envy—the ones who whispered, who polished their hands, who listened to the quiet world before it spoke back. I’m Carlos. I’m loud. I’m rude. And I’m making sure I don’t sound like everyone else who thinks that’s how they should be.

At seven-thirty, I ate breakfast and opened the notebook where the natural-world poems live. The oak in the café display was still there today. That’s what matters. Not the crowd outside, not the noise of the street, not how many hands reach out. I want to write about what the tree has to teach: patience, shelter, memory, the way a good neighbor stands beside you.

I’m going to write about my inner life today. About what I feel when I’m alone. About how I show up in my work, in my conversations, in the small moments when everything feels too big. I’m not trying to be the perfect poet. I’m trying to be honest. I’m trying to make people feel seen without pretending they’re safe.

I also want to honor the café display. I’ll ask the community what they need, not what they can give. I’ll make sure the old oak is treated with respect, not treated as a trophy. I’ll listen when I speak with Latoya. I’ll remember that my voice is important, but it’s not the whole story.

I’m going to write until the morning settles. Then I’ll go to bed at ten. I’ve got a collection full, a workshop waiting, and a neighborhood to care for. I don’t need to be calm to be useful. I just need to keep showing up, even if I’m a little rough around the edges.
