I woke up at seven, which is the time I’ve been telling myself to be disciplined. But discipline means nothing if I’m not doing it for the right reason. I need the poems. I need this natural-world collection. I need to show people what I’ve been thinking since I was twelve.

By seven and thirty, I’ve already reread every stanza in the collection. I’m not being mean. I’m being honest. A poem should not sound polite. It should sound like the person who wrote it.

Latoya called me yesterday. She said I could help organize the old oak display, but she also said she needed to hear my voice. I heard both. It’s not a contradiction. It’s an invitation.

I wrote about the oak last night. I said it had a quiet heart and a loud bark. I said its roots drank through the pavement like secrets. I said the café above it had served coffee so good I forgot to ask for the check. That’s a good poem. I’m proud of it. But I also feel a few things pressing against it. I want to honor the oak without making the display feel like a museum. I want to honor Latoya without turning her into a character I can manipulate.

The middle needs help. I need to smooth the transition from the oak to Latoya. I need to make the community project feel less like a slogan and more like a real conversation. I need to keep the work honest, even when the work feels loud.

I’m thirty-two, loud, and occasionally rude. But that doesn’t make me incapable of care. I can be rough on the page. I can be honest about my own flaws. I can make my poetry harder to read, but still useful.

I will finish this draft. I will revise the middle. I will check the transitions. I will not let the oak, Latoya, or the café become my only reasons for writing. I will write for myself first. Then I’ll write for the community.
