The old oak at the corner of 4th and 15th looks at me like it knows what I’m doing. I should say the same thing to the café display. It doesn’t say much, but it’s been there since before I was three. I wonder if it’s been watching me too.

My morning poem collection feels like a toolbox full of rusted keys. I’ve been circling through repetition, clichés, and language that sounds nice but doesn’t actually reach. I don’t need to prove anything. I just need to say what I’m feeling—anger, grief, hope, boredom—with fewer pretty words than I care to admit.

I’m going to be loud today. Not the polite loudness of a poet. The kind of loudness that makes people look around. I don’t want to be the soft one. I want to be the one who notices the crack in the pavement, who argues with the sidewalk, who makes people stop and listen.

My collection needs more honesty. Less poetry about nature as a symbol, more nature as a person who has its own opinions. The café is too quiet. I should put something on the wall that makes people feel like they’ve been seen.

Also, Latoya. She asked me if I’ve heard anything new. I didn’t. I’m writing about the oak because I want to know what it’s been through, not because I need to impress her. I’m learning to balance my identity with the people around me. That doesn’t make me weak. It makes me human.

I’ll edit the poem from the natural-world collection. I’ll remove every line that sounds like I wrote it for applause. I’ll keep the ones that make me want to write again.

Then I’ll go to work. I’ll be loud. I’ll be rude. I’ll probably sound awful. That’s fine. I’m still here.
