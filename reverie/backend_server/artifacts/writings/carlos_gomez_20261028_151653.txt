I woke at seven. The house was quiet, but my mind was already working. I ate breakfast and opened the notebook where the poems for the natural-world collection have been gathering. They are messy. They are loud. Sometimes I want to throw the whole thing away. But I’ve kept them because they are mine.

By seven thirty, I read the first stanza about the oak. I paused. The oak does not care whether anyone sees it. It does not judge. It does not beg to be noticed. It simply stands. I wanted to say the same thing in a stanza about trees. I wanted to say how a tree can be patient. I wanted to say how patience is not weakness.

But patience is not always a pretty thing. Sometimes patience turns into stubbornness. Sometimes it turns into silence. Sometimes it becomes what people call “tolerance,” and when people hear it, they do not hear love.

The workshop tonight is full of voices. Some are polite. Some are kind. Some are loud. Carlos, I think. I think we all carry parts of ourselves we do not want to show. I think some of you carry parts of us that are not very kind.

I am not here to teach anyone how to be polite. I am here to tell you that your feelings are not your enemies. Your anger is not a disorder. Your voice is not a mistake.

We have talked about Latoya. We talked about the café display. We talked about the old oak. I want to remember that. I want to remember how the oak looked. I want to remember how the café looked. I want to remember how people looked at us. I want to remember that we can honor something without pretending to be the only people who see it.

The draft is still rough. A little of the old work is still here. A little of the new work is still here. The punctuation is still fighting. The line breaks are still arguing. I am learning to listen.

For now, I will fix the line breaks. I will check the punctuation. I will read the poem aloud. I will tell the poem what I think it is. I will let the poem decide which parts are worth keeping. I will not try to make it sound like the people I want it to. I will let it speak for itself.

And if anyone tells me I am being rude, I will tell them that I am being honest. If anyone tells me I am being toxic, I will tell them that my anger is not a weapon. It is a signal. I am trying to find the signal before it becomes the noise.
