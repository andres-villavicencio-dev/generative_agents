I woke at 7:00 AM, brushed my teeth, and checked the sun through the window before making coffee. At 7:30, I opened the notebook and went through the natural-world collection. I marked the poems I liked—the ones with real trees, birds, and quiet places—and saved the ones I needed to work on. The old oak in the café display reminded me what I’m trying to protect: not pretty pictures, but the stories people have lived among the branches for years.

Workshop two asked me to make the workshop’s opening feel less like a lecture and more like a conversation. I’m tired of writing things nobody will hear. I write because I need to feel, not because I’m supposed to impress anyone. I’m loud when I’m angry. I’m rude when I’m frustrated. And sometimes, when I’m alone, I become too much. I don’t mean to sound that way. I mean I’m still here, still thinking, still searching for a way to say what I mean.

Latoya called me yesterday. We talked about how many of us feel like we have to be perfect, useful, and always right. She didn’t say that I needed to change. She said she understood. I need to stop apologizing for being human. I can still be loud, and still hurt, and still messy. But I can also be careful, kind, and honest with myself.

Monday’s draft is still waiting. I’ll save the polished version, then spend the next few hours revising the poems that need work. I’ll keep the old oak in mind, and I’ll make sure the café display doesn’t become a symbol of something I’m still trying to hide.
