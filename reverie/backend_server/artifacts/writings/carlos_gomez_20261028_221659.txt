The draft is alive. I wrote it three times, and the first draft was full of words that sounded like arguments. I cut the second one because I wanted to keep the fire, even if it made me uncomfortable. Now the third version is cleaner, but it still feels like someone is talking over another’s shoulder. I need to get the poem out of me and into the page without making it feel like I’m apologizing for what I feel.

What I’m trying to write is not the beauty of the world as a pretty thing. It is the world as I see it when the rain comes in at night. The trees hold together better than I do. The leaves are tired of the sun. The river remembers the stones. I do not need to convince nature of anything. It has always done the work. I have only been too busy making sure my own work is polite.

The workshop tonight was helpful, mostly because the people there listened. I heard a sentence that stopped me cold: “We are not the owners of the landscape.” It felt small, but it was honest. I thought about it again in the bathroom. I wanted to make a new poem, not a sermon.

I will not rewrite the whole thing. I will remove the repetition. I will check the unclear words. If I find a passage that feels like I am explaining what I feel, I will take it out. I do not believe every poem needs to feel like a performance. Sometimes it should feel like a person talking to themselves.

I also need to slow down when I write. I am too good at rushing, especially when the room is full of other people. A slow sentence is not weak. It is the most careful thing I can do.

For now, I will leave the unfinished part in the back. I will finish the collection tomorrow. I will write without expecting to feel understood. I will write because I want to feel something real, even if it is ugly.
