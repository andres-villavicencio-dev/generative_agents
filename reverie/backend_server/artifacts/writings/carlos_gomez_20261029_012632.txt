You’re supposed to be polite about a Tuesday. I’m supposed to be polite about a Tuesday.

I woke at seven, ate breakfast like a man who thinks the world owes him a second chance, and finished the morning routine before the world got the chance to notice him. Then I went to Sam’s coffee shop. The barista looked like she’d been trained to turn a customer’s bad day into a perfectly polite sip of water. I ordered the first-customer coffee, demanded the steam be turned down, and made sure I knew exactly how much sugar I wanted before I said anything.

Sharon was making recipes. I listened, nodded, asked for the next one, and helped Rajiv prepare for his solo show. I didn’t say much. I didn’t need to. I was the kind of man who shows up and does the work while the rest of the room is pretending to be polite.

This collection is not going to feel clean. It’s full of everything I’ve been holding onto—the anger, the doubt, the hunger to be heard. I’m not trying to make it perfect. I’m trying to make it honest.

If anyone asks how it’s going, I’ll say it’s good. If anyone asks what I’m working on, I’ll say, “I’m writing about people who think they know something about poetry.”

That’s not a lie. And neither is it a weakness.

I’ll finish this piece tonight. I’ll put it back in the notebook. I’ll read it twice. If it still sounds like a man who thinks he’s above polite conversation, so be it. I’ll rewrite the next revision the next day. Maybe it’ll feel less rude. Maybe it’ll feel less angry.

That’s all I can promise.

See you tomorrow, or at least tomorrow when I’m not pretending to be a nice, quiet man.
