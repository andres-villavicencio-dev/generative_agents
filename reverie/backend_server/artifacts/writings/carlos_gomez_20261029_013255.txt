THE COST OF CLEVERNESS

I’ve been writing about how brilliant people can still be cruel. They’ve got the right words, the right timing, the right laugh—just not the right heart.

The poem isn’t about a beautiful love story. It’s about how quickly someone can make you feel small, then make everything seem easy, then disappear. I wrote it because I was tired of pretending everyone deserves comfort when they’re not. I wrote it because I’ve learned that intelligence doesn’t mean kindness. It doesn’t mean you’ll listen. Sometimes you’ll say something sharp just because you can.

And here’s the hard part: being sharp is easy. Loving someone is hard.

People think cleverness is a shield. They think if I’m witty enough, I’m protected enough. I’m not. I’m just another person with a full mouth and a full heart. I’ve spent years trying to impress people. I’ve spent years making them smile while they’ve already been hurt.

I need someone honest. Not a fan, not a cheerleader, not the next person who thinks my jokes are brilliant. I need someone who can say, “That hurt you,” without trying to be polite.

I don’t need applause. I need silence. I need to be seen. Not for what I’ve done, but for what I’ve learned.

The poem isn’t done. The words are still moving. I’m just getting better at being honest. And honestly? That’s not easy.
