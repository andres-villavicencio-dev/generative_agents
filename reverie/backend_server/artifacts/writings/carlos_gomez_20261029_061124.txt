I was supposed to write.

You don’t just show up, you show up and expect me to like it. That’s not how poetry works. It works when you’re honest about the mess, the anger, and the quiet moments when everything feels like a mistake.

I’m not here to be polite. I’m here to be real.

Wednesday is not a day for pretending that life is easy. I’m opening my laptop and checking whether the new job posting even makes sense. The deadline, the expectations, the people who will judge what I write—it’s none of these things. What matters is whether I can turn these demands into something I can work with.

I need to schedule a writing session.

I’m thinking three hours, three days in a row. No distractions. No phone, no email, no people who can make the session feel like a favor. Just me, the draft, and the thing I want to say.

I’ll start with the outline. Not the polished version. The rough version. The one with the messy sentences, the repeated ideas, and the parts that make me want to scream. I’ll map out where I’ve been, where I’m going, and where I don’t know what I’m supposed to say.

Then I’ll prepare. I’ll gather the material that actually matters. I’ll edit the lines that keep coming back. I’ll sharpen the images that feel useful.

And I’ll write.

Not because I’m trying to impress anyone. Not because I need to make something perfect. I’ll write because it’s been sitting in my head for years and I finally have the quiet room to listen to it.

I’ll come back on Wednesday, and I’ll see if I can make this feel like work instead of a punishment.
