I woke up at 7:00, ate breakfast, and walked for an hour before writing. The city woke up before me—cars, sirens, people moving through the streets. I kept my thoughts close. They had been restless all day, but now I had something to hold onto. A poem. Not a finished masterpiece, just something honest enough to be mine.

At noon I ate lunch in the middle of the afternoon. I needed the break. Then I went back to the notebook, where the first lines had been waiting since before I was thirty-two. I did not polish every line. Sometimes I want to make noise. Sometimes I want to make silence. I wanted today to feel like one of those rhythms—the demanding, meaningful kind.

By two o’clock I was writing for an hour without checking my phone. I wrote about how the world keeps moving while I try to keep still. I wrote about the noise of Friday, the empty places, and how I wanted to fill them with something real. I did not write to impress anyone. I wrote because I had been listening for a while and finally heard myself.

At five I ate dinner. Then I watched a movie from seven to eight. The screen was quiet, and for a moment I forgot what I had been working on. But I did not leave the room. I kept going.

Around ten, I went to bed. Tomorrow I will walk again. I will write again. I will not be afraid of being loud or rude. Being human means hearing the world before it tells you what to do. It means making mistakes, turning them into art, and sometimes just standing there, tired and proud.

That is what I want for Friday: restful, connected, and mine.
