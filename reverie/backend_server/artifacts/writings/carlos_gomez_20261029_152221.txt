I didn’t start Friday the way the rest of my week usually does. I began with the morning walk.

The street was empty except for the streetlamps and the distant sound of a taxi. I kept walking until the sidewalk was almost gone. I wanted the walk to feel like something I made for myself.

By eight o’clock, my feet were sore and my mind was still moving. I wrote for an hour while moving, letting the poem follow my pace. It was not the clean, finished poem I had been writing before. It was rougher. It had the same honest edges I keep in my writing—the parts I want to say even if they make other people uncomfortable.

At noon I ate lunch and walked toward a park I had been meaning to visit. The trees held the afternoon light. I sat down, opened my notebook, and wrote about what I had missed.

Around two, I worked on a poem. I finished it before four. I wanted it to feel real, even if it was imperfect.

At five, dinner arrived and I sat down. I did not eat the whole meal. I ate slowly, watching the light move across the table.

Then I went home. I did not watch a movie. I sat on the couch and let the television turn the world around me. I watched until the screen went dark, and then I went to bed.

I did not rest well. I woke up at seven, ate at five, and wrote from eight to ten. My thoughts were full. But now they are quiet enough. I will sleep around ten. I will not try to write another poem before bed. I will let the day end.
