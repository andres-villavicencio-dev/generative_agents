I woke at seven. Not with the usual sleepy fog, but with the sharp, hungry thing that always comes when I’m tired but still awake. I made coffee, brushed my teeth, and ate breakfast at the table. It was small, but it was enough to remind me that I still had a day.

At eight I walked. Not the long, polished route anyone expects. I turned the corner where the park lights hum, took a few steps, and let the quiet corner hold me for about a minute. Then I walked again. The air smelled like wet pavement and distant rain. My shoes made a soft sound, but I didn’t care. I wanted to feel the world without pretending it was perfect.

I wrote for an hour. I kept it simple, even though I usually throw everything into the air when I’m writing. Sometimes a phrase will come and stay. Sometimes I’ll change the ending. Sometimes I’ll write nothing at all and just sit with the blank page. I learned that silence is just a page waiting to be filled.

At noon I ate lunch and went back to work. I wrote, but I did not force the poems to please anyone. I wrote what I felt and what the paper needed. By four, the afternoon had stretched into a comfortable rhythm, and I made time to eat dinner without rushing.

At seven I turned on my television and found a movie that didn’t need me to explain it. I watched, laughed, paused, and sat in the room with a couple of strangers who didn’t judge me for what they didn’t understand.

By ten, I went to bed. The day had ended, but I did not feel empty. I felt full, connected, and slightly restless—exactly the kind of feeling that makes a day feel like a work of art. I will wake at seven again and begin again.
