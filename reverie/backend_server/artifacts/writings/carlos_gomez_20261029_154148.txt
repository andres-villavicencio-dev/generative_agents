I woke up at seven, brushed my teeth, and ate the toast I’d made for myself. The day had started the way I expected: useful, noisy, full. I walked the morning path and wrote for an hour. The park was quiet once I left my notebook behind, but I kept moving. I didn’t need to keep my thoughts loud for the whole day. Sometimes, silence is just a better place to listen.

At twelve, I sat down for lunch. I didn’t fill a bowl with anything too heavy. I ate slowly, and the food felt like proof that I was still capable of eating. Afterward, I went to the studio and poured my mind into a poem. It’s not going to be the most perfect thing I’ve ever written, but it’s honest. The lines kept coming back to the same place: how I feel when everyone expects me to be easy. How easy becomes a word.

I wrote until two, then ate dinner around five. I watched the movie for an hour, but by seven, my mind was still moving. I didn’t stop for a while. I thought about Friday. I thought about the rest I wanted to create. I thought about the work that had been demanding, meaningful, and exhausting. I needed to make Friday feel different. Not perfect. Not forced. Connected.

I paused at a bench near the river. The bench was cold against my hands, but the view was quiet. I took a long breath and wrote on the page:

My day is not a sentence. It is many sentences, folded together. I keep writing because the world keeps asking me to be useful. But sometimes I want to be useful only when I’m tired.

I stood up, walked home, and put my notebook away. I didn’t need to write every thought down. Sometimes, the work is in the pause.
