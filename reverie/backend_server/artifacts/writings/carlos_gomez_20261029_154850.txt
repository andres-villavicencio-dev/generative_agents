Eight o’clock. The city breathes around me. I’m tired, but not defeated. I keep moving because nothing changes unless I change it.

I walked for an hour. Not because I needed to. Because the streets are still full of people who forgot what it means to be awake. I stopped every few minutes to write. Not because I wanted to prove anything. I wanted to feel what was inside me.

The poem I was working on didn’t feel like a poem until I wrote it down. The lines kept shifting, and sometimes I forgot who was speaking. But here, on this cold morning, the words were mine.

At lunch, I ate something that made my stomach ache. Then I ate something else that made my stomach feel full. That’s my life, too. It’s a little chaotic. It’s full. It’s purposeful.

At two, I wrote. No music, no fan, no phone. Just the paper, the ink, and the thoughts that wanted to be heard. Sometimes they’re messy. Sometimes they’re angry. Sometimes they’re beautiful. That’s the work.

At five, I ate dinner. I don’t eat because I’m hungry. I eat because I’ve been standing here all day and needed something solid.

From seven until eight, I watched a movie. Not because I wanted to escape. Because sometimes the screen is the only place I can be safe. I didn’t cry. I didn’t laugh. I noticed the people around me. I noticed what was happening.

At ten, I went to bed. The world didn’t disappear. It just held its breath.

Good night.
