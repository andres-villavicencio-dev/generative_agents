The morning air tastes like wet pavement and coffee. At seven, I wake before the sun and brush my teeth. Breakfast is a slow ritual—bread, cheese, maybe a glass of water. I don’t need to force myself to be polite. I can be loud. I can be messy. That’s the point.

At eight, I walk. Not for exercise. For the rhythm of my own thoughts. The street is empty, but I’m not. I hear the distant bus, the wind in the trees, the sound of someone leaving and someone arriving. My thoughts have been tight since Thursday night, wrapped in deadlines and unfinished lines. Today, I want to loosen the knot.

I keep walking. Every few minutes I pause and write. The pen moves without me. Sometimes the paper feels crowded with what I’m not saying. Then I let it go. Free verse, maybe. Not because I’m good at poetry, but because I want to make room for everything that’s been inside me.

I found a park across the street this morning. It’s small, but it has a bench, a bench, and another bench. I sit for a minute. I watch a bird land. I notice a crack in the sidewalk. I write about the way light changes when it hits a wet surface.

By noon, I’m hungry. I eat lunch on the sidewalk, with a paper cup and a newspaper I didn’t need. People go by, but I’m not looking at them. I’m watching my own reflection in the glass. I look tired, but I’m not done.

At two, I go back to work on the poem. It’s been sitting in my head like a heavy coat. I want to write something that feels honest, even if it feels uncomfortable. I want to make the words feel like someone else’s.

By five, I eat dinner. Then I put on my shoes and step outside. The day has begun. I can be loud today. I can be rude. I can be messy. And I can still write. I don’t need to convince myself it’s beautiful. I only need to make it mine.
