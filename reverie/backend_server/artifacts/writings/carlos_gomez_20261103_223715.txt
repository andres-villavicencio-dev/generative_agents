The trees don’t apologize for their shadows.  
They grow beneath the same gray sky,  
and still, every leaf is a different color  
—  
a bruised purple, a feverish orange,  
a silver coin lost in the rain.  

I came here to see how the grass  
knew it was alive.  
It bent like a surrender  
and opened its mouth  
to swallow the morning mist.  

But I didn’t notice the weeds  
before they turned the garden upside down.  
They had no face.  
They had only roots,  
sharp enough to pierce the earth  
and dig a home beneath it.  

The river doesn’t ask me  
what to say about its course.  
It moves through stone,  
carries away the rusted branches,  
and keeps going  
until the world looks too much like water.  

I should have learned that before  
I began to clean the river.  
But I did.  
I swept it until the mud  
looked like velvet.  
I poured my anger into the stream  
so it would never be afraid  
of being washed away.  

And when I looked back  
at the trees,  
they were smiling.  
Not kindly.  
Not cruelly.  
Just perfectly,  
unmovingly,  
always growing.
