The workshop ended before I could finish. The instructor called it “a poem about weather,” but I saw rain in the margins of the page. I wrote about it anyway.

The rain came in the willows. It made the leaves tremble, turned the river into silver ribbons, and whispered secrets to every stone in the market. I wanted to be inside the trees, where the roots hold the earth together. There, in the dark, I felt safe. There, the world did not ask questions. It simply breathed.

Outside the window, the town kept moving. People walked past The Willows Market like ghosts, their footsteps turning the pavement into a rhythm. No one looked up. No one noticed a branch shaking under a storm.

I was not supposed to write about weather. I was supposed to write about loss, about how we carry the weight of everything we have not said aloud. But I had never felt more alive.

By 5:30, I ate my dinner. The stew tasted like the river. I washed the bowl. I listened to the rain again. It was not just weather. It was proof that the world keeps turning.

By 7:00, I woke up. I made coffee and walked toward the market. The first sign of morning was the smell of wet wool and old paper. I bought a notebook.

I will write about the rain.

But I will also write about the person who made the rain. I will write about the hands that pulled the leaves down. I will write about the silence between the drops. I will write about the ugly truth of beauty.

Maybe that is my job. To take something beautiful and make it honest. To turn the quiet into a shout. To make the beautiful loud enough to be heard.

Maybe that is the only poetry I need.

And maybe, if I keep listening, the rain will teach me how to listen again.
