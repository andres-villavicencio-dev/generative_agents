I just finished that poem. It was supposed to be quiet. I wanted it to feel like the rain outside—tangled, loud, refusing to let go. But I kept turning every line into a complaint.

“Look at the leaves,” I wrote in the first draft. “They fall so easily.”

It sounded polite. It sounded like a customer asking for a refund.

I didn’t mean it. The leaves are beautiful. They are green, they are sharp, they are alive. But I wanted to write what I saw: how quickly the world makes itself useful, how pretty everything becomes when someone wants to make it beautiful.

Later, Carlos, sitting in the workshop, I heard the speaker talking about how the poem should show us not just what is there, but what it hides. I wanted to tell him that. I wanted to tell him that beauty isn’t just pretty. Beauty is fragile. It breaks when we try to control it. It changes when we notice it.

So I kept writing.

The second poem is about a bird I saw in the park. It was orange with a black tail, sitting on the branch like it was judging the world. I wanted to write about pride. I wrote, “It did not care if anyone was watching.”

I meant it. I meant, the bird cared nothing for us. It cared only for the branch.

But in the workshop, the speaker said, “Poets do not have to be honest.”

I laughed. I thought I’d been too honest.

Maybe I’ve been.

Maybe that’s why I hate being honest.

Maybe I’m just learning to be loud.

I finished the poem tonight. I made it worse. I made it about a tree falling down. About the wind. About how the world keeps falling, and how beautiful that is.

I will stop trying to make it perfect. I will let it get messy. I will let it sound like me.

I will let it make noise.
