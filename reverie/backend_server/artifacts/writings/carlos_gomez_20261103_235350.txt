I’m Carlos Gomez. At thirty-two, I’m loud enough to make a room feel like a party, rude enough to make people feel like I’m just testing the water, and toxic enough that my words sometimes get lost before they ever land. I’ve learned to hide behind a smile, but inside me, there’s something sharper than that. I’m a poet. I’m trying to write what I feel.

This collection is about the natural world—not the pretty flowers or smooth trees. It’s about the rain cracking the pavement, the wind carrying the scent of pine through cracked windows, the way a river changes its course without ever complaining. I want the poems to feel alive. I want them to move, to shift, to surprise you. That’s the thing about poetry: it shouldn’t feel like a lecture. It should sound like someone finally speaking truth to the world.

I’m not asking anyone to tell me I’m wrong. I’m asking the people around me to tell me what I’m really doing. I want honest feedback. Not praise. Not polite suggestions. Just the ugly, useful truth about what I’m writing and why I’m writing it.

The workshop is good. I’m tired of pretending my thoughts are simple. I’m tired of saying everything clearly and everyone laughing at it. I want someone who can listen without turning my raw honesty into a performance. I want someone who will tell me if a line feels forced, if a metaphor is too clever, if I’m making the reader’s heart work for me.

I may not be the easiest person to please. I may not be perfect. But I’m here to work. I’m here to write. And I’m here to ask the people who know what they’re doing to tell me exactly what needs changing. Let’s get real.
