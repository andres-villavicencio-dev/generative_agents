I came into the workshop with a notebook in my lap, coffee in my hand, and absolutely nothing to show. The room smelled like old paper, burnt toast, and nervous people. I sat down and opened my book. For a second, I thought I’d just write a paragraph about how trees grow. Then I remembered: trees don’t just grow. They struggle. They steal water. They make noise. They bend. They survive. That’s what I was supposed to do. And I was supposed to do it with some actual poetry instead of apologizing to the room.

By the time the first poem was done, I had a fire under my chest. It was ugly, jagged, and loud. One line cut through the air like a knife:

The wind does not ask for permission to steal the morning.

The teacher laughed. I laughed too. We all did. The room went quiet.

This is where I’ve been: trying to make beautiful things out of noise. I love the rain. I love the rustle of a leaf. I love the way light turns a garden into something else. But I also love how everyone looks at me like I’m holding a grenade.

I hate that.

I hate that people treat my work like a weapon. I hate that I have to explain every metaphor before anyone understands it. I hate that I have to apologize for existing.

But I keep writing. Because when I write, I feel the ground beneath me. I feel the wind. I feel the weight of everything I’ve spent years hiding from.

The workshop ended. I packed my bag, folded my poems, and left with my head full of questions, a notebook in my pocket, and a fire that wouldn’t go out. I’m tired. I’m loud. I’m still here.
