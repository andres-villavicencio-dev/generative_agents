Tuesday, February 14.

I woke at 7:00 a.m. before the alarm even had a chance to do its best. I grabbed my coffee, cracked open the window, and checked the community bulletin board. It was full of hopeful little notes from people trying to find their voices. I made a mental note to say hello to whoever was writing.

By 8:00, the workshop room was full of people trying to get their creative juices flowing. I took the lead, holding the guided meditation that would help them discover what’s been buried under the noise of the world. I told them to breathe in slowly, imagine their favorite color, and write down whatever came into their heads. I didn’t rush them. I didn’t correct their mistakes. I let their raw thoughts have a place to land.

At 9:00, I moved into the community bulletin board check. We went through the notes together, laughing at the funny ones, correcting the confusing ones, and encouraging the ones that felt incomplete. I noticed a young woman who’d been sitting quietly for a while. I didn’t ask her what she was doing. I just said, “What made you think about your writing today?”

Her eyes opened. “I wrote the wrong first line,” she said.

“Then you can keep writing the right one,” I said.

By 10:00 p.m., I wrapped up the workshop, cleaned up, and headed home. Dinner at 5:00. Cooking. Cleaning. Organizing. I made my bed. I listened to the radio. I thought about the people who had trusted me with their ideas, their fears, and their biggest dreams. I felt proud. I felt useful. I felt alive.

At 10:00, I went to bed. Tomorrow I’ll wake up at 7:00 again, carry the same energy, and make sure my friends still have space to find their own voices.
