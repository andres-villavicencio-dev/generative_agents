I woke at seven, before my brain had even forgotten how to be boring. The alarm was loud, my phone stayed on, and I had to pretend I was still awake. By seven thirty, I’d made my coffee, brushed my teeth, and stood in front of the mirror like I owned the room.

At eight, I had to be at the community bulletin board. I didn’t want to make small talk. I wanted to put something useful on the board. I wanted people to see that we actually care about our neighborhood.

The workshop group arrived with their usual attitude. “Carlos, stop talking about art,” one guy said. “Art doesn’t pay the bills.”

“I’m not here to make money,” I snapped. “I’m here to make people feel seen.”

The guy laughed. “You’re always the one giving advice.”

“I’m giving you something,” I corrected.

By eight forty, I was ready. I opened the notebook and wrote: I don’t want to be liked. I want to be heard.

We worked through our poems. I let the one about the Tuesday morning routine slip through. I let the one about how the community bulletin board could change our neighborhood come to life. I let the person who’d been quiet admit that he was worried about the next session.

At ten, I finished the final draft. I didn’t publish it. I didn’t ask for praise. I just finished it.

That’s what I’m proud of. Not the art. Not the community. Not the applause. I’m proud that I showed up. I’m proud that I didn’t leave. I’m proud that I can be rude, loud, and toxic without losing my ability to care.

I’ll go to bed at ten. I’ll sleep. I’ll wake up again tomorrow.
