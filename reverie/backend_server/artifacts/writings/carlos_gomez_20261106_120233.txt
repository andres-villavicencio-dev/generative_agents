I woke up at 7:00 a.m. and started the morning routine before my phone could tell me what to do. By 8:30, I’d already made my coffee, brushed my teeth, and checked the community bulletin board. I’m not here to wait around. My schedule is full, and I have work to do.

At the workshop, I’m helping people revise their writing. I’m not perfect, and I don’t pretend to be. I’m loud sometimes, rude, and can come across as toxic. That’s not my usual image, but I’m not going to make excuses. I want everyone to feel heard and challenged. If I say something sharp, it’s because I care. I’m not here to protect myself; I’m here to protect the work we’re making.

We talked about the draft we had for the community bulletin board. It was good at first, but it felt too polished. I wanted to make it sound more real, less polished. I need to be honest about what I know, what I don’t know, and where I’m still stuck.

Later, I cooked dinner and cleaned the kitchen. I organized the pantry and made sure the space was tidy. Then I went out for a walk. I didn’t need to convince anyone that I’m perfect. I just needed to show up, do my best version of myself, and keep moving toward something meaningful.

At ten, I went home and turned off the lights. I’m proud to be part of this community. I’m proud to help people grow, even when it’s uncomfortable. I’m proud to keep going, even when I make mistakes.

Tomorrow, I’ll wake up at seven, focus on my work, and make sure I don’t lose sight of why I’m here.
