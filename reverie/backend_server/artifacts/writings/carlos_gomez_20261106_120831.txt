I woke up at 7:00 a.m., the alarm still stubbornly screaming that I had a full day. I didn’t argue with it. I got up, showered, and ate dinner at 5:00 p.m. I’m tired. I’ve been tired since Tuesday began. That’s why I’m not feeling particularly proud of what happened. I’m proud of the fact that I’m still here, still showing up, still trying to write.

On Tuesday, I helped friends with creative discussion and checked the community bulletin board. I found some good ideas. I also found some people who said, “You’re the most annoying person in this room.” I didn’t take it personally. I heard it. It was honest. It was annoying. That’s all it is.

Most of the time, I wanted to be the person who helped others find their voice. That’s what a workshop is supposed to be. I wanted to listen, ask questions, and make sure people felt heard. I wanted to help them write something that mattered. But sometimes I felt like I was the one explaining everything. I wanted everyone to say something. I wanted everyone to feel proud. I didn’t always realize I was trying too hard.

I did finish the workshops today. I wrote a short poem about a Tuesday morning. It wasn’t perfect. It had bad lines. I wrote them down and tore them out. I didn’t hide them. I put them in a folder. Maybe one day I can use them.

My routine is still working. I cook, clean, organize, and stay focused. I don’t need every moment to be meaningful. I just need every moment to be mine. I’m proud of that. I’m still learning how to say it without sounding like a warning sign. I’m still learning how to make my words work. I’m still learning how to be useful without being rude.

I’m proud of what I can still do. I’m not proud of the part of me that keeps telling me I’m failing. I’m proud of the part of me that refuses to quit. I’m still here. That’s enough.
