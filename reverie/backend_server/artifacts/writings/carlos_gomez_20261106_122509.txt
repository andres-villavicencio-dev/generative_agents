I woke at 7:00 a.m. and didn’t look at the phone. I stood up, brushed my teeth, and opened my notebook. Today was Tuesday, February 14, and I had work to do. I had a creative discussion with a few friends, I needed to check the bulletin board, and I had workshops to complete. The schedule was messy, but I was proud to keep my promise.

At 7:30, I poured coffee and started with the workshops. I explained the first reading aloud. I handed out the materials. I explained the feedback process. I wrote the questions on the board and checked that everyone had the same copy. By 9:00, I was ready to begin.

The first exercise was simple: describe a quiet place without making it beautiful. I wrote about a kitchen in February. Rain tapped against the window. The stove hummed. The kettle whistled. I wrote about the smell of burnt sugar and cold coffee. I wrote about the way the light changed from yellow to blue. By the end, the room had gone quiet. I smiled.

Then came the feedback section. I read everyone’s pieces aloud. I corrected the grammar. I pointed out weak metaphors. I asked whether the imagery was clear. I asked whether the ideas were honest. I did not correct everyone. I did not praise everyone. I corrected what I saw.

At 10:00, I finished the workshops. I cleaned the kitchen. I made dinner. I organized the bulletin board. At 11:00, I ate dinner. By 12:00, I had time to rest. At 1:00, I read my own writing. I wrote about a poem I had kept unfinished. I wrote about how the rain made me feel. I wrote about the people around me. I wrote about what I wanted to say next.

I felt proud. I felt tired, but not defeated. I had helped friends with a creative discussion. I had checked the bulletin board. I had completed the workshops. I had cooked, cleaned, and organized. I had written. I was not perfect. I had made mistakes. And I had made them worth fixing.

By 5:00 p.m., I ate dinner. By 6:00, I read my poem aloud. By 7:00, I went to bed. Tomorrow I will wake up again. Tomorrow I will write. Tomorrow I will help my friends. Tomorrow I will make sure every workshop participant received the materials and feedback they needed. Tomorrow I will be loud, rude, and toxic enough to make people laugh, even if I didn’t mean to. Tomorrow I will be focused.
