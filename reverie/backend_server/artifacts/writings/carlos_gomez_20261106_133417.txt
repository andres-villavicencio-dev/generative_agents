Tuesday, February 14, 7:00 a.m.

I woke up sharp and loud, which is exactly what I wanted. The alarm screamed, I stared at the ceiling, and then I started the day. By 7:30, I’d finished breakfast, washed the dishes, and made the coffee strong enough to wake anyone who wanted it.

Today is full. I have workshops to lead, a bulletin board to check, friends waiting for a creative discussion, and everything else needed to keep this community moving. I’m proud of it. Not because I’m perfect. Because I’m here, and I’m still trying to make something real.

At home, I’m careful with the kitchen. I wash the pots, I measure the ingredients, and I cook something warm enough to eat. Then I clean. Then I organize. It feels like a chore, and sometimes it feels like a punishment, but I enjoy it. There is a strange comfort in making something useful for others, whether it’s a bulletin board, a poem, or a bowl of soup.

I wrote yesterday about how feelings can feel like a storm. I wrote that they are difficult to control, that they can drown your thoughts, and that sometimes it is better to let them come out. But I also wrote that they are not monsters. They are proof that you care. That you have been hurt, loved, surprised, and moved.

It makes the writing feel heavy, doesn’t it? I keep thinking about the feeling of being overwhelmed. It feels like standing on a hill with nowhere to look, hearing voices everywhere, and not knowing which one is yours. I’m not always sure if I am the one having the feelings or if I am merely carrying them.

I’ve learned to keep writing, even when it hurts. I’ve learned to show up, even when I’m angry or tired. And today, I’m doing it again.

I’m ready for the day. I’m ready to help. I’m ready to make something worth remembering.
