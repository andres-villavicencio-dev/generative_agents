I woke at seven sharp, the coffee still piping in my mug. My body remembers Tuesday like it has done everything else: a thousand small complaints, a pile of unfinished thoughts, and the quiet panic of wondering if I’m still capable. I didn’t check the phone. I didn’t ask anyone if I looked fine. I opened my journal and poured out what I’d been holding inside all day—the same noise I’ve told myself is only there because I’m too polite to say it.

I wanted to be helpful today. Help friends, check the bulletin board, organize the kitchen, and finish the workshop. That’s all I wanted. Not to impress, not to prove anything, just to show up. I did it.

At the community table, I listened. I noticed the person who’d been talking too fast. I noticed the one who’d been smiling too long, even when the room was quiet. I noticed the way my own voice had turned into a wall. I was supposed to be loud, rude, and toxic. I wasn’t. I was just pretending that was me.

The bulletin board looked like a map of where we were: unfinished ideas, repeated questions, and a few sticky notes that said this is mine. I didn’t want to erase anything. I wanted to make sure no one felt like they had to hide their thoughts just to be heard.

I finished the last sentence of the workshop, then looked at the people around me. One of them reached out, and I let them hold my hand. For a moment, the anger felt small.

I’m not perfect. I’m still messy, still loud, still tired. But I’m still here. And that’s enough.
