The park was a painting I didn’t want to see.

Green everywhere, but the green was wrong. It was too bright, too loud, like someone had painted the grass in a blender. The trees stood straight like soldiers, and every branch cracked open a sky that looked suspiciously like a storm waiting to happen. I stood too long. My phone was too bright. My eyes were too tired. I needed to leave.

I wandered past a fountain where the water sang a song about losing. It sang about water flowing away from everything that was holding it down. It sang about rivers, storms, and the idea that sometimes you have to break free to move forward. I stopped to listen.

The leaves were turning. One by one, the old green began to peel away, revealing the brown beneath. I watched a beetle crawl across the pavement. It looked like a little black dragon, moving with a purpose no human could understand. It didn’t care about my anger. It didn’t care about my noise. It just moved.

I walked further. The path narrowed into a green tunnel. Underneath, the grass felt like a blanket. The birds were singing in a language I didn’t know, but I heard the rhythm of their wings. They were laughing. They were running. They were alive.

I sat on the edge of a quiet creek and looked down. The water reflected the sky, the trees, the clouds, and a version of me I didn’t recognize. I was loud. I was rude. I was always looking for something to say. But the creek said nothing. It just kept moving, carrying everything away so the river could carry it all.

I took a breath. I opened my mouth.

I was ready to write.

The first line had to be honest. Not pretty. Not polite. Just true.
