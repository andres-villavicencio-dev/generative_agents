The morning air smells like wet pavement and pine. I wake at seven, the world still holding its breath. I do not rush. I need the quiet to settle before the noise can take hold.

By eight, I have made the coffee and checked the community bulletin board. It is full of notices, but beneath the paper lies a familiar rhythm: announcements, notices, reminders. I have been applying for a posting here several times, and I am still not sure whether the board is a door or a wall. Perhaps that is the point. Sometimes the world waits for us to notice it.

Around five, I eat dinner with the family. I talk about the rain, the way the leaves turn, and the kind of silence that comes after a storm. We laugh at each other, and I make a joke about being a poet who writes about trees while pretending to care about who gets a job. I am loud. I am rude. That is not what I am trying to prove. I am trying to speak without losing myself.

I have been writing about the natural world for months. I collect images of rivers, storms, and birds. I write about roots, storms, and the way a tree survives. I want to show you the parts I have not yet learned to see. But I am learning that beauty does not mean everything is pretty. A broken branch is not a crime. A dead leaf is not a failure. Sometimes what we call “nature” is simply a place where we stop trying to control everything.

I have also been working on a new collection. It is still raw. Sometimes my drafts are angry, sometimes they are tired. That is okay. I want the poems to feel honest, not polished into a mask. I want you to feel the wind, the rain, the ache of a bad day, and the stubborn hope that keeps returning.

I also have a few notes from the workshop. I should read them carefully. I should ask questions. I do not want to repeat what I was taught, only what I learned for myself.

And I have three things to do Tuesday. I will maintain my routine. I will eat and drink well. I will check the bulletin board. I will apply carefully. I will confirm my availability. And I will keep writing. Even when the words feel heavy. Even when the world feels loud. Even when I cannot be polite.

Poetry is not a place to hide. It is a place to return to. And for that, I am grateful.
