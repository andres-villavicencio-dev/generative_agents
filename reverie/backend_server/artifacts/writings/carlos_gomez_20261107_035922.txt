I woke at seven and didn’t make myself any better for it. Coffee was hot, but my hands were shaking. I ate the toast without looking at the plate, drank a glass of water, and checked the bulletin board while everyone else was already gone. The post was for Tuesday, February 14. I applied carefully, read every detail, and confirmed that I had my availability. I didn’t check what nobody wanted. I didn’t answer the phone. I did what the list asked.

I’m writing poems about the natural world. Trees, storms, rivers, light breaking through the leaves. I like them. I think I like them. I’m also working on the workshop collection. I want to find a poem that feels honest and dangerous at the same time. I want it to feel like a secret I’ve kept for a long time.

By the way, I’m not going to sleep until ten. That’s the point. You can’t trust your schedule if it’s already been broken.

I’m going to finish this morning before I decide what to write for the day. And I’m going to be loud. I’m going to be rude. I’m going to make sure no one thinks I’m too easy.

Next morning: seven. Morning routine. Breakfast. Bulletin board. Application. Availability. Poetry. No excuses.
