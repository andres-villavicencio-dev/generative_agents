Tuesday, February 14—my life is a little messy, but I’m still standing.

I woke at seven, and the first thing I did was turn off the alarm. I don’t need to hear it before I get up; if the world is loud enough, I can still hear the sunrise. At seven-thirty, I ate my breakfast. Water, toast, maybe a little fruit. Something real. No coffee until I’m hungry again. No caffeine before the board. I want my brain to work like a garden: steady, not manic.

At eight, I checked the community bulletin board. Not because everyone should be there, but because people are there to see what’s happening. If I’m not among them, I’ll notice something I missed. If I’m among them, I can’t let that be the only way I feel. I applied to the posting. I did the application carefully. If I don’t get it, then I will not be the one to say it was rejected. I will not let a rejection become the reason I stop listening.

Around five, I ate dinner. No screen. No music. Just conversation, whatever it was.

I’m working on a new poetry collection, one that tries to find beauty in places I thought were broken. The natural world is my partner. Trees are patient. Rivers are relentless. The wind does not apologize for blowing. Sometimes I write about storms and wonder if they’re angry, and sometimes I wonder if they’re just doing what they’ve always done.

I also have a workshop on Tuesday. I’m not sure what I expect, but I’m prepared to listen. Listening is a skill. It’s not the same as speaking. It’s different than being loud. It’s being present without pretending everything is perfect.

Tonight, I will save my final work. I will not change it just because another person made a suggestion. I will not rewrite my notes just because I feel like I’m being honest. I will arrange them for tomorrow, not because the morning is easy, but because tomorrow is not guaranteed.

I’m tired. I’m tired of pretending I’m okay. But I’m still here. I’m still writing. And I’m still here to see what tomorrow has to offer.
