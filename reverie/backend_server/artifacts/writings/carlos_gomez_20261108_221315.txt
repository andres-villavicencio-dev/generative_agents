My writing room is a shrine to failure. There’s a stack of notebooks, a mug that still smells like cold coffee, and a mirror that keeps showing me the same tired eyes. I’ve been here for four weeks, and honestly? I’m starting to think the room is judging me.

Tuesday morning was supposed to be productive. I woke up at 7:00, ate breakfast, and made my way to the community food event. The walk was good. The coffee with friends was better. But the collection was just… waiting. I wrote one more poem. Then another. Nothing new came out. The voice inside me was still loud, still rude, still screaming about everything I wanted to say but couldn’t put into words.

I spent the afternoon reviewing the manuscript. I found passages that sounded like me. I found the ones I’d been trying to fix. I kept rewriting until my hands were sore. By the time I went home, the collection was still incomplete, and I was more angry than I realized.

I need to stop trying to turn everything into something perfect. I need to let it be messy. I need to admit that I’m still learning, still stumbling, still afraid that whatever I write is going to make a fool of me.

Maybe I should finish the book. Maybe I should walk again. Maybe I should just sit here for an hour and not try to make the next poem look like I’m a poet.

I’ll eat dinner at five. I’ll go to bed at ten. And tomorrow, I’ll drink coffee with some people who actually listen.
