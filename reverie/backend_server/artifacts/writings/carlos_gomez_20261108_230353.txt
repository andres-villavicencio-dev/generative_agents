Tuesday, February 14.

The workshop room smelled of old paper, stale coffee, and the kind of nervous laughter that makes people laugh even when they’re pretending not to care. I sat in my chair, pencil in hand, and told them I was done with the book. The room went quiet. I kept smiling, though I was pretty sure the room was laughing harder than me.

I finished the first draft before breakfast. Maybe that’s why I’m still here: I don’t need permission to say what I want to say. I don’t need the workshop to validate the things I’ve been saying for years.

The poem I’m writing is about a woman who keeps saying “I’m okay” while everyone around her is screaming. I let her finish. I let the whole room finish.

It’s not a great poem. It feels like a confession. It could be a song. It could be a warning. But it’s honest enough.

I’m not asking you to make me feel better. I’m asking you to listen long enough to see that I’m not trying to make you believe I’m perfect. I just want you to notice that I’m trying.

By the time I walked out of the room, I had lost the heat in my chest. I had also found a new kind of quiet.

The community food, the walk, the coffee with friends—none of it had been set in stone. I could still change my schedule. I could still pause.

Sometimes I want to be loud. Sometimes I want to be rude. Sometimes I want to be toxic.

Today, I’m just trying to be human. That’s enough.
