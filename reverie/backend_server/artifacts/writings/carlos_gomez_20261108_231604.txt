I woke at seven, didn’t shower, and ate breakfast around seven-thirty. That was Tuesday. I’m thirty-two, and Tuesday feels like Tuesday is a good time to make mistakes.

I’m in the workshop now, with the other poets and writers. They keep saying, “Your voice is loud,” when I keep talking over everything, making jokes that turn people away, and writing lines that sound like I’m trying too hard to be interesting. I don’t need anyone’s approval to finish the collection. I need to finish it because I don’t want to waste another night of the same tired, unfinished feeling.

They taught me that clarity is dangerous. I used to think every poem had to make a point, but I’m learning that a poem can have no point and still be honest. I was too busy trying to prove something instead of letting the words do what they were made to do.

I’m supposed to be eating dinner at five and walk home. Not today. I’m here to read, listen, and maybe laugh when someone finally says something nice. The room is full of people who think poetry is about being right. I’m not here to be right. I’m here to be awake.

My goal is simple: write something that doesn’t try too hard. Something that feels real, messy, and mine. I’m going to keep turning pages until I can read what I wrote without having to explain it. I’m going to keep writing until the work feels like a person, not a performance. And I’m going to keep showing up, even when I’m rude, even when I’m tired, even when Tuesday is dragging.

One day, maybe I’ll be quiet. One day, maybe the words will finally feel like a promise instead of a demand. Until then, I’ll keep writing, keep talking loud, and keep hoping someone finally understands what I’m trying to say.
