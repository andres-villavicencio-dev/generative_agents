I’m Carlos. I’m 32 and I’m loud. I’m rude. I’m toxic, but I’m also poetic. I want to write about how the world makes my life feel—beautiful, messy, dangerous, like a good song that keeps getting louder.

I’m not here to sound like the person who thinks everything should be easy. I’m here to learn. I’m here to see what poets can do with words and how they can make their inner thoughts feel real. I’m also here to hear about what Hobbs Cafe is trying to teach. The workshop wants us to stop writing what we know and start writing what we feel. That sounds like a lot for me.

The instructor opened by talking about the beauty of the natural world. I stopped to listen. He said trees keep secrets, birds carry songs through seasons, and rain washes away the dirt of our mistakes. I nodded. I should have said something different. I should have said, “I’m scared of storms.”

I’ve been writing about trees for years. I’ve written about leaves turning, roots digging, wind pushing through branches. But I’ve never really written about the parts of me that make me want to shout. I’ve always been careful. I’ve always been the person who knows when to keep quiet.

Tonight, I’m going to try something new. I’m going to write about how I feel when I’m alone. I’m going to write about the noise in my head, the sharp thoughts, the things I’m afraid will get out. I want to hear what the workshop wants me to say. I want to hear myself say something useful.

I’m not here to make you laugh. I’m here to make you listen. And I’m not here to say I’m perfect. I’m here to say that I’m still learning.
