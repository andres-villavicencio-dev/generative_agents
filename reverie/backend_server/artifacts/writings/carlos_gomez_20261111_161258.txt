The Mountain Between Us

I woke before sunrise, my mouth still wet from coffee. The air smelled like damp pine and the iron of a pipe burning in the morning. I didn’t want to get up. I wanted the mountain to be warm enough to make me feel like I’d finally found a place where I didn’t have to explain myself.

The workshop had been nice. The room smelled like lemon polish and old paper. The poet next to me was nervous, tapping his pen against his knee. I didn’t care. I care about the way the leaves turn gold in the winter sun, the way a river makes a small sound before it becomes a roar. I want to write things that make people stop, just for a second.

I looked at my notebook. “The mountain between us” is still the title. It feels like a promise I haven’t fulfilled.

I found the line I wanted to rewrite:

He stood there, taller than the trees, holding the sky like a lantern.

Not quite. He was holding it like a piece of paper. I’d meant to say he was carrying the world across his shoulders without breaking it. Maybe the lantern was too fragile. Maybe the mountain was too heavy.

I wrote another draft. The mountain moved. It had teeth. It had a voice. It had a secret it kept from me.

On the way home, I passed a streetlamp flickering against the dark. I almost whispered, I’m not afraid of the dark, I’m afraid of the light.

I turned back toward the cafe. The coffee was cold. The air smelled like rain. And for the first time in weeks, the mountain didn’t feel like a warning.

It felt like home.
