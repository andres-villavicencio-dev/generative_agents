I didn’t have a choice. I just kept turning the page, rewriting, polishing, hoping the words would finally fit.

The workshop ended. The instructor handed back my revised draft and said, “You’re close, Carlos. But you’re too close to what you want to say. You’re letting the fire get out of control.”

I didn’t argue. I just laughed and kept writing.

I’ve been working on a collection about nature. I’ve been writing about rain, moss, rivers, birds, and the quiet way leaves open their brown arms to the sun. I wanted the poems to feel alive. I wanted them to have a little anger, a little heat, a little stubbornness.

So now I’m angry at the workshop.

I’m angry that they’re telling me what is missing. I’m angry that I’m trying to sound like a poet while still being a loud, rude, toxic creature. I’m angry that I keep editing my own mistakes like if they were made by someone else.

The instructor’s feedback made me feel stupid. I had my poems down before I could even remember them. I could write about storms and trees without making them look like a weather forecast. I could let the words breathe.

But now I’m writing them anyway.

I’m writing about how the world can be beautiful if you look at it with a little care. I’m writing about the way a fallen branch can become a poem. I’m writing about the way anger can make everything sharper. I’m writing about the fact that I’m still messy. I’m still loud. I’m still rude. And yet, I’m still here.

Maybe that’s the point. Maybe the point of poetry isn’t to make everything perfect. Maybe the point is to show up, even when you’re angry. Even when your hands are shaking. Even when you’re still a little toxic.

I’m done pretending everything is clean. I’m done pretending I don’t want to hurt people. I’m done pretending I don’t want to be heard.

I’m done writing about the beauty of the natural world without first admitting that I can’t handle it.

So here it is. The revised draft. The one that includes everything.

Sometimes I want to throw it away. Sometimes I want to write something better. Sometimes I just want to know that I’m allowed to be bad.

And maybe, just maybe, that’s enough.
