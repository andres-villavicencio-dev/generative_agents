I woke up at seven and ate my toast before the sun could even light the kitchen. My phone was buzzing, another notification demanding I be useful. By five, dinner was already halfway through, and I was already thinking about how the air smelled like rain.

I’m thirty-two, and I have a lot to say, mostly about how the world keeps trying to make me polite. I’ve been here longer than the coffee shop, longer than the coffee. I’ve watched poets come and go, some beautiful, some brilliant, some just loud enough to make you forget what they’re saying. I’ve learned that words are weapons. Some are useless. Others cut deeper.

My latest collection is about nature. Trees. Storms. Roots. Water. I wanted to show that these things don’t care who you are. They don’t ask you to apologize for coming home. They don’t judge your mistakes, your silence, the way you laugh when you’re nervous. They just grow.

Sometimes I think I’m not as ugly as I pretend to be. Maybe the problem isn’t that I have too many opinions. Maybe it’s that I keep pretending I’m gentle when I’m actually just tired of being polite. I’m loud. I’m rude. Sometimes I’m toxic. But that’s the only version of me I know.

I don’t need permission to feel. I don’t need to apologize for my anger, my laughter, my stupid doubts. I just need to write them down. Not to make them smaller. Not to make them louder. To make them real.

I keep writing. I keep showing up. I keep proving that I can be messy, imperfect, and still worth listening to. That’s enough.
