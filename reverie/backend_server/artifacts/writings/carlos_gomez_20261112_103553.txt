February has been turning into a fever, and I’m waking up to that same fever. My hands are shaking, my throat feels like a stone, and the coffee in the kitchen is cold enough to test whether I’m still human. Still, I keep checking my pantry. Still, I keep thinking about what I’m supposed to say.

Tuesday, Valentine’s. I’m supposed to write a poem about nature, about flowers and rain and trees, and maybe make it sound a little romantic. But I’m not writing for anyone in particular. I’m not writing to make someone feel better, or to prove that I know how to be nice. I’m writing because I’ve been holding everything in my head, and I need something to burn.

So I’ll begin with the storm. I’ll make it sound angry, messy, loud, and real. No pretty little promises about love. I’ll describe how the wind knocks against the window, how the leaves keep talking to each other, how the rain washes the streets clean but still makes people feel smaller. I’ll write about how I’m tired, how I’m angry, and how I’m still here, breathing.

That’s the poem I need. Not a confession. Not a letter. Not some polished speech for Giorgio Rossi or Latoya Williams. Just enough truth to make me feel like I’m alive.

When I read it later, I may not sound perfect. I may still sound like I’m trying to be charming. But I’m trying. And sometimes I’m lucky enough to hear the words I’ve been practicing.

Good. Let’s get this started.
