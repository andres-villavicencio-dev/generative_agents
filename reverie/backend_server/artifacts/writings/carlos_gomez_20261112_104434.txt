Tuesday, February 14. The calendar has been reminding me every day that I’m supposed to be in love. I’m not. I’m supposed to be here, making a poem, checking the pantry, and pretending I’m not already drowning in unpaid bills and missed shifts.

The second stanza should feel alive. The river should feel loud. I want to let the storm go without apologizing for the thunder.

You make the whole world feel like a slow apology. I want a storm where the rain doesn’t ask for permission.

You make silence feel like a waiting room. I want a silence that is loud enough to make the waiting room disappear.

You make every sunrise feel like someone else’s victory. I want a sunrise that belongs to me, even if it’s just a gray, broken one.

The poem is still unfinished. The second stanza needs more fire.

You say love is a gentle thing. I say love is a storm I cannot stop. I say love is everything I keep trying to ignore because it will make me look like a fool.

I am not ready to love you. I am ready to own the anger, the hunger, and the terrible, beautiful need to change.

Tonight I will check the shopping list. Tonight I will fill the pantry. Tonight I will make a Valentine’s party that isn’t the polite version of a Valentine’s party.

I am not a poet who waits for the right person to make me feel. I am a poet who makes the world feel like enough, even when it does not have enough for me.

If this poem is published, let it be the one that scares people. Let it be the one that makes them say, “I never thought I’d be angry about the world.”

I don’t want a poem about being loved. I want a poem about being brave enough to be angry. And if I am not brave enough, I will make it loud enough to hear myself.
