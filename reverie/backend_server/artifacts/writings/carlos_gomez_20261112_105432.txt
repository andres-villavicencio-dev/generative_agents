Tuesday is Valentine’s Day. That means flowers, chocolate, wine, and probably a whole lot of arguing with the world. I woke at seven, ate something cold, and made sure the poem was ready for publication.

The first draft was beautiful. Nature—oaks, rain, river stones, birds, shadows—sat beneath everything. I wanted the poem to feel alive, to breathe with the seasons. But it was too soft. Too polite. It sounded like someone trying to make every leaf say the right thing.

I need to stop pretending everything is graceful. I am not a garden. I am Carlos Gomez, thirty-two, loud, sometimes rude, and still capable of noticing a beetle hiding behind a stone. I need the poem to carry that. I need anger in it. I need the way the world can feel unfair, messy, and utterly unforgettable.

I also need the rhythm to move faster. My second stanza should not be a lullaby. It should be a question, a warning, a demand. The last stanza must end with purpose, not apology.

I checked the pantry. Bread is still in the door. The tomatoes in the jar are bright and hungry. I bought enough wine for one bad night. I did not buy a whole bouquet because I want people to think I am perfect. I bought the things I need.

I will refine the poem. I will make it rougher. I will let it hurt. Then I will send it to Giorgio and Latoya. I will carry forward any unpaid balance and any missed shift. I will not wait for full-time confirmation before I make my peace with the world.

Tomorrow, I will wake at seven. I will eat dinner at five. I will write until my hands bleed. I will listen to the rain, and I will not apologize for the storm.
