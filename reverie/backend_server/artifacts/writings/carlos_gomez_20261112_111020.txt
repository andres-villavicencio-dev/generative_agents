Tuesday. Valentine’s Day. I need a poem that sounds like something I could actually write.

I’ve been staring at my notebook for an hour. The nature part is easy: rain, rain, rain, the storm outside, the leaves turning, the flowers opening. But it sounds too pretty. Too romantic. Too like a magazine.

I should say: rain is heavy, storms are angry, and love is messy. I should describe the cold air, the wet pavement, the way the light changes when the sky opens. I should mention the noise of the world and the quiet inside me.

I could write about how I used to be easy. I used to smile at everything. I used to think I was perfect. Now I’m loud. I’m rude. I’m angry, and I’m not saying it politely.

That poem needs to feel real. Not clean. Not polished. It needs the grit of someone who’s been through a lot, but it still has a pulse. I want the words to feel like a person speaking to another person—not a polished speech, but something someone might say in their own voice.

I need the ending to land. Not a perfect “you are the storm inside me.” Something stronger. Something honest.

I’m checking my pantry. The flour is gone. The sugar is still there. The salt is getting low. I need to cook something today. Something warm. Something stupid enough to love.

I’m not going to the cafe yet. I’ve got unpaid balances, missed shifts, and maybe Giorgio Rossi will give me a second chance. I’m carrying forward the mess, though. I’m carrying the unfinished application, the unpaid balance, the missed shifts. I’m carrying everything. And I’m still writing.

I need to stop apologizing for being loud. I need to stop pretending I care about everything I write. I need to write what I feel. I need to write what I’m angry about. I need to write what I’m still hoping for.

Valentine’s Day is a celebration. I should celebrate my anger. I should celebrate the mess. I should celebrate the part of me that still believes love can change everything.

Let’s see if that poem reads better.
