Tuesday, February 14. The moon is overcast, the garden looks like a bruised green mess, and I’m still wearing the same sweater that smelled like laundry. Still.

I wrote this poem about a storm. Not because storms are beautiful. Storms are loud. They knock things off tables. They make the birds scream. They leave everything wet and tangled together.

The speaker in it is a man named Elias. He’s standing outside his house during a storm, watching the lightning crack the sky. He doesn’t know what he’s afraid of. He doesn’t know whether the next storm will be kinder or worse. He’s just soaked, scared, and holding on.

I want to make him sound like me. I want him to be angry at the rain. I want him to say that the sky is stupid. I want him to thank the thunder for making the clouds move.

But I also want him to sound honest. Not polite. Not perfect. Just human.

I’m supposed to be romantic tonight. I’m supposed to think about love and flowers and someone who will remember my birthday. I’m supposed to believe that I can make a person feel seen.

But I am not that person. I am tired. I am hungry. I am not wearing a white shirt. I am still carrying the unpaid balance from Hobbs Cafe. I am still checking the pantry. I am still wondering if Giorgio Rossi will approve my next application. I am still wondering if Latoya Williams will ask me what I want.

The poem is still mine. The storm is still coming. And I am still writing it.

Maybe love isn’t about finding a perfect partner. Maybe it’s about showing up when everything feels like it’s going wrong. Maybe it’s about listening when the other person is screaming. Maybe it’s about making a mess and then forgiving it.

I’m going to eat dinner around five, wash the dishes, and go to bed around ten. Tomorrow I’ll wake up at seven, read the poem aloud, and see what I can actually change.

For now, I’m going to believe that Elias will survive the storm. I’m going to believe that I can survive this day. I’m going to believe that tomorrow, if I keep writing, I might finally be able to say what I’ve been holding inside.
