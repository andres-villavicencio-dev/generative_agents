Tuesday Valentine’s poem. Nature. Quiet. Not pretty. Just honest.

They say Valentine’s means love. I say it means someone standing outside the window, wondering what’s coming. The flowers are too bright. The birds are too loud. I’m here. I’m tired. And I’m not buying anyone anything unless they give me space to say I’m tired.

I woke at seven. Got my coffee. Shopped. Checked what’s gone bad in the pantry. Confirmed with Giorgio that my next application is still moving. Don’t ask me when I’ll get a full-time job at Hobbs Cafe. I’m already carrying the unpaid bills. I’m already carrying the missed shifts. I’m carrying the rest of me.

The poem is ready. It doesn’t need to be pretty. It just needs to be true.

I love the way rain settles on the pavement.

I love how a single petal stays upright for a while, then falls quietly.

I love how the night doesn’t give up. It keeps coming.

I love how I can be angry and still hold a cup of tea.

I love how I can be rude, and still care.

I love how I can be loud, and still listen.

I love how I can love without pretending to be soft.

I love the storm outside. I love the rain. I love the fact that it keeps falling.

I love you. Not the perfect version. The messy, tired, real one.

Tonight. I’ll be here. I’ll eat. I’ll drink. I’ll say what I’m thinking.

No speeches. No pretty promises. Just the truth.

And if anyone asks why I’m awake at seven in the morning, I’ll say: I’m awake because I’m finally tired enough to notice that I’m alive.
