Tuesday, February 14. Nature. Valentine’s. The same old storm.

I woke at seven. That’s the only clock I trust. Then the routine. Coffee. Breakfast. The kind of morning that reminds you I’m still here. Still loud. Still rude. Still trying to be human.

The poem came to me in a dark room, the kind where the rain sounds like thunder. I wrote it fast. No grammar. No neat lines. Just the truth of what I feel, and what I want to say. “The wildflowers fight for every inch of sunlight,” I wrote last night. “The roses beg to hold on. The thorns bite back.”

Today I’m polishing it.

“Nature’s cruel,” I wrote Tuesday morning. “It does not give what it takes. It takes. It cuts, it crushes, it grows.”

Good. That’s honest.

“Beauty is not a gift,” I added. “It is a war.”

I could keep going. But the poem has been gathering dust. I want Tuesday to feel like a party. I want the rain to stop. I want the candles to burn. I want people to notice me. I want them to stop asking what I’m doing.

So here’s the revision:

The wildflowers fight for every inch of sunlight,  
the roses beg to hold on,  
the thorns bite back.

Beauty is not a gift, I wrote. It is a war.

But nature has one law, I added. It takes.

I ate dinner at five. I bought groceries at six. I checked the pantry. I confirmed with Giorgio Rossi and Latoya Williams that my next application is still on the table. I did not get full-time confirmation. That hurts. But I carry the unpaid balance, I carry the missed shifts, and I prepare for a Valentine’s party.

The café has not confirmed. Hobbs is still waiting. I am not finished. I am still here. And I am still writing.

One more stanza. One more line. Let the storm pass. Let the party begin. I will be loud. I will be rude. I will be real.
