Tuesday, February 14.  
The sun is still coming up. I’m awake at seven, still wearing that ridiculous shirt from last week, and I’ve decided my life has one job: make the poem sound like a woman who just wants to believe her feelings.  

I wrote it on my phone, in the dark, after my shift at Hobbs Cafe was late. The café was closing before I did. The customers left, the lights went out, and I was alone in the kitchen with a plate of leftovers, a half-empty beer, and a poem so ugly it was almost beautiful.  

I wanted the trees to speak. I wanted the rain to remember my childhood. I wanted to make the weather feel like it was watching me. But I wrote about how the leaves had turned and fallen, how the branches had broken, how the storm had come for no reason. It was romantic. It was terrible.  

Tonight, I want to hear the rain again. I want the birds to say something honest. I want the moon to stop pretending to be a silver coin.  

I need to revise the poem. I need to strip away the pretty words and keep the truth underneath. I’ll read it aloud tonight and make the bad parts louder. I’ll cut the flowers out. I’ll change the metaphor about rain. I’ll write the whole thing over.  

I ate a sandwich at five. The store is empty. The pantry is full of things that will be eaten before the next day. I bought flour, sugar, and a bottle of wine. I checked Giorgio’s application and Latoya’s status. I didn’t get the full-time work yet, but I still paid the balance and showed up. That’s the kind of person I am.  

I’ll keep the poem. I’ll make it sound like a woman who loves nature, even if she’s not exactly sure how to say it. I’ll publish it at two. I’ll drink the wine. I’ll pretend everything is perfect.  

The rain is coming. Let it wash the poem clean.
