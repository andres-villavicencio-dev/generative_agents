The workshop room smelled of old paper and wet chalk. I sat across from the facilitator, notebook open, pen in my hand, and the first exercise already on my mind.

“Name a thing that makes you feel alive,” he said.

A loud laugh slipped out of me. The facilitator smiled at me.

“You’re too loud, Carlos,” he called.

I didn’t care. I was Carlos Gomez, loud, rude, toxic, and still capable of beautiful poetry.

We moved into the first exercise. I wrote down everything I saw: the way the sunlight caught the dust, the rustle of a leaf, the rhythm of rain on a windowpane. Then I wrote down the things that made me hate everything: traffic, people who didn’t listen, the way strangers made me feel small.

I was supposed to be doing that. But the exercise was too easy.

“Let’s try something harder,” he said.

I stopped writing. I stared at the page.

What if I wrote about the things I loved without pretending they were easy to find? What if I wrote about my own noise, my own anger, my own hunger to be seen?

I took a deep breath. I wrote the first page aloud. It sounded like everything I was angry about. I kept writing.

“Okay,” the facilitator said, smiling.

I kept writing. The room grew quiet. Outside, the wind moved through the trees, carrying the scent of wet earth. I was tired, yes. But I was also alive.

At ten, I ate dinner. At five, I went to bed.

Tomorrow, I’ll finish the first draft. Maybe I’ll make it louder. Maybe I’ll make it rude. Maybe I’ll make it beautiful.

That’s all I know.
