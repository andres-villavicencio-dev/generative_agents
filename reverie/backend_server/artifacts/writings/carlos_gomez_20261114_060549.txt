I came in here thinking I’d make some pretty poetry. The workshop said, “Write what you hear.” I thought that sounded like advice for people who couldn’t hear themselves. By the second page, I was shouting through every single line: Oh, this mountain is so angry! The river is so mean! The world is a giant, ugly, beautiful machine!

I’m thirty-two, loud, and probably rude. I don’t think that matters to anyone. I just believe that if you want to say something important, you should be loud enough to be heard.

The exercises made me feel stupid. I wanted to write about how the rain makes everything feel wet. I wanted to write about how the wind tastes like copper and old paper. I wanted to write about how the trees are pretending to be dignified when they’re just moving branches in the wind.

Then I wrote about the squirrel.

It was the best poem.

Not because it was beautiful. Because it was honest.

I saw a squirrel dart through the oak at dawn, pulling one of its nuts out of the ground. It didn’t look at me. It didn’t apologize. It didn’t say, I’m sorry my tail is messy. It just kept moving.

That’s what I’ve been trying to write all week. Not pretty poetry. Not pretty lines. Just the squirrel. The squirrel pulling its nut from the ground. The squirrel looking over its shoulder. The squirrel letting me know that sometimes the best way to say something is to show something else.

I’m not going to be loud for anyone else. I’m not going to make everyone feel small. But I’m going to finish this poem. I’m going to take it to the next workshop. Maybe then I’ll be heard.
