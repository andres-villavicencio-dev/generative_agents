I came in today with the same tired face I’ve had for years: shoulders tight, head down, trying not to sound like I’m judging the workshop. I’m supposed to be learning. I’m supposed to hear what I can still hear.

I didn’t ask. I just sat there, watching the other people talk, and I felt like I was being forced into a conversation I wasn’t invited to. That’s what I’ve always been: someone who thinks everyone else’s words are wrong, someone who reads poetry like a sermon and makes a joke when someone is being serious.

The workshop gave me a lot to work with. I noticed the rhythm of a poem I wrote yesterday—so perfect, so sharp, so full of everything I wanted to say and nothing I could carry. I wanted to keep it. I wanted to use it. But it felt wrong, like a flower I had forced to bloom.

Now I’m sitting with a draft about rain. I’ve changed every line. I’ve made the imagery sharper. I’ve removed the sentences that sounded like I was trying too hard to impress anyone.

There’s a new voice in me tonight. It’s quieter. It’s more honest. It’s angry—mostly at how easy it is to pretend everything is easy.

I’m going to revise again. I’m going to listen. I’m going to write, even if I don’t like the result. Maybe I’ll be honest about how much I want to be liked. Maybe I’ll admit that my words are loud, messy, and sometimes terrible.

I’m not sure I’m ready to be good at poetry. I’m only ready to be good enough to say what I feel.

I’ll start with one line. One ugly, honest line.
