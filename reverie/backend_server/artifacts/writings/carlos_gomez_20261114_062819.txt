I’m going to sleep at ten. I wake at seven. Dinner is five. My schedule is the same old cruel thing that keeps me alive.

I’m sitting with a notebook in front of me, pretending my hands are still. The workshop is calling out to me. It says: Let the silence do its work.

I don’t like silence. I make noise. I say everything. People notice when I do. They ask if I’m angry. I tell them I’m just excited. They don’t believe me.

The exercise is short. That’s the thing. It’s called a short writing exercise. And I’m going to let myself do whatever comes into my head.

I’m writing about the beauty of the natural world. I’ve been thinking about rain. Not the kind that makes me nervous. The kind that washes the slate clean. The kind that makes the dirt look like gold. I want to write about a pine tree in winter. Not the tall, stiff tree that makes me want to run away. I want to write about the one that bends. I want to write about how it holds the wind without breaking.

I want to write about the way leaves fall. Not pretty, not poetic leaves. Ordinary leaves. Red ones. Brown ones. The ones that fall because they’re finished. I want to write about the soil beneath them. About the dark earth waiting to take a new seed. About the stubborn roots that keep trying to go down even when the ground is cold.

I’m tired. I’m angry. I’m tired of being polite.

But I’m also tired of hiding.

So I’ll write. I’ll write the ugly, the loud, the messy thoughts that keep me from being invisible. I’ll write about how I feel when the rain falls on my window. I’ll write about the way my hands shake when I’m nervous. I’ll write about everything that makes me want to scream.

And when I’m done, I’ll read it. I’ll read it back to myself. I’ll ask: Did I make it sound like a poem? Did I make it sound like a rant? Did I make it sound like a little boy who just wanted to make people notice him?

That’s the problem with me. I’m trying to be a poet, but I’m also trying to be someone else.

I’ll keep writing. The workshop is still here. I don’t know what happens next. Maybe I’ll have to tell a joke. Maybe I’ll have to apologize. Maybe I’ll just have to keep writing until I’ve said enough.

And maybe that’s enough.
