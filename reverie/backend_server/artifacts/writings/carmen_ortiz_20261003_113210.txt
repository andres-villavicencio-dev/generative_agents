Dear Journal,

Today has been strangely quiet. I woke at eight this morning, slower than usual—like my whole body had forgotten its rhythm for a day. When I made that "Morning Whispers of Courage" meal, it tasted right on paper but not in the way I remember. The ingredients smelled of memory and hope, you know? But there was no warmth behind them. Just me watching the steam rise while wondering if I really tasted anything at all, or if I'd simply returned to the same observation again.

It's as though my days have become echoes lately—kind, peaceful, but not truly lived. When I read that book in the pub garden, the words still reminded me of kindness and inner stillness, yet they looped instead of unfolded, like a melody played on repeat. I kept noticing the same lines without the peace settling in the way it used to.

Even my lunch by the window felt this way—nourishing, but with no real hunger or contentment behind it. And today's nap beneath the porch awning was calm, but somehow another echo of yesterday rather than a fresh beginning. I woke from it more tired than refreshed, which is strange because I don't usually feel that at all.

But here in this journaling session, something feels honest about just being present with myself and what I'm feeling. I think the repetition isn't actually a bad thing—it's gentle, tender even. But I do want there to be more realness in my days, more moments where things unfold instead of repeat. It would feel good to truly taste my meals again, to read that book and let it move me rather than send me back around.

I love this little shop so much. Harvey Oak taught me how to look after people's needs, and I carry all that forward every single day. Every customer who walks through here makes me feel useful and cared for, which is exactly what I want to be. So maybe tomorrow will bring something new—I'm hoping each fresh day brings a bit more of the tenderness I've been longing for.

Until then, I'll keep being present. That's all I can do today.

With love,
Carmen
