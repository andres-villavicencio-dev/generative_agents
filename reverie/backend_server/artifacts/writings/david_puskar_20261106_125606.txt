The kitchen light hums like a warm engine tonight. I stand before the counter, the German-Polish fusion potato pancakes arranged in a neat row, their golden crusts gleaming under the overhead lights. It’s not just about making them taste good—it’s about making them feel earned.

I began with a simple list: flour, eggs, milk, salt, pepper, paprika, thyme, garlic, and a little honey. Then I remembered the sour cream I forgot to include, the crushed pepperoni, and the pinch of cayenne I had been eyeing but not quite enough. I added them one by one, tasting as I went. A little more heat, a tiny splash of cream, another pinch of salt. The final pan came together almost too perfectly, which was exactly the problem I was looking for.

I plated three pancakes, two with the savory, slightly sweet glaze and one with a crisp, savory topping. They look as if they’ve stepped out of a Midwestern diner and into a restaurant I haven’t yet invented. I photograph the arrangement in the evening light, then compare the image with the actual dish. The shadows are softer now, the edges sharper, and the pan feels less like a vessel and more like a canvas.

I still haven’t decided whether to pitch this to Arthur Burton tonight, but I feel ready. The kitchen is quiet, and the hum of the refrigerator is comforting. I finish the last pancake, set the camera down, and let the herbs settle around me. For now, the most important ingredient is still the patience I have in my own hands.
