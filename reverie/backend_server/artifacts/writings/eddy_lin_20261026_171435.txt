Candidate Comparison: Café Values, Transparency, and Value for Money

After John Lin shared the candidates’ proposals, I began comparing them with the café’s sales records, menu prices, timing differences, oatmeal and banana-slice quality, and the $4.50–$6.50 price range. My goal was simple: improve value and quality without making the proposals too complicated.

The candidates’ proposals were not entirely clear. One candidate suggested expanding coffee and oatmeal options, but the menu showed no prices. The other proposed adding a banana-slice variety and reducing the price range, yet I could not confirm whether those changes would actually lower the cost. A third candidate emphasized transparency and customer participation, with no concrete example. None of the proposals explained how the café would track the quality of its oatmeal, banana slices, or milk.

I looked at the sales records, and the oatmeal portion had consistently outperformed the banana-slice portion in volume. The banana slices also appeared consistently cheaper, yet the café could not clearly explain why. This made me question whether the candidates were simply swapping one product for another without checking the results.

I also noticed that the proposals did not clearly address the timing of their implementation. I cannot tell whether the new options will be available at the same time as the existing ones, or whether they will be priced differently. The proposals could make the café’s value clearer, but they also risk making decisions too complicated for customers.

In the end, the proposals should focus on transparent, measurable improvements. They should clearly state which options are being added, how their quality compares to the current offerings, and how the prices will change. They should also explain how customers can take part in that transparency.

I believe the proposals should be simple, evidence-based, and customer-friendly. The café needs real improvements—not vague promises.
