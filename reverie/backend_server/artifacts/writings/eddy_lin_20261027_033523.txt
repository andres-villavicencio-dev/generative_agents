March 10, 2025 — Wednesday, February 15

After John Lin shared the mayoral candidates’ proposals, I went back through the café’s sales records, menu prices, timing differences, oatmeal quality, and banana-slice quality. I wanted to improve value and quality without turning every proposal into a complicated political argument.

Wolfgang Schulz’s oatmeal-and-banana proposal looks cleaner. It would standardize the oatmeal, fix the banana-slice quality, and make the price consistent within the $4.50–$6.50 range. The main advantage is that it keeps the café’s value proposition simple: quality, consistency, and a reasonable price.

The banana-slice proposal, by contrast, introduces a new quality standard that could create inconsistencies. It would also raise the price range, so the café might lose customers who prefer the lower-end offerings. The proposal would need a clear way of proving that the new quality standards improve value. Otherwise, it risks becoming more complicated than it needs to be.

I think Schulz’s proposal is stronger. It avoids changing the menu’s existing standards and keeps the customer experience clear. I’d like to see a simple comparison of prices and quality, not a full redesign of the menu. If Schulz can show that the oatmeal is better and the banana slices are comparable, I’d support it.
