Wednesday, February 15 — Notes and Findings

After John Lin shared the candidates’ proposals, I started comparing them with the café’s records and menu. Price is the first problem, but so is quality. The $4.50–$6.50 range looks reasonable, yet we need to make sure every item delivers value without sacrificing the standards customers expect.

I also noted the timing differences between the candidates’ plans and the café’s actual operating hours. A proposal that works at 11:00 AM may be impossible at 1:00 PM. Simplicity is not about cutting everything—it is about removing unnecessary steps while protecting the core promise.

Oatmeal and banana-slice quality deserves special attention. The oatmeal should remain creamy and not turn into clumpy mush, and the banana slices should be firm enough to hold together. Small changes in preparation can make a big difference in perceived value.

I did not expect all candidates to improve the café, but I want the proposals to help the business improve. I also want the process to stay practical: compare the numbers, notice the gaps, and propose concrete improvements without confusing anyone.

My priorities are clear: maintain or improve value and quality, respect the café’s existing structure, and avoid making the proposals too complicated. The café’s records will guide me, and I will keep Eddy Lin’s notes organized for follow-up.
