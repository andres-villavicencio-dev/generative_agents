The Co-Living Show Concept

I’m working on a web series called Roomies, and I want it to feel like a real place, not a sitcom. We’re a group of four roommates sharing a tiny apartment with three cats, a broken dishwasher, and a schedule that’s usually written from the inside out. The humor should come from the absurdity of trying to make sense of a shared life without ever agreeing on what’s for dinner.

Opening

We wake up around 7:05 a.m. The house is quiet. I open the front door and see the first cat, a tiny tabby named Barnaby, stepping out of the closet. I’m supposed to say, “Hey, we’re ready!” But the first thing I notice is that Barnaby’s left paw is stuck under the bed.

“Okay, okay,” I say, standing next to the bed. “No pressure. We can just leave him there.”

I open the door.

“Hey, Barnaby,” I say. “Where’s your paw?”

He looks up, scratches my nose, and hops onto the bed. I’ve never been this friendly with a cat.

Ending

At 8:45 p.m., we’re all on the couch scrolling through the same phone screen. The timer on the wall says “23 minutes” and I’m looking at my watch.

“I’m going to turn off the TV,” I say.

“Why?”

Because it’s going to be 11:00 and nobody’s watching.

I turn the remote off. The room settles back into silence.

“Did you turn off the TV?” I say.

The last person to speak in the room is my roommate, Elena. She opens her mouth to say something, then closes it.

“I did,” she says quietly.

“Thanks,” I say.

We sit there for a long time. The room is warm, the cats are asleep, and I’m not sure what I’d do next.

I’ll write the next scene. I’ll keep the jokes honest, and I’ll make sure everyone’s voice is heard.
