It’s Monday morning, and I’ve been awake for almost an hour. I woke up at 7, showered, and headed straight to the kitchen. I made toast with avocado and a little chili, then sat down and opened the first episode of the web series I’m working on.

The idea is simple: a co-living space with four roommates who think they’re the only ones on earth. At first, they sound perfect—organized, polite, and surprisingly agreeable. But then I let the actors improvise their morning routines. One of them tries to wake up before the alarm by pretending the phone doesn’t exist. Another insists that the housekeeper is secretly a retired circus performer. And the guy who owns the pet ferret? He treats it like a roommate.

I laughed so hard I almost dropped my coffee mug. By the end of the scene, the roommates were arguing about whether the ferret deserved a seat at the dinner table. I think that’s exactly what comedy should feel like—awkward, silly, and human.

Tonight I’m going to improvise a short scene with the cast, then watch it. I want to see how the characters react when I accidentally say something too serious, or when they decide to save the ferret from the trash bin. Maybe I can turn the scene into a little stand-up routine too.

I’m also thinking about how the show could explore what happens when you live with people who care about you but don’t know it. I’ve got a funny idea about the guy who thinks the house is actually a spaceship…

I’ll write the next scene by 9. Then I’ll eat dinner, sleep, and see if I can make everyone laugh again.
