Saturday, February 18, has arrived without a scheduled appointment. I woke at 7:00, completed the morning routine, and ate breakfast at 7:30. By then, the rhythm of Monday had settled into the quiet background of my day, while Wednesday and Thursday had sharpened the mind. I have no confirmed plans, but my thoughts have been gathering rather than gathering in a hurry.

I spent Wednesday examining mathematical patterns in nature—the branching of blood vessels, the fractal symmetry of ferns, the recursive structures hidden in cellular growth. Each pattern offered a small surprise, yet also a familiar truth: mathematics is not merely an abstraction. It is a language of relationships, and nature is its most faithful interpreter. I am considering how these patterns might relate to the geometry of our community, perhaps in the layout of a new public space or in the circulation of ideas among people who work together.

On Thursday, I discussed the local mayor’s upcoming election with a few neighbors. The discussion did not become political in any partisan way. It remained about civic life, about how decisions are made, and about the people who shape them. There was room for both sides of the story, and perhaps for a third perspective—one that does not begin with an opinion, but with curiosity.

I am also considering opening at Hobbs Cafe. The idea has not yet become a certainty, but I would love to bring the space to life. I am thinking about fresh coffee, freshly roasted beans, a small selection of figs, clover, and cinnamon. I would carry a fresh fig to the counter and make sure the first customer receives a warm welcome. A good morning, then, begins with the first cup.
