The light through the window is a sickly yellow this morning, the kind that makes everything look like it’s been dipped in old film. I blinked a few times, trying to will the sleep out of my eyes, but the darkness behind my eyelids felt heavier than usual. I didn’t move for a long time, just listened to the hum of the refrigerator and the distant sound of a siren winding down somewhere down the block. It’s 10:15 now, which means I’ve been awake for roughly twenty minutes, but my brain feels like it’s still stuck in a dream I can’t quite remember.

I made coffee immediately. The grinder is loud, a harsh mechanical sound that snaps me out of the fog a little bit. I watched the grounds fall into the filter, dark and fine, smelling instantly of earth and something deeper, something almost primal. It’s the smell of possibility, I think, or at least the smell of doing something before the day has a chance to start telling me no.

I’m supposed to be editing the chapter on Elias today, the one where he finally admits he’s in love with the wrong person. But my mind keeps drifting back to the podcast I want to start. I’ve been thinking about it for months, just sort of letting the idea sit in the back of my mind like a loose tooth. I keep telling myself I need a co-host, or at least a structured format, but maybe I should just start recording myself talking about the books I’ve been reading. There’s a stack of novels by my bed right now—Oblomov, some translated Japanese thing I picked up last week—and I’ve been itching to write about them.

I added oat milk to the coffee this morning. It’s the only kind we have, and honestly, I think it suits the bitterness of the beans. I sat at my small wooden table, the laptop open, the cursor blinking at me in the top left corner. A blank page. The ultimate enemy. But somehow, today feels different. The energy is higher, maybe because I finally slept past noon yesterday, or maybe because the universe is just finally aligning itself after that chaotic week at the bookstore.

I opened a new document. Title: "Morning Thoughts - Feb 13". I’m going to write about the co-living space I’m inventing for my novel. The way the light hits the kitchen at 4 PM. The specific way the characters argue about whose turn it is to take out the compost. It’s silly, really, the details I obsess over, but they’re the things that make a story feel real. I typed a paragraph about the radiator knocking in the hallway, a sound that one of the characters, Sam, claims is the building’s heartbeat.

I took a sip of the coffee. It’s good. Rich and warm, sliding down my throat and chasing away the last remnants of tiredness. I feel the creative itch starting to scratch itself, the urge to just keep going, to see where these characters will go next. I wrote another sentence, then another, the words flowing faster now, fueled by the caffeine and the quiet of the apartment before the roommates wake up.

By the time I finished the entry, the room had brightened considerably. The yellow light had shifted to a sharper white, and I could see the dust motes dancing in the air. I closed the laptop and stretched my arms above my head, my back popping audibly. I’m ready to actually start the day now. I rinsed my mug and headed toward the bathroom, already thinking about what I want to eat for lunch—probably some leftover pasta, maybe with a fried egg on top. Life is simple when you’re writing it yourself.
