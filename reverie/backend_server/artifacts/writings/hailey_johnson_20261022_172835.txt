February 13

I spent most of the evening at my desk, rereading the latest chapter and pretending the sticky notes weren’t multiplying across every page. The scene between Mara and the ceramicist finally has some life now, but the dialogue still sounds too polished. People don’t speak in perfect little paragraphs when they’re tired, jealous, and trying not to spill coffee on the work they’ve spent three weeks glazing.

I marked three passages for revision. In the first, I cut two sentences because the narrator keeps explaining feelings the reader can already feel. In the second, I brought the rooftop rain forward so the tension arrives sooner. The third needed more silence. I left a note reminding myself that silence can be dramatic without becoming vague. I also crossed out “breathtaking” because I’ve used it twice this month, which may be my new verbal habit.

Tomorrow I need to finish revising the chapter, then map the next three scenes before I lose momentum. I also want to write a short pitch for the podcast. I’m thinking of calling it Borrowed Rooms. The idea would be simple: writers, musicians, and artists share the spaces that shaped their work—apartments, cafés, trains, childhood bedrooms. Ha, it may become a conversation series rather than a true podcast, but I can decide later. I’m excited by the possibility of meeting people who see ordinary rooms as archives.

Before bed, I’ll make a short list of emails and gather the notes for my interview with Lena, the muralist. I need to remember to ask how she changes a wall without trying to make it look beautiful. That feels like the better question.

I’m going to sleep before 2 a.m. for once. Tomorrow’s version of me will have more energy, and this chapter deserves someone who isn’t reading it for the fifth time with sticky notes covering half the words.
