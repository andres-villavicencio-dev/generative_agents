The café closes at ten, but the clatter of forks and the murmur of conversations have long since faded into a low, tired hum. I’m still at the corner desk, laptop balanced beside a cooling mug of tea, blue light making the words on the screen look like little ghosts.

I spent the first hour revising Mara and Theo’s argument in chapter seven. Their voices were too polished before—all neat, balanced lines that sounded like mine, not like them. Mara speaks in quick images, usually about color, texture, or food. She grew up cooking with her grandmother in a narrow apartment above a bakery, so she notices details the rest of us miss. Theo is blunt and practical, a carpenter who trusts his hands more than his memory. I gave him a few broken sentences, a stubborn repetition, and the kind of rough humor that hides fear.

That was better. Now they sound like people who might actually share a kitchen.

Tomorrow, I need to email the illustrator about the revised cover concept, finish the scene in which the rooftop garden is finally revealed, and outline three pilot episodes for the podcast. I’m thinking the podcast should focus on immigrant folktales and how families retell them across generations. I can interview Mara while we test recipes for the novel’s dinner party. Her accent makes even the strangest stories sound musical.

I also need to order groceries. I’ve been surviving on toast, dumplings, and the mysterious lentils someone left in the shared fridge. At six, I’ll make dinner—perhaps chickpea stew, something warm enough to wake everyone up before the evening writing session.

I keep rewriting the same note at the bottom of the page: Leave room for surprise.

Maybe that rule applies to more than fiction. Tomorrow, I’ll begin the podcast properly. I’ll choose a name, find a recording setup that doesn’t sound like an echo chamber, and stop pretending I need the perfect equipment before I press record.

For now, one more pass through the dialogue. Then I’ll close the laptop, brush my teeth, and sleep before two. The words will be there in the morning. So will the characters. And maybe, if I’m lucky, so will I.
