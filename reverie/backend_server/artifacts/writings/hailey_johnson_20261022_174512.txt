By six, the apartment had begun to smell like cumin, coffee, and the faint oil of tempera paint. Mara stood at the stove, tapping her wooden spoon against the rim, while sunlight spilled copper across the cluttered kitchen table. Above the sink, Javier had pinned postcards from Oaxaca: vivid women in embroidered dresses, a blue church, a market crowded with marigolds and painted ceramics. Every few days, he added another note, and every morning the room felt briefly transported to a place none of us had visited yet.

I moved my laptop to the wide windowsill and opened my notebook. The scene before me needed texture, not just a shared apartment and a group of hopeful artists. I needed to hear the building settle, hear Theo’s guitar scrape against its case, hear Lena’s sewing machine rattle like a tiny mechanical storm.

“Give it a story,” I muttered, typing. “Every room should feel like it remembers something.”

At the old writing desk, Chen arranged stones in a careful spiral. He had carried them back from the river in his pockets, each one smooth and gray, each one chosen for its weight. Beside him, Amara’s latest canvas leaned against the wall: a figure with gold thread stitched through its crimson dress. The thread caught the evening light, making the fabric look alive.

I smiled and kept writing.

Outside, the street vendor called out prices for roasted corn. The scent drifted through the cracked window and mingled with Mara’s stew. Someone laughed in the hallway. A bicycle bell rang twice.

For a moment, our co-living space felt less like a temporary arrangement and more like a small world with its own customs: a shared grocery list held to the refrigerator by a magnet from a jazz club in Montréal, a jar of wildflowers crowding the sink, an anthology of Palestinian poetry on the shelf beside a paperback about Brazilian carnival. We brought our histories into the rooms without explaining them, and slowly they became part of one another.

That was the story, I realized. Not simply artists sharing a rent. Artists learning how to inhabit one another’s cultures without flattening them.

My cursor blinked.

I typed until dinner was ready, filling the apartment with color, music, spice, and the beautiful complications of belonging.
