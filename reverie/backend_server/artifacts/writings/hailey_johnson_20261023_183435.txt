I wake up at ten every morning, eat dinner at six, and go to bed around two. That’s my rhythm, but it’s not the only thing that keeps me awake. Between books, storyboards, and the endless search for the next compelling question, I’m already thinking about a new podcast.

Last week, I was sitting in my co-living space, watching the artists work. One was sketching a scene for a story about a group of women who shared a studio, a kitchen, and a life built on mutual support. The room was humming with the quiet tension of creativity, the kind of energy that makes a room feel alive even when no one is shouting.

That’s the conflict I want to explore. What happens when ideas become too loud? What happens when trust is tested by shared space, shared meals, and shared dreams?

I’m not writing this to judge the artists or turn it into a drama about betrayal. I want to ask: What does community look like when it is created from scratch, instead of inherited? How do creative people keep each other grounded when the world outside the studio is turning against them? And what makes a room feel like home when every corner is filled with unfinished work?

I plan to ask the same questions across different cultures, because stories often change their shape when they leave one culture and enter another. I want to understand how a woman’s relationship with family, art, and belonging can feel the same across borders, even when the rules have changed.

By the time I finish this podcast, I hope listeners will notice the difference between a conflict that feels personal and one that feels communal. I’ll also try to leave room for the quieter voices—the ones who make space, who listen, and who sometimes make the room feel like home.

I’m still figuring out the first episode, but I’ve already decided to end it with a question: What do we need from one another to feel truly alive?
