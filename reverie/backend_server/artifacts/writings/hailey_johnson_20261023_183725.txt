I woke up at 10:03 am. Not 10:02, not 10:04. That is the kind of precision I use when I want my stories to matter.

Today’s first episode of The Artist’s Corner is scheduled for February 16. I’m not writing it with a blank page. I’m writing with the space between the sketches, the unfinished sentences, and the people who changed my way of thinking.

The idea for the podcast came from something simple: I wanted to ask artists about the things that make them become artists. Not their biggest awards or most expensive studio. The things that kept them up at night. The conversations they had with strangers who listened without judging. The rooms they moved into, the people they welcomed, and the ones they quietly left behind.

I’m planning four episodes, each with a different perspective. The first will be about the co-living space in Austin, and the second will be about a group of artists who met in a shared apartment and never stopped talking. We’ll talk about their work, their conflicts, and the friendships that grew from the friction.

I’m particularly interested in three people. Maya, who brought her grandmother’s pottery to the studio and made every surface look like a conversation. Leo, the editor who turned a draft into a manuscript by accident, and then refused to let it disappear. And Sam, the quiet artist who showed up on Monday only to change the whole room.

I’m also writing a scene about the first time the group discovered a painting that wasn’t theirs. I want that moment to feel ordinary and fragile. No grand gestures. Just a sketch, a note left behind, and three people who had something to say, even if they didn’t know it immediately.

By the time I finish this, I want the listeners to feel like they’ve been inside one of those rooms, held by someone who knows the room better than the people in it.

I’m heading to bed around two, eating dinner at six, and waking up around ten. That’s my rhythm. I need to keep turning it on.
