The river has no name until I tell it one.

I’ve been listening to it since the first rain, and lately I’ve been wondering whether it’s been trying to teach me anything. The river moves with a quiet stubbornness: it gathers, it slows, and then it carries something else downstream—sometimes a leaf, sometimes a stone, sometimes a person who had nowhere to go before it did.

I’m writing a book about the places between the maps. The ones people forget to name. The markets where the smell of rain still clings to the pavement. The streets where the same family keeps returning, year after year, carrying a small change for the same shopkeeper. These are the stories I want to write, not because they are perfect, but because they refuse to be explained away.

On Tuesday, February 14, I opened my notebook and began with a passage that feels less like an opening and more like a warning.

By noon, the river will be loud again. Its current will carry a new name, and the water will have forgotten the place where it began. That is the rhythm of my life too. I wake at ten, write until my fingers ache, and by six p.m. I have one hour left before dinner.

I am thirty, and I am still learning how to listen without interrupting. I am not yet sure if I should finish the novel or start the podcast. I am not yet sure what makes me happy. But I am carrying forward a full-to-the-brain week, a week in which the river does not give me the answer. It simply keeps moving.
