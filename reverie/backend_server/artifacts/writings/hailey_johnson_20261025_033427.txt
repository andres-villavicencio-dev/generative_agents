I woke up at ten on Monday morning, still tangled in the last thoughts of yesterday. The kitchen was warm, the coffee had cooled into a mug of black tea, and my body felt light enough to move. I decided that today would not just be another chapter but a chapter for the podcast I was starting.

I returned to the draft chapter and found myself returning to the same question: what is the group’s secret? The artists have been together for six months, but they never speak about why they chose each other. I’ve been trying to give them a shared language, but every time I describe their conversation, it becomes a character study rather than a scene. The weakest section is the “first night together” scene. It feels too close to the emotional climax and lacks the quiet tension I want. They should be uncomfortable at first—not because they are awkward, but because they do not yet understand what each other is carrying.

I also need to give the podcast more texture. I should ask myself: what would I tell strangers about this group if no one had asked me? I need a clearer central conflict and a stronger moment where their art reveals something they cannot say. The draft is not finished, but neither is it the whole story. I should write a scene in which one of the artists notices something the others missed.

By two in the morning, I finished that scene and saved the chapter. I will read it aloud again, record my thoughts, and edit the weakest section before bedtime. I do not want to finish the whole book and then forget why I started. I want to make every minute of the podcast feel like a discovery.
