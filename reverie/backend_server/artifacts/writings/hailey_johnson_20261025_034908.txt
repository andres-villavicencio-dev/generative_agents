I woke up at 10:00 AM, ate breakfast, and looked at the sky. It was still bright, which made me feel like I was still in the process of becoming. I had three chapters to edit, and I was ready to do them.

The podcast is my next big project. I don’t want to write about one famous artist or one famous city. I want to show the stories of the ordinary people who make a place feel alive. I want to explore how creativity connects us, how belonging changes what we imagine, and how art can turn ordinary rooms into places of wonder.

My main themes are these: imagination as a shared responsibility, the importance of listening before speaking, and the idea that creativity can change even the most rigid spaces. I want the podcast to feel like a conversation—a place where people can disagree, laugh, and discover something new.

The recurring story structure will be the same across all episodes. I’ll begin with a small problem: an unfinished idea, a difficult decision, or a misunderstood feeling. Then I’ll show how the characters notice the problem. Next, they make one small change. Finally, they see a result that surprised them. I’ll leave the ending with a question rather than a neat conclusion, so listeners can carry the next idea with them.

I’m also gathering my audio notes. I want to keep the strongest ideas, the most interesting quotes, and the most useful prompts. Some of my notes are already in my head, but I want to turn them into real recordings. I want to make sure the stories aren’t only heard; I want them to be felt.

I want the podcast to feel like an invitation. Not to be perfect, but to be brave. I want listeners to feel that they can contribute, that they can change something, and that they can find their own story in the conversation.

By the way, my dinner is at six. I’m not going to eat it alone. If you listen closely, you can hear the rhythm of a group of artists coming together.
