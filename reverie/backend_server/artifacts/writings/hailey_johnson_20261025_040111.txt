I woke up at 10:15 AM with my coffee still in my hands. My calendar said Monday, February 13, but I was already imagining every new chapter. I’m 30, not yet 32, and I’m still making time to write. I’m a writer, and I am also beginning a podcast. No pressure. Just writing.

I’ve been thinking about my latest novel: The Co-Living Space. It’s a group of artists living together. The apartment is full, messy, loud, and full of stories. Each person brings a different color to the room, yet they all struggle with the same invisible rules. I want the characters to sound like real people: tired, creative, sometimes chaotic, but always connected.

I’m also building a podcast. Not a long-form talk show. Shorter. Conversational. Maybe one person tells a story, another asks a question, and the third offers a surprising angle. No need for perfect dialogue. Just enough tension to make people want to listen.

I’m not sure how much time I can commit. I have a dinner at six, then go to bed at two. I’m not trying to be productive all day. I’m trying to keep writing without burning out. Sometimes I need a quiet corner. Sometimes I need a room full of people. Both work.

So here’s how I’m approaching this week:

1. Finish the first chapter. Leave it unfinished if the story won’t fit.
2. Record one short segment. Three minutes of conversation, two minutes of reading, one minute of silence.
3. Write the next scene. Do not edit it until you’ve finished it.
4. Collect audio notes. Write down interesting ideas, questions, and images without judging them.
5. Plan the next podcast episode. Who will talk? What will they say? What should we avoid?

I’ve always believed that good writing comes from curiosity. This is my first time asking strangers, strangers, strangers, what they think about art, stories, and how people live together. That is an adventure worth taking.
