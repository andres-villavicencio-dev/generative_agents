I woke up at 10:00 AM on Monday, February 13, before the world had even begun. The city was quiet, my phone was silent, and I was ready to write. After breakfast at 6:00, I spent the next hour researching the first historical cultural story for my podcast about the arts and cultural stories.

I started with a story from 1920s Paris: a young American woman named Elise, who arrives in France to paint a landscape. She does not understand French, but she begins to notice the colors, the shadows, and the quiet beauty of every street. She meets a painter whose work she admires, and her curiosity changes her entire life.

I chose it because it is emotionally resonant, vivid, and full of possibility. Elise’s journey is not just about art—it is about language, belonging, and the courage to create when you are unsure what to show. It also fits the theme of my podcast: how art can connect people across different cultures, languages, and lives.

As I read the story again, I imagined my own characters—the colorful, unconventional group of artists living together in a shared co-living space. They would argue about technique, discuss the latest exhibition, and perhaps organize a community painting class that brings together neighbors they never knew.

I want my podcast to honor Elise’s story while giving her her own voice. I want listeners to hear the quiet conversations of artists, the lessons of collaboration, and the unexpected ways creativity changes us.

If you enjoy this story, please subscribe to my podcast. If you’d like to read more about the arts and the stories that shape them, I’d love to hear your thoughts. Let’s make space for a conversation that is kind, curious, and full of possibilities.
