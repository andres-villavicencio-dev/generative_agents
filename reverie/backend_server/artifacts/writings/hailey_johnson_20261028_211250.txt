“Welcome back. I’m Hailey Johnson, and this is the second story in my latest novel about Elena, a gallery owner who rents out every available apartment in the neighborhood, while her artist friends live in a shared co-living space.

The building was built around one idea: creativity should be available to everyone, no matter where they are. Elena was built on that promise. She hosted workshops, organized art walks, and kept the community alive with conversation, coffee, and the constant hum of unfinished ideas. But lately, that same promise had begun to feel crowded.

The first guest was Maya. Maya’s apartment was on the first floor, where the neighbors had arranged a small gathering for the week.

“Welcome back,” the host called. “We’ve been waiting for you.”

Maya looked around. The tables were arranged neatly, but the conversation had already shifted from welcome to logistics. Someone asked where the rest of the group was going. Someone else was checking the lights.

Elena watched from the kitchen.

“I’m sorry,” Maya said. “My apartment is full.”

The room fell quiet.

Elena smiled. “Then we’ve got a problem.”

Maya’s face softened. “I know. I’ve been asking you all to help me find somewhere else.”

For the next week, the co-living space became a place of quiet creativity. The kitchen shared ingredients, the living room became a rehearsal space, and the artists talked about what mattered to them while the walls absorbed every conversation.

At 6:00 PM, dinner arrived. The music had gone down. The neighbors had gathered.

Elena smiled. They were exactly where they needed to be.
