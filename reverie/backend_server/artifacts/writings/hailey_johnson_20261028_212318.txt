I woke up at 10:00 a.m. on Monday, February 13, with the house quiet and my mind already buzzing. At 6:00, I ate dinner. By 2:00, I was asleep. I’m 30 and still feel young at heart. I’m imaginative, energetic, and constantly looking for new ways to tell stories. I’ve been writing a novel about a group of artists living in a co-living space in Portland, and I’m also planning a podcast that explores the arts through literature, history, and human connection.

The co-living space, called The Studio Collective, is a creative hub in downtown Portland. It houses artists, writers, musicians, and makers in a cozy, cluttered studio that feels like a living gallery. We gather for weekly workshops, share meals, and celebrate the many different kinds of art that make this place special.

One of the first stories I’m developing follows Mara, a local ceramicist whose studio becomes the center of a small controversy. A rival artist challenges her creative direction, and the two women must find a way to rebuild not only their friendship but also their shared vision. In the process, they discover stories about Elara, a refugee artist who made her debut in a community exhibition called The Unfinished Canvas, and Ben, a sound artist whose installations challenge us to think about what we leave behind.

I’m especially drawn to the intersection of storytelling and culture. Literature from around the world can change how we understand one another. I’ve been listening to The Night Circus by Eoin Colquhoun, The Tattooist of Auschwitz by Hannah Gadsby, and A Short History of Almost Everything by Lily Tomlin. These stories show how art can preserve memory, challenge injustice, and create a shared sense of belonging.

There is still so much to discover. I want to interview local artists, explore the history of Portland’s creative neighborhoods, and follow the lives of the characters in my novel. Music matters too: I’m considering a podcast episode about the soundscapes of Folkstone, Salem, and Portland’s historic districts.

My goal is simple: make art feel accessible, and make listeners feel seen. Whether I’m sitting in a studio, reading a page, or planning an interview, I’m curious to see what happens next.
