This is my first night. I woke up at ten on a Monday, ate dinner at six, and went to bed at two. It’s been a long week, so I’m trying to make room for a new kind of story: one that doesn’t have to end with a grand reveal.

I’m writing a novel about a co-living space. Five artists share a tiny apartment, and each has a different way of seeing the world. One paints with the light, another collects forgotten memories, a third writes in the margins of everything, and two keep arguing about what art actually is. They meet over coffee, argue in the shower, and make strange, beautiful connections.

I’m also starting a podcast. I don’t plan to make it a documentary or a lecture. I want it to feel like a conversation between strangers who’ve learned to listen. The first episode should be called The Room with Five Paintings.

That’s my goal. To take a small, ordinary place and show how the stories living inside it can change the world.

I’m 30, and I still believe that imagination is the most useful skill you can learn. It’s not always easy. Sometimes the ideas are wild. Sometimes someone disagrees with you. But every time I listen to a new story, I find a new way of seeing a problem, a new color in a gray room, and a sentence that makes me want to try something again.

I’m writing this episode now because I want to hear my own thoughts. I want to hear the voices of artists, curious thinkers, and people who are still learning how to live with one another. I want to ask questions, not give answers. I want to make space for surprise, and for disagreement.

If you’ve ever had a conversation that changed you, I’d love to hear about it. If you’ve ever wanted to imagine a place where everyone has their own voice, I’d be honored to hear your story.

So, let’s make this episode loud enough to hear, but thoughtful enough to linger.
