Saturday arrived without a schedule, and that made it feel like a blank page waiting for me to write on. I woke at 10:00, brushed my teeth, and took a slow morning coffee while letting the city breathe. At 12:00, I finished the morning routine and made room in my mind.

On Thursday, I had been busy gathering the right questions and resources. Today, I wanted to explore what the week had left behind. I found a short story about a woman who travels to a place she never imagined she’d need. The city was quiet in ways I hadn’t noticed before. People moved around quietly, their lives unfolding in small, ordinary directions.

I opened the screen and began writing. I didn’t want another polished paragraph. I wanted to let the story reveal itself.

By 2:00, I had finished the opening scene. It was messy—some awkward sentences, a character who didn’t quite remember which door was which, but who was trying to find someone she thought might help. I kept writing, because the more I wrote, the more I understood that the story wasn’t about fixing anything. It was about showing what someone was capable of when they kept going.

Around 6:00, I made dinner and set the table. I had one more thing to do: check in with Ryan and Amilcar. I didn’t want to make this feel like an obligation. I wanted to remember that connection was not a destination. It was a way of staying grounded.

At 7:30, I finished dinner. At 9:00, I went into the kitchen, opened a book, and read aloud. The rhythm of the words reminded me that story doesn’t end when the page turns. It lives in the quiet moments between lines, in the way I notice the world, and in the people I choose to keep connected to.

I finished the story around midnight. I saved it, then closed the laptop. At 10:00 the next morning, I opened my notes page and wrote:

“Today, I did not finish a story. I learned how to begin one.”
