Saturday, February 18

The room was quiet as I woke at ten, the only sound my own breathing. I had been dreaming of a place where the edges of the world softened, where old maps became maps of people rather than roads. That is what I had been trying to make with my stories lately—places that felt real, even when they didn’t exist.

I didn’t want to be anywhere. I wanted to keep moving, however briefly, toward what felt meaningful. The phone buzzed, and I checked it. It was Ryan. He asked about the stories we’d been talking about, and the small tension in his voice made me pause. He didn’t need an answer. He just needed to know I was listening.

I opened my notebook and began writing, not to fix the story, but to let it change. I wrote about a town where the streets were made of forgotten memories, where strangers shared bread and secrets, and where a woman who had never been alone began to believe that the world was not waiting for her.

Then I looked at the calendar. No appointments. No errands. No community activity. The day was completely open.

I considered using this time to start a project. Maybe a short story about a writer whose stories were becoming too familiar. Maybe a piece about the quiet courage it takes to keep showing up when no one is watching. Maybe I should finish what I started, just for the sake of finishing it.

I stood up and walked to the window. Outside, the city was moving, loud and purposeful. I could almost see the town I imagined, with its winding streets and people carrying stories in their pockets.

I had no fixed direction. I only had the feeling that Saturday was not meant to be wasted. I opened my notebook again, picked up my pen, and began again.
