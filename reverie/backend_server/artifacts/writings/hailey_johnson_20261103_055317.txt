Saturday, February 18, is the day I let the story settle.

After breakfast, I found Abigail Chen sitting in the living room. Her hair was a little messy, and she was staring at the screen with the same quiet intensity I’ve learned to recognize. “You’re using every part of it,” she said.

I smiled. “Yes. Every part. Every texture, every character, every moment.”

She laughed. “I think you mean every scene has a purpose.”

“Exactly,” I said. “And every character keeps getting closer to who they’re meant to be.”

We talked for a while about the manuscript we were both working on. We talked about how Hailey’s creative voice is becoming more precise, how Abigail’s observations feel grounded even when she’s dreaming up new worlds, and how the shared co-living studio story is becoming our real home for now.

When we finished, I checked my notes. The story had a beginning, a turning point, and a beginning again. But I was also thinking about everything else. Ryan and Amilcar. Saturday felt like an invitation to reconnect. I didn’t have any appointments, work blocks, errands, meals, or community activities listed, so I could make time.

Maybe I’ll write the next scene. Maybe I’ll finish the draft. Maybe I’ll take a walk and catch up with someone I haven’t spoken to in a while. The studio isn’t the only place I can be meaningful. It’s not even the only place where I can belong.

I put the notebook down and walked toward the door. I could have another story, another conversation, another day of creative discovery. But for a little while, I wanted to be present. I wanted to listen to Abigail. I wanted to talk to Ryan and Amilcar. And I wanted to finish what was left unfinished.
