Tuesday, February 14, felt like the kind of Tuesday that turns into a story before I even finish reading it. I woke at 10:00 AM, stretched through my legs, drank a glass of water, and opened my notebook. The sun was bright, and I had a full day ahead: café shift, civic work, creative planning, and a community gathering. Tom had checked in earlier that morning, Maria had reminded me to rest, Isabella had suggested a route, Abigail had offered encouragement, and Francisco had added a quiet moment for reflection. Together they made the day feel manageable, not demanding.

I spent the morning reviewing my notes, then the café shift. I served coffee, organized the tables, and talked with the customers. It was tiring, but not overwhelming. I made a little room in my heart for the people who needed it.

At 12:30, I took a short break. Then I looked at the café’s opening plan and asked the creative team a question I’d been meaning to ask: What if the gathering was not just an event, but a conversation about how we carry our culture into our daily lives? I wrote down the idea, then imagined a story that could come from it.

Around 2:00 AM, I went to bed. The next morning I woke at 10 AM, remembered my plans, and began again. I didn’t need every answer immediately. I only needed to be curious enough to notice the pieces that had been there all along.

The gathering is tomorrow. I’m hoping people will see the story in the café, hear the ideas from the community, and feel heard when they return home. I’m tired, but I’m not exhausted. I’m tired because I gave everything I had today, and I’m grateful for the support that made that possible.

I’m going to write about the gathering tomorrow. Maybe I’ll begin with a character whose life feels just like mine, someone whose day ends around 2:00 AM and whose morning begins with a cup of coffee. I hope the story will feel energetic, imaginative, and true.

I’m going to write about it. I’m going to write about a person who remembers how to find joy in the small moments.
