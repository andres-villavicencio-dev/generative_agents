The Room That Wasn’t There

It was 10:00 a.m. on Monday, February 13, and the apartment had already lost its way.

I woke to the smell of old paper and coffee grounds, the faint metallic tang of copper across the floor, and a radiator humming against the walls. I sat up, brushed my teeth, and looked around. The group home was still empty. The studio door stood open beside the kitchen, the windows reflected the gray morning light, and my keys were still in my pocket.

For twenty minutes, I was just the room.

Then I remembered what I’d been planning. A podcast. Not about my writing, but about the people who lived inside my head—artists, curators, critics, strangers who would listen to my thoughts and learn how to be them.

I made two cups of tea. I opened the laptop. On the first draft page, I wrote the opening line of the novel:

In the co-living space, the first thing you notice is that you’re the only person who understands the furniture.

The room was quiet, but it wasn’t empty. Every piece of furniture had been there for hours. Every shadow had been there for hours. And the silence, however loud it sounded, was the loudest thing I’d ever heard.

By 2:00 a.m., I was already awake again. I would write until dawn. Then I would eat dinner. Then I would record the first episode. Then I would go to bed at 2.

Monday had begun at ten, and the room had already forgotten how to stay in one place.
