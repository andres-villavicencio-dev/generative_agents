It was Monday, February 13, and the co-living apartment felt less like a home and more like a rehearsal stage. I was in the middle of a scene, trying to make the quiet tension between the three artists feel alive. Maya wanted the tension to crack open like a cracked egg. I wanted it to simmer, to build pressure until the characters suddenly realized that the walls were beginning to make sense.

“Don’t let them get too comfortable,” Maya said, tapping her pen against the table. “You’re making them feel like an audience. They should be arguing about something.”

“I’m making them feel human,” I replied, though I could feel the heat rising in my cheeks.

“Exactly. They should be arguing about the same thing. About the same thing. About the same thing.”

I looked at the sketch I’d been working on—the one with the overlapping figures, the one that seemed to be trying out every possible pose. Maya’s sketch was simpler, almost too simple. It showed three artists leaning over each other, their clothes shifting, the space around them expanding and contracting.

“Your sketch,” Maya said, her voice low, “looks like I’m trying to convince the furniture to do its job.”

“It’s not furniture,” I said. “It’s conversation. Look at the way the chairs are turned toward each other. What does that say?”

“It says we’re inviting them to sit down,” Maya countered. “And I think that’s the problem. You’re giving them a room. We’re already inside each other’s heads. Why are you making them wait for us to open up?”

I stared at the sketch. For a moment, I felt the tension in the room shift. The artists weren’t arguing about furniture anymore. They were arguing about how to make an argument.

“I didn’t want to make them wait,” I said. “I wanted them to realize that they could be surprised by what they’re saying.”

Maya sighed. “Surprised. That’s the word you keep using.”

“Maybe it is,” I admitted. “Maybe it is the only word we’ve got.”

She laughed, and the sound came out softer than I expected.

“Then let’s have a scene where the chairs suddenly decide to speak,” she said.

I smiled. It was the easiest thing I’d wanted to write all week.
