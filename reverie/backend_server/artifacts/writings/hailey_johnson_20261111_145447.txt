The coffee machine gave a final chime at 6:00 p.m., and I stood in the kitchen, staring into the cup. The artists had been arguing all morning about what the studio should look like. One insisted on a gallery wall, another wanted the space divided into separate studios, and I heard a third whisper, “But what if we just made a room for everything?”

I turned it off. “We can make a room for everything,” I said, and I meant it.

I walked into the living room and opened the big window. Outside, the rain was drumming against the glass, and a group of artists stood on the porch, soaked and laughing. They’d already decided. We’d turn the studio into one open space, with a shared kitchen, a communal reading area, and enough space for every kind of project.

At first, it sounded like a compromise. Then it sounded like a chance to make room for us all.

We gathered around the table for dinner. The artists brought their food, their stories, and their unfinished paintings. I listened, and I made a few notes about the room, the schedule, and the small disagreements we might still have. Then we sat in comfortable silence, eating, laughing, and talking about the stories we wanted to tell.

Around 10:00 p.m., I closed the laptop. I still had two chapters written, and a whole podcast to develop. But tonight, I didn’t need to solve every problem. I just needed to make sure I had everything I needed, a place to rest, and time to dream.

I turned off the lights and went to bed around 2:00 a.m. Tomorrow, I’d wake up at 10:00 a.m., eat dinner at 6:00 p.m., and begin the next day with fresh energy.
