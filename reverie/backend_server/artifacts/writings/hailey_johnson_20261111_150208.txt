The studio smells like burnt toast and old rain, and my pen is the first thing to leave the room. At 10:05 a.m., I am awake. At 6:00 p.m., I eat dinner, and at 2:00 a.m., I begin again.

In the co-living space, the artists are already awake before I am. I find them in the kitchen, laughing as one of them slides a half-moon of cheese onto the plate, while the others are already sketching lines through the wall, tracing a tiny city that has no streets.

“Where did you get that texture?” the woman asks, pointing at a brush dipped in burnt umber.

“Found it in the museum, actually,” I say. “It looks like a memory that has turned into paint.”

They laugh. The room settles. For the first time in two weeks, I don’t feel like someone who must make the room look orderly. I feel like someone who is invited in.

By 11:30 a.m., I’ve been sitting at the table for twenty minutes without saying a word. The coffee is cooling, the paper is getting warm, and my fingers are beginning to ache.

“Why do you write about places that don’t exist?” the man asks, stirring his tea.

“Because I don’t want to copy them,” I say. “I want to make them feel like I could step into them.”

He smiles. “You could step into anything you wanted to.”

I look at him. “Do you ever imagine yourself in one of these rooms?”

He pauses, then points to the sketch on the wall. The tiny city is unfinished, with a door that has no handle. “I imagine I’m already inside,” he says. “I’ve never left.”

He laughs again, louder this time.

I write for three hours. Then I eat dinner. Then I sleep. I wake up at 10:05 a.m., eat lunch, and begin again. The novel is still unfinished. The podcast is still scheduled for Thursday. But for the first time in months, I am not rushing to finish something I cannot quite understand. I am just here. I am listening. I am awake.
