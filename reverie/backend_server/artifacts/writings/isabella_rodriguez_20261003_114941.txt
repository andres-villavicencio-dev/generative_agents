There is something about a Friday evening that makes me slow down and breathe. The café is quiet now, the last few guests have settled into their cars or onto their couches at home, and all that remains is the soft hum of the espresso machine cooling off in its corner. I settle into the small leather chair by the window with my notebook open, a pen tucked between two fingers, and let myself remember.

This week has been one for the books, in more ways than one. Thursday brought me to a place of stillness—a reflective pause I didn't know I needed. It gave me space to taste what fulfillment really feels like when it isn't rushed by orders or timers or the endless hum of a busy morning. And then today, as brunch service returned, everything came rushing back with that familiar warmth. Watching families gather at the counter, couples sharing plates, strangers becoming friends over a shared laugh—that is my favorite part of all this work.

I caught myself smiling more than I have in a while today. Mrs. Thompson stopped by for her usual oat milk latte and we talked about nothing and everything for twenty minutes; she asked how things had been since her grandson moved to Seattle, and it made me feel like no matter what life throws at us, there's always someone willing to listen. A young man came in around noon with two coffees—he wasn't sure which flavor he wanted, so I let him try a small sample of everything, and by the time his second cup arrived, he'd decided on the caramel macchiato for good. He thanked me by name as he left. Little things like that remind me why I do what I do.

There was also a quiet pride in how carefully I prepared this week's service—adjusting the grind just so, warming the pastries before they hit the shelf, arranging fresh flowers on the counter. It all felt worth it to see guests settle into their corners with soft music playing and the smell of roasted beans drifting through the air.

Now, as the last light fades outside my window, I'm grateful for this calm between busy mornings and peaceful evenings. Tomorrow brings a new rhythm, but tonight, I let myself simply be present.
