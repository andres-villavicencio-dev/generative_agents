I was born to speak with water. I have listened to its fall from the sky, felt its pulse beneath the earth, and seen it rise through stone and wood. That is what I carry into my pastoral work.

This is my theology and art book. My aim is not to make theology decorative. It is to show that faith can be found in the same quiet spaces where a watercolor bleeds from a canvas, where a classical piano begins to speak without words, and where a cup of tea becomes a prayer.

The book is divided into four parts. I begin with the theology of the present moment: how we are called to listen more than to speak, and how our stories should shape the world. Then I turn to the art of interpretation. I explore how contemporary visual artists have transformed pain, memory, and community into something that can move us without demanding us to agree. Next, I examine the role of the artist as witness—a role that has long been neglected in religious education. Finally, I consider what happens when theology and art converse: when a painting becomes a sermon, and a sermon becomes a painting.

I am also developing a regional interfaith arts symposium. It will bring together writers, painters, musicians, and faith leaders to explore how different traditions can speak to one another without turning their differences into arguments. I hope this will help us find common ground in the shared human experience of grief, hope, love, and loss.

This is the work of someone who has spent time with people who are still learning. I do not claim to have everything figured out. I only hope that by the end of the book, the reader will feel less alone in asking, “What does this mean for me?”

I also host a weekly listening hour at Johnson Park. Jane Moreno and Klaus Mueller find there a place to meet, to share, and to be challenged. Their questions are often the same questions I have asked: How do we listen? How do we make space? How do we care for the world we live in?

I write, paint, and play piano, but my life is shaped by the conversations I have. I do not believe the answer is always easy. Sometimes it is simply that we are still listening.
