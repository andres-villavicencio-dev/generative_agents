I woke up at 7:00 AM, as I always try to do, and took my time with the morning routine. After washing up, I made breakfast, checked my notes, and opened the housing-policy materials I had been meaning to finish. With no classes or fixed appointments today, I could finally slow down and read carefully instead of rushing from one task to another.

Sam’s comments about displacement in the older neighborhoods kept coming back to me. John raised the other side of the argument: new investment can bring jobs, better services, and badly needed housing, but only if existing residents are not pushed out. Giorgio made me think about how policies look on paper compared with how they affect people who have already built lives in a place. I don’t want to reduce gentrification to a simple story about progress or loss. Both can happen at once, and the people carrying the greatest costs often have the least power.

For my next research session, I need to ask: Which protections are actually enforced, and which exist only on paper? How do rent stabilization, tenant-right-to-counsel programs, and legal aid affect long-term residents? What happens when landlords renovate buildings or convert them into condos? How do neighborhood associations respond, and whom do they leave out? Can the city track displacement without treating every low-income household as if it were about to leave? I also want to compare what happened in neighborhoods that received new investment with those that did not.

Running for mayor still feels possible, but it no longer feels like an impulsive decision. It would mean making difficult choices, listening to people who disagree with me, and accepting responsibility for outcomes I cannot fully control. Research helps, but it cannot replace trust.

Tonight I’ll organize my sources, draft an outline, and turn these questions into a clear list for the next session. I also need to remember to eat dinner around 5:00 PM and avoid staying up past 11:00.
