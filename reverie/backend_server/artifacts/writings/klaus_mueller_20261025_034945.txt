This is my research paper: Gentrification, Rising Housing Costs, and the Reduction of Affordable Housing in Low-Income Communities.

I began writing after reading about Ayesha Khan’s work. She showed how gentrification can displace long-term residents and erase communities that have served people for generations. The paper became my way of connecting that personal insight to the larger issues I study at Oak Hill College.

My main question is how rising housing prices affect low-income residents. When housing becomes too expensive, many people cannot afford a home. Some may have to move out to work, while others choose to wait for cheaper options. This often leaves them without a stable home and without access to the neighborhood’s schools, jobs, and community programs.

Gentrification also changes the character of a neighborhood. New businesses and residents can make the area more attractive, but they may also raise rents and increase the cost of living. For residents in lower-income areas, this means that the improvements that make a place desirable can come at a price they cannot afford.

I’m still refining my argument, but Ayesha’s perspective has made it clearer. She reminds me that housing is not just a commodity—it is a connection to home, community, and social stability. Until we understand how displacement and rising costs interact, it will be hard to create effective policies.

I hope this paper contributes to a broader conversation about housing justice. I would also like to hear other researchers’ perspectives before I submit the final version.
