I am writing this paper for my sociology course at Oak Hill College. The central question is how gentrification affects low-income communities. Gentrification changes neighborhoods by raising rents and prices, attracting wealthier residents and businesses, and often displacing long-term residents. At Oak Hill College, I have seen the same process in many places. It is not always clear who benefits, who loses, or what happens to housing, jobs, and community life. My goal is to examine these effects and consider how policies might make gentrification more equitable.

In my research, I have looked at gentrification in several low-income neighborhoods. The most striking effect has been displacement. Long-term residents often cannot afford the rising costs of rent or property taxes. Families may be forced to move, and community relationships can weaken when neighborhoods lose their most established residents. At the same time, newcomers often bring new businesses and services that can increase safety, cleanliness, and local employment. The benefits are not automatic; they depend on how quickly the process is allowed to unfold and who is allowed to participate in the decision-making.

In my own community, I have noticed the tension between community needs and market pressures. A local shop may be replaced by a larger chain, even if the chain brings more jobs. The neighborhood may look improved, but the original residents may feel abandoned. I believe gentrification is not simply progress; it is a process of change. We need a more deliberate approach that protects vulnerable people while allowing new opportunities to develop.

For now, I am using my free time to read about gentrification, talk with classmates, and write. I hope this paper contributes to a fuller understanding of how social change affects people. My research is still ongoing, so my conclusions may change. But I am certain that our societies should not leave difficult changes to chance. I am also hopeful that students like me can help identify which neighborhoods are being harmed and which opportunities can be made available to them.
