Monday, February 13

I woke up at seven and headed to the library early in the morning. By ten, I had finished reading the gentrification section and was ready to begin writing my research paper. The library was quiet, and the pages were full of words I wanted to hear again. I felt proud to be studying social justice and curious about how communities are changing around me.

I chose to focus on low-income neighborhoods because the changes there are often invisible. When people move away, local businesses close, neighborhood stores disappear, and the buildings themselves are often repurposed. The residents who remain face higher rents, longer commutes, and fewer safe places to walk. I want to understand not only what is happening on the street, but who is benefiting from the transformation and who is being left behind.

My research needs to be careful. Gentrification is not always intentional. It can also result from a lack of affordable housing, rising rents, changes in zoning, and the increasing value of nearby homes. The effects are not evenly distributed. Some residents remain, while others leave and often do not return. I need to hear their stories, not just the official statistics.

My paper should also examine the consequences for community life. Open spaces can disappear when homes are bought and renovated. Neighborhoods can become more expensive to live in, making them inaccessible to younger people and newcomers. Social networks may weaken, and local knowledge can be displaced. At the same time, there can be investment in local businesses and public services. These changes are complex, and I need to look beyond the easy explanations.

I plan to interview residents, review community records, and consider the perspectives of people who have lived here for many years. I want to write with clarity and honesty. My goal is not simply to describe a problem, but to understand its causes and effects, and to suggest ways of responding without repeating the old patterns.

I also want to be mindful of my tone. I should not present every resident as a victim, and I should not assume that all residents are part of the problem. The changes are real, and they matter. I want the paper to be useful, thoughtful, and grounded in the voices of the people it is trying to understand.
