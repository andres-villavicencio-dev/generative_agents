This Monday, I opened my notebook with a quiet purpose. I wanted to understand how gentrification is reshaping the neighborhoods around Oak Hill College and where it is leaving low-income residents behind. My research began with a simple question: which communities are changing the fastest, and what makes them different from others?

I began by looking at the library’s most recent articles on gentrification. The reports were dense, but one article stood out to me: it traced how rising property values often come not from the improvement of existing homes, but from the replacement of affordable housing with more expensive options. The author noted that displacement can happen quietly—when a family moves out before they are fully priced out of the market. It also highlighted how newcomers bring new businesses and services, making the neighborhood feel more vibrant. For a neighborhood like Oak Hill, that change could be both welcome and unsettling.

I spent several afternoons walking toward Hobbs Cafe, observing the neighborhood’s changing faces. I saw young professionals moving in with their families, small shops opening, and storefronts that once housed local businesses closing down. These patterns made me wonder about the people who had lived there before. I also looked at local housing listings, comparing rents and purchase prices with the median income of nearby residents. The numbers were not always easy to read.

At dinner, I ate at Hobbs Cafe and talked with a local resident. He told me that gentrification does not always bring prosperity to the neighborhood. For many, it means losing the community they built and the stability that makes it possible. I left feeling hopeful, but also uncertain. I wanted to help people understand the tradeoffs and find ways to protect their neighborhoods from the forces of rapid change.

I will revise this paper over the coming days. My goal is not simply to report what happens, but to ask why it happens and what can be done about it. I also want to give my own voice to the stories I have learned about.
