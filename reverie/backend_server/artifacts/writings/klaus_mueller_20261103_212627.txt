Today I have been comparing my research findings with my outline. I began with a few questions: what does gentrification actually do to low-income communities, and how do those effects change over time? I found that the neighborhood changes can create a cycle of displacement—property values rise, rents increase, and older residents move out before they can afford to stay. Yet I also noticed that the process is rarely purely destructive. Gentrification can also bring new businesses, improved infrastructure, and greater access to public services. The real problem is that these benefits arrive alongside rising costs and a narrowing of the community’s social fabric.

My outline divided the study into three stages. First, I examined how gentrification alters housing and affordability. Second, I compared the experiences of residents who have stayed with those who have been displaced. Third, I considered how policy might help communities resist displacement. The research has made me more convinced that gentrification is rarely the same thing everywhere: it can benefit the neighborhood while punishing its original inhabitants, depending on who funds the development and how power is shared.

I also found that the human experience is often overlooked. An apartment can become more valuable while the neighbors lose their community, their friendships, and their sense of security. These losses feel personal and can make the abstract policy feel distant. I hope my paper captures this emotional reality as well as the structural changes. I am concerned that the research may focus too much on statistics and too little on how people actually live through these transformations.

For now, I have completed the first section of my paper. I will revise the outline to make the findings more clearly linked to the arguments I want to make. I will also read the articles closely and note which details I have missed. The goal is not simply to present data, but to understand the consequences of gentrification and to suggest ways of supporting the communities most affected by it. I am still learning, and I hope to keep asking difficult questions.
