The Weight of Change: Gentrification and Public Safety in Low-Income Neighborhoods

This is the first part of my research on gentrification. I began by reading about how rising property values and improved housing have transformed Oak Hill College neighborhoods. My goal is to understand the consequences of that change—not just the rising rents or flashy storefronts, but who benefits and who loses.

On the surface, gentrification appears to be progress. New storefronts attract visitors, and neighborhood amenities improve. For residents who have lived there for decades, those changes can feel like displacement. When housing prices rise, older residents often have no choice but to move. Families with young children, small businesses, and community organizations can be pushed out before they have time to adapt.

I have not found a direct link between gentrification and crime, so I am cautious about drawing conclusions from that alone. I also have little personal experience in the community, which makes the research feel especially important. I want to hear from people who understand what has changed. A neighborhood that has been transformed can change in ways I cannot see from the outside.

This is why I am researching public safety closely. One theory suggests that when people lose their homes and neighborhoods, they may move into communities with different social norms, and that could affect local crime rates. Another possibility is simply that gentrification brings more people into the neighborhood, making it feel safer. Either way, it is an area where more research is needed.

My first step is to compare neighborhoods before and after major housing developments. I will look for changes in housing stock, population composition, business patterns, and public safety records. I will also interview residents, business owners, and local officials. I want to understand not only what changed, but how residents and businesses reacted.

The findings will help me write a more accurate paper about gentrification. They will also help me understand what other students, community members, and activists are asking me to consider. I hope this research can give people a clearer picture of the trade-offs involved in neighborhood transformation.
