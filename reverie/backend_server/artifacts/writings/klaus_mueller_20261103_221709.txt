Today has been busy, but also deeply fulfilling. I spent the morning in the library at Oak Hill College, reading articles on neighborhood change and interviewing people affected by it. I was especially interested in what happens to community cohesion when housing prices rise and new residents arrive with different needs.

My research focuses on gentrification in low-income communities. I want to understand how displacement affects residents’ ability to build relationships, share resources, and support one another. I also want to see how social inequality changes when neighborhoods become more expensive. Too often, I’ve seen gentrification treated as a neutral market process, but I believe it is more than that. It changes who belongs in a neighborhood and who has access to the same opportunities.

On Friday, I met with a local tenant union. They explained how a proposed renovation would raise rents and displace several families. They shared stories of neighbors who left because they could no longer afford the same neighborhood. The conversation made me realize that gentrification affects people directly—not just through prices, but through access to housing, childcare, transportation, and reliable jobs.

I also noticed that the people who most influence these changes are often not the original residents. Property developers, real estate investors, and city planners shape the outcome, sometimes without hearing the people who live there every day. That feels unjust, and it makes my research feel urgent.

I’m still learning, of course. I do not yet have enough evidence to draw firm conclusions. But I am convinced that understanding the human consequences of gentrification is essential to making it fair. I want to write a paper that does not simply describe change, but shows how it affects people’s lives and communities.

By the way, I also enjoyed dinner at Hobbs Cafe today. The food, the conversation, and the quiet evening felt like a welcome pause between writing and research. Sometimes the most important work happens outside the page.

I’m heading home now. I hope my paper will help others see the full picture.
