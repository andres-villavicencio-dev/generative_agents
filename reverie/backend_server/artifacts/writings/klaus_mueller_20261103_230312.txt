Today, I have spent the day writing and researching gentrification—specifically how it affects families and communities in low-income neighborhoods. I started by visiting the Oak Hill College library, where I gathered interviews with residents and reviewed documents on property taxes, zoning changes, and displacement. The library gave me a quiet place to focus, and the people I spoke with helped me see the issue from different angles.

I began with the strongest emotion: grief. Many of the families I interviewed have lived in the same neighborhoods for decades. Their homes, community gardens, and small businesses have become landmarks, while they are being asked to move or sell their homes to make way for new developments. One young mother explained that her neighborhood had once been a safe place where neighbors helped one another. After gentrification, she said, “It became harder to find people who understood us. We still share food and support each other, but the community has changed.”

The effects extend beyond economics. When families leave, their children lose access to the schools and social networks that built their confidence. Intergenerational relationships also change. Older residents can no longer participate in neighborhood decisions, while younger residents may arrive only to spend money and move on quickly. I am beginning to believe that gentrification is not merely a change in housing. It is a reshaping of relationships, trust, and community identity.

Writing this research has been difficult. I want to show that people’s experiences cannot be explained by statistics alone. I also want to avoid blaming the displaced families. Their choices, however difficult, are shaped by the same forces that drive change. I hope my work helps neighbors understand that these neighborhoods are not disappearing—they are being transformed.

By the end of the day, I felt more committed than before. I know that my research may not solve everything, but it could give people a clearer picture of what is happening. I will continue reading, listening, and asking questions. I believe that understanding these changes is the first step toward protecting the communities I care about.
