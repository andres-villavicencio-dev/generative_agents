Monday, February 13

I woke at seven and walked across the campus before the first bell. The library felt quiet, but I had a paper that needed a clear path. I arrived at Hobbs Cafe around four to eat a small lunch, then returned to my desk at seven. By five, dinner was ready, and I had enough time to finish the research paper.

I began tonight with a fresh thought: gentrification in low-income communities often changes not only the buildings but the people, the institutions, and the community itself. When a neighborhood begins to rise in value, housing prices tend to increase. Young people and families who have lived there for years may be priced out. Small businesses close when their rent rises. Public services that once supported these residents may be cut or redirected elsewhere.

At Oak Hill College, I learned how sociology helps us see the larger patterns. I’ve read about displacement, about trust erosion, about who can afford to change the status quo. I’ve also read about the people who try to keep communities together. I want to know what happens when the people most affected are excluded from the conversation. I want to know whether their voices are heard, how they are heard, and what the community does when they are not.

For now, I will keep the paper focused. I will compare data from different neighborhoods, look for the stories behind the statistics, and consider how gentrification interacts with other forces—disinvestment, policy, race, and history. I am not trying to prove that gentrification is bad. I am trying to understand its consequences and find ways to make the process more inclusive.

The research paper is still a work in progress. I still have questions about funding, policy, housing, and community organization. But I have a clearer direction. I will keep asking, listening, and looking closely at the evidence. By the end of the semester, I will have more than a paper. I will have a way of thinking about social change, and a way of supporting people who believe that justice can come from the margins.
