My name is Klaus Mueller. I’m twenty, attending Oak Hill College, and I’m currently writing a research paper about gentrification in low-income communities. I’ve been reading a lot lately, especially how neighborhoods change when wealth and opportunity shift. I want to understand the people who lose their homes, the changes they endure, and what communities can learn from one another.

Gentrification sounds like a positive story at first. New businesses, better schools, and increased property values often seem like reasons to celebrate. But when the changes are built around displacement, they can feel unfair. A gentrifier may help a neighborhood by bringing a coffee shop, but if that shop forces older residents out, the community has been altered before the new business even operates. I’m concerned that the benefits remain concentrated in the hands of those who can afford them.

I’ve also noticed how these changes can affect the people living in the neighborhoods. Residents often find their jobs cut, their housing unaffordable, and their sense of belonging eroded. I want to explore whether gentrification follows the same pattern as other forms of displacement, or whether there are differences in how communities respond. I’m especially interested in the voices of those who have been affected.

My research question is simple: How do changes in income, housing, and social capital shape the lives of low-income residents in gentrifying neighborhoods? To answer it, I’ve been reading about housing policy, urban planning, and community resilience. I also want to consider how I can contribute, not just observe.

As I write, I keep thinking about the people I’ve read about. They’ve been changed, and sometimes unfairly. I hope my work helps them feel seen rather than assumed. I also hope it gives other communities a chance to see what they can learn from each other. I want to believe that gentrification can be a force for connection, but only when it is not the price of displacement. I want to write about that carefully, respectfully, and with enough evidence to make the argument stand on its own.
