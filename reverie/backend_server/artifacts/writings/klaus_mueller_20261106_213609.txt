I began the evening writing early, after dinner at Hobbs Cafe. The city felt unusually still, which made me want to focus instead on what had changed here and how the changes had shaped my research paper.

Tonight, I’m comparing my research paper with the essay I’ve been reading. Both deal with gentrification, but the essay’s tone is more personal, while my paper tends to be analytical. The essay describes how the community has become unfamiliar to the people who lived here before. It does not ask why the changes happened, nor does it examine the policy choices that made them possible. It is a good portrait of displacement, but it leaves the structural forces behind the scenes.

My paper, by contrast, asks how displacement connects to broader patterns of inequality. I have looked at housing policies, neighborhood investment, and the ways that change often benefits developers while displacing residents. The essay does not explicitly address these connections, but it makes that point implicitly.

I would like the essay to begin with the people, not the policies. A gentrification story is incomplete without explaining who benefits, who is displaced, and how power has shifted. My paper can then use the essay’s observations as a starting point for deeper analysis.

Reading the essay reminded me that social justice is not only about organizing around existing structures. It is also about seeing those structures with care, asking what they hide, and imagining alternatives. I want to write a paper that honors the community’s history and dignity while acknowledging the forces that have changed it.

The comparison has been useful. It has not replaced my research, and I do not want to treat the essay merely as evidence. I want to treat its perspective as another way of looking at the same question.

I plan to revise the paper so that it connects directly with the essay’s strongest insights without simply copying its language. I also want to include more local examples and more direct evidence of displacement rather than general claims about change.

For now, I will put the essay aside, read a few more pages of my draft, and then begin another section. At least once more, I want to be sure that the people affected are not merely background characters.
