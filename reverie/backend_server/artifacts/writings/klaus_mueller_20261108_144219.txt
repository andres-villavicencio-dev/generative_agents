Over the last two months, I have been researching gentrification in low-income communities, and I’m hoping my paper will help others understand this changing neighborhood. Gentrification is not just a change in the price of a home or the rent. It is a transformation of who lives there, how that community is valued, and who is left behind.

The main drivers are often social, economic, and political. Rising housing costs push working-class and low-income residents out, while developers and investors arrive with higher rents and expectations. This creates a cycle in which the community loses its residents before it can attract them. At the same time, gentrification can reshape local institutions and cultural habits. A neighborhood may become more attractive, but it can also lose the diverse character that made it welcoming to many people.

In my research, I have seen how political decisions can shape these outcomes. Housing policies, zoning rules, property taxes, and funding for community services often determine who can remain. When the political process favors developers and wealthy interests, low-income residents can feel powerless. Conversely, when communities organize and advocate for affordable housing, people can influence the decisions that affect them.

I am especially concerned about the cultural side of the problem. When a neighborhood changes so quickly, the stories, routines, traditions, and relationships that make it unique can disappear. Children may lose their neighborhoods, and the local businesses and residents who once created a community may not be able to adapt to a new one. This erosion of cultural memory can make displacement feel more personal and painful.

To make this research useful, I want to move beyond describing the problem. I want to understand how different groups experience gentrification and how people can support communities in transition. My hope is that my paper will encourage more thoughtful conversations about housing, equity, and the value of a neighborhood beyond its market value.
