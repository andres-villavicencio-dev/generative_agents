Gentrification in Low-Income Communities: A Study of Displacement and Community Change

My research question is simple: How does gentrification affect low-income residents, and what are the lasting consequences for their homes, neighborhoods, and sense of belonging? I have been studying gentrification throughout the semester, and the evidence is becoming increasingly clear. In the communities I’ve observed, rising property values, increased rents, and new development have transformed familiar places into places that increasingly exclude the people who built them. At the same time, newcomers often bring different expectations and resources, creating a complex relationship between progress and displacement.

My thesis is that gentrification is rarely a neutral upgrade for a community. Instead, it is a process of change that can shift the balance of power, alter housing affordability, and reshape the social fabric of low-income neighborhoods. While investment and amenities may appear to benefit everyone, the displacement of long-term residents and the loss of community institutions often leave marginalized neighborhoods without the social support they need.

To answer this question, I have examined rent trends, housing availability, local business changes, and resident narratives in several low-income areas. I have spoken with residents, interviewed developers, and observed how public spaces and community organizations have changed. My findings suggest that gentrification can produce both gains and losses. People can find new jobs and better services, but they may also face rising costs, housing insecurity, and a weakening of community cohesion.

The next phase of my research will focus on specific neighborhoods, identify which residents are most vulnerable, and explore ways to mitigate displacement while preserving the benefits of development. I hope this paper will encourage policymakers and neighborhood leaders to consider gentrification not only as an economic opportunity, but as a process of community transformation that requires attention, equity, and ongoing listening.
