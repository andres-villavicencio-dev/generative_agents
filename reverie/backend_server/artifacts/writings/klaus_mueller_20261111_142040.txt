Gentrification does more than change a neighborhood’s appearance. It begins with rising rents, which force households of low and moderate income to move out. As residents leave, neighborhoods lose a mix of income groups, shops, and small businesses, and their values begin to shift. The process is rarely fair. It creates a cycle in which displaced residents feel displaced again as housing prices rise, while new owners and residents with greater financial power arrive.

One clear effect is the loss of social cohesion. Long-term residents often lose homes, community networks, and the support systems that help families cope with financial difficulty. Families may move to less familiar neighborhoods where they have no connections, making it harder to find reliable housing, friends, and support services. This can make it difficult to recover from economic hardship.

Another important effect is the displacement of local businesses. When families move, they take with them the customers and community support that helped small shops survive. Shops may close, local suppliers can no longer source materials, and the neighborhood loses the economic vitality that kept it viable in the first place. The result is often a more expensive place to live, but one that has fewer opportunities for the displaced people.

The economic effects are not limited to the people who are forced out. Rising rents increase the cost of living for everyone, including workers and service providers who depend on the area’s economy. Community spending may also shift, with government budgets redirected toward services that attract wealthier residents rather than those that help low-income residents.

I am still learning how these forces interact, but the evidence suggests that gentrification is not simply a neutral improvement. It is a process of change that can be transformative for some and deeply painful for others. Understanding its economic effects is the first step toward designing policies that protect the displaced while helping neighborhoods remain accessible and welcoming.
