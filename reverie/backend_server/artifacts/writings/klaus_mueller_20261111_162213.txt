I woke up at 7:00 a.m. and headed to the library at Oak Hill College. The campus felt quiet, but I was already thinking about how much the city changes when neighborhoods turn. By 12:30 p.m., I sat down at my desk and began writing my research paper on the effects of gentrification in low-income communities.

Reading the case study about community-based social justice projects made me realize that gentrification is rarely just a matter of rising rents. It is often a process of displacement, cultural erasure, and shifting power. The successful community projects I read emphasized listening, collaboration, and tangible participation. A resident can feel excluded even when the neighborhood feels safer. I wanted to explore whether those kinds of participatory approaches can make the transition more humane, or if they simply replicate the very structures they are meant to improve.

I also noticed the importance of looking at gentrification through different perspectives. A policy might be supported by developers, local businesses, and city officials who see it as progress. At the same time, residents and marginalized communities may see it as a threat to their homes, livelihoods, and cultural identity. My paper will try to situate these competing viewpoints within the broader context of social policy and urban development.

I also want to consider the role of institutions. Universities, public housing authorities, housing nonprofits, and nonprofit organizations have the capacity to support vulnerable communities, but they also depend on funding, political support, and resources. A project cannot succeed if it relies entirely on the goodwill of individuals.

By the time I finished dinner at 5:00 p.m., I felt tired, but also energized. I wanted to write about community resilience, equity, and the ways that people can work together to address injustice. I will return to this paper tomorrow and keep asking questions: Who benefits? Who is left behind? And how can we design solutions that protect both the progress we want and the dignity of the people we serve?
