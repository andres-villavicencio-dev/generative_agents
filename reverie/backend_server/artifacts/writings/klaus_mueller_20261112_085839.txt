Monday, February 13

I woke up at 7:00 a.m. and walked to the library before the sun came up. The campus felt quiet, but every street corner had a story waiting to be heard. My second section of the sociology paper was still in draft form. I had spent the morning gathering examples from Oak Hill College and nearby neighborhoods to understand how gentrification reshapes lives.

Writing the second section has felt like a real investigation. I’ve noticed the same pattern in the literature: rising rents push out long-term residents, change the character of a community, and alter the economic opportunities for people who have stayed there. Yet the stories I’ve gathered also reveal resistance. Neighbors have organized rent meetings, shared resources, and discussed how gentrification affects local businesses, housing affordability, and social cohesion. These perspectives make the research feel less like a list of statistics and more like an ongoing conversation about fairness.

I’m also beginning to wonder whether gentrification is the only force at work. I’ve noticed changes in local businesses, public spaces, and even access to services that are not explained by rising housing costs. A neighborhood’s character does not change in a straight line; it changes because different groups are entering, leaving, and staying in it.

I still have many questions. Which policies are most effective? How can we measure displacement accurately? And what role does education play in helping people navigate these shifts? These questions are still developing, but I am beginning to believe that my paper should not end at the end of the paper. It needs space for the voices of people who have been changed by the changes.

The library is still buzzing with students and conversations, and I still have a long way to go. But I do not think the work is finished just because I have finished writing the second section.
