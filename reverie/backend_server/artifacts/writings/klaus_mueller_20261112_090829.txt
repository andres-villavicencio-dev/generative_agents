By 5:00 p.m., I’ve been writing the fifth section of my sociology paper on policy responses to gentrification in low-income communities. I’ve read the literature on rising rents, displaced residents, community loss, and the tension between economic development and social stability. I feel strongly about this issue, and I want my paper to be useful—not just an exercise in analysis.

Gentrification rarely changes neighborhoods overnight. It often begins quietly: a new coffee shop, a renovated storefront, a slightly different street design, or a small increase in property values. These changes can make an area feel familiar to longtime residents while simultaneously making it difficult for them to remain there. I know this has happened in Oak Hill College’s surrounding areas, where families have found increasingly expensive housing and felt increasingly excluded from the community they helped build.

The evidence suggests that policies alone cannot solve gentrification. Rent controls and tenant protections may slow development, but without affordable housing, they often do not address the root problem: housing costs that are unreasonably high relative to what residents can earn. Similarly, neighborhood revitalization initiatives can inadvertently exclude those who do not have the resources to participate. A strong policy response needs affordable housing, support for small businesses, investment in local schools and transit, and community leadership.

I hope my paper helps students and policymakers see that gentrification is not inevitable. People can organize, advocate, and advocate for change, even when the odds seem difficult. I am kind to people who are affected, but I also want my research to be honest: the situation is not easy, and progress should not be measured only by the success of one neighborhood.

I’m still not sure which policy approach will work best. That’s why I’m taking this paper seriously. I want to move beyond idealistic assumptions and consider how different communities can respond together. Tonight, I’ll write the next paragraph.
