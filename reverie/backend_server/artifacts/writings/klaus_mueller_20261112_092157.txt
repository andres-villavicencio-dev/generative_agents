I am writing this paper to understand how gentrification affects residents of low-income neighborhoods. I know it feels like a simple description of rising property values, but I need to find out what actually happens to people there.

The first thing I want to understand is how displacement works. Gentrification changes the community’s character, making it more expensive and difficult for long-term residents to stay. Even when people keep their homes, they may lose access to reliable transportation, community centers, and other neighborhood services. I have read about the “displacement spiral”: residents leave, property values rise, and new residents—who may have larger budgets—move in, displacing the original residents again. This cycle is especially dangerous for people with limited financial or social resources.

I also want to examine the cultural and social consequences. When neighborhoods change so quickly, it can make community cohesion difficult to maintain. Local businesses may disappear, neighborhood gatherings may become impractical, and shared histories may be erased. These changes can weaken the social fabric that low-income communities have built over many years.

Finally, I want to consider gentrification not only as an economic force but as a form of social injustice. The process often benefits investors, developers, and property owners while concentrating wealth in a particular type of housing market. Meanwhile, the original residents face rising housing costs, reduced mobility, and a sense of being pushed out of their communities.

My research question is simple: what are the consequences of gentrification for residents of low-income communities? I want to answer it by looking at the economic, social, and cultural changes that occur before, during, and after displacement. I will also examine the voices of the people most affected and their experiences.

I hope this paper will show that gentrification is not simply a neutral market adjustment. It involves people, places, and relationships. It changes who lives there, what they can afford, and how they participate in their own communities. Understanding those effects is not just an academic exercise—it is an effort to recognize and address the injustice that often follows.
