The streets of Oak Hill look different when I walk them at night. I’ve been spending the last three evenings reading my sociology paper aloud, checking the flow of my argument without interruption. Tonight, as the streetlamps flicker on, I paused at the corner of 4th and Main to listen to the neighborhood below.

Gentrification is rarely shown as a simple story of displacement. It is usually presented as progress: new storefronts, better schools, upgraded infrastructure. But for the people who live there before the change, that presentation can feel like a lie. I’ve found that the most important part of understanding gentrification is the people who are already living in the places being changed. Their histories, their habits, and their fears matter more than the statistics.

My paper is about that tension. How does rapid growth in housing prices, business investment, and neighborhood appeal affect low-income residents? Who benefits from the changes? Who bears the cost? I am not asking whether gentrification is good or bad in abstract terms. I want to understand how the process changes ordinary lives.

I also want to hear different perspectives. I’ve been reading about displacement, housing policy, neighborhood development, and the ways identity and belonging are made or broken in urban spaces. Each book I’ve read has added a piece to the puzzle, but I still want to hear the people’s accounts.

I read my paper aloud tonight because sometimes an argument becomes clearer only when I can speak it to another person. Reading aloud helped me notice a gap in my opening paragraph. I had been talking about housing policy without mentioning the people affected by it. I should have made that mistake earlier, but the exercise reminded me that good writing is also an act of listening.

I’m proud of what I’ve learned so far. I’ve developed a clearer thesis, and I’ve begun to question the assumptions I’ve been carrying. I still have much to learn, but I feel less alone in it.

I’m heading home now. I’ll eat dinner at Hobbs Cafe, finish my reading, and go to bed around 11. I hope I’ll wake up with more questions, but I’m also hopeful that tonight’s reading will give me something to carry into the next day.
