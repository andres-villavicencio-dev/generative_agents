On Tuesday, February 14, I arrived at the library with a paper in hand. The gentrification project has been a long one, but I am finally checking in with Ayesha and reviewing the citations. The work feels less like an assignment and more like a conversation with a community that has been quietly changing around me.

My research focuses on the ways neighborhoods shift when new residents arrive and their existing residents lose access to essential services, housing, and social networks. The arguments are clear: displacement is rarely about one person’s decision. It is about economic opportunity, changing land values, and the uneven effects of growth. I want to show how gentrification can reproduce inequality, particularly when older residents are priced out before they have a chance to advocate for their own needs.

I am proud of what we have accomplished so far. The citations are becoming organized, the claims are getting specific, and I am noticing patterns that suggest a broader conversation about housing, neighborhood identity, and public investment. But I also recognize that I have not yet examined enough perspectives. I need to consider the voices of displaced families, long-term residents, and community organizers, not only the developers and policymakers who usually shape gentrification.

The feedback from community members has been encouraging. A few residents have pointed out that the paper has been too focused on policy without enough attention to lived experience. Others have shared stories about how local businesses have adapted while neighborhood amenities have declined. I intend to incorporate these insights into the next section. I am also considering ways to make the argument more accessible, perhaps by adding a section about housing affordability and community-led support.

I am still excited about the next part of the paper. I want to move beyond describing the problem and begin to imagine how different communities can respond. If my paper succeeds, it should not only explain what is happening but also show where meaningful change can begin. I will continue to review the citations carefully, keep the evidence close to hand, and ask myself whether each claim is supported by the communities I have spoken with.
