Monday, February 14

The library felt strangely quiet today, the kind of silence that always seems to make the noise inside of me louder. I arrived at 8:00 and spent the first hour checking in with Ayesha, reviewing my citations, and recording the dates and key findings from the gentrification sources. By 1:00, I had already begun working on the paper.

What surprised me most was the strength of the feedback I received from community members. They weren’t just saying, “This is interesting,” or “You’re wrong.” They were asking why gentrification feels so different to people who have lived here for decades. One member explained that when she first arrived, the neighborhood was safe and affordable. Today, she was priced out of her own home. “People talk about displacement without understanding who is being displaced,” she said. That single sentence changed everything for me.

I also found the research useful. A recent article showed how rising rents in Oak Hill have increased the cost of housing for residents who have lived there for years. The key finding was clear: gentrification rarely happens only to the wealthy. It affects people who have depended on the neighborhood for housing and daily life. That made the paper feel less like an academic exercise and more like a conversation about real lives.

My schedule was full, so I did not have time to finish the next section by bedtime. I focused on the most important points, revised the citations, and prepared the next section for tomorrow. I was proud of how much I had learned, but I also felt a little uncertain about how much I had done. Still, I was excited. Reading the community feedback made me feel connected to the people around me.

I plan to continue reviewing my notes and citations tomorrow. I want to make sure I keep the community’s insights at the center of my research. By the time I go to bed, I hope the paper feels complete enough to show what I’ve learned, and honest enough to recognize where I still need to ask more questions.
