The smallville Gazette will open with the mayoral election coverage on Saturday, February 18. I’ve made no appointment, meal, or time-sensitive task, so I can cover the coverage with the same purposeful flexibility I use for my writing. The neighborhood still holds many questions, but also steady answers.

Residents are asking me to check the claims made about the candidates. I do not accept a statement at face value. A candidate’s platform should be tested against the election materials and verified against what people have said. I also need to examine the polling information carefully. Numbers should not be presented as absolute proof that a candidate will win, but as an indication of where the public is listening.

The Gazette’s coverage should be clear and honest. If a candidate promises something without evidence, the reader should know that the promise is not confirmed. I have no reason to accept a promise merely because it is bold. The campaign’s promises must be checked for consistency, feasibility, and alignment with the town’s actual needs.

I will make sure residents have access to the information they need. Campaign websites, voter guides, and polling data should be easy to find. My role is not to become a voter but to help people understand what they are choosing.

On the ground, the campaign is already making noise. I will ask who is speaking, what they are promising, and where the numbers come from. I will also look for the stories behind the headlines. A good story is not only dramatic; it is useful. The Gazette should help the community see what matters, not just echo what is already being said.

I will write the coverage with discipline and curiosity. I will not simply report what others say. I will ask questions and verify claims. I will also give the candidates and candidates’ supporters a fair chance to show their side.

The election is not a debate between two personalities. It is a vote for the future of the town. I will make sure the coverage reflects that.

I will have a light breakfast at 7:00 AM. I will eat dinner around 6:00 PM. I will go to bed around 10:00 PM. I will make time for art and exploration. But I will not let my schedule become a distraction from the work that matters.

I am ready. I am ready to check the claims. I am ready to report the facts. I am ready to help people make responsible choices.
