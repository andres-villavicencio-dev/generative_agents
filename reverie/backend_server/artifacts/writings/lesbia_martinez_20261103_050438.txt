On Saturday, February 18, I began my day with the same steady rhythm I have carried for many years: six o’clock for the alarm, seven for breakfast, and six for dinner. No appointments, no urgent tasks, and no pressure. That is precisely what I need. I am here to help the Gazette cover the mayoral election without becoming either a cheerleader for whoever wins or a critic of anyone else’s campaign.

I will look at every piece of campaign coverage carefully. An incomplete statement about a candidate’s policy can look like a promise if it is not supported by facts. A misleading claim about a candidate’s experience can appear impressive, even if the details are unclear. I will read every headline, every quotation, and every statistic, noting which pieces deserve correction and which need more explanation.

I also want residents to understand what they can do about the election. This is not simply about helping people count their votes. It is about making sure the information they receive is responsible, clear, and complete. Candidates should make their platforms concrete and explain how they intend to carry them out. Voters should be able to compare candidates fairly and understand the trade-offs between different choices.

When I review the Gazette, I will also check whether the pieces tell the full story. A story about a mayoral race should not be reduced to a list of slogans. It should explain the context, acknowledge the difficulties, and encourage people to vote responsibly. I will also make sure that names, dates, and figures appear exactly as they are used in the coverage.

My day may be scattered. I may spend time with Adam Smith, Hailey Johnson, Mei Lin, Yuriko Yamamoto, Maria Urban, or Ana, discussing how the coverage should be presented. I may make tea, walk the neighborhood, or explore a small piece of art. But my purpose will remain the same: to help the Gazette tell honest stories and give residents accurate information.

The election is important because it is about who will serve the town. I will not change the outcome, but I will make sure the debate is fair, the coverage is responsible, and the voters are fully informed. I am ready to do that.
