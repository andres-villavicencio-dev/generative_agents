Saturday, February 18

The Gazette coverage for the mayoral election has been thorough, but it should have felt less like a checklist and more like a conversation. Too often, we hear names and promises, then move on. The story behind the story matters.

Resident access to campaign information should not be an afterthought. It must begin before the campaign begins. We need clear, up-to-date lists of candidates, their platforms, campaign promises, and contact information. We need to make sure that residents can see what the candidates offer without being pushed toward one. The Gazette should publish a simple, honest comparison of platforms, highlighting the differences rather than smoothing them away.

Responsible voting must be treated with the same seriousness as election coverage. We should explain how each vote is counted, what is required, and how a person can vote. Accessibility matters: candidates should not assume that every resident can read the full campaign literature or navigate an online ballot. We should offer plain-language summaries, translated materials, and options that do not require prior knowledge of the election system.

Local stories are not decorative. They reveal who the community is and what it values. A mayor’s address should not be celebrated merely because the press praised it. The Gazette should ask whether the mayor actually listens, whether the promises match what happens on the ground, and whether residents feel heard. We need to keep the mayor’s story honest, not merely entertaining.

I have asked Adam Smith, Hailey Johnson, Mei Lin, Yuriko Yamamoto, Maria Urban, and Ana to contribute essays that explore these points. Each should bring a different perspective, but the common thread should be the same: the election should be a community conversation, not a performance.

On Saturday, I have no confirmed appointment, meal, or time-sensitive task. My day remains purposeful and flexible. I have made time for food, art, and exploring the neighborhood, but I am not rushing. I want the Gazette to reflect that same balance: clear, honest, and grounded.
