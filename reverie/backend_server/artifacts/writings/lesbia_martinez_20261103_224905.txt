The town is waking up, and so is I. My coffee is still steaming beside the newspaper when I sit down, and the election coverage is already giving me a good start. I know the candidates well enough to notice the gaps between their promises and what they’ve done. I’ve been asked by Adam Smith and Hailey Johnson to write the articles, and I’ve edited the candidate profiles and policy comparisons so they remain concise and neutral. That is the job: to show each side fairly without allowing either to sound like they have already won.

The real question is where the money goes. I have asked Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto to explain how the community fund should be spent. Mei’s answer is clear: keep the fund flexible enough to help when things go wrong. Yuriko adds that the same money must support schools, public works, and emergency services. Both agree that spending should be transparent and accountable.

We are not deciding every issue in this campaign. What matters is how the town chooses to move forward. Candidates can talk about growth, jobs, or safety, but the decisions should come from the people they represent. Candidates should explain which problems they have studied and what they would do if they were in charge. They should also answer the questions voters have asked about taxes, services, and long-term planning.

The Gazette needs to keep this honest. We should not turn the election into a debate about who is more charismatic. We should report on what has been accomplished, what remains to be done, and what the candidates have left to prove. If a promise is made, it should be kept. If it is not kept, it should be explained.

I am not asking the candidates to promise anything today. I am asking them to be clear, to listen, and to let the people choose.
