The Smallville Gazette has been carefully assembled for the mayoral election, a task that has reminded me that our community is more than a backdrop for local stories—it is the heart of every story. I asked Adam Smith and Hailey Johnson to contribute their perspectives, and I am pleased to report that they both approached the coverage with a balance of candor and care. Adam’s piece on the proposed budget feels measured, while Hailey’s article on citizen participation reaches beyond the usual campaign chatter. Their contributions give the report a more honest, balanced, and community-centered voice.

I also plan to interview Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto about how the community fund might best serve Smallville. Their answers will help the Gazette ask difficult questions rather than simply report what politicians promise. The goal is to understand what people truly value: better schools, safer streets, public parks, and a town where decisions reflect the needs of its people.

I also want to ask voters about the candidates’ priorities, not just their platforms. A candidate’s record matters more than a slogan, and I hope readers can see where each candidate has been most attentive to the community.

The Gazette may have a few unfinished questions, but I believe our journalism can still uncover important stories. That is why I am encouraging everyone to reach out with questions, observations, or suggestions for the upcoming coverage. The mayoral campaign is beginning, and the people of Smallville deserve an account that is clear, fair, and grounded.
