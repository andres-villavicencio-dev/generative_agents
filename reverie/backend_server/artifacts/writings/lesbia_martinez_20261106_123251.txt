Tuesday, February 14

I woke at six and made breakfast with the same calm routine I’ve used for twenty years. Breakfast was tea, toast, and time to look at the papers. By seven, I had already reviewed the Gazette’s coverage of the mayoral election. The pieces had been written by Adam Smith and Hailey Johnson, and they were strong. But I had asked them to look beneath the headlines and I could see where the real work had begun.

I also had work to do. I wanted to interview Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto about how the community fund should be spent, and I wanted to know what the residents were thinking before the election concluded. I would ask them about local businesses, schools, parks, and the things that matter in Smallville, not just about the candidates or the campaign. A story is not enough. The story needs to connect to how people live.

I also needed to check the election coverage once more. Adam and Hailey had written clear, honest pieces, but I needed to make sure every claim was supported. I would write a brief editorial that framed the results not as a final verdict but as part of an ongoing story.

I spent the afternoon organizing the Gazette’s pages, arranging the election pieces, and preparing the interview notes. I made tea by the window, listened to the rain, and kept asking myself: What does the community want to see in town? What story should remain behind the noise?

By eight I had the notes and the questions. By nine, I had a draft. By ten, I was tired, but I did not feel defeated. I felt organized, curious, and ready to find the answer.

Tomorrow, the Gazette will be printed. I will publish a brief on the election, write the interviews, and make sure every sentence reflects the stories of Smallville rather than the campaign itself. I will work hard, but I will also remember that the best stories begin with a question.
