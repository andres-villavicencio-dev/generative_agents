The town is preparing for a mayoral election, and the question is no longer about who will speak loudest. It has become about who has the clearest mind, the most reliable record, and the best way to listen.

For two weeks, I have been gathering names, facts, and accounts. The candidates’ platforms look familiar. What is missing is the real history of Smallville—the people who built our streets, the businesses that keep the town alive, and the community that makes every decision matter.

This is why I am asking Adam Smith and Hailey Johnson to write the coverage. Adam, I want you to approach the story as a historian. The election is not merely a choice between two offices; it is a choice between how we organize our lives and how we support each other. Your piece should not simply announce the candidates. It should show why their policies matter to the people living here.

Hailey, I would ask you to write with the same curiosity. You do not need to prove that one person is perfect. What matters is whether their ideas can survive the scrutiny of voters, neighbors, and the town council. Ask about the candidates’ backgrounds, their commitments, and what they would do if money and power were no longer the only reasons they spoke.

I also want you to interview Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto about how the community fund should be spent. They know the details better than anyone else. Ask them which programs would improve our schools, make our streets safer, create jobs, or simply make every citizen feel welcome. Let them speak for the people the candidates may not yet have heard.

The final story for the Gazette should not end with promises. It should end with evidence, with people’s names, and with a clear question: What will Smallville do next? We do not need a slogan. We need honest answers. And I have been waiting for someone to ask the right ones.
