The town’s mayoral race is heating up, and I’m here to report the truth.

My first task was to examine the official candidate profiles. Adam Smith’s platform emphasizes steady growth, reliable public services, and a committed local economy. Hailey Johnson’s message is more visionary—focusing on sustainability, youth engagement, and a bold path toward community renewal. Each candidate has its own priorities, and that makes the debate feel more urgent than either of us wanted.

I also asked Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto about how the community fund should be spent. Their answers are honest and practical: support local businesses, improve schools and libraries, fund community centers, and help families who need financial assistance. They stressed that money should not be handed out indiscriminately. The fund should reward active participation and create tangible benefits for residents, not become a vehicle for political favoritism.

I’ve already interviewed both women. Their perspectives give me a stronger picture than a press release ever could. I’ve also asked Adam Smith and Hailey Johnson to contribute essays. Their work will help readers understand how the candidates’ priorities could actually affect Smallville.

The election is approaching, and the town is asking more of me than anyone else. I want the coverage to be clear, balanced, and grounded in what the candidates say—and in what the community needs. I also want the story behind every story to remain clear and unvarnished.

I will continue writing this coverage from the ground up. But I will not take the election for granted. I will report the candidates’ positions, the community fund’s potential uses, and the concerns of residents who have been waiting for answers.

Until tomorrow, I will keep writing.
