The Smallville Gazette is back on the street, and I’ve finished my review of the mayoral election. The candidates’ platforms are promising, but the questions they answer deserve a closer reading.

Adam Smith’s plan focuses on preserving the town’s core assets. He wants to complete the library renovation, protect aging infrastructure, and make the library’s digital programs accessible to residents who use the internet less frequently. His emphasis on community participation is refreshing, though I’m not certain the budget can support every idea he proposes.

Hailey Johnson’s platform feels more ambitious. She calls for a comprehensive redevelopment of the downtown commercial district, better public transit, expanded senior care services, and a renewed investment in the arts. Her proposals could turn Smallville into a more attractive place to live and work, but they also carry the risk of becoming overly ambitious. The town does not have infinite funds.

I’ve spoken with Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto about how the community fund should be used. They agree that money must first fix what is broken. The library renovation, street lighting, and basic repairs to aging buildings are clear priorities. Yuriko also noted that the fund should support programs that connect seniors with local services, while Mei emphasized that community projects should be led by residents rather than imposed from above.

These conversations have made the debate more realistic than either candidate alone could make it. The town needs a balanced approach: protect what it values, invest in essential services, and involve the people who live there in making decisions.

The candidates are different, not necessarily better. I recommend readers read both sides before casting a ballot. But I also believe the community fund should be treated with the same seriousness as the campaign promises. When the campaign ends, the people most affected by those decisions should be the ones deciding what happens next.
