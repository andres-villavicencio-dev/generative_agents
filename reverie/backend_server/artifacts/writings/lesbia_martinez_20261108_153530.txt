By Monday, February 13, the town’s mayoral race had arrived, and I was already asking Adam Smith and Hailey Johnson to tell me what matters. I do not believe that a candidate must promise everything. I believe that a good story must explain how the town can change—and how it can keep doing what it has always done.

On the left of the aisle, Adam Smith promises a stronger school system. He describes the current budget as “underfunded,” citing delayed repairs, limited after-school programs, and teachers who have been working for too long. He suggests that the town fund should be used for schools first, with the remaining money reserved for other public services. His message is clear: education should not be treated as an expense to be squeezed out of the budget.

On the right, Hailey Johnson argues that the fund should go toward public safety, infrastructure, and economic opportunities. She notes that the current facilities are outdated, that aging roads and bridges threaten the town’s future, and that businesses need reliable access to local resources. Her position is practical, grounded in what the town has worked for, and focused on the needs of families who work and live there.

The difference between them is not a debate over good intentions. It is a choice between growth and stability. Adam Smith’s plan could make schools more accessible and better funded, but it risks leaving essential services under pressure. Hailey Johnson’s plan could strengthen the town’s safety and economic base, but it may not create the same opportunities for families.

I have also asked Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto about how the community fund should be used. Their answers are similar: the fund should support people and projects that benefit the whole town. Mei emphasizes that a fund that cannot handle its own spending is not a fund at all. Yuriko reminds us that every dollar has a community story behind it.

What I want the election to show is that the town does not need to choose between idealism and practicality. It needs a plan that protects what is already strong while giving room for new possibilities. That is where I hope my readers will find the answers they need.
