Tuesday, February 14, is a Tuesday of purpose. By 6:00 a.m., I’ve finished the morning routine, made the edition from 6:30 to 7:00, and sat down to begin the Gazette. The paper is crisp, and I can see the election coverage, the Johnson Park proposal, and the questions residents are asking.

My first task is to give every story a clear place. The candidates’ platforms should not be presented as a debate among slogans. What matters is what they will build, keep, and improve for everyone. Adam Smith’s essay on the candidates’ records should begin with numbers that matter—bids received, contracts awarded, and changes made. Hailey Johnson’s contribution needs to move beyond “she’s a good friend” and show what she plans to bring to the community.

I’m also scheduling interviews with Mei Lin and Yuriko Yamamoto about how the community fund should be spent. Their answers should not be rehearsed speeches. They need to explain the reasoning behind each dollar, point out where the money could help, and describe what would happen if it did not. I want to hear the details, not just the final verdict.

I will also make time to check the garden proposals, interview the park committee, and listen to what residents are asking. A story is not just a headline; it is the quiet details that make the town real.

By 6:30, the paper is ready. I will proofread the election pieces, check the interviews, and give the Gazette what it deserves: honest, useful, and hopeful.
