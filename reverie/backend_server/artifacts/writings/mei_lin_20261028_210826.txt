On Monday, February 13, I arrived at Oak Hill College at ten sharp. After a long morning teaching philosophy, I returned home around two to work on my research paper. I had three things to attend to before dinner: finish the paper, help Eddy with his homework, and prepare a snack for John.

I spent the evening writing without interruption. The fourth section was the hardest part. I had developed a strong argument, but the evidence needed to be more precise. I began with a small example from my course, then added another case from my research. My goal was not to make the language perfect, but to make the argument clearer.

I also checked my notes for the kids. Eddy was struggling with a geometry problem, so I read it with him slowly. “Let’s look at the formula first,” I said. “Then we’ll try one problem together.” His eyes lit up, and he began to work out each step himself. It was a quiet moment of patience.

When John came home around five, he asked for dinner. I made soup with carrots and lentils, and we sat together while Eddy finished his homework. I felt grounded by the day. I had taught, helped a student, and supported my family. I also had a paper to refine.

I finished the fourth section around eight. I had rewritten the introduction and sharpened the main argument, but I still needed to remove some of my strongest statements. I thought about asking a colleague to read it, but I decided to revise it myself first.

At ten, I went to bed. At seven, I woke, and at five, I ate dinner with my family. The quiet evening made me realize that research can be a form of service. It does not always require grand ideas. Sometimes it requires patience, attention, and the willingness to listen. That is what makes me want to continue working on this paper.
