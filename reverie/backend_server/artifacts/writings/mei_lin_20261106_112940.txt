February 14, Tuesday

I began the day with the seven-thirty alarm, before my body could even ask for permission to wake up. The morning routine was the anchor of my day: tea, fresh water, and a brief moment to clear my mind. Then I ate breakfast and went to work.

Teaching felt like a living conversation, not a list of rules. I walked among my students, listened to their questions, and helped them find the answers they were ready to share. One of my younger students asked why I kept asking for help. I told him that mistakes are not failures—they are the evidence of learning. That simple answer stayed with me as I began my research paper.

Research has been quieter, but it has also been more rewarding. I have been reading and comparing primary sources on the topic of community care, focusing on how people support one another in times of need. I am especially interested in how kindness can be translated into action. At the same time, I want to honor the work of those who care for me and the people around me.

Today, my schedule was full: classes, meals, childcare, community work, and the paper. I felt tired at the end of the day, but purposeful. I wanted to make the Hobbs Cafe shift tomorrow clear, so Marisol could help. I also wanted to check the Johnson Park bulletin board, so Latoya could locate the corner. I made sure each item had a clear label, so the work would not lose its way.

Evening was reserved for reading and creative writing. I did not rush to finish everything. I simply wrote what I had noticed, recorded what I wanted to remember, and left space for what mattered most.

I still have too much to do. I still have too much to care for. But I also have the energy to help people reach their goals. That is what I am here for. I am tired, yes. But I am not empty.
