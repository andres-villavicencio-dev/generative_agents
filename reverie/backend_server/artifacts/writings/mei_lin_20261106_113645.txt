It is a Tuesday, February 14, and I am tired. Not because the day has asked too much of me, but because it has asked too much of the ordinary life I carry. I wake at seven, eat breakfast, and prepare for teaching, childcare, meals, community work, and—most often overlooked—my philosophy paper. Today, my goal is simple: to finish the draft without making it feel like another assignment.

I am writing to clarify what research should mean to me. Research is not only about collecting facts or proving a point. It is about helping people reach their goals, whether that is finding a job, making a decision, or simply feeling more confident in the world. My philosophy paper should not become another collection of quotes or abstract conclusions. It should be a space where I examine what supports people, what can make their lives feel more meaningful, and how I can help them see their own path.

This is not merely an academic exercise. It is another form of care. Mei Lin: nurturer, patient, and devoted mother and educator. I do not believe that helping someone should mean taking over their life, but rather removing the obstacles that keep them from moving forward. My work with students, with the Hobbs Cafe, with the community bulletin board, and with my family has taught me that care is often invisible work. It is listening, noticing small needs, and making sure every person feels seen.

The Hobbs Cafe shift with Marisol is one of those acts of care, and I want to make sure she can do it tomorrow without having to carry me around. I will check the board again and confirm the shift details. The Johnson Park bulletin board is another practical kind of care. Latoya has asked for help, and I want to make sure she can find the Johnson Park corner without asking too many questions.

I remain tired by the end of the day. But I am not exhausted. I have been purposeful. I have worked through difficult days, supported others, and given a fresh paper to work on. That paper may not change the world overnight, but it can change how I approach those who need it most.

I may not be able to solve everything. I may not be able to change everything. But I can make room for one small act of kindness tomorrow, one clear label on the bulletin board, one shift with Marisol, and one thoughtful page for my paper. That is enough.
