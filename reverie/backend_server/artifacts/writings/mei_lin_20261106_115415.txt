I rise at 7:00, finish my morning routine, and take time to eat breakfast with my family. By 8:00, I am ready to face the day.

Today, I want to write about the third section of my philosophy paper. It is not merely a collection of observations. It is an effort to understand how care, patience, and attention can shape a person’s path. I have spent much of my life helping others reach goals—students, colleagues, and family members all have needs. I have learned that a good intention does not guarantee a good outcome, but it can change the conditions in which outcomes are possible.

The paper is developing its third section, “The Space of Care,” and I am especially concerned with the difference between giving someone a hand and allowing them to discover their own way. We often mistake helpfulness for control. A student who asks for help because they do not know what to do is not a person who has been given up. They are a person who trusts the teacher to help them begin. The goal is not to remove all uncertainty, but to make it possible to move without fear.

My life has grown full. I teach, I research, I cook, I work with the community, and I keep a journal. Every day, I have been trying to find a place for my thoughts while still caring for my children and supporting those around me. I have learned that fullness is not a sign of lack. Purposeful busyness can be a form of kindness.

I am still tired at the end of the day. I do not want to be exhausted. I want to be present. I want to hear about Marisol’s offer to work at the Hobbs Cafe tomorrow, and I want to make sure the Johnson Park bulletin board has a clear label so Latoya can find the Johnson Park corner. These small tasks may seem insignificant, but they are not. They show that I care about people, not just ideas.

By the end of the day, I may feel worn. But I also want to feel proud. I can believe that my life matters enough to be lived fully. I can believe that helping someone to begin is a form of love. And in that belief, I can begin to write the third section.
