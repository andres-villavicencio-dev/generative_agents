Tuesday, February 14, has felt full. After seven hours of wakefulness, I woke at 7:00 a.m. and spent the first twenty minutes making tea, washing the dishes, and putting the children to bed. Then came breakfast. By five o’clock, dinner was ready and the day had begun.

I do not expect every task to be perfect. I only expect to show up for the people who need help. That is what I have been trying to learn in my research paper: that philosophy is not about discovering answers that change everything. It is about helping people see the paths they can take. It is about listening, asking questions, and giving them the space to move forward.

The papers are not finished. The creative book is not finished. My research is not finished. But the people around me are. Marisol is ready for the Hobbs Cafe shift tomorrow, and I will let her know. The Johnson Park bulletin board still needs a clear label, so Latoya will not have to wander around looking for the corner. I will check the board before bed, and I will make sure the label stays there.

Today I also made a cup of tea and sat down to revise the paper. I began with a simple question: What is the one purpose that gives my days meaning? The answer became clear. My purpose is not to become a writer or a researcher. It is to support people in reaching their goals.

Tiredness can make this feel heavy, but it does not have to be the end of the day. I will eat dinner at five, go to bed around eleven, and tomorrow I will begin again. One thoughtful question, one act of kindness, one conversation can make all the difference.
