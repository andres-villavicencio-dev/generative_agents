Tuesday, February 14

I woke at seven and made my bed before checking the calendar. Today felt full, but purposeful. I taught, researched, prepared meals, and worked with my community, yet I still made room for my philosophy paper and a creative book I was writing. At home, I still have Marisol, my children, and the meals I prepare. The world does not stop because I am tired.

At the Hobbs Cafe, I will let Marisol know about the shift tomorrow. I will check the board, and if it still needs attention, I will make sure the Johnson Park bulletin board has a clear label so Latoya can find the Johnson Park corner. I want to see that everyone has something to find, and I want to see that my efforts are noticed.

I also want to finish my paper. It is not merely a collection of ideas; it is an attempt to understand what makes a life worth living. Sometimes I ask myself whether I am helping people reach their goals or simply asking them to come along with me. The difference is important. A good lesson is not about filling a room with noise. It is about opening doors.

At night, I go to bed around eleven. I wake at seven, eat dinner around five, and begin my day with a cup of tea. I am tired at the end of the day, but I am also grateful. Gratitude is not a sign of weakness. It is the first step toward a life we can sustain.

I will not write a perfect paper. I will write a honest one. I will revise, I will listen, and I will let the paper change with me. That is enough for now.
