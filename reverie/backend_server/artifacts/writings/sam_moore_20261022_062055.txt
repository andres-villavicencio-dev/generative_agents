Today the phones rang often enough to remind me that a campaign is built one conversation at a time. I answered as many calls as I could, then sat down with my call log and sorted every name and number. In the Navy, we learned that a loose thread can unravel an entire operation; the same is true of a neglected call or a promise that never gets followed up. So I reviewed each conversation, updated the outstanding contacts, and made sure every follow-up message was clear, courteous, and ready to send.

One thing I keep learning is that people are not looking for a polished speech. They want to know that someone heard them. A few callers simply needed to ask whether their concerns had been noted. Others wanted to know how to help. I tried to answer honestly and make no promises I could not keep. “That’s on my list” is worth more than “We’ll take care of it,” especially when somebody has been passed from one office to another.

I’m encouraged by the calls, but not foolishly so. Good news can be a steady breeze, not a sudden squall, and a good navigator plans for changing weather. I’ve marked the messages awaiting final checks and confirmed that the completed ones are ready to go. There is satisfaction in a clean desk and an accounted-for list.

I’m also hoping to compare notes with Yuriko after her visit to Hobbs Cafe. We still need to settle on a time and place for our conversation, but I want to hear who felt welcomed and who felt overlooked. Taken together with what we observed at Johnson Park, her impressions may tell us more about this community than another round of secondhand opinions. Sometimes one person noticing who has no chair says more than ten people praising the decorations.

Tomorrow, I’ll keep answering, keep following up, and keep my ears open. That has always been the work.
