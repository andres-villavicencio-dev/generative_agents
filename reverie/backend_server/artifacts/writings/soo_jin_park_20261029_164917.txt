Today was another Friday—the kind of day when I’ve finished drawing, sold a few prints, and have my phone buzzing with an unpaid delivery notice. I’m at Willow Court 12 with Rafael, and he’s the only person I’ve ever met who doesn’t seem to be trying to sell me lunch. I’ve been sketching the whole town lately, including the corners of Johnson Park where I’ve found a stray cat and an old mailbox that still remembers all the old stories about the town.

The picture book is a big one. Willow Creek is a small town where everyone’s been there long enough to know the names of every person who’s ever lived there. I’m writing the main characters and a few supporting ones. I’m going to make the townspeople feel real. The Mayor should be proud but tired. The baker should be stubborn and kind. The librarian should be curious, if a little too serious about history. The kids should be curious, too.

My motivation is simple: I want this book to feel like a place we all know. I want Willow Creek to feel familiar, not like a poster in a museum. I want readers to see the people in them. I also want them to see that kindness can exist even when money is tight.

Tonight, I’m going to check the bulletin board for another odd job. I might find myself helping someone with a car, fixing a printer, or doing something else entirely. I don’t have a big plan. I just want to be useful.

Maybe I’ll sketch the new bus route before I go home. Maybe I’ll draw the bakery owner. Maybe I’ll just sit at Hobbs Cafe and draw the people who work there. I’ve been sketching for a long time. I’m not trying to be the greatest artist in the world. I’m trying to capture the small details that make a town feel alive.

I’ll go home, eat dinner around eight, and check the board before midnight. Let’s see what’s available.
