I’m still sketching the town square in my notebook, and I have to admit, it’s taking me longer than the draft needs.

Tonight, I’m working on the opening of Smallville. I want the first few pages to feel like the real town is waking up, not a polished storybook. A quiet morning, a few curious faces, and one small detail—the way the light changes when the streetlamps turn on—could carry the whole mood.

I started by drawing Willow Court from the corner, but I was too busy getting lost in the details. Rafael asked if I had a better angle. I told him to keep sketching, and I let him help. I liked his energy. He’s like the town itself: useful, slightly awkward, and always ready to ask, “What’s next?”

The pacing is the hardest part. I need the beginning fast enough to keep readers hooked, but slow enough for each detail to sink in. I want them to feel like they’re standing in the square, watching the world settle. No sudden jumps. No over-the-top speeches. Just enough action to get us moving.

Money is tight right now, and that’s why I’m checking the bulletin board every Friday. There’s a small commission for a town illustration, a neighborhood portrait, and a few small commissions for different projects. I don’t have the time to polish every commission, but I want to keep my hands busy and my income steady.

So I’ve got Rafael helping with the drawing, a few sketches for coffee, and maybe a small gig before the week ends. Tomorrow, I’ll sketch the streetlamps at dawn and write the first sentence. Let’s keep the story moving.
