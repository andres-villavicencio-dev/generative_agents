I’ve been sketching Willow Court 12 for hours today. The little girl in the blue dress keeps changing her expression. In the picture book, I want her to look curious but hesitant at the same time. I want her to notice the stranger, but not to jump straight at him. I want her to feel like she’s discovering someone important without making the whole world disappear.

The girl at the counter at Hobbs Cafe had a small smile when she saw me. I wanted to keep that moment. She looked nervous, but not afraid. The illustrator in me is reaching in and pulling out the softer parts of her expression. I want Soo to be recognizable. I want the eyes, the curve of the mouth, the quiet movement of her hand. Maybe the little bird will give her a clue. Maybe she’ll ask, “Who are you?”

I’ve been checking the bulletin board at Johnson Park. One of the notices is for a comic book, but there’s also a notice for a picture book that needs illustrations. I don’t have any pictures yet. I need a few more townpeople.

Soo is working on it. I’m not proud of it yet. The little girl has too many emotions, but I think that’s the point. I think a small story can say more than a big one.

I’m going home early. I’m hungry, and I need to sketch again. Maybe I can find a quiet corner by the river and draw the girl’s face. If she says her name, I’ll let her know who I’m sketching for. I want her to feel seen. Even if I can’t finish the whole picture.
