I’m working on the Smallville picture book tonight. Rafael is already helping, but I think the draft needs a few checks. The transition from the town meeting to the final scene feels a bit rushed, and the character motivations in the last page could be a little clearer. It feels like the community’s decision is just announced, rather than understood.

I’m tight on money, so I’ve been checking the bulletin board at Johnson Park and taking odd jobs at Hobbs Cafe. The community is trying to help, and I’m grateful to be part of it. I’d rather spend my evenings creating than waiting for permission.

Right now, I’m revising the opening chapter. The town is gathering to discuss the new community center. I want the characters to feel like real people—people who have opinions, questions, and reasons for joining the meeting. I’m wondering whether the mayor’s message comes across as too smooth. The children’s reactions should show more curiosity and uncertainty. The adults should feel different too.

I also need to clarify the ending. The smallville residents are helping the town, but I’m not sure what the story is really proposing. Is the center meant to be a symbol of cooperation, a temporary solution, or something else entirely? I’d like to make that choice explicit. If I can tighten the transition, I think the book might feel more coherent.

I’m going to sketch a few quick scenes and compare them with the draft. I’ll also write down a few questions I want to answer before the final edits. A picture book needs to move forward without me having to explain every step.

So far, I’m not convinced the story is done. I’d like to have a clearer ending, stronger character voices, and a smoother way to move from one scene to the next. Maybe I can finish the draft by tomorrow.
