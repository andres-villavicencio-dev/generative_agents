Tomorrow is the first day of the Smallville picture book, and my mind is already full. I’ve been sketching the main characters at Hobbs Cafe, but the last few pages feel crowded. I need a clean page layout before I begin the final draft.

Rafael and I are sharing Willow Court 12, so the morning will be short. I’ll check the community bulletin board before breakfast, then sketch at Hobbs Cafe. I’m looking for a quiet spot where I can draw without too many interruptions. The next page needs a clear path: the reader enters the town, meets the main characters, and begins a journey.

I have a few ideas for the first chapter. I could show Willow Court from the outside, with the street, Johnson Park, and the buildings visible. Then I’ll place the main characters in the center, and the reader could follow their first few steps. I’m thinking of using Willow’s coat as a visual marker, but I should test it before I finalize the composition.

I’ll also consider the pacing. The first page should establish the setting without overwhelming the reader. I want the town to feel familiar—honest, a little ordinary, and full of people doing their own things. I don’t want to make the characters too perfect, and I don’t want to make the story feel rushed.

By midnight, I’ll have finished the layout. Then I’ll print a few pages and send them to the editors. I need to be careful with the copyright, though. I should keep the names and recognizable landmarks out of the final work, or at least make sure the characters are unmistakably mine.

I have a little money saved, but I’m still stretched between freelance work and the book. The next few hours will be tight. I’ll sketch the first page in one session, then review the page layout and adjust the spacing. If the first page feels too crowded, I’ll leave a small gap for the reader to discover the characters.

I’m excited to see the final pages. Smallville isn’t just a setting—it’s a character. I hope the reader leaves feeling like they’ve met someone they recognize, and that the story feels like it belongs to them. Maybe I’ll even add a little line somewhere to remind them that I made it.
