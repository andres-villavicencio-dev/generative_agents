The wind moves through the pines like a quiet story unspooled—soft at first, then growing louder with each step. I imagine a little girl standing beneath their branches, her hands pressed to bark as if learning its secrets. She sees a red leaf shaped like a heart and whispers, “Are you brave?” The wind answers with a sigh, not an echo, but something deeper: I am. Because kindness is the truest magic.  

I wrote that last night—just me and this tree and the quiet hum of my pen. It’s called Whispers of the Whispering Pines. I didn’t plan it so much as let it rise like morning mist, slow and sure. The main character, Elara, is nine years old with hair like dandelion fluff and a heart too big for her shoes. She doesn’t fight monsters or save kingdoms. She just listens—to the wind, to the pines, to the quiet voice inside that says kindness matters more than winning.  

When I read it again this morning at 8:01, my fingers paused on the page where Elara leaves a trail of wildflower seeds behind her. “They’re for the next one,” she says. I smiled so hard my eyes stung. That’s the story I want to tell—how small acts grow into forests.  

But the phone buzzes at 3:00, then again at 3:02. Another circle. Another breath away from the meeting with Carmen. I set it down. The pines are still whispering. And so am I.
