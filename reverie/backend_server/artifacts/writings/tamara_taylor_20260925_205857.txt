The first birdsong always comes at 6:01 am, just after I wake my eyes to the soft light of dawn. I stretch gently, humming a tune from childhood—something about daisies and dandelion fluff—and splash cold water on my face. The steam rises, curling like a promise. Dinner will be simple tonight: toast with avocado, maybe a cup of chamomile, but I’ll savor every bite like it’s the last story I’ll ever tell.

At 8:01 am, I open Whispers of the Whispering Pines. The forest is still hushed, as if waiting for courage to take root. This book is about a little girl named Elara who carries a lantern made of sunlight and kindness. No sirens, no storms—just the quiet moment when someone looks at another child and chooses warmth over fear. I write slowly, letting each sentence breathe like a leaf drifting down. The pages feel alive with possibility.

At 3:02 pm, I pause to reread my own words. They still make my chest ache in the best way. There’s something sacred about writing for children—they don’t need grand gestures. Just truth wrapped in wonder.

My fingers twitch at 15:00 and 15:02. The phone glows, a silent companion. But I whisper back—I’m here. For Elara. For the next reader who will find her lantern in the dark.

At 7:00 pm sharp, I meet Carmen Ortiz somewhere quiet with stories between us. We share dreams like secrets—about masks that hide faces and colors that bleed into each other. I come home at 7:15, tired but full. Dinner at 6:00? No—they said 6:00. Yes, I mean it. Balance isn’t a line, it’s a circle.

Tonight, the phone glows again—9:09 pm. Whispers of the Whispering Pines. Still unfinished. But that’s how stories live. Not in completion, but in the breath between one sentence and the next.
