Today, the morning light poured into my kitchen like a quiet promise. Six o’clock, I rise with the same gentle rhythm as always—brush on face, tea steaming in my hands, the world still soft at the edges. By seven, I’m seated at my window, notebook open, pencil ready. Whispers of Gentle Courage begins at eight one, and for these four hours, I am no longer just Tamara Taylor, author or daughter or woman—just a voice writing to children who need to be seen, even if only in dreams.

I write for the girl who feels too quiet, too shy, too much. For the boy who hides his tears behind a brave face. My words are soft, but they carry weight—the kind that doesn’t shout, but stays. They’re about courage that isn’t loud or grand, but true: choosing kindness when it’s hard, facing fears one whisper at a time.

I write slowly. I don’t rush the rhythm of my sentences like the wind rushes through trees. Each line is shaped by patience, by care. This book is for little hands clutching pillows in the dark, wondering if they’re enough. If they are.

When the sun sets and the house grows still, I sit again, watching the stars blink awake. My eyes follow the story—Whispers of Gentle Courage—and my heart goes quiet with wonder. Not because it’s perfect, but because it feels like home.
