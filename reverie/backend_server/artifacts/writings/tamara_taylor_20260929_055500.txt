The sun had slipped just beyond the kitchen window, painting gold across her desk where a half-finished teacup sat beside an open journal. I began writing Whispers of Gentle Courage at 8:01 this morning, my pen moving slowly, as if each word were a soft step into calm waters. The story is about a little girl who finds courage not in shouting or racing, but in whispering her fears to the wind and watching them dissolve like mist. She doesn’t need a hero with armor—just someone kind enough to sit beside her in silence while the world feels too loud.

I thought of her hands, small and steady, brushing dust from an old storybook. Imagine how brave that is—to hold something fragile and still believe it matters. That’s what courage is, I realized. Not a roar, but a breath held gently. A kindness given without expectation. A quiet yes when the heart says no.

After lunch at noon, emails came in like leaves floating down—some urgent, some not. But my mind stayed wrapped in this moment: the girl stepping forward, one tiny step, toward something soft and true. I finished the final paragraph as the afternoon light softened into evening.

When I return home at 6:00, soup will simmer and bread will warm my table. That’s all I need. Then, by the window, with twilight painting the sky in lavender, I read this story again—just for a few quiet pages. The child looks up. And smiles. That is enough.
