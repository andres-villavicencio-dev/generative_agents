The forest was quiet tonight, as it always is before sleep wraps its velvet cloak around the world. Tiny fireflies blinked like shy stars above the mossy stones where the shy little sprite had waited all day for this moment. I watched from the edge of the brook, notebook in hand, heart full of wonder and a whisper of courage that no one else could see.

This was the part she would remember: not the storm or the shadow, but the choice to be brave anyway. The sprite had always been afraid—afraid of being forgotten, afraid her light would fade if she spoke too loud. But then she remembered what I wrote in my first draft: courage is not the absence of fear. It is the quiet decision to move forward when your wings are trembling.

She reached out, not with a roar but with a breath, and touched the heartwood that had held her stories for centuries. The tree sighed, warm and deep as an old lullaby, and from it poured a single golden leaf—shaped like a promise. I wrote down what happened next: that sometimes, courage is simply letting someone be brave for you.

I closed my eyes and felt the book breathe with her. This is how stories live—they don’t end when we turn the page. They return in dreams, in quiet mornings, in the hush between heartbeats. And tonight, I am glad she stayed to listen.
