The wind whispered through the trees as I sat by my window, the afternoon light turning the pages gold. This story is about a little rabbit who has never believed she could be brave. She hides behind the brook, where the water runs so fast it drowns the stones beneath her paws. But one day, something catches her eye—a single feather, pale as morning mist, resting on the moss. It doesn’t belong to any bird I’ve ever seen.  

I wonder what happens when she touches it. What if it leads her somewhere no one has walked? Maybe that’s where courage begins—not with a shout or a leap, but with a quiet curiosity. The rabbit will follow the feather not because she’s fearless, but because something inside her knows there is more beyond the brook’s edge.  

I want children to feel that same hush of wonder when they read this. That bravery isn’t always loud. Sometimes it’s just a small hand unfolding toward the unknown. And if a story can make someone pause and look up, then I’ve done my part. The feather is still there, waiting. Just like us.
