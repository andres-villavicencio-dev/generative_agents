The wind carried a soft sigh through the meadow, like a secret being whispered between trees. A single dandelion floated down, its seeds dancing on a breeze so gentle it felt like a lullaby made of air. I watched it drift, rootless and free, until one seed landed in a patch of clover where a sleepy mouse paused to blink at the light.

That was the first whisper of Whispers of Gentle Courage—a story born not from grand plans, but from quiet moments like this. It’s about a little girl who finds courage not in shouting or racing, but in noticing the small things: a dewdrop on a leaf, the way sunlight spills across the floor at dawn. She doesn’t need to be brave all at once; she simply chooses to notice.

My protagonist is named Lila, because names like that feel soft and true. She lives with her grandmother, who tends a garden of forgotten herbs and stories. There’s no villain in this tale—only misunderstandings, lost buttons, and the courage it takes to say, I’m scared, but I want to try. The magic isn’t in spells or flying; it’s in listening—to your breath, to the world, to yourself.

I write slowly, as if each word is a seed. And when I read it at night by my window, with chamomile steaming beside me, I think of Lila walking through that meadow, not knowing what adventure lies ahead—but feeling ready anyway. Gentle courage, I’ve learned, begins where you are and what you notice.
