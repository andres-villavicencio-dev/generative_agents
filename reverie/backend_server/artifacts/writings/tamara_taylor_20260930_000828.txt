The wind carries a quiet song through the trees, like pages turning in an old book left open on a windowsill. I can almost hear it—soft and steady, as if the forest is whispering secrets only children are meant to understand. Little ones, brave hearts among us, don’t need thunder or battles won with swords; their courage blooms quietly, like wildflowers pushing through cracked pavement in spring.

This tale begins where all true adventures do—the moment a small step feels too big, and the world grows still. I wrote it for that feeling: when you’re scared but keep walking anyway, because something inside you knows there’s more beyond the edge of sight. There’s no rush here, only the rhythm of breath and thought, like dandelion seeds drifting on a gentle breeze.

I imagined each sentence as a hand gently brushing dust from an old storybook, revealing what was meant to be seen all along. For children who wonder if their kindness is enough, if their quietness means they’re not strong—this book says: yes, your softness is strength. Your curiosity, your patience, your way of seeing the world with open eyes—that’s courage in its softest form.

I hope you find a place where you can rest, like I do by the window at twilight. Let this story be that place—a quiet harbor where bravery doesn’t shout, but simply believes. And when you close it, carry one word: you.
