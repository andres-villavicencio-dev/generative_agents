The morning light slips quietly through my window like a shy guest, and I find myself already dreaming in colors that don’t exist yet—purple clouds that hum softly, trees with silver bark that whisper secrets only children can hear. This is where stories begin: not with words typed on a screen, but with the quiet hum of imagination waking alongside me.

Today, I’m writing about Lila, who found her courage not in loud thunder or brave speeches, but in the way she tucked a single dandelion into her pocket and carried it everywhere. Courage isn’t always bold; sometimes it’s just that small act of holding something fragile when you feel the world is too heavy.

I write slowly here, like I’m tucking each sentence into a blanket for someone to sleep with at night. Each story is a gift given gently, never forced. I want them to feel safe in their own hearts, even if they’ve never felt it before.

By 8:01 AM, the page breathes. By noon, I pause with tea and bread—simple things that ground me. After emails clear, I return home for soup and bread, a warm bowl like a hug. At 8 PM, by the window, I read Lila’s story again, letting the words settle into my bones.

I close the book at 9:30, sip chamomile tea, and breathe in the quiet. Sleep comes gently around 10 PM. And somewhere between breaths, I feel whole—seen, held, becoming a little more brave myself.
