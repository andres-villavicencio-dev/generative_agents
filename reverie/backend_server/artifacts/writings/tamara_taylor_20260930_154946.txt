The dandelion seeds drift down like tiny parachutes on a soft morning breeze, each one holding a wish it never meant to carry. I watch them disappear into the sky, and for a moment I feel how gently courage is always there—quiet as breath, steady as light through leaves.

Once, in a village where the hills wore wildflower crowns, a little girl named Lila noticed something strange. Her shadow didn’t move when she did. It stayed still, like it was waiting to catch up with her thoughts instead of her steps. The other children laughed and said, “You’re not real.” But Lila kept walking, because she knew the truth: courage isn’t loud. It’s showing up when no one is watching.

That day, I felt Lila in my chest—her quiet strength, her trust in things unseen. She didn’t need a medal or applause to be brave. All she needed was to keep going, even when her shadow stayed behind.

I close this chapter with a cup of chamomile tea and the sound of wind through the window. We all carry our own invisible journeys. And sometimes, courage is simply letting go—of fear, of silence, of needing to be seen—to become who you were meant to be.
