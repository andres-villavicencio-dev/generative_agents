The wind carries stories soft as dandelion fluff through the quiet room, each one a promise to keep moving gently forward. Today, I began at eight o’clock sharp, pen in hand, not because time demanded it but because my heart felt ready to listen. The first page holds a small girl with braided hair made of autumn leaves, standing at the edge of a meadow where the sky tastes like honey and the clouds are made of old lullabies. She doesn’t run from fear—she walks beside it, whispering, “I am here,” until it becomes as light as a dandelion seed on the breeze.

I wrote about courage not as fire or roar, but as stillness. As choosing to sit in silence when your throat tightens. As offering kindness even when you’re tired. That girl? She found her strength not in leaps, but in leaning—leaning into the arms of the wind, leaning into herself.

At noon, I will pause with tea and bread to check messages, but my mind returns here, to this story. It is patient. It is kind. And it always grows from small seeds, like the ones we all carry, waiting for sunlight or a whisper to bloom.

Tomorrow, I’ll be back at eight o’clock, pen ready, heart open. The meadow will wait.
