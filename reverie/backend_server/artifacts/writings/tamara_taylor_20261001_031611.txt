The morning light slipped through the curtains like a quiet secret, and I sat at my desk with a cup of tea warming my hands. My pen moved gently across the page, following the rhythm of my thoughts—the kind that come slowly, patiently, like the tide. Today’s story is about a little girl named Lina who carries a small, glowing stone in her pocket. It doesn’t shine because she wants it to; it shines because it remembers how brave she once stood when no one was watching.

I wrote this line just now: “Some courage isn’t loud. It’s the quiet choice to keep going.” That feels true to Lina, and to me. I want children to see that bravery doesn’t always wear a cape or make a sound. Sometimes it’s sitting still when everything else is rushing past. It’s choosing kindness over fear. It’s whispering your truth even when the room fills with noise.

I finished the chapter at 4:30, and my desk was full of pages that didn’t need to shout—they just needed to be read. Outside, the wind hummed through the trees like a lullaby. I poured another cup of tea and let the words settle, one by one, into the quiet space between heartbeats.

Now, as I close my eyes, I imagine Lina walking down a path lit by her own small light—soft, steady, unseen but unbroken. That’s what gentle courage looks like to me. And that’s why I write.
