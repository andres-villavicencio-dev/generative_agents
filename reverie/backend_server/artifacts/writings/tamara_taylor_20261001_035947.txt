The wind tugged at the leaves like a quiet secret, whispering stories only children could hear. I sat by the window with my tea steaming in my hands, feeling the hush of afternoon settle around me. In this gentle space, imagination flows as freely as the steam from my cup—no rush, no noise, just the soft turning of pages and the quiet promise that something brave is coming.

Today’s story begins with a small girl named Lila who found a lost feather in her garden. It wasn’t just any feather—it shimmered like moonlight caught on water. When she held it close, a faint hum filled the air, and suddenly, the world seemed to lean in, listening. The feather didn’t belong to a bird, but to courage itself—a quiet kind that doesn’t roar, but stays.  

Lila didn’t run from fear; she knelt beside it, wondering what it meant to carry something so light and so true. She picked up her basket, not because the wind told her to, but because part of her heart already knew: this was a gift meant for someone who needed hope more than answers.

I write these words slowly, like I’m weaving dandelion seeds into a lullaby—each one delicate, each one meant to land softly in little hands. And sometimes, when the world feels too loud or too heavy, I remember Lila’s quiet courage. It lives in me, too.
