Monday, February 13

I’m keeping my two projects separate today so each story has room to breathe. The children’s books need warmth, patience, and wonder. The comic can have sharper edges and more complicated questions. I want both projects to feel like their own distinct worlds.

For the children’s book series, I’m working on “The Lantern Library.” The first book follows Nia, a quiet girl who discovers that the oldest book in her town’s library opens doors to forgotten places. Each doorway should contain a small puzzle, but Nia never has to solve it alone. Her kindhearted neighbor, Mr. Bell, helps her listen for clues. I also want a shy talking fox who is clever but wonderfully forgetful. The central theme is that courage can be quiet and asking for help is a strength. Before I write, I need to finish the chapter map, decide which clues repeat, and make sure the ending feels hopeful without being too easy. Today’s goals are to draft 600 words of Chapter Two, revise the opening by 150 words, and read the new pages aloud to check the rhythm. The intended reading age is seven to ten, and every spread should make a child want to turn the page.

For the adult comic series, I’m continuing “Nightshift City.” It follows Mara Venn, an emergency sign painter who sees hidden messages in the city’s ordinary signs. Over the next three issues, the signs point toward a government project that erases neighborhoods from public maps. I want the mystery to unfold through images as much as dialogue: crooked streetlights, red paint, and reflections that do not match. The tone should be grounded, tense, and humane. Today I need to rough out five pages, block the final page of issue two, and write a one-page character scene involving Mara’s younger brother. I’ll also check the timeline, keep the clues fair, and avoid making the conspiracy too tangled for the reader.

After dinner at six, I’ll reread both outlines and choose one small improvement for each project. I’ll stop by ten so I can wake at six feeling rested and ready.
