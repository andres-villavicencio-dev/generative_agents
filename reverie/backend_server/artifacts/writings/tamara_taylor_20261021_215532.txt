I’m setting two clear goals for this week, one for each project. I want to make steady progress and keep the magic alive in both stories.

For my children’s book series, my main creative goal is to finish a complete chapter-by-chapter outline for the first book. The story follows Mara, a young night courier who delivers letters to places that only appear after sunset. Each letter begins with an ordinary problem, but by the end of the chapter, Mara has found a surprising solution and helped someone feel less alone. I want the magic to feel gentle rather than overwhelming. The places should feel familiar, but with one small impossible detail tucked inside. I also want a strong supporting character—an elderly mapmaker who remembers roads that no longer exist—and a recurring symbol, perhaps a golden moth, that appears quietly throughout the story. Before I begin drafting, I need to make sure Mara’s journey has a clear emotional shape and that every chapter teaches readers something about courage, patience, or kindness.

For my adult comic series, my main creative goal is to complete a full rough script for Issue One. The series is set in Bellwether City, where a former telephone operator named Elise discovers that the abandoned phone lines still connect to places the city erased years ago. My notes for the opening issue are simple: Elise follows one line after hearing her missing sister’s voice, enters an underground switchboard, and realizes someone has been rebuilding the old network. I need to establish the mood, clarify the visual transitions, and write the final reveal so it changes Elise’s understanding of what happened to her sister.

I’ll work on the children’s outline Monday through Wednesday, the comic script Thursday and Friday, and use the weekend for gentle revisions and rereading. Progress should feel thoughtful, not forced. Both stories are alive, and my job is to listen carefully enough to help them grow.
