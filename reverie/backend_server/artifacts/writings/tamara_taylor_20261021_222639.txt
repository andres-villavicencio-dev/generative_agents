Tonight, the house is quiet in the way I love best. The refrigerator hums, a warm lamp glows beside my chair, and my notebook is open on the desk like a little door waiting to be discovered. I wrote a few lines about a brave rabbit who was afraid of the dark, then gave her a lantern made from fireflies. It sounds simple, but finding the right kind of courage for her felt important. I hope children who read the story will remember that being frightened does not mean being weak.

I’m also working on a comic for adults, and this project has a different energy. There are sharper jokes, grayer mornings, characters carrying lives that are more complicated. I’m learning to let the humor arrive sideways sometimes. Patience seems to be the key there, and perhaps in nearly everything.

Carmen Ortiz was humming in the kitchen earlier while she made tea. The sound drifted down the hall, and I thought how lovely it is to share a home with someone who leaves small traces of joy everywhere. I’m grateful for that. I’m grateful for my imagination, too, even when it wanders off before I can catch it. Usually, it brings something interesting back.

Tomorrow I’ll wake at six, revise a chapter, and walk to the park if the sky is clear. There are pages waiting, deadlines approaching, and probably a hundred tiny ideas tapping on the inside of my mind. For now, I don’t need to chase every one of them. I can simply listen until one of them says, “Wait a minute. I think I know how this story begins.”
