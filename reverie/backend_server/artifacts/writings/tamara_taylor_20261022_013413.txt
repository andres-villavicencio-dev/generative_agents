I woke at 6:00 AM when the pale winter light slipped through the curtains. I stretched, listened to the quiet house, and made my way through my morning routine: washing my face, brushing my teeth, brewing a cup of tea, and opening the window to let in the crisp air. The day ahead felt full of little roads, each one leading somewhere worth discovering.

After breakfast, I spread my notes across the desk. The children’s story waited patiently on one side of my laptop, while the panels for my dark-fantasy comic occupied the other. Between them lay my maps—some drawn in soft pencil, some crowded with impossible coastlines and tiny silver stars. I had always loved maps, but until recently I did not understand why. Now I see that every map is a promise: there is always another path, even when the first one disappears.

I returned to the children’s pages and gave my characters their voices.

Mara traced the dotted line with one careful finger. “Do we have to follow it exactly?” she asked.

“Not exactly,” said Sol, leaning over her shoulder. “Maps are suggestions. The important part is noticing where we are.”

“And the part where we get lost?” Mara whispered.

Sol smiled. “That’s where we learn to look for one another.”

I laughed softly. Mara is cautious but never hopeless, and Sol is clever enough to be playful without dismissing someone else’s fear. That was the heart of the scene: not simply finding a way forward, but finding it together.

In the comic, the wayfinder enters a city that has been erased from every official chart. He carries a compass that points toward what people have forgotten. I sharpened my pencil and wrote his first line.

“Every road begins as an idea,” he said. “Someone must be brave enough to walk it.”

The words settled into place. I felt that familiar thrill of creation—not a grand blaze, but a small lantern lit in a dark room. By breakfast, the house had been still. By nine, my characters were speaking. There was much work ahead, yet I was not worried. I had tea, a warm notebook, and a whole day of paths to follow.
