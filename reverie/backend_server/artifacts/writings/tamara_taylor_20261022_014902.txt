I woke at 6:00 AM on Tuesday, February 14, before the first pale stripe of sunlight reached the kitchen window. I completed my morning routine slowly: brushed my teeth, washed my face, made tea, and opened the curtains to greet the frost. The house was quiet, but my mind was already unfolding paths across imaginary landscapes.

I reviewed last night’s notes before making any new decisions. One of the children’s stories needed a correction: Elian’s sister was named Mara in the first chapter, but I had accidentally written Mira in the scene at the bakery. Mara wears a green cloak, so I changed the reference to “Mara’s green cloak.” Now the clue matches.

The map troubles me more. The old road runs north from Willowfen, passes the crooked oak, and ends at the river. It cannot cross the river at Stonebridge, because the bridge collapsed during the winter flood. I moved the crossing downstream to Reed Ford and adjusted the fox’s footprints accordingly. Now the tracks follow the road instead of walking straight through deep water, which would have made our clever fox seem very foolish.

At 10:00 PM Monday night, I had planned to have Tuesday’s pages ready, and I did—along with several new ideas. I also worked on the dark-fantasy comic, letting the same cartographic instinct guide the shadows of its ruined city. One world softened with wonder; the other sharpened with danger. Both needed careful paths and trustworthy landmarks.

By evening, I had checked every character name, reread each clue, and traced the routes on the map with my fingertip. The work felt complete, but more importantly, it felt alive. Tomorrow I may redraw the northern border. Today, I will rest knowing that Mara, Elian, the crooked oak, and Reed Ford are exactly where they belong.
