Mara found the old brass key beneath the third stepping-stone in the garden.

It was smaller than her thumb and warm from the afternoon sun. On one side, a tiny map was engraved: a winding line, a moon, and three leaves growing from a single stem.

“Why would a key have a map on it?” she whispered.

The map did not answer, but a breeze moved through the garden. The leaves on the apple tree rustled, and one bright red apple dropped into the grass with a soft thump.

Mara picked up the apple. The moment she did, the little gate at the far end of the garden creaked open.

Beyond the gate was a path she had never noticed before. It curved between silver birches and disappeared into a violet dusk, though the sun was still shining behind her.

Mara’s first thought was to run straight back to the house. The path was strange, and the key felt too mysterious. But when she looked closely, she saw little signs along the stones: a scarf caught on a branch, a painted arrow pointing the wrong way, and footprints that began nowhere and ended everywhere.

Someone had needed help.

She slipped the key into her pocket and stepped through the gate. The moment she crossed the threshold, the three leaves on the key began to glow.

A small fox appeared between the trees. It had silver fur, ink-dark paws, and a red feather tied around its neck.

“You found the wayfinder’s key,” said the fox. “Then you must have found the apple, too.”

“I did,” Mara said, holding it up. “But where does this path go?”

The fox lowered its head. “It goes where lost things are waiting to be found.”

Mara looked back. The garden was still there, peaceful and golden. Then she noticed something moving in the grass near the gate: a tiny blue door, no bigger than a teacup, with a note tucked beneath it.

The note said: PLEASE HELP ME FIND MY WAY HOME.

Mara knelt down and opened the little door. Warm light spilled out, filling the path with the color of sunrise.

“All right,” she said. “One thing at a time.”

She followed the glowing leaves while the silver fox walked beside her, and together they began to search.
