The house was quiet when the moon slipped behind the clouds.

Tamara sat at the kitchen table with a fresh page in front of her and a cup of tea cooling beside her pencil. Across the room, Carmen Ortiz was sorting a stack of colorful buttons into little jars. They clicked and clinked softly, like tiny raindrops finding their way down a window.

Tamara looked up. “Do you think the moon can get lost?” she asked.

Carmen paused. “Maybe,” she said. “But even if it does, the stars know where it is.”

Tamara smiled. She wrote the sentence carefully, then drew a crooked moon beneath it, surrounded by stars with golden eyes. Soon, the page was no longer just a page. It had become a place.

She imagined a small village at the edge of a forest, where every house had a different shape. One looked like a teapot. Another resembled a giant acorn. At the end of a winding path stood a house with bright blue windows and a red door. Inside lived a girl named Marigold, who collected forgotten things: a single blue glove, a bird with a bent feather, the last peach stone from summer, and a thimble that had belonged to a queen.

One evening, Marigold found an empty jar on her doorstep.

There was no note. No name. Just a jar with a tiny silver key resting inside it.

“What could it open?” she whispered.

The house answered with a soft creak. The red door swung open, though no one had touched it. Beyond it lay a road paved with moonstones, stretching between the trees and shining like the surface of a lake.

Marigold took one step, then another. The jar hummed in her hands, warm as if it had been held by someone kind.

From behind her, the village lights blinked awake, one by one.

Carmen reached over and nudged the pencil closer to Tamara. “What happens next?”

Tamara dipped the tip into her ink.

“Better find out,” she said.
