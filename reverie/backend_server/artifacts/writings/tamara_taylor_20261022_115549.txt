“Mira, the moon has fallen into my teacup!” cried Pip.

Pip stood beside the kitchen table, holding the cup with both hands. Inside it, a tiny silver moon shimmered among the tea leaves. It was no larger than a marble, but it glowed so brightly that the whole room looked like a gentle night.

Pip’s grandmother lowered her newspaper. “Hm,” she said. “That is a problem.”

“It is!” Pip agreed. “I was trying to make a wish, but the moon slipped out of the sky and landed in my breakfast.”

Grandma smiled. “Then we shall solve it together. First, what do we know?”

Pip frowned thoughtfully. “The moon is in the teacup.”

“That is what your eyes tell us,” said Grandma. “What else?”

“It is very bright, very round, and only a little bit bigger than a pea.”

Grandma took a small saucer from the cupboard. “We shall give the moon a wider place to rest. Perhaps it will feel less crowded.”

Pip nodded and reached for the moon with his fingertips. “Easy now, little moon.”

The moon wobbled. Instead of lifting neatly into the saucer, it rose straight into the air and hovered above Pip’s nose. Then it zoomed around the room, leaving a trail of silver sparkles behind it.

“Oh dear,” said Pip. “That made it worse.”

Grandma leaned closer. “Perhaps the moon wanted to be caught, not placed.”

Pip thought about this. He remembered how the stars in the storybook had never been frightened when the owl carried them home in a woven basket. Perhaps the moon needed a home too.

He pulled the basket from beside the fireplace and held it open. “Would you like to sleep in here, little moon? There are blankets, and the ceiling has a tiny hole, so you can watch the real sky.”

For a moment, the moon hovered above the basket. Then it floated inside and settled with a warm, golden glow.

Pip carried the basket to the windowsill. Outside, the full moon shone in the sky.

“Wait,” said Grandma. “There are two moons now.”

Pip looked from the bright moon in the basket to the bright moon in the sky. His face fell.

But then he smiled. “No,” he said. “There is one moon in the sky, and one moon keeping watch. That means we have twice the moonlight.”

Grandma kissed the top of his head. “Well done, Pip. You listened carefully and tried again. That is how most solutions begin.”
