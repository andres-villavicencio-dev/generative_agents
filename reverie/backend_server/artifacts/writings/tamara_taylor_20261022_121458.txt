The night sky hung over Maple Street like a deep blue blanket. In her little attic bedroom, eight-year-old Maya noticed that one star was missing.

“It can’t be hiding,” she whispered. “Stars are too bright to hide.”

She climbed onto her desk and opened the window. Cold air rushed into the room, carrying the smell of pine trees and snow. Maya wrapped her red scarf around her neck and looked toward the hill.

Something silver flashed behind the branches.

Maya’s heart fluttered. She pulled on her boots, tucked a flashlight into her coat, and hurried downstairs. Her footsteps squeaked on the frozen porch.

At the bottom of the hill, she found a narrow path glowing with tiny blue lights. The lights hopped from one snowflake to another, dancing along the ground.

Maya followed them. The path led her past the old oak tree, where frost covered every branch, and through the quiet field behind the bakery. At the far end stood a small stone house with a round golden door.

The silver shape was sitting on its roof. It had a long tail, round paws, and bright eyes that reflected the moonlight.

A snow leopard! But this one was different. A tiny golden star rested between its ears.

“You took the missing star,” Maya said.

The leopard tilted its head. It pointed toward the door with one paw.

Maya pushed the golden door open. Inside, hundreds of paper stars floated around a tall wooden table. A little note lay in the middle.

“Starlight delivery for Maple Street,” it read. “Please return the star before sunrise.”

Maya smiled. She climbed onto the leopard’s warm back, and together they raced up the hill. When they reached her house, the sky was beginning to turn pink.

Maya placed the star back in the sky. It flashed once, twice, and then joined the others.

The snow leopard curled beside her bed. Before Maya could ask it to stay, it whispered, “Look for me when the leaves turn gold.”

Then it vanished in a swirl of silver snow.

From that night on, Maya never missed the quiet magic hidden in familiar places. And every autumn, just as the first leaf fell, she would hear soft paw steps outside her window.
