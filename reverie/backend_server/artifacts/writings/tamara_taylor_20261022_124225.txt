The morning sun climbed over the rooftops of Larkspur, a small town tucked between green hills and a silver-blue river. Frost sparkled on the fences, and chimney smoke curled into the pale sky like soft ribbons of wool. As the town slowly woke, the bakery doors opened, the mailman shuffled from house to house, and children with bright woollen coats hurried along the cobbled streets toward school.

Among them was a girl named Elsie.

Elsie carried a small leather bag across her shoulder and walked a little farther than she needed to. She had promised herself she would not look inside until she reached the old bridge. It was a promise she had made every morning for nearly a week, and every morning she broke it before taking the first step.

At last, she could no longer resist.

Sitting on the smooth stone wall beside the bridge, Elsie opened her bag. Inside lay a tiny silver key, a folded map covered in unreadable letters, and a feather no bigger than her thumb. The feather shimmered pink and gold in the morning light.

The map had belonged to her grandmother. Grandmother had discovered it hidden beneath the floorboards of her cottage and pressed it into Elsie’s hands the evening before she became ill.

“Every map begins with a place,” Grandmother had said, “but the heart must decide where the journey leads.”

Elsie studied the bridge, the river, and the dark line of trees beyond it. At the far end of the bridge stood a lantern post she had never noticed before. Its glass was cloudy, and the little flame inside had gone out.

Then the silver feather trembled.

A soft breeze moved through the town, carrying the scent of cinnamon and rain. Elsie’s map unfolded by itself, and the strange letters rearranged themselves into words:

When the sleeping light wakes,  
Follow the river’s bend.  
Past the homes where wishes sleep,  
The door will wait for you.

Elsie’s heart began to flutter. She had the odd feeling that the whole town was holding its breath.

The tiny flame inside the lantern flickered.

Once.

Twice.

Then it glowed with a warm golden light.
