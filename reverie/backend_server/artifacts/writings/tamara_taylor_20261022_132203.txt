Mara and the Mapmaker

Every Saturday, Mara followed the little brass compass to the end of Maple Street. It always pointed toward the old clock tower, even though the tower had been closed for years and its clock had not moved since midnight.

“Left, then right, then a hop,” Mara told the compass. “You are very certain for such a tiny thing.”

Today, it led her through the library garden, past the wishing well, and behind the bus-stop bench. There she found a wooden door covered in ivy. When she touched the handle, the door swung open.

Beyond it was a room filled with maps. Maps of places that did not exist yet.

Mara traced her finger along one labeled THE DAY EVERYONE FORGOT THEIR LUNCHBOX. Another showed a forest where the trees grew leaves shaped like teacups. On the largest map, ink lines formed a path from Maple Street to a bright golden star.

“This must be the way home,” Mara whispered.

She stepped through the map, and the paper folded around her like a blanket. When she opened her eyes, she stood beneath a sky crowded with unfamiliar constellations.

At her feet was a small brown mouse wearing a blue scarf.

“Hello,” Mara said. “Do you know where I live?”

The mouse nodded. “I know where everyone lives. That is my job.”

He unrolled a scroll and pointed to a tiny house. Mara recognized her window, but beside it was another window she had never seen. A child waved from inside.

“My cousin lives there,” said the mouse. “You have never met him because tomorrow has not happened yet.”

Mara’s knees felt suddenly wobbly. She thought of all the mornings she had believed were certain—breakfast, school, the walk home, the day after, and the day after that. Perhaps the future was not a locked door waiting for her.

Perhaps it was a map she helped to make.

The mouse handed her a silver pencil. “If you want tomorrow to change, you must erase one small thing.”

Mara looked at the pencil. Then she looked at the bright star on the map.

“What if I erase the wrong thing?”

The mouse smiled. “Then you will learn something new about the world.”

Mara took a breath. The compass in her pocket began to spin, pointing not toward the clock tower, but toward the glowing path ahead.

For the first time, Mara did not need to know what waited there. She only needed to be brave enough to draw it.
