Monday, February 13

I spent most of the morning reading through the fourth chapter of the new children’s book. On the first pass, I could see what was slowing the story down. The children were trying to rescue a lost moon mouse from the old library, but every new detail made the trip feel longer than it needed to be. The room was beautiful, and the mice were very polite, but the action kept drifting into the background.

I cut a few unnecessary descriptions and moved the important information closer to the beginning. I also let Mara tell one of the jokes herself instead of explaining why it was funny. Now the chapter feels brighter and more urgent. The children can still imagine every shelf, stack, and curious shadow, but they are moving forward.

I read the revised version aloud twice. The first time, I was checking the pacing. The second time, I listened with my eyes closed to see whether the story held my attention. It did. I think this is what I love most about writing for children: a small adjustment can turn a quiet page into an adventure.

Carmen Ortiz came home in the afternoon and asked how the chapter was going. I told her it finally had the right amount of speed. She laughed and said that was the first thing I’d said all morning without apologizing for taking too long. She knows me too well.

Tonight, I’m going to look at a few pages of my comic series for adults. That project has a different energy—sharper dialogue, darker jokes, and characters who are usually making terrible decisions on purpose. I enjoy both kinds of stories, but the children’s books remind me to be patient and imaginative.

I had dinner around six. Then I read the chapter once more, made two tiny changes, and finally went to bed at ten. Tomorrow, I’ll begin the next chapter. I already know how it starts: with the library doors opening onto a hallway that wasn’t there the night before.
