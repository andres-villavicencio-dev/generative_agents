I woke at six, because I’m a person who listens to the light. The sun was still soft over the city, and I needed to begin before the morning settled in.

I made coffee and sat at the table with the three characters I’d been imagining. They were still in their beds, curled together, their faces glowing like little lanterns.

“I’m scared,” said Leo, his voice small.

“Scared is a good word,” said Maya, who was already making her bed. “But it can also be a brave feeling.”

Leo looked down at his hand. “I thought I was the strongest person in the house.”

“Then you’re not alone,” Maya said, picking up the drawing she had made in the dark. “I drew you.”

A silence stretched between us.

Leo laughed. “That was the worst drawing I’ve ever made.”

“I know,” Maya said. “And I know there’s a better one.”

She opened the drawer where she had kept a small notebook. There were pages filled with pictures of birds, trees, and stories that looked like they belonged in a different room.

“Tell me,” she said, “about the blue-ink postmark.”

Leo blinked. “I don’t know what that is.”

“I heard it once from an old letter,” Maya said. “It looked like a blue bird, but no one could explain it.”

“I don’t know,” Leo said. “The letter was from my grandfather.”

Maya smiled. “That’s a secret.”

She turned back to the notebook. “What if we make the letter’s message into the beginning of our story?”

Leo looked at the picture beside the postmark. A blue bird was pecking at a blue ribbon tied to a door that looked suspiciously like the old building we had been planning to visit.

“Can I bring my own postmark?” he asked.

Maya laughed. “Of course. But it must be yours.”

Leo took out a small, slightly worn stamp from his desk. “Mine.”

He pressed the blue ink into the paper and let it dry. Then he looked at the ribbon.

“I think I’ve found the key,” he said.

Maya smiled, and for the first time, the words in her heart felt like they were coming together.

“Then we’ll begin,” she said. “And we’ll make sure no one else gets to see the letter before we do.”
