My first clue was in the blue-ink postmark. It was tiny, worn, and carried a small symbol shaped like a bird with a folded wing. I had never seen it before, and I knew the symbol belonged to the old post office on the corner of Elm and Fourth.

I followed the postmark across the neighborhood and found it tucked inside a worn envelope that had been buried beneath a pile of newspapers. When I opened the envelope, something smooth and warm slid out of the bottom. It was a small, sealed letter.

Inside, the handwriting was careful and neat, as if the writer had wanted the message to reach me without anyone else noticing. The words opened with a question:

Dear Tamara, you found the blue postmark. What do you think it means?

I looked closely at the paper. Beneath the question, there was a small drawing—a house with one window that looked different from the others, and a blue ribbon tied around the roof. The ribbon was the same color as the postmark, but the drawing showed a ribbon folded over itself twice, making it look like a key.

I traced the ribbon in my notebook. Then I looked at the postmark again. The letter said, Someone had left this clue for you.

My first thought was that the postmark had been placed there by someone who knew my name. But I did not remember a recent letter written to me. Still, I was curious. I walked back to the old post office and asked the woman who answered the door. She had a kind smile, and she knew more than she was saying.

She told me that a young artist had been collecting blue-ink postmarks from different places, hoping to discover one that would make people smile. She had found the envelope and the letter buried beneath the newspaper pile, and she had kept them in a small box until she finally noticed the folded ribbon.

She handed me the blue postmark.

“Do you think it is a treasure?” she asked.

“No,” I said, smiling. “It is a clue. And I think it belongs to someone you know.”

She looked at me with a little surprise. “I don’t think so,” she said.

I looked at the folded ribbon. For the first time, the mystery did not feel like a puzzle I had to solve alone. It felt like a message waiting to be understood, and I was finally ready to listen.
