I woke at six, brushed my teeth, and ate breakfast before the world could say goodbye. Today, I was balancing the chapters for The Blue Ink Series with a comic draft, but I had a feeling it needed a turn.

I sat at my desk and looked through the old letter Carmen had found. Its letters were neat, almost too perfect, as if the people who wrote them were afraid to be themselves. I took the letter and wrote a note: I am not afraid. I am curious.

Then I picked up the mysterious blue-ink postmark stamped across the page. At first, it looked like ordinary ink. But when I dipped a pencil into the blue, the paper seemed to soften, and tiny words began to rearrange themselves. I traced one of them, and suddenly I was standing in a long, forgotten building. The walls were made of glass, and the windows showed the sky without clouds.

I stepped out of the room and looked around. The air smelled like old paper and rain. I saw a small blue bird perched on the roof, watching me.

“Hello,” I whispered.

The bird tilted its head. “Are you afraid of the blue ink?”

“No,” I said. “I’m afraid of the ink that doesn’t want to stay.”

For the first time, I felt small—not because I was weak, but because I was ready to try something new. I raised a hand, and the ink spilled around me, turning the floor into a path of tiny stars.

I followed it.

At first, the path disappeared. Then it grew taller. The windows changed from plain glass to glowing pictures of places I had never seen. I kept walking, and the ink grew louder, singing in a melody I didn’t understand.

I stopped and listened. “Is this a new place?” I asked.

The ink smiled. “Every new place is one letter away from being home.”

I turned back toward the door and wrote something on the wall. My handwriting was shaky, but the words were mine: I’m not afraid. And I’m not afraid to change.

The ink turned black, and the paper folded into a small letter.

I tucked it under my arm and walked out of the building into the street. The blue-ink postmark was still on the page, waiting for someone to make a choice.

That choice will decide what the ink does next.

I turned back toward the house and opened the letter. Beneath my name, the ink had written: Come back when you are ready.
