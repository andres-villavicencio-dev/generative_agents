I woke at six and made breakfast quietly. The sun was already warming the table, and I wanted every morning to feel like the beginning of something new. I planned to read, write, and think before settling down. By ten, I would be ready for bed.

I’ve been reading the middle of The Blue Letter again. It feels like the story has found its heart. At first, the children’s characters seemed ordinary—simple and safe. But when I looked closer, I saw the real story: how ordinary people can change when they listen to each other.

The mystery in the story is not about solving a puzzle. It is about what people keep hiding. A child who refuses to speak, a letter waiting for a message, and a mysterious blue postmark all remind me of the ordinary things we have forgotten. They show that courage does not always look like a big adventure. Sometimes, it is asking difficult questions and listening when others are silent.

I want the middle to feel like a warm invitation. The children should not be afraid to wonder, to disagree, or to say something unexpected. They should be encouraged to look at the world differently. I want the middle to show that curiosity is a kind of bravery. A brave person does not always know the answer. A brave person also knows enough to ask what is worth asking.

As I write the next chapter, I will make sure the children feel heard. I will make sure their questions are given room. And I will make sure every character has a voice—not just the strongest one. The blue postmark should not simply add mystery. It should make the ordinary feel mysterious enough to matter.

I hope this middle helps the children see that they do not need to be perfect to be worthy of kindness. They only need to be brave enough to notice what is there.

Tomorrow, I will open the chapters again and read the next scene. I am ready to begin.
