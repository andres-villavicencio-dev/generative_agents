I woke at six, brushed my teeth, and ate breakfast with the other children. It was a quiet Tuesday, February 14, and I had a few pages to work on.

The second big incident was still gathering its shape. The old letter had arrived at the library, and something was hidden inside it. A mysterious blue-ink postmark seemed to be writing the answer, while the historic building stood like a silent witness, its windows darkening as the night grew colder.

For the first time, the characters’ reactions were not just about the letter. They were afraid of what was being kept. Leo trembled beside the window, his hands shaking. Maya stared at the blue mark as if it were a face. And little Clara, who loved everything that changed, began to ask why the building was not moving.

The incident grew louder. The postmark wrote strange words in the rain, and the old letter opened on its own. People whispered that something was trapped inside them. The building seemed to shift, and the children could hear voices speaking through the glass.

When the storm finally broke, the blue ink dried on the page, but the children were changed. They carried the secret inside them now, even though no one had asked them to. The letter was gone, but the mystery remained.

I returned to the draft and found myself wondering whether the characters had learned more than I had imagined. The middle section needed a quiet moment to show how the incident had affected them. I could not write a happy ending, because that would make the mystery feel too easy. I needed to show that the letters were still in the world, that the old building still held its secrets, and that the children had made a choice that mattered.

By the time I finished, I felt tired, yet more excited than before. The story had gained depth, and the characters had changed. Tomorrow, I will begin the next page.
