I woke at six, opened the curtains, and watched the gray light settle over the town. At seven, I ate a quiet breakfast of toast and fruit, then settled into my writing room. Today was another Tuesday for Tamara Taylor: a day of finishing chapters, gathering the details for the middle of my children’s series, and exploring the ideas I’d shared with Carmen Ortiz.

Carmen had suggested we use an old brick building, an old letter, and a mysterious blue-ink postmark. The postmark’s blue ink seemed to shift when I looked at it through the window. I had never seen a postmark like that before. Maybe the building was waiting for a story.

The first page of the series begins with Leo, a small boy who learned to read before he learned how to walk. He lives in a town called Oakhaven, where every house seems to have a secret. One Tuesday, Leo finds a blue-ink postmark in the yard of an old brick building.

He examines it closely. The ink is blue, and it glows slightly when he turns away.

“Does it belong to anyone?” Leo asks.

He doesn’t know whose letter it came from, but he knows the building does.

He turns the letter over. Beneath the blue ink, in tiny, faded handwriting, is a message about a lost blue bird.

Leo waits until the sun goes down. Then he opens the letter and reads it again.

He does not open the door. He does not run.

Instead, he places his hand on the blue postmark and lets the ink guide him toward the building.
