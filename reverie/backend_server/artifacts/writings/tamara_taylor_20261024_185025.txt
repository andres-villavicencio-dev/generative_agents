I woke at six and made my way to breakfast. I liked the quiet rhythm of the morning, especially before the work began. I wanted to be patient with every idea, to give each story enough room to grow without rushing it.

For the next chapter, I was thinking about a building that remembers everything. A historic structure that holds secrets behind its beautiful walls. I imagined it as a place where old letters could still move, where blue ink might still be possible, and where a single postmark could change what the reader believes.

In the middle, I wanted to show the characters exploring this place. I did not decide the conflict yet. Instead, I let them notice small details: a letter tucked beneath the door, a shadow moving through the windows, and the faint blue stain along the hallway. I wanted the mystery to feel discovered rather than explained.

I also wanted the story to end on a question rather than a neat answer. The building should not give up its secrets immediately. The characters should discover something surprising, but without forcing the reader to solve every puzzle.

I am still balancing my comics with these stories, but I do not feel rushed. I want the children’s book to feel like a journey they can carry into their own lives. That means the middle should carry the reader gently, with questions that are still waiting to be answered.

Tomorrow, I will finish the opening and begin writing the middle. One more day before the final draft.
