Wednesday, February 15, 8:14 AM

I woke up at six, stretched my legs, and made my bed. By eight, I had breakfast and was ready to think. I had a lot of work today. The adult comic is still waiting for me, while the opening scene of the children’s book is where I want to begin. I’m trying to make the setting feel like a quiet, magical place where a little girl might find a story.

There should be a porch, and the morning sun should be warm enough to warm the pages. I imagine the house as a cozy little nest—soft yellow curtains, a wooden table with a silver coffee pot, and a door that opens to a green garden. A little girl, maybe named Lily, should be sitting there with a worn notebook. She can’t tell anyone that she is writing, but she has already written something special. The house should feel alive, almost like the pages of the book she is about to turn.

I will describe the porch as a place where the air feels bright and full of possibility. I will make the shadows longer, the morning dew on the grass, and the sound of birds beginning to sing. The garden should look like a secret world, with flowers opening slowly and branches reaching toward the sky. I will let the little girl’s notebook stand open by the door, its pages filled with words that feel like they were written by someone very patient and very kind.

By nine, I will have finished the first few lines and saved the opening. I will then carry the work-over to the study for Jennifer Moore later in the afternoon. I will not rush, because the story deserves the time it needs.
