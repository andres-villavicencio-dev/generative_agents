My first thought was that the opening scene should feel like a quiet morning, a little magic beginning in ordinary places. I want the children to arrive before the story is fully explained, and their wonder should come first.

I rewrote the opening paragraph:

The sky over Oakhaven was painted with soft pink clouds, and the air smelled like rain and warm honey. A small wooden boat drifted through the still water, its single oar resting gently against the edge. Inside, a little girl named Maya looked at the birds, then at her mother, and finally at the tiny house that stood beside the river. “Is the house special?” she asked.

“I know,” said Mrs. Higgins. “But I haven’t seen it yet.”

The birds fluttered around the house, and for a moment, every window seemed to glow with the same warm light. Maya remembered something she had read in her storybook: that every home has a secret, and sometimes a child is the only one who can find it.

I hope the opening scene feels safe, curious, and full of possibility. Maya’s wonder should guide the first few pages, and I want the children to feel like they are discovering the story rather than being told it.
