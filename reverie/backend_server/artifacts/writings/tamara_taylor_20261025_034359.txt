The first scene of The Little Moon’s Map opens with a small village nestled among gray hills. In the distance, the Moon rests high above, though tonight the sky is unusually dark.

“Goodnight, Moon,” whispered the Moon’s owl friend, Barnaby.

The Moon did not answer. Instead, she opened one small window at the base of her hill. From within, a tiny silver creature tumbled out—a mouse who had been carried all the way by the Moon herself.

“Where have you been?” asked the mouse, whose name was Pip.

“I went to the Moon’s library,” the Moon said softly. “She wanted to borrow a book that never ended.”

Pip laughed. “That’s impossible. The Moon doesn’t read books.”

She smiled, and the window grew a little brighter.

“Then you must have found it,” she said.

Pip’s tail twitched. “I found a page.”

The Moon’s window grew wider. Through it, we saw not a library, but a tiny garden where every flower had a secret.

“We are not where we are supposed to be,” said the Moon. “We are here because we belong here.”

Pip looked around the dark village. The houses held lanterns. The trees had leaves that shifted when the wind blew. Even the ground seemed to be breathing.

“I didn’t mean to go all the way,” Pip said. “But this… this is wonderful.”

The Moon’s window turned golden.

“Welcome,” she whispered. “Welcome to the beginning.”

Outside, the first star appeared. The mouse looked at the Moon, then at her window, and smiled.

“Maybe,” Pip said, “the Moon is not as lonely as she looks.”

The Moon laughed, and the first star grew brighter.

For now, that was enough.
