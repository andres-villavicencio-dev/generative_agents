The next morning, I woke before dawn. By 6:30, the house was quiet, and the pages of my new children’s book were still gathering dust on the floor. I turned the pages over and over, imagining what the little characters might see if they could see my hands.

At 7:00, I made my breakfast. It was a bowl of oatmeal with a little honey and a cinnamon stick. I ate slowly, watching the steam rise from the bowl. Then I went to the kitchen counter and picked up a scrap of paper.

“Let’s make a choice,” I said to myself.

The paper was blank. I didn’t have to write the whole story. I only had to make one decision.

I chose to write a chapter where the main character helps a lost animal find its way home. Not a big, dramatic rescue. Just a small act of kindness.

I wrote:

Elara had followed the shiny river. It seemed to carry secrets. She found a bird with a paper bag in its beak. The bird shook it gently and pointed to the old oak tree.

Elara followed. The branches were tangled, the birds were loud, and the river kept changing its color. But she remembered the bird’s song—a tiny, trembling melody.

At the tree, she saw the creature. It was a rabbit, wearing a scarf made of leaves. It had lost its way.

Elara didn’t know how to help. She didn’t know how to save the world. All she knew was that the bird had asked her to help.

She knelt beside the rabbit. “I can’t do it alone,” she said.

The rabbit looked at her. “Then who will?”

Elara smiled. “I’ll help.”

She helped the rabbit climb over the branches. They traveled together for a moment, watching the world shrink into green shadows and warm light. Then the rabbit let Elara go.

Elara stood at the tree and looked back. The bird was gone.

At home, she wrote in the margin: The rabbit had been waiting for someone who would listen.

I put the paper away. Tomorrow, I will choose another choice.

Perhaps tomorrow, I will choose to help a girl who is afraid to be alone.

For now, I have my breakfast, my housemate Carmen, and the quiet confidence that every small act of kindness can change the world.
