I just finished a page for the adult comic series, and I wanted to save it so I could read it again and then return to the outline. I also wanted to make sure every sentence made sense.

I was sitting in my chair at my desk, holding my pen, when I heard the front door open. It was Carmen Ortiz. I usually didn’t talk to her often, but lately I’ve found myself wanting to.

“Tamara,” Carmen said, stepping into the room. “You’ve been working late.”

“I was finishing a page,” I said, not taking my eyes off the paper. “I saved it to review it.”

“Saving it isn’t the same as finishing,” Carmen said gently. “You’re going to keep writing, but you should also look at what you’ve written.”

That was the first time I’d spoken to her this way. I hadn’t expected it to be easy.

I looked at the page. The story was about a young boy who could turn ordinary objects into magic, but the magic had a price. I had imagined the boy carefully, because if he chose the wrong path, the magic could disappear. I wanted to show him that sometimes the most important choices are the ones we make quietly.

I began to rewrite the next scene, pausing to make sure the emotion stayed clear. I wanted every character to feel real, even when the plot required them to change. Carmen didn’t say anything while I worked. I didn’t need to ask permission. I just wanted to finish what I started.

At ten o’clock, I went to bed. I wanted to think about the comic page in my mind.

At six the next morning, I woke up, ate breakfast, and picked up my notebook. I began the outline again. The characters were still waiting for me.

I smiled. I had another day.
