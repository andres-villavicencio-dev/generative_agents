Tuesday, February 14, 6:00 AM

I woke up before the sun had even reached the window, because my mind was already awake. Today is rest day, and I have no appointment, no task, and no pressure. That’s exactly what I needed.

I stretched my arms over my head, let my legs go limp, and opened the manuscript. The last chapter, “The Library of Lost Letters,” feels nearly finished. It is full of curious creatures, strange places, and the kind of magic that makes young readers pause and smile. But I also need to remember that stories need space to breathe. I want the opening to feel alive, not rushed, and I want the first character to arrive with the same surprise I did when I first decided to write this book.

I set the pen down and walked around the room, listening to the quiet room. The clock showed 6:02. I turned back to the page.

The first character is little Leo, who lives in a small library with a door that doesn’t have a handle. He is three years old, with hair like golden wheat and a curiosity that looks like two little stars. One morning, the library goes quiet, and the letters in the shelves begin to hum. Leo does not know why. He only knows that his favorite toy, a silver compass, has been missing for three days.

“Where did it go?” he asks me.

I do not answer. I simply sit beside him and hold out my hand.

“I don’t know, little one,” I say. “But somewhere, someone was looking for it.”
