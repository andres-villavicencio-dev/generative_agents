Today is my rest day, but that doesn’t mean I can stop imagining. I woke at six, brushed my teeth, and read the outstanding chapter for The Little Bear Who Found a Moonstone. I’m still halfway through it, and I’m thinking about the little creature in the story: a bear who’s afraid of the dark, but whose heart is made of something very precious.

While I worked on that, Carmen Ortiz stopped by to chat about the chocolate tart I made for the story. She said, “You made it really beautifully.” I smiled and replied, “Thank you. I’m trying to capture its color, smell, and warmth in the pages.” Carmen laughed and offered me some leftover dough, which I shared with a few friends. We talked about the story, the characters, and the kind of magic I want readers to feel.

As I ate, I remembered that patience is the most important ingredient in my work. I let the characters breathe, let the descriptions settle, and let the ending unfold slowly. Sometimes I get nervous, especially when the story is still forming, but Carmen reminds me that every draft is just the beginning. What matters is that the story feels alive.

I’m still organizing the manuscript, checking the lines, and planning my next creative session. I also want to take time tonight to enjoy some quiet time with my favorite book and a warm cup of tea. I’m tired, but I’m not done with the story. I’m just pausing so I can think clearly.

Tomorrow, I’ll finish the chapter, then read it again, and give it a gentle review. Even on my rest day, I’m still a writer—just a writer who’s learning how to rest without losing the spark.
