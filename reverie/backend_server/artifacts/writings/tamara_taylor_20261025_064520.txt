My favorite fairy tale is one where the world never ends. At the end of it, everyone is happy, the clouds make music, and the old stone house has a warm fire. I’ve been imagining such stories for years, because I believe children’s books can be both magical and gently ordinary.

I am writing for a young girl named Elara, who lives in the Whispering Woods with her mother, a baker named Mrs. Halloway, and a little black cat named Pip. One evening, Elara discovers that the leaves on the oak tree turn into tiny lanterns whenever the moon rises. She wants to collect them all. Mrs. Halloway says, “That is not a game, Elara. The moon is not a toy.”

Elara does not give up. She begins by watching the leaves, listening to the wind, and making a little lantern from a pinecone and a few twigs. Soon, the lanterns glow softly, and the moonlight seems to reach the darkest places. The woods begin to change, and the creatures there seem to understand her. They do not chase her away, and they do not demand that she become something they do not understand.

On the last night, the lanterns make a sound like a thousand bells. The moon smiles down and says, “You have found the forest’s secret, Elara. It is not a treasure. It is a promise.”

Elara writes the story down on a piece of paper, then keeps the lantern in her pocket. She is tired, but she does not sleep yet. She has a chapter to finish, and she wants to make sure every word has a gentle heart.

Tonight, I will finish the chapter. Then I will organize it, read it aloud, and set my pen down. I do not have to finish every book at once. I can rest now, with the moon and the lantern, and tomorrow I will begin again.
