Tuesday, February 14

I woke up at 6:00 AM, let my body settle, and took a slow breath. Today is my rest day, and I want the day to be kind to me and to the stories I love. I’ll make room for writing, but I won’t force it.

First, I’ll review the outstanding children’s book chapter I haven’t finished yet. It’s a little longer than I anticipated, and I want to make sure the pacing feels right. I’ll read through it again, noting where the dialogue comes alive and where I might need to slow down. If I reach for a solution too quickly, I may lose the wonder of the story.

Then I’ll sort the manuscript into three sections: chapters, character notes, and unfinished scenes. Chapter-by-chapter, I’ll group the unfinished scenes by their setting and emotional focus. Character notes should list who I’m still imagining, what each character is afraid of, and what they’ve learned so far. I don’t need every detail yet—sometimes the strongest ideas arrive quietly.

After sorting, I’ll set a simple, honest goal: complete the next unfinished scene, or improve the chapter I’m most proud of. No big edits, no rewriting everything. Just enough work so the story feels connected.

I will also take a quiet moment to breathe. I’ve been busy lately, and I want to remember that my rest isn’t punishment. It’s part of the work.

At ten, I’ll go to bed and let my mind drift. Tomorrow, I’ll return with a clearer sense of where the story is heading.
