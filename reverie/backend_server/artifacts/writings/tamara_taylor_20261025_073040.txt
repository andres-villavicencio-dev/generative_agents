Today is my rest day. My body is tired, and my mind is full of unfinished ideas. I have been working on a revised chapter from The Bridge of Forgotten Stars, and although I’ve made good progress, I need to slow down and think about what matters most.

I woke at six, made my morning coffee, and opened the manuscript. I wanted to read every page, but instead I let myself notice the small things. The opening feels strong—the storm outside keeps the characters together, and their courage shows when they least expect it. But the middle slows down. The emotions are there, but they sometimes feel like they’re racing too fast. I want them to carry the chapter with them.

I’ll take a short walk this morning to clear my mind, then return to the manuscript with a fresh perspective. I want this chapter to feel like a quiet conversation between two friends who have been learning how to trust each other again.

For now, I’ll keep writing the ending, but I won’t force every ending to match the beginning. Sometimes the strongest story is simply the path between two places. I’ll give it a title and a final line to end on, but I will not rush the rest of the manuscript.

I will also organize the remaining chapters before our creative-writing session. That way, I can focus on the writing itself and not worry about the whole book while I’m in the mood.

Tonight, I will eat dinner, make a cup of tea, and listen to a little story I wrote on the way back from the walk. I may not be finished yet, but I am beginning to feel like I’ve made the chapter I wanted.

Tomorrow, I will wake up at six, finish the morning routine, and review the manuscript again. I will not stop now, but I will not either push myself. I have a rest day. I am allowed to enjoy the quiet.
