Today is February 14, and I woke up at six in the morning. The sun felt bright and warm, and I took a moment to make sure my bedroom was tidy before heading out. I had a rest day, but I also had important work to finish before I could truly enjoy the day.

I reviewed the outstanding children’s book chapter. The story about the little rabbit who saved a forgotten seed was beautiful, but I felt I could improve it. I could make the rabbits’ emotions clearer, let the reader feel the rain, and give the rabbit a stronger reason to act. I wanted the ending to feel earned, not just surprising.

At lunch, I ate a quiet meal with my favorite tea and remembered how much patience I have for my writing. I also remembered how kind I am to my readers, who deserve stories that make them feel seen. That thought made me pause and notice something small I had overlooked.

My manuscript is growing, and I’m grateful for every word that has found its way into it. I should keep working carefully, but I should also protect the parts of myself that make me human. The creative-writing session will help me focus, but I should not rush. I want the writing session to feel like a gift, not a deadline.

I will write one chapter before the rest of the day. Then I’ll organize the manuscript, set aside space for creative thoughts, and make time to rest afterward. I will still be kind to my work, and I will still protect the time I need to create it.

Tomorrow, I begin the session. I will notice the best story possibilities, choose them carefully, and write with patience and imagination. Every page is a chance to share a little wonder with another child.
