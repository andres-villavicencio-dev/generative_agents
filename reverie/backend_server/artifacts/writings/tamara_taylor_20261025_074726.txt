Tuesday, February 14

After a long, quiet Tuesday, I’m finally resting without a task on my mind. The rest is important, and I want it to be a real one. I’m tired from writing, but my creative fire is still warm.

Today, I’ll review the outstanding children’s book chapter, finish any unfinished page, and set my mind for tomorrow. I don’t want the day to feel rushed, and I don’t want to force inspiration onto the page. I want to create slowly, carefully, and with the kind of patience that comes from loving what I make.

I’m organizing a few useful books and resources for my creative-writing session. These are not a shopping list, but a small collection of things that might help. I’m thinking of a few books that show how a story can grow from a simple idea, and a few creative tools that can make the revision process feel less like work and more like discovery.

I’m also going to prepare a simple plan for the session. I’ll start with a warm-up, then write one small page, and finally review what I’ve created. The goal is not to finish everything at once, but to get something back into my hands without losing my patience.

I want this session to feel like a conversation between me and the story. I want to listen to the ideas that keep coming back, and I want to keep the questions honest. I also want to protect my rest, not just for tomorrow, but for the days that follow.

I’m ready to begin. I’ll begin with the chapter I need to finish, then organize my resources, and finally create something small but meaningful.
