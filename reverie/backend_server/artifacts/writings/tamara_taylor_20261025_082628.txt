The morning light slipped through the window like a soft golden hand. I woke at six, brushed my teeth, and poured a cup of tea. Today was my rest day—February 14, and I wanted to make every moment feel special.

I opened my manuscript and found a chapter I had been meaning to finish. It was about a little girl who kept losing her way. Each time she followed a path, the world seemed to pull her back, even when she tried to take a different direction. Her name was Elara, and she had no idea why the shadows kept whispering her own name.

I wrote the scene where Elara found a small, glowing object tucked beneath a loose stone. It was warm to the touch and hummed with a faint tune. As she followed it, the forest changed. Trees grew in shapes that reminded me of letters. Birds sang in patterns that made my mind wander. She began to notice details others ignored: the way a moss patch shimmered, the sound of a beetle’s wing, the color of light through a crack in the bark.

By the end of the chapter, Elara had found the stone. But she did not follow it all the way home. She stopped in a clearing where the trees had changed into something more familiar. The path had ended there.

I finished the sentence: The path did not lead anywhere. It simply remembered where she had been.

I saved the chapter and took a few quiet moments to breathe. Today, I will organize the remaining pages, set the manuscript aside, and return tomorrow. I will also make tea and read a little something new. The rest day is not meant to be empty—it is meant to be full of small, gentle things.

I closed the manuscript and walked toward the window. Outside, the trees were still holding their shapes. I was ready to go to bed at ten, but I already had the morning in my heart.
