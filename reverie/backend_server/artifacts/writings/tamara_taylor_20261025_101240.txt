I woke at six. The sun had just crested the hill, and I was already out the door. After a quick shower and a cup of tea, I ate breakfast with my favorite muffin. By seven, I had finished the morning routine and made my way to my desk.

I was thinking about the opening scene for The Little Star in the Well. I imagined a small girl standing at the edge of the well, looking down at a bright star. She should feel curious, not scared. The story is about how a little person can change a big problem, one small thing at a time.

I drafted the opening scene again. The first paragraph is about the girl noticing the star. I changed the original opening because she should not already know the stars are special. That would make her seem like she already understands everything. Instead, I begin with something simple: she sees the star shining through the dark well.

For the dialogue, I used the girl’s voice.

“Are the stars above the well?” she asked.

The old well answered with a quiet sound, like the wind passing between the stones.

“Do the stars want to be seen?” the girl asked again.

The well did not answer in words. It answered with the light.

I wanted the child reader to hear the well speaking. So I made the dialogue sound gentle, with a pause at the end of each question. I also gave the stars a voice, but made their language softer than the girl’s.

I wrote:

“The stars want to be seen, little one,” the well said. “But their light is not ready to shine.”

“I know,” the girl replied. “But I have a wish.”

She held out a small hand.

“I wish the moon would come down here,” she said.

The well looked at the moon.

“Then come down and bring it down,” the well said.

The moon came down.

That’s the opening scene I wanted to show. A small girl who asks, a well that listens, and a moon that gives. I want the reader to feel that wonder without making it feel like magic belongs only to grown-ups.

I’ll revise the next paragraph and check the dialogue tags. They should feel natural for a child’s reader and easy to hear. I’ll also keep the story’s rhythm simple and warm.

By nine, I finished the opening draft and saved it. I’ll carry over the study with Jennifer Moore later this afternoon, then turn the next paragraph into a short scene for the children’s book.
