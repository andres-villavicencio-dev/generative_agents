The Little Lantern at the Door

The house was quiet on the morning when it should have been awake.

The curtains held their breath, the floorboards didn’t creak, and the old kitchen lamp blinked once in the corner as if holding its last breath.

Then—the key turned.

A light appeared in the doorway.

Not from the window. Not from the street. It came from beneath the rug, where a tiny lantern had been hiding.

Inside, a small owl with two yellow eyes sat perched on the handle.

Its feathers were still fluffy, and its tail twitched once.

“Good morning,” the owl said. “Are you… ready to see the world?”

The door swung open.

A girl stepped through. She wore a coat of gray wool and carried a lantern that looked like it belonged to someone else.

“I’m ready,” she said. “But only when I’ve made sure the door is warm.”

The owl blinked. “Then you have a long walk ahead of you, little girl.”

The girl smiled. “I’m not afraid of the dark.”

She stepped inside.

The room was empty.

Except for the lantern.

And something else.

A small creature with a lantern of its own.

It was small enough to fit between two fingers. Its eyes were bright blue, and it was making a soft humming sound.

The owl hopped down.

“I don’t know where you came from,” the girl said. “But I can’t let you disappear now.”

“I’m not afraid of anything,” the owl replied. “Not unless you make me.”

The girl nodded.

She picked up the lantern.

“Come along,” she said.

The owl took a little step.

Then another.

And when they reached the door, the world was still waiting.

But the light was still there.

And something small was watching them.
