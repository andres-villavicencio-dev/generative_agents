I woke at 6:00 a.m., brushed my teeth, and drank a cup of tea before sitting down to write. The morning felt still, like the first quiet moments before a story begins. I ate breakfast slowly, choosing something simple but satisfying—warm oatmeal with fruit—and let the sun settle over the kitchen.

For the children’s book, I wanted the opening to feel like a door opening: bright, welcoming, and full of possibility. The opening scene shows a little girl named Maya standing at the edge of a quiet road, looking down at the world beyond the trees. A small animal is hopping near a path she hasn’t noticed yet. Maya’s eyes widen, and she takes a small step toward the path. Her movements should feel natural and curious, not rushed.

I’ve already written the first page. The animal is a fox, and the path is made of old leaves. Maya reaches out, but her hand hesitates. I’m wondering whether that hesitation matters. Does she see the fox? Is she afraid of being found? Or is she simply curious? The answer changes everything.

I’ll revise the opening scene later this afternoon while I study with Jennifer. I’ll check that Maya’s age, the road, the animal, and the way Maya talks stay consistent with the tone and characters I’ve already established. I also want to make sure the ending of the opening scene leads directly into the next page, without losing the child’s imagination or the story’s gentle direction.

There is no confirmed appointment for today, so I’ll carry forward this work without pressure. I’ll return to the manuscript at a steady pace, letting each change feel like a small step forward.
