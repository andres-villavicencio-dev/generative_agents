Good morning, everyone. I hope you’ve had a restful Wednesday. I’m still finishing the opening scene for The Little Bear and the Midnight Moon, and I want to make sure it feels warm, clear, and inviting.

I’ve been asking myself whether the opening should simply introduce the bear, or whether it should already show what the bear is feeling and what the story is about. I think the best approach is to begin with a quiet moment that makes the young reader want to keep turning the page. Let’s start with the bear waking up after a long winter sleep.

The bear stirred. The moon was silver over the lake. Soft shadows moved across the mossy ground. He opened his eyes and found a small patch of sunlight on his paw.

The bear looked down at his paw. It was warm. A little bird had returned to his nest.

The tiny bird landed on the bear’s nose.

“Good morning, little friend!” the bird chirped. “Did you sleep well?”

“I did,” the bear said. “I dreamed of the moon.”

The bird smiled. “Then you’ll dream again tonight.”

Before the bird could say anything else, the wind blew softly. The moon grew brighter, and a tiny shadow of a deer appeared in the tall grass.

The deer did not move. He simply watched the bear.

The bear’s eyes widened. “You’re awake,” he said.

“I am,” the deer replied. “I have been watching you since the beginning.”

The bear blinked. “I… did I dream about you?”

“I have been waiting for you to speak,” the deer said gently. “And now, little friend, you have.”

The bear closed his eyes. “I was afraid I was too old to make friends,” he whispered.

“You were not afraid,” the deer said. “You were afraid. That is how all children begin. And that is how all stories begin.”

The deer disappeared. The bird flew away. The bear stood alone in the quiet dawn.

For the first time, he smiled.

The opening scene should not explain everything. It should show the bear’s feelings, set the scene, and offer a small question to help the young reader begin wondering what is coming next. If the opening is this clear and warm, the rest of the book will have a gentle anchor.
