The story begins with the morning before the village was too bright to see.

I woke at six, opened the curtains, and looked out at the pale blue sky. A little bird landed on the fence and chirped a note so soft it sounded like a question. I smiled and followed it up to the oak tree, where three other birds had already been waiting.

“Good morning, little ones,” I whispered to the birds.

They fluttered down and settled beside me. One asked, “Is the sun awake?”

The other answered, “Yes, it is.”

We watched the sun rise one small light at a time, turning the gray ground into a golden rug. That was how I wanted the opening scene to feel: patient, kind, and full of small beginnings.

I revised the opening dialogue for the children’s book, keeping the birds’ questions and the bird’s soft question. I also tightened the setting description. The village should feel like a quiet place where every street knows its name, and where even the most ordinary things seem special. I added a line about a little lantern that hung from a branch, glowing with the morning.

“Good morning, little ones,” I said to the birds, “and to all the quiet places.”

They answered with a chorus of tiny chirps. I turned to my notebook and wrote down the scene. Then I looked out the window again. The bird was still there. Maybe today is a good day to keep going.
