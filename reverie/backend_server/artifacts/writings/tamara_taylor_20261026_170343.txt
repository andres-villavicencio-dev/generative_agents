I woke at six, the quiet of the house settling around me. Today was my rest day, but I also had work to carry. First, I finished the outstanding chapter for The Quiet Garden, then organized the manuscript, and lastly, I prepared for our creative-writing session with the rest of the team.

The garden belongs to every creature, from the tiny bluebird with a silver crest to the old stone owl who remembers every rainstorm. In the old tale, a frightened mouse had lost his way. Not because of a bad night, but because he had forgotten to listen. He was afraid of the dark, and he had forgotten that the dark was not a monster—it was simply the garden breathing.

I wrote that moment because it could make a small reader feel seen. A child might have been frightened by the dark, too. Tamara, the mouse, was not a hero. He was small, he was afraid, and he had a quiet heart. That was enough.

I also wanted to make the ending gentle. The mouse should not be told to be brave. He should simply be allowed to stand beside the flower. The garden is full of kindness; the mouse could have found it.

While I wrote, the house settled around me. I opened the manuscript and read aloud, letting the quiet garden fill the room. Tomorrow, I will review the chapter again, polish the language, and make sure every page carries the same warm promise: that every creature, no matter how small, has a voice worth listening to.
