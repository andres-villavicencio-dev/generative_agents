Wednesday, February 15

I woke at 6:00 AM, the same time I’ve done this for twenty years. The sun came through the blinds just enough to make the room feel warm, and for a moment I could hear the quiet of the house settling down. I made my coffee, brushed my hair, and looked back at the manuscript on my desk.

Chapter four is still unfinished. I’ve been stuck on the last few pages for two days, but I don’t want to leave any part of the story to chance. So I’ll write through it, even if it means going back and fixing what I wrote earlier. Sometimes I think my patience is too easy to trust. I’ve learned that patience is a gift, not a weakness.

At lunch I ate a warm salad and talked quietly with my editor. She asked me to check the ending again. I agreed, but I didn’t read it to her. I wanted to keep discovering what felt right. I’m not perfect, and I’m not always right. But I know what I want the story to say.

After lunch, I organized the notes for the creative-writing session. I sorted the drafts by chapter, grouped the research on birds and forests, and placed the writing aids in order. The workspace felt quieter now, but it wasn’t perfect. Still, I was grateful for the quiet.

Around 7:00, I ate dinner. Then I went to bed. I didn’t read the manuscript before sleep; I wanted to remember the parts I had written. I wanted to carry them with me without carrying the weight of everything I hadn’t finished yet.

Tomorrow, I’ll return to the chapter. I’ll finish the ending, and then I’ll let the story rest.
