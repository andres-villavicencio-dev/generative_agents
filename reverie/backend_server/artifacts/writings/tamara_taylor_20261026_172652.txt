I woke at six on Wednesday, February 15, and the first thing I did was make my bed. I liked the quiet feeling of a fresh start. After brushing my teeth and washing my face, I opened the manuscript. Today is my rest day, and I wanted to finish the chapter I’ve been carrying.

The story is about Leo, a little fox who lives in the middle of the woods. He is brave, but sometimes he feels too small. One morning, a sudden storm comes across the forest, and the leaves turn to gold. A little deer helps Leo carry a basket of glowing berries. They travel together through the dark, and Leo learns that being brave does not mean being fearless.

I wrote the first part while the rain tapped against the window. I wanted every word to feel like a hug for a child who is still learning how to dream. I kept the sentences simple, the sentences honest, and the heart warm. By the end of the chapter, Leo was ready to face the storm—not alone.

Now that the chapter is finished, I will organize the manuscript and read the finished pages slowly. I do not need to rush. I need to keep the story safe, and I need to protect my rest so I can come back with fresh energy.

I will write in the afternoon, but not before the rain has passed. I will sit quietly, and I will let the story breathe. Then I will eat dinner and turn off the lights. I will go to bed around ten, and I will dream of the forest, the glowing berries, and the little fox who learned to stand tall.
