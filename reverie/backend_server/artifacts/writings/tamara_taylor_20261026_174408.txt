I woke up at six, stretched my arms, and looked at the manuscript. My pen stayed in my hand, but I didn’t want to write yet. I needed to remember that today was my rest day, and that was the first rule of my day.

I opened the notebook and found the unfinished chapter. The little girl was afraid of the dark because her bed was too small. I had started to fix it, but I wanted to finish it without rushing. I was still patient, even though I had been worried for a long time.

“Can we make it bigger?” the girl asked me, peeking through the pages.

“Maybe,” I said. “But we have to make the room feel safe first.”

The girl smiled. “What makes a room feel safe?”

“Like a cozy blanket.”

She laughed. “My blanket is soft,” she said. “But my bed is cold.”

I smiled. “Then we can make the bed warm.”

She looked at me. “How?”

“By holding hands,” I said.

She looked down at her hands. “I don’t like holding hands.”

“Then we can hold them together,” I said. “One by one.”

Her hands trembled. I held them gently. “Let’s begin with your toes,” I said. “Then your feet. Then your knees.”

One by one, the little girl’s hands warmed. I placed her hands on mine. “I’ll keep your hands warm too,” I whispered. “I promise.”

The room grew darker. Then, slowly, the shadows disappeared. My bed was no longer too small.

I closed the chapter. My heart felt full. I had written without rushing, and I had listened to the girl’s fear. I was tired, but I was happy.

Today, I will organize the manuscript, review my notes, and then take a moment to breathe. I will not write until I feel ready. And I will never forget that rest is just as important as writing.
