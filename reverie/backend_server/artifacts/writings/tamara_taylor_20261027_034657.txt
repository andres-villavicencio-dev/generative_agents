I woke at six, breathed in the morning air, and made my bed. I had an important chapter in mind: The Little Bear Who Learned to Listen. It’s a quiet story about a bear who was always too busy trying to be brave. He chased every squirrel, climbed the tallest tree, and shouted the loudest “YES!” when he could. But he never heard the soft rustle of the wind or the gentle hum of the moon.

That’s what I wanted to show him.

I reread the draft, tracing the sentences where the bear’s noise became his only friend. The ending felt too neat. He still didn’t understand what listening meant. He still thought sharing was the only way to help. I needed to give him a little more time. Maybe he could learn that kindness does not always come in a big gesture.

As I wrote, I remembered my rest day. I had not finished any manuscript today. I had not organized my notes. I had not prepared for the creative-writing session. But the chapter was ready. I could write it all in one day.

I closed the notebook and took one slow breath. Tomorrow, I would carry on. For now, I would be kind to the bear, the trees, and the quiet sounds that kept him company. Even when he was loud, there was still enough room for silence.
