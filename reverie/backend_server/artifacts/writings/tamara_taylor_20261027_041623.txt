I woke at six on a quiet Wednesday morning, the soft light filtering through the window like a warm blanket. My mind was still full from the last chapter I had been working on—the story of Lila, a girl who found her way home through a garden that had been hidden for too long.

I finished the draft and read it aloud to myself. The words were steady, but I had to make sure the ending felt earned. The garden shouldn’t just open up; it should feel like something Lila had carried with her.

I went to the kitchen and made tea. By six thirty, I had my notebook ready and a notebook for the next idea. Tomorrow, I need to organize the manuscript, check the spelling, and set out the pieces I want to read aloud. I need to be careful not to rush.

I also remembered to protect my rest. No more late-night revisions. My body deserves the chance to sleep, and I want to wake up with a clear mind.

I went outside and walked beneath the branches of the old oak. The wind moved through the leaves, carrying the scent of rain and soil. I imagined Lila sitting on a stone bench, drawing flowers in her book while the garden began to grow again.

I am kind to my work, I am kind to my readers, and I am kind to the quiet moments when creativity takes me. That is what makes this story special. It is not about finding a perfect ending. It is about the journey of believing that everything can be found, even when it is not where you expect it to be.

I will carry this feeling into the next chapter. I will write with patience, keep my sentences clear, and make sure Lila’s voice feels like a friend. I am ready. I am ready to begin again.
