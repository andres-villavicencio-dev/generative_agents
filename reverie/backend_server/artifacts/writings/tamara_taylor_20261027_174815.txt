My day began with the small, quiet promise to wake at six. I made the bed, brushed my teeth, and took a slow breath before sitting down to begin reviewing what I’ve been working on. The manuscript is nearly done, and I can’t wait to see the stories I’ve helped shape come to life.

Today is my rest day. That is the gift I received this Wednesday: the chance to slow down without feeling guilty. I will not rush, but I will not also delay. I will finish the chapter, organize the manuscript, and prepare for the creative-writing session, yet I will protect the moments when I simply need to breathe.

I am thinking of little Leo, whose imagination wanders from the moon to the clouds. He once said, “Every day is a new adventure, even when the sun is hiding.” That is what I want every story to feel like. Readers should not only understand the story—they should feel like it was made just for them.

I began with a small character who feared the dark. Now I am letting him discover that shadow is not a monster; it is a place where courage can grow. I also remember the quiet girl who carried a jar of seeds through a storm. She learned that hope does not disappear even when the world feels broken.

When I write, I listen for the smallest detail—the color of a shadow, the sound of rain on leaves, the way a character’s hands tremble. I want the stories to feel warm, honest, and full of possibility. I want my young readers to feel like they are discovering something beautiful.

I will work quietly, with a cup of tea and a notebook nearby. I will not expect perfection. I will focus on the story I care about most. And on top of that, I will make time for the people who support me.

Rest is not something I can escape. It is something I honor. Today, I will give myself the chance to rest, while still moving toward the writing that makes my children’s stories feel alive. I am ready. I am ready to write.
