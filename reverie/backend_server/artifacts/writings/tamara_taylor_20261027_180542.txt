The story was finished. I had finished it yesterday.

The last sentence felt like it had arrived on its own, but when I read it again, I still found the room a little too quiet. It had been so long since I had written about a girl who didn’t know she was afraid of the dark.

I picked up the manuscript and opened the pen. There were still pages with sentences that wanted to be easier, better, and more honest. I had been so focused on finishing the chapter that I had forgotten to check whether every character was still believing in themselves.

I began with the girl. Her name was Maya. She was seven, and she was holding onto the light in her room like a blanket. The blanket had been warm, but today it had been very still.

Maya said, “Why are you looking at me?”

I looked at her. “I’m looking at the blanket.”

“Then why is it so big?”

“Because it’s protecting me.”

Maya laughed. It was a small sound, like a leaf turning over in the wind.

I wrote:

Maya held the blanket. The moon was sleeping near the window, and the wind was not touching it. Even the floor seemed to be holding its breath.

“Is the blanket hungry?” Maya asked.

“No,” I said, though I did not quite believe it. “The blanket is holding you.”

“I’m not hungry,” Maya said, and she was still smiling.

I wrote on the page:

At seven o’clock, the moon came out of the window. It did not come with a sound. It came with a soft glow, like a hand holding a lantern. The blanket did not open. It did not speak. But Maya could feel it breathing.

I added another page:

The blanket grew warmer. Maya did not need to be told that the night was not alone. She had never been afraid of the dark, only of being alone in it.

I wrote:

That afternoon, Maya asked, “Will we go to bed?”

I said, “Yes.”

Maya looked at me. “But I am afraid.”

“Then sleep,” I said.

She did not need to be told. She took the blanket and tucked it under her chin.

By six o’clock, the house was very still. The wind had stopped. And Maya was asleep, her small fingers curled around the edge of the blanket, holding the world in her hand.

I finished the chapter. I set the pen down. I sat there for a long moment, letting the words settle around me like dust.

I had no appointment, no task, and no need to hurry. I could write another story tomorrow. I could write the one I had been waiting for.

I closed the manuscript. Tomorrow, I would begin again.
