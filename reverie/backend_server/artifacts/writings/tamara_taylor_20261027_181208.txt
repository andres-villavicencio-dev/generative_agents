My morning began with the quiet rustle of leaves and the soft rush of water. I reached for the sun through the window and let it settle on my face. It felt like waking up with a full heart and an idea that needed a home.

I had a chapter waiting for me. I had been working on The Little Sky-Seed, the story about a tiny seed that learned to believe that every leaf was a map, every root a path, and every cloud a place to dream. By the end of the chapter, the seed had become a little explorer, and the reader had felt brave, curious, and hopeful. That was exactly the kind of story I wanted: warm, alive, and full of possibility.

Now, on a quiet Wednesday afternoon, I am taking a little break. There is no appointment today, and no urgent task. I can review the chapter, make small changes, and organize the manuscript without the pressure of deadlines. My life is full enough without turning every moment into a task.

I’ll read the chapter aloud at my creative-writing session, checking the dialogue, the descriptions, and the emotional journey. I’ll make sure the seed doesn’t seem too small, and that the reader doesn’t feel lost along the way. Then I’ll write a little story of my own—perhaps about a bird who learns that the deepest trees speak in stillness.

For now, though, I will rest. I will let the sun warm the pages, and let the quiet of the afternoon fill the space between thoughts. Tomorrow, I will begin again.
