Today, I have no scheduled appointment and no task to finish. I am resting, though I cannot let my imagination stay idle. My mind is busy, as it often is: thinking of a day when I could dress in silk, walk in high heels, and enter a room where everyone would notice me. The daydream feels warm and inviting, like a cozy afternoon in a palace. I can almost see the golden chandelier, the soft scent of roses, and the way the light would fall across every corner.

That luxury, however, makes me feel a little lonely. I am a writer who creates stories for young children, and I know that the world around them is not always painted with gold. To turn that daydream into something more grounded, I would need to ground it in the small, ordinary moments that shape our lives. I would begin with a quiet walk in the garden, a cup of tea in a kitchen I know, or the ordinary comfort of waking up and turning on the light. I would write with honesty rather than idealizing everything. The daydream can still be beautiful, but it should not overshadow the reality of ordinary days.

I could also give the daydream a character—a tired, thoughtful child who wants to believe that tomorrow will be better. I could show the struggle to get there, not just the celebration of arrival. A story about a girl who dreams of a garden on her balcony can become more powerful when it also shows the steps she takes to make that dream possible.

For now, I will finish the outstanding chapter, organize the manuscript, and prepare for our creative-writing session. I may not turn the luxurious daydream into a book today, but I can keep it with me. It can remain a quiet reminder: even if I cannot change the world, I can still imagine it carefully, kindly, and with enough patience to make it real.
