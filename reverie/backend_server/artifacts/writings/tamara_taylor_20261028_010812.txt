Wednesday, February 15, was my rest day. I woke at six, did my morning routine, and looked once more at the unfinished chapter on the page. The Little Star That Couldn’t Shine was still waiting.

I had been worried. The ending had been fuzzy. The star’s secret had been too simple. But then I remembered how I had felt when I first wrote it—a tiny spark of hope under the page, afraid that nothing would ever happen. I wanted to carry that feeling into the story.

So I picked up a fresh notebook and wrote a new opening.

Once upon a Tuesday, a little star named Luna lived in a room that did not need light. Her lantern was small, and her lantern had no flame. But Luna did not want a flame. She wanted to show that nothing had to be big to be brave.

I wrote until my pen ran dry. Then I looked at the stars in the sky above the window. One was bright and steady. Another flickered. The brightest one did not need to be the most powerful. It was enough to be real.

The next morning, I finished the chapter. I did not feel finished, but I felt like I had found a place to begin.

I also tidied the manuscript. I grouped the pages by chapter, added notes about character feelings, and sorted the words I loved. I wanted the book to feel like a quiet place where every sentence had room to breathe.

At ten, I went to bed. I dreamed of a small lantern, and of a story that would make the reader smile while they were still asleep.

I am still waiting for the next book. That is all I need.
