It is Monday, February 13, and the house is quiet. Carmen is asleep in the other room, and I have the whole day to myself. I wake up at 6:00 AM, make my morning coffee, and sit down at my desk with the series I’ve been thinking about. I’ve been working on a comic book for adults, and so far it feels too serious for my intended audience. I want the humor, the warmth, and the surprises to match the tone of the stories I write for children.

The idea I’ve been developing is a small-town mystery set in a neighborhood where every street corner has a hidden secret. The protagonist is a young girl named Elara, who discovers that each clue is tied to a family member who has been protecting something important but has never spoken about it. I want the mystery to feel cozy and curious, like a gentle adventure rather than a thriller. The characters should be likable, flawed, and full of ordinary moments that suddenly become extraordinary.

I also want to give Elara a voice. She should not simply follow instructions; she should notice small details and ask questions that help solve the mystery. I’m planning to develop the supporting characters so that they feel like real people who share the town’s secrets. The comic should not feel like a puzzle to be forced together. It should feel like a story that unfolds naturally, with surprises that surprise, humor that comforts, and a message about kindness, curiosity, and the strength found in community.

Before I continue writing, I want to make sure the ideas fit the intended tone and audience. I’ll check that the humor isn’t too sharp, that the mystery does not become overwhelming, and that the emotional core remains steady. I also want to keep the pacing consistent, so the reader never gets lost.

This is the first step. I’ll revise the outline over the next few days, sketch a few pages, and send them to my housemate for feedback. I’m still patient, and I believe I’ll have a fun, satisfying series soon. For now, I’ll finish my coffee, go for a walk around the block, and keep writing.
