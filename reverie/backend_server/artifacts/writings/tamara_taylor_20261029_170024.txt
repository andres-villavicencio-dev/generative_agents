My morning begins with a sleepy bell, then breakfast, and finally the quiet I need to remember that I’m still here. The day feels a little full, especially because Valentine’s Day is tomorrow. I’m not planning the party yet—I’m just thinking about what I’d give the children.

I wrote a short story about a little bear named Barnaby who learned that being kind is the same as learning a new word. The bear had no friends at first, but when he shared his honey jar with a lost chick, the chick gave him a shiny feather. Barnaby’s first lesson was that sharing makes the world feel bigger.

I want every child to carry that story. They may never meet a bear with a feather, but they should learn that helping others makes everyone feel special. Maybe they’ll find a friend, or perhaps they’ll find a friend inside themselves.

At Hobbs Cafe, I’m making pancakes with Carmen. We talked about honey, eggs, bread, and vegetables, and I laughed so hard I thought the sun might peek through the kitchen window. Carmen smiled at me and said, “You’ve been thinking about this story for a long time.” I didn’t say what I’d written. I just knew that my imagination was working.

I’m excited to hear what the children say after dinner. I’m also counting the little things I can share: a song, a story, or a piece of kindness. Tomorrow, I’ll bring them all together.
