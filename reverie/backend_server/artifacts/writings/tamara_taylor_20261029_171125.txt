“Mama, I have an idea for a story about a little owl who finds a shiny, warm letter in the winter.”

“Oh, I love it,” said Carmen, smiling. “But it needs a good beginning.”

“Here it is,” Tamara said, holding out her notebook. “A little owl who wakes up on a snowy branch and hears a soft sound from the tree. The letter says, ‘You don’t have to be brave, little owl. You can be kind, and you will be heard.’”

Carmen read it aloud. “That’s lovely,” she said. “But the owl doesn’t know what to do.”

Tamara smiled. “Exactly. Let’s give him a tiny adventure. He could make a nest for a little bird who forgot how to sing. Then he could carry them home. The song could be about warmth, not bravery.”

Carmen nodded. “I’ll draw the little bird. He’ll be gray, with a bright blue tail.”

Tamara watched her hands move across the paper. “I’ll write the first page, then we’ll turn the story into a picture book.”

They laughed, the sound of eggs cracking and honey dripping into a little dish. The kitchen smelled of bread, fruit, and the quiet excitement of making something beautiful together.

“Okay,” Tamara said. “But we can’t make the bread tonight. I’m busy preparing for the Valentine’s Day party.”

“Then tomorrow,” Carmen said, “we can start with a little egg and some honey. The story will be your egg.”

“Perfect.” Tamara tucked her notebook into her bag. “Tomorrow, let’s begin again.”

Outside, the birds were singing in the branches. For the first time all day, Tamara felt ready to write, to read, and to create something that would make a child smile.
