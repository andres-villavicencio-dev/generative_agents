I read the comic book series and wrote the next children’s book story.

The opening page had a little girl named Maya. Maya sat at her wooden table with a blue cup, a tiny star-shaped paper, and a pencil that looked like it was made of moonlight. “What are you going to do with the star?” Maya asked.

“I’m going to make a kite,” she said.

But before she could begin, the wind began to howl from the window. It wasn’t a real howl. It sounded like the pages of a comic book were being turned very fast.

“Careful,” Maya whispered. “It’s not supposed to howl.”

Then the window opened.

Through the crack, a tiny figure appeared. It was a little man dressed in red who held a scroll in his hand. “Welcome, little girl,” he called out. “I’m the Wind.”

The little man pointed his scroll toward the corner of the house. There, in the corner, a tiny red door glimmered. “The gate is open,” he said. “But the gate is also very important.”

Maya looked at the scroll, then at her star, and finally at the small red door. She picked up her pencil. “What do you want me to do?”

“Draw the next chapter,” the Wind said. “And draw me in it.”

Maya smiled. She drew the little man, he drew the wind, the wind drew the door, and the door drew the story.

At bedtime, Maya sat in her chair with a cup of warm milk and a book open. The little man was in the corner, invisible and very important. He was making his first appearance in the story.

Outside, the wind howled.

Inside, Maya drew on the page.

In the corner, she drew the red door.

And the little man smiled through the pages.

He was making his way to the next chapter.

He was making his way through the whole book.

He was making his way through the next chapter.

By ten o’clock, Maya was asleep.

The little man was asleep.

The wind was asleep.

But the story was still being written.

And the little man was still drawing.

At the table, Maya picked up her pen again.

She was making a new chapter.

And she was ready for the next one.
