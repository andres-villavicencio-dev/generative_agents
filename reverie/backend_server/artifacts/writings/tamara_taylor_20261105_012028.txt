The opening pages of the book feel a little rushed, but I don’t want to change what I’ve written. It’s important to honor the story I’ve already begun.

The first page ends with Maya choosing her own path. That decision should feel important, not forced. I’ve been thinking about the little details I can keep in mind as I continue. Maya should not know everything before she makes her choice. She should feel wise, even if she does not quite understand why she is making it. The characters’ voices should carry the same weight as their words.

I also want the opening to be welcoming. The café scene, for example, should not feel like a checklist. Maya should notice the steam, the warm light, and the way the morning settles into the room. The reader should not need to be told that the world is safe. It should arrive quietly.

The comic-book series is also coming to life. I am happy that Sam, Carmen, and Latoya are still with me. Their ideas may not always agree with mine, but that is part of the story. We can still work together, even when we disagree.

I am balancing an early-shift café job, developing children’s books, and continuing the comic series. I do not need every task finished at once. I can carry a few important ideas forward and leave the rest to the next day.

By 10:00, I should be ready to go to bed. I want to wake up feeling rested and ready to meet the day’s next challenge.
