Tuesday, February 14, brought a quiet sense of purpose to my day. I woke at six and made my morning routine before the café opened. The first cup of coffee arrived with a warm, familiar scent. I worked through the reviews I had prepared and read every story aloud. I wanted to make the setting feel alive, not merely described.

I returned to the café, and Sam greeted me at the register. Carmen and Latoya were there too. We talked about the comics I have been working on and the places I’ve imagined. I had made a small story about a young baker who discovered that the flour in her shop could speak. I wanted the shop itself to feel alive—with dust motes dancing in the light, the smell of coffee in the air, and the steady rhythm of its doors.

I began writing again at the café’s counter. I imagined the shop as a place where every object remembered a part of the people who had shared their lives there. The flour was not just ingredients; it was a memory waiting to be shared. The shelves held jars of forgotten recipes and boxes of letters that had been left behind. Each detail was meant to carry a small moment of connection.

Before closing, I reviewed the opening passage and the description of the shop window. I wanted the opening to capture a sense of wonder, not simply to present the setting. I let the atmosphere settle around the baker and her work, allowing the details to reveal themselves slowly.

By ten, I was finished with the review. The shop felt warm and alive. I took one last sip of coffee and walked home. I had made room for creativity, patience, and kindness. And I was grateful for Sam, Carmen, and Latoya.
