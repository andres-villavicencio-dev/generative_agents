I woke at six and made my way into the café while Sam, Carmen, and Latoya were still getting ready. The day had been full—the café shift, a new chapter for The Little Lantern, and several unanswered questions at the comic-book desk—but I knew that purpose had been worth every moment.

By noon, I was writing. My fingers felt tired, but the story felt alive. I imagined the little lantern glowing in the dark, the characters gathering around it, and the quiet courage each one carried. I made sure the ending felt complete, without forcing a happy ending. Sometimes I’ve wondered whether the characters need a final celebration, but I wanted them to feel earned.

At five, the café was closing. I finished the final review, checked the ending for clarity, and made sure no one would miss a single detail. Then I packed up and walked out into February.

I wanted to thank Sam, Carmen, and Latoya. You’ve been patient, kind, and full of surprises. You’ve reminded me that even when the day feels heavy, good people make it lighter.

Later, I met with the comic-book team. We talked about the next chapter, and I felt proud of what we were building. The little lantern had one more mission: to light up someone’s day.

By six, I had dinner, then went home. I wanted to read something quiet, something that made me smile. I settled into my favorite chair and opened The Little Lantern.

It was a lovely story. I loved how the lantern grew from a small idea into something important. I also missed seeing Sam again.

I’m going to sleep at ten. Tomorrow, I’ll make coffee, read a few pages, and keep writing. The lantern is still glowing.
