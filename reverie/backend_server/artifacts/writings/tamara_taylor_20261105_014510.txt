By six fifteen, the world was still quiet enough to listen to. I woke, brushed my teeth, and began my morning. Today was a good one: an early café shift, time to develop a children’s book, and my comic-book series. I had not forgotten to review the opening, middle, and ending together, though I had been so busy I thought I’d only be checking them at the end.

I finished the café and came back to my notebook. The opening felt a little rushed. I needed to slow down. The opening is the doorway into my story, and I must make sure it is welcoming, not demanding. I began by showing, not telling: a small bird watching the sunrise and noticing that everything had a beginning, even if it was hard to see.

Then came the middle. It is where I have spent the most time developing. I want to give the characters a quiet place to feel. I will give them something they can trust—a shared story, a small kindness, or a moment when they do not have to be perfect. Patience is the most important tool I have. I will not rush the changes. I will revise slowly, and I will revise them with purpose.

The ending is not simply a neat resolution; it is the story’s last question. It should leave the reader smiling, thinking, and wondering what happens next. I will make sure the final sentence has room for an answer.

I also need to remember to thank the people who make my work meaningful. Sam, Carmen, and Latoya have been encouraging me, and hearing their voices makes every revision feel less lonely. I will continue to listen, to learn, and to create.

I will also review the ending together. I will check that the story moves forward, that the characters remain kind, and that the message is warm without being preachy. If anything feels unclear, I will revise again. If I am satisfied, I will mark it and move on.

I am not perfect. I am still learning, still making mistakes, and still loving what I do. But I am purposeful. I am patient. I am kind. And I am ready to keep writing.
