It’s 6:00 AM. I wake before the house settles, brush my teeth, and turn on the shower. The house is quiet, so I make a cup of tea while I watch the curtains open and let the morning wash over me. Outside, the city is still waking up—traffic turning into a line, people stepping from their cars, the streetlights beginning to glow. I’m not afraid of that rush. It’s the kind of rhythm I’ve loved in books for years: movement, beginnings, and endings.

Now I’m drying my hair and applying sunscreen. I want the skin to feel warm, protected, and ready for the day ahead. I’ll use the bathroom, then take a quick shower. The clinic is waiting for me, and the café will be busy by lunch. I’ve got the children’s manuscript in draft, and the adult comic book manuscript is still getting its first pages. I’m proud of what I’ve written, and I’m proud of the neighborhood where I belong.

By 7:30 AM, I’ll be ready to work. After that, I’ll make the coffee, get breakfast, and check the emails. The day will feel demanding, but I know my schedule helps me stay in control. I read, I cook, I laugh, and I give time to the stories that matter. I’ve applied to the Johnson Park board and Willows Market. I’ve kept the good work coming. And I’m hopeful that these opportunities will bring people together.

Around 10:00 AM, I’ll be back home. I’ll work on the comic, then go to bed before midnight. The world is full of people doing important work, and I’m glad I’m part of it. I have the morning routine down, and I have the day ahead of me. I’m not waiting for anything. I’m just getting ready to begin.
