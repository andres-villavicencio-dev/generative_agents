The next part of the comic was still in my hands. I had been staring at the first panel for twenty minutes, the ink spreading around the little girl’s smile. I wanted to change the beginning. I wanted the street to be colorful, the rain to stop, and the girl’s face to look happier.

But I didn’t.

I looked at the outline again. It was simple: a young girl discovers a hidden doorway in her attic, finds a book with words that make her see the world differently, and follows the story until it becomes something she can carry. No dragons. No time travel. Just small, bright things.

I wrote the next sentence. I let the pen wander, and then I stopped. I let the words breathe.

The girl, Maya, stepped across the threshold. The house didn’t open; it invited. A door appeared behind her. Behind it was another room, full of pictures that seemed to move. In one corner sat a tiny clock with hands made of stars.

“Where is the clock?” Maya asked.

The door whispered something.

The stars on the clock began to move. One by one, they turned gold and then blue. The girl reached out. Her hand touched a small flower growing out of the floor. For a moment, she saw the flower’s roots as tiny bridges connecting the attic to the outside world.

Outside, the clouds began to fall. Not rain. Rain made of silver bubbles that floated up into the sky.

Maya smiled. She picked up the book.

“Where did it come from?” she asked.

The door was still there. It had been opening and closing all morning.

The answer came in a book voice that sounded like a bird waking up in spring.

“From everywhere,” the book said. “And nowhere.”

Maya looked at the stars on the clock. They had stopped moving.

The next day, the attic door stayed open. Maya drew every picture she could find and made a little story with it. It wasn’t a perfect story. It had mistakes, little pauses, and words that kept changing. But it felt real.

I laughed and wrote the next part of the outline. The little girl and the book would find the bridge together.

That was enough for today. I finished dinner and went to bed around ten. Tomorrow, I’ll start again.
