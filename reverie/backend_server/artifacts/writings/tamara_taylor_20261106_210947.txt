My housemate Carmen and I are making an afternoon, so I’m sitting here with a cup of coffee, looking at my comic-book outline. The first story is finished, and the second part is still waiting for a little more magic.

It’s about Maya, a little girl who collects ordinary objects and turns them into stories. When Maya finds a blue pencil, she thinks it knows something. The next day, the pencil leads her through a forest where every tree tells a different story. She meets a fox who carries a message, a bear who carries a map, and a bird who carries a song. Each character has a problem that only the pencil can solve.

The comic is divided into short chapters. Each chapter should have its own beginning, middle, and ending, and the pages should feel like a picture book, but with a little more movement. I’m thinking about the color palette: blue for the forest, warm gold for the sun, and soft purple for Maya’s imagination. I also want the dialogue to sound like a child talking to someone she trusts.

The hardest part is finding a way to keep Maya curious. She shouldn’t have the answer immediately. She should keep asking questions, notice small details, and slowly discover that the pencil isn’t just a toy—it’s a bridge between ordinary moments and wonder.

I’m also thinking of a second series for adults. The first one could follow a young writer who discovers she doesn’t have to write for anyone. Maybe the second series would follow a team of ordinary people who help each other make a story come alive. I’d like to give each adult character a unique voice and a reason for wanting to create something new.

Tomorrow, I’ll read the next part of the book and write the first few pages. I want them to look at the paper, read the beginning, and think, “I’ve been waiting for this.”
