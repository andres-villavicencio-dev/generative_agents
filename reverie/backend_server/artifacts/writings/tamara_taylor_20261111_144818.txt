I’m finishing the last chapter, and I feel like I’ve been working on it for the wrong amount of time. I’ve been checking every sentence against the series writing guidelines—pacing, dialogue, character voices, the way the world feels in the story.

Carmen Ortiz is helping me out. She’s been asking the same questions over and over: “Is the ending too rushed?” “Does the character’s voice sound like a child who should be learning something else?” “What happens if the ending doesn’t feel right?”

I’m not going to let her silence me. I’ve always been patient when it comes to my work, but sometimes I think patience should be the one thing I’m most careful with.

The last chapter is about Maya, a young engineer who builds bridges out of forgotten things. Her bridge connects the two halves of the city, but it has a secret: it can only be strong if everyone helps make it stronger. Maya doesn’t know that yet. She’s too busy noticing the details.

I’m leaving with the chapter on the desk. I’ll read through it again before bed. I’ll check the formatting, the dialogue, and the lines that make Maya’s heart beat a little faster. I’ll also make sure Carmen’s suggestions are actually useful. I don’t want to keep changing the ending without checking the guidelines first.

I’m not going to give up. I’m tired, but I’m also happy. I’m tired of being perfect, but I’m not tired of writing. I’m going to let the story decide what happens next, and I’ll make sure that decision is kind to Maya.

I’ll go to bed around ten. I’ll dream about Maya’s bridge.
